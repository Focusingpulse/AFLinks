"Sheldrake's morphogenesis is supposed to touch all self-generated forms, from
crystals to embryos, from language to our behaviors. A vast ambition, which the
modern sciences can hardly integrate. Morphic resonance calls into question all
contemporary disciplines.

The Theory of Morphic Fields of Rupert Sheldrake

In his first book, A New Science of Life (1981), this young Cambridge biologist
tried to prove everything from A to Z, citing numerous striking experiments, on
crystals as well as on rats or on humans, but his approach always had the same
shortcoming as parapsychology: a great show of statistics and/or testimonies, but
the absence of a model of understanding connected to current physics.

In his book, Presence of the Past (1988, translated as The Memory of the Universe),
Rupert Sheldrake asserts that everything that happens becomes a matter of habit,
of custom. Even light, which travels at 300,000 km/s? Is it because it has taken
on this habit? Nothing would be immutable. Everything could move, could evolve.
We would be influenced by "form fields" from outside space-time, but we would in
turn influence these fields — as if a divine idea were shaping us while being
continuously shaped by us.

With his book The Rebirth of Nature (1991, translated as The Soul of Nature), the
researcher enters into philosophical reflection. After two hundred years of rabid
mechanism, he says, we stubbornly insisted on comparing nature to our machines,
transforming it into a tangle of inert structures, and now a new animism is
announcing itself. Nature has a soul!

On the occasion of the release of his latest book "The Science Delusion" Rupert
Sheldrake was invited to give a talk at TEDx Whitechapel (2013) where he presented
science as a belief system, drawing up a list of ten dogmas which, according to
him, are highly questionable. He then argued against the dogma of the universal
constants of physics, explaining that the speed of light could vary. Sheldrake is
not a physicist, however. Is he aware that physics could not exist without
constants? Yet his results are there nonetheless. It will come as no surprise
that this talk subsequently became controversial and suffered an attempted
censorship.


---


His work of argumentation in favor of his hypothesis of morphic fields remains
interesting all the same, especially since morphic fields can be compared to the
archetypes and to Jung's collective unconscious. Where Sheldrake is surely right
is when he says that science will make a fabulous leap forward when it becomes
capable of grasping psychic fields. Here are as many qualifying terms for them:
archetypes, formative thoughts, morphic fields, quantum psyche...? In every case,
we have here information that seems to be located outside our space-time."
