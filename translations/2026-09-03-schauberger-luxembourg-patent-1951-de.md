Patent Application in Luxembourg
  No. 31,480         30 May 1951
  Applicant: Mr. Viktor Schauberger, in Linz / Upper Austria
  "Method and Apparatus for Controlling Molecular Decomposition and
Build-up Processes in Moving Liquid, Gaseous and Air-like Media and
for Achieving Mechanical Performance Enhancements."
  It has not previously been known that it is possible to move liquid, gaseous
or air-like media in devices in such a way that molecular processes can be
controlled at will.
   With the movement methods currently in use (e.g. in straight, smooth
pipes), the flowing media are indeed moved forward, but as a side effect
a reactive, structure-loosening tendency promoting molecular decay
occurs, which cannot be controlled, wherein e.g. with increasing velocity,
through pressure increase, through additional heating, mechanical
shattering, etc., this decay phenomenon grows considerably.
  If the molecular structure of a moving liquid, gaseous or air-like
medium is to be preserved, and especially if a molecular build-up
process is to be initiated, these aforementioned structure-loosening
tendencies must above all be prevented.
  The subject matter of the invention is a method and devices
that not only prevent the undesired molecular decay and energetic
degradation of moving liquid, gaseous and air-like media, but also
enable the achievement of molecular and energetic build-up, and which
also lead to mechanical performance enhancements.
  In certain cases, these molecular processes must also proceed
in a special rhythmic interplay of "expansion – contraction –
expansion – contraction etc.", that is, in a specific staged developmental
rhythm.
  In preventing molecular decay, the matter may involve, for example,
eliminating incrustations, deposits, etc. in moving water or other liquids.

1
   According to the invention, this desired effect is achieved by a
special method, according to which the medium is primarily imparted
a special laminar, multiply "inwardly spiraling" movement in channels,
pipes or vessels of special form that enable this type of movement.
  The molecular build-up (regrouping, transformation, energetic
upgrading, biocatalytic reduction, etc.) is achieved with the method
according to the invention:
a) through the laminar multiple inward spiraling of the media to be
   moved in forms conducive to this spiraling, made from specific
   materials, and where appropriate
b) through the addition of substances to be incorporated of various
   molecular and atomic structure, possibly trace elements,
   active substances and the like, and
c) through energetic binding (coupling) of the media and the added
   substances, e.g. by catalytic means, also through direct or indirect
   irradiation of light of various frequencies (e.g. blue light, UV light,
   types of light of other frequency ranges) or also through other
   types of vibrational excitation (e.g. ultrasound). Excessively strong
   structure-loosening or structure-disrupting light influences of certain
   frequency ranges must be reduced to a minimal value prescribed for
   each medium.
  An exemplary embodiment of a channel for achieving a multiply
inwardly spiraling flow movement that at least preserves the molecular
structure of the medium to be moved is to be equipped with an "open
profile" that must exhibit the following features:
a) a special variable profile cross-section, conceived as derived from
   the pointed part of an egg shape, wherein one half each is drawn in
   mirror-symmetrically at the extreme values of the profile. (Fig. 1
   "open profile").
b) A longitudinal profile, designed as shown in Fig. 2, formed
   in a wave-like manner.
  The form of the channel is now produced in such a way that along
the longitudinal profile, the drawn-in part of the profile cross-section
migrates respectively from the upper part of the wave crest to the lower
part of the wave trough.

2
During this cross-section migration, the extent of the draw-in
decreases and gradually shifts from its extreme value at the apex
of the wave crest to its minimum value at the wave zero point, in
order to gradually increase again from there to its extreme value in
the following wave trough, although this extreme value is now
positioned mirror-symmetrically to its predecessor.
  In natural channels (streams, rivers, etc.), the channel form
described above is a precondition for the inward spiraling and thus
for the regeneration of the watercourse and for the maintenance of
the biological river laws.
  If the complementary rounded part of the egg shape is placed on
the pointed part described above, the cross-sectional form for the
"closed profile" results (Fig. 3).
  Fig. 4 shows an exemplary embodiment of a device according to
the invention, in which a pipe with a closed profile cross-section
is wound around the mantle of an imaginary circular cylinder.
  This embodiment can be applied, for example, for drinking and
service water pipelines and for pipelines of all kinds.
   If an acceleration of the flow movement is additionally sought,
e.g. for the purpose of cross-section reduction and the resulting
material savings for the pipes used, then the closed pipes profiled
in this way (e.g. also pipe bundles) are wound onto the mantle of a
circular cylinder serving as a supporting body, and this cylinder is
set into rotation. The delivery rate, as well as the mechanical
performance enhancement, can be regulated by changing the rotation
speed. This embodiment is particularly suitable, among other things,
for the conveyance of liquid, gaseous and air-like media.
   An exemplary embodiment that serves particular syntheses
(transformation, build-up and upgrading processes, etc.) is shown
in Fig. 5. In this device, a closed profile according to Fig. 3 is likewise
used.

3
   In this embodiment, a pipe having the named cross-section is
wound around the mantle of a conical body of revolution. Depending
on the application purpose, this cross-sectional profile tapers in the
direction toward the apex of this body of revolution (e.g. for the
conveyance and conversion of seawater into fresh water) or conversely
in the direction toward the base (e.g. for the separation of mixtures).
  For special purposes, several pipes wound in this way can be
coupled, e.g. apex to apex, or base to base (e.g. to excite pulsations
for synthesis processes).
  Likewise, several such helices can be bundled around a common axis.
  Open, or slotted, perforated or partially open and partially closed
pipes or pipe systems of the above wound type can also be used (e.g.
to achieve diffusion-filter effects, etc.).
  With the help of the regulation of the rotational speed of such
pipes or pipe systems, not only is the flow speed of the media to be
moved and thus the mechanical performance enhancement increased,
but also the speed of the molecular transformation is controlled.
  In non-rotating pipes of this type, the molecular transformation
process takes place over correspondingly long distances instead.
   A particularly practical form of the vessels used, especially for
purposes of mixing, stirring, etc. of media or for carrying out
biochemical processes, fermentation processes, etc., has proven to be
those whose inner profile cross-section can be developed from an egg
shape or from egg-shape-like bodies of revolution, where appropriate
from parabolic, hyperbolic and similar bodies of revolution, wherein
these vessels can likewise be set into controllable rotation in
necessary cases.
  The drive device for all the aforementioned bodies of revolution
can also be designed in such a way that it imparts a rhythmically
alternating direction of rotation to these bodies of revolution. Drives
of this kind

  The addition of the substances to be incorporated can take place
in any manner and concerns substances e.g. in solid, liquid, gaseous,
or air-like form and depends on the type of desired molecular build-up
(molecular organizations).
  For example, substances must be added to water to be upgraded
in appropriate dosing, as shown e.g. by the analysis of drinking and
medicinal waters.
  The energetic coupling (binding) of these additives, and of the
media in general, is achieved in interaction with the above-described
types of movement, e.g. by biocatalytic means, among other things
through appropriate choice of the materials from which the
above-described pipes, channels and vessels must be made. Materials
that have proven particularly suitable for use include, for example,
copper, silver, gold, and their alloys, synthetic resins with and without
metallic or mineral inclusions, natural stone, woods (e.g. larch, oak,
etc.) and combinations of the aforementioned materials.
  For example, water of appropriate quality and healing power can
already be built up in a form made e.g. from an appropriate copper
alloy.
   Catalyst and substances to be incorporated, active substances and
the like must naturally stand in a certain energetic relationship to each
other, as is otherwise known from the field of application of catalysts.
  The energetic coupling can, as already mentioned above, also
take place through direct or indirect irradiation, e.g. of light of various
frequencies (blue light, UV light, etc.) or through vibrational excitation
e.g. by ultrasound, etc., or be supported by mechanical vibrational
excitation.
  The above-mentioned peculiar multiple inward spiraling, which
is above all characterized by an "inwardly spiraling tendency in and
of itself" of the moving medium, leads, among other things, also to
a temperature drop (in water, e.g. in the direction of the anomaly
point), to specific densification of media moved in this way, etc.

  These effects are integral prerequisites for enabling the control of
the stated molecular processes and for achieving substantial performance
enhancements, which can be brought into effect, e.g., in the propulsion
of turbines, ships, vehicles and aircraft, in the conveyance of media of
all kinds, for increasing the carrying and tractive forces (e.g. of water
in shallow waterways, etc.).
   The performance enhancement itself is attributable, among other
things, to the essential elimination of the centrifugally acting reactive
wall pressures, which increase substantially with increasing movement
velocity in all current movement methods and lead to self-reinforcing
molecular decay processes, and also to the aforementioned specific
densification of such inwardly spiraling media.
  In rotating pipes, pipe systems, vessels, etc., a controllable
counter-rotation between the moving forms and the media moving
particularly within them also occurs, which accelerates or increases
the aforementioned processes, including the special effect of mechanical
performance enhancement.
   The fields of application of the invention are therefore of the
most diverse and comprehensive kind. The application of this method
and the devices for its implementation proves particularly suitable for
preventing incrustation in pipes, deposits in channels, for the conversion
of, for example, seawater into fresh water of various quality grades, for
the biological purification of contaminated drinking and service waters,
and for high-molecular synthesis, for energetic build-up and
transformation processes, and also, for example, for the conversion of
molecular structures of liquid type into those of gaseous, air-like or
etheric type and vice versa, as nature and predominantly the plant world
(e.g. blood and sap build-up, etc.) demonstrates.
  It should also be mentioned that this novel, particularly inwardly
spiraling movement also yields constructive redesigns of turbines, ship
and aircraft propulsion systems, novel water power and water
conveyance installations, etc.

  For further clarification of the subject matter of the invention, the
following is emphasized in connection with the schematic drawing
representation:
  The special multiply inwardly spiraling (e.g. also counter-rotating)
movement mentioned above is to be understood approximately in the
sense of the tendency schematically shown in Fig. 6. In this figure,
which represents a closed profile cross-section of a particularly wound
form, D indicates, for example, the direction of rotation of the form
and E indicates the direction of the inwardly spiraling tendency.
  Wound forms are also to be regarded as those which, in addition
to the exemplary winding around the most diverse bodies of revolution,
whose base lines are schematically shown in Figs. 4 and 5a, b, c, d,
also undergo a winding or rotation within the form itself.
  Conical bodies in the above sense are also those which have, e.g.,
an elongated (teardrop-shaped) or compressed egg shape (extreme
egg shape).
  In the vessel forms mentioned above, particularly wound pipes or
pipe systems (e.g. Fig. 7) can also be incorporated.
  Likewise, in the above-mentioned conical and other bodies of
revolution, these particularly wound and twisted pipes or pipe systems
can be arranged in such a way that counter-rotating helical pipes or
systems are arranged both on the outer mantle surface and on the
inner periphery.

Patent Claims:
  1. Method for controlling molecular decomposition, restructuring
     and build-up processes in moving liquid, gaseous, air-like, etc.
     media and for achieving mechanical performance enhancements,
     characterized in that these media are imparted a special laminar,
     multiply inwardly spiraling movement, in channels, pipes or
     vessels of special form and made of special materials that enable
     this type of movement.
