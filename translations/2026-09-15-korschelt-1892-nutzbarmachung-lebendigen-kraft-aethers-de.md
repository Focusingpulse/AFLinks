                           I.

Theory.
It is a fact that presumably no one doubts that
all the forces active upon the Earth originate
from the Sun. In the sun's rays, immensely
great living force streams to the Earth and
converts itself here in the most manifold ways.
More or less directly, all terrestrial forces can
be traced back to the power of the Sun.
Thus the driving force of flowing water
derives from the sun's rays, for their heat was
consumed in evaporating the water on the
Earth's surface, which returns to the Earth
from the clouds as rain and fills the rivers.
Likewise the sun's rays generate the wind
through unequal warming of the layers of air.
All the power of steam engines comes from
sun's rays that reached the Earth millions of
years ago and gave up their living force to
the plants from which the coal was later
formed. Thus the electric light is, in truth,
sunlight, for it is produced by means of steam
or water power.

Human life, too, as well as that of animals and
plants, is impossible without the forces that come
to us from the Sun, for our food is condensed
solar power; we also take in the living force of
the sun's rays directly, as the healing effect of
sun baths and the fact proves that in darkness
the human being withers, just as the plant does.
The process by which force is transmitted from
the Sun to the Earth has been conceived in various
ways. Newton assumed that very small particles
are hurled away from the Sun's surface at very
great velocity and then radiate outward in
straight lines into space. When they strike other
celestial bodies, they give up, for example at the
Earth's surface, their living force by impact and
remain lying upon it.
Later it was assumed — and this is the view
still prevailing today — that the molecular
vibrations of the Sun's surface propagate
themselves to the Earth through vibrations of
the so-called aether, i.e. a substance consisting
of much smaller particles than molecules, whose
particles, separated from one another by mutual
repulsion at distances very great in proportion
to their diameter, remain motionless in their

5
place in space. Thus they receive vibrational
impulses from the adjacent aether particles and
pass them on, describing short paths about their
immutable station in the process. In this way
the vibrations of the Sun's molecules are
transferred to the Earth without any migration
of material particles taking place.
Newton's theory is called the emission theory,
the one last mentioned the undulation theory.
Other conceivable possibilities for the migra-
tion of living force from celestial body to
celestial body, it seems, do not exist. Much
dispute has therefore been carried on over which
view is the correct one, and people finally agreed
to hold the undulation theory to be correct.
Recently, however, Zöllner in particular has
pointed out that the transfer of living force
between celestial bodies takes place in both
ways simultaneously, so that, as often happens
in life, both of the contending parties are right.
Zöllner was led to this by his investigations
of the Crookes radiometer, an evacuated glass
vessel in which vanes mounted on an axis begin
to rotate as soon as they are irradiated by
the Sun.
