## Abstract

From the system of equations of compressible oscillating ether, the following have previously been derived: a generalized nonlinear Maxwell-Lorentz system of equations invariant under Galilean transformations, whose linearization yields the classical Maxwell-Lorentz system of equations; the Biot-Savart-Laplace, Ampere, and Coulomb laws; representations for the Planck and fine-structure constants; formulas for the electron, proton, and neutron; and an ether theory of the atom and atomic nucleus has been developed. In the present work, an ether theory of gravity is constructed, and the similarities and differences between gravitational and electrostatic fields are explained. It is shown that gravity does not involve attractive forces, but rather pressing (compressive) forces, and that the gravitational constant is not in fact constant, but weakly depends on the chemical composition of the interacting bodies. Gravitational interactions between bodies do not propagate from one body to another at some velocity; rather, at any moment in time, the gravitational fields of all bodies exist together with and around the bodies, for which reason neither gravitational waves nor gravitons exist in nature. Using the experimental values of the gravitational constant, the values of all ether parameters are found, including the density of its unperturbed state.

**Keywords:** Compressible oscillating ether, charges, electrostatic and gravitational fields, gravitational constant, ether parameters.

## 1. Introduction

Gravity is one of the most mysterious physical phenomena. No modern physical theory is capable of clearly explaining the nature of this phenomenon. In Newtonian theory, gravity is identified with the gravitational interaction of material bodies, which is described by the very simple law of universal gravitation:

`F = G m₁m₂/r²`

The proportionality coefficient G is called the gravitational constant. It characterizes the intensity of gravitational interaction and is one of the fundamental physical constants.

In modern physics, preference is given to the concept of action through a medium (short-range interaction), in which the transmission of interaction between bodies is accomplished by means of a special kind of material medium — the gravitational field, whose force characteristic is its field strength. It is considered that any body of mass m creates around itself a gravitational field with strength E = Gm/r², having the dimension of acceleration m·s⁻² in the SI system. But then, as follows from experimental data, the speed of transmission of gravitational interaction must exceed the speed of light by several orders of magnitude, which has no reasonable explanation.

An attempt to resolve this paradox is Einstein's general theory of relativity, according to which gravity is considered not as the propagation of a force interaction in space, but is allegedly the result of masses somehow miraculously curving the four-dimensional spacetime continuum in their vicinity. This hypothesis is merely a geometric abstraction contradicting common sense, and can in no way be considered an explanation of gravity.

Moreover, the presence of the second power of the distance between bodies in formula (1.1) clearly indicates that the space surrounding us is a three-dimensional flat Euclidean space, and that gravity is somehow closely related to electric charges. Indeed, Coulomb's law for the interaction of two electric charges has an analogous form in the CGS system:

`F = q₁q₂/r²`

and the strength of the electrostatic field created by a charge q is E = q/r².

The similarity between electrostatic and gravitational fields is also indicated by the fact from the theory of compressible oscillating ether being developed by the author, that in the case where the ether density has the dimension [ρ] = g^(1/2)·cm^(−3/2)·s^(−1), the dimensions of all physical quantities in the ether theory coincide with the dimensions of these quantities in the CGS system.

However, along with the similarities, gravitational and electrostatic fields also have significant differences:
1. Gravitational forces act between any bodies, while electric forces act only between charged bodies
2. Gravitational forces are incomparably smaller than electric forces
3. In gravity there exist only attractive forces, whereas in electricity there are also repulsive forces
4. The precision of experimental measurements of the gravitational constant G is several orders of magnitude lower than the precision of measurements of other physical quantities

## 2. Charges and Fields of Elementary Particles

The electron and the proton are the fundamental elementary particles from which matter is composed. These particles, together with their antiparticles (the positron and the antiproton), are born from folded photons and annihilate, producing photons.

The electron and the proton have charges equal in absolute magnitude but opposite in sign, and consequently possess electrostatic fields. And since the intensities of electrostatic and gravitational fields have a similar form, they must be sought among the solutions of the system of equations of elementary particles.

### 2.1. System of equations of elementary particles

The system of equations in a stationary spherical coordinate system, taking into account that the ether is globally motionless:

`u = u_r r̂ + u_θ θ̂ + u_φ φ̂ = V_r r̂ + V_θ/r θ̂ + W/(r sin θ) φ̂`

### 2.2. Charges and masses of the electron and the proton

The electron and the proton are spherical wave solutions generated by half-waves of folded photons. The charge density wave σ of a folded photon is split into two half-waves σ₊ and σ₋ of the charge densities of the particle and the antiparticle.

To the proton with positive charge density corresponds exclusively the half-wave of periodic compression of the particle's volume, and to the electron with negative charge density corresponds exclusively a periodic expansion of the particle's volume.

Thus, the ether inside the positively charged proton is slightly compressed compared to the undisturbed ether of constant density ρ₀, while the ether inside the negatively charged electron is slightly rarefied.

### 2.3. Electrostatic fields of the electron, proton, and neutron

The field intensity of electric charges is introduced by analogy with the electric field intensity:

`E = E_r = (∂(ρV)/∂r + W/(r sin θ) ∂(ρV)/∂φ) r̂`

The electrostatic field of the electron is directed away from its center, and the electrostatic field of the proton is directed toward its center.

The neutron consists of a superposition of unidirectional waves of a compressed proton and electron. Therefore, the electrostatic field of the neutron is equal to zero as the sum of oppositely directed and equal in magnitude electrostatic fields of the proton and electron.

### 2.4. Gravitational fields of the electron, proton and neutron

The system (2.7) has other solutions that can be interpreted as the gravitational fields of the electron, proton and neutron. The electrostatic and gravitational fields outside the spheres of the electron and proton must be described by the same small perturbations of the ether density, while the radial velocities of propagation of these perturbations may differ.

Consequently, the gravitational field of the proton is directed toward its center, inversely proportional to the radius of the proton and inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the center. The gravitational field of the electron is directed away from its center.

Thus, the gravitational field of a charge is smaller than its electrostatic field by a factor of c·rₑ,ₚ/V₀ >> 1, and the gravitational field of the electron is smaller than the gravitational field of the proton by approximately a factor of 1836.

The total gravitational field of the neutron equals the gravitational field of the proton and is directed toward its center.

## 3. Conclusion

In this work, an ether theory of gravitation has been constructed, and the similarities and differences between gravitational and electrostatic fields have been explained. It has been shown that:

1. **In gravitation there are no forces of attraction, but forces of pressing**
2. **The gravitational constant is in fact not a constant, but weakly depends on the chemical composition of the interacting bodies**
3. **Gravitational interactions between bodies do not propagate from one body to another, but at any moment of time the gravitational fields of any bodies exist together with the bodies and around them**
4. **Neither gravitational waves nor gravitons exist in nature**

The different values of the gravitational constant are determined not by poor accuracy of the conducted experiments, but by the different chemical composition of the material bodies participating in the experiments.
