## 1. Who he is (baglis.tv author page, translated)

Guy Thieux was born February 8, 1932, in Amiens. His childhood unfolded "among mountains of rubble" — Amiens had been bombed and destroyed to three-fifths of its extent [in the Second World War].

After a preparatory diploma in micromechanics (École Normale de Cluses, Haute-Savoie, 1948–1952), he performed his military service in the French Air Force in 1952.

He then specialized in geophysics: measurement of the Earth's magnetic field, of the Earth's gravitational field, and the seismic propagation of waves (reflected, refracted, diffracted) through the ground. He worked for 34 years in petroleum prospecting, with long assignments in every corner of the world.

He contributed to the collective work *Le Belvédère du labyrinthe* by Fred Berthelot (Éd. Larousse).

As legatee of the occultist Prince Michel Vladimirovich Skariatine ("Enel," 1883–1963), he brought out at Ibacom Éditions *Le Monde astral et l'Occultisme*, then *Science égyptienne et Médecine de l'Astral*. In 2019 a third work was to appear at Ibacom, *Radiesthésie thérapeutique*, and in 2020 a work on Georges Lakhovsky at Cosmogone.

## 2. The precise professional record (Thieux's own author note, El Secreto de la Vida prologue, 2007/2010 — translated from Spanish)

> **Note on the author:** Guy Thieux was born February 8, 1932, in Amiens (France). A graduate of the École Nationale de Cluses (1948–1952) as an engineer in geophysics, between 1955 and 1981 he carried out thirty-four petroleum and mining exploration projects for the Compagnie Générale de Géophysique (CGG), on behalf of terrestrial energy exploration and production companies. During this period he applied magnetic, gravimetric, electro-telluric, and seismic methods (reflection and refraction).
>
> For ten years (1975–1985) G. Thieux worked at the Association for Research in Molecular Biology (NYNAPSE), alongside E. Guillé, J. Coppey, Sissoef, Grisvard, and several other scientists. There he studied the action of Hertzian dipoles, of Lakhovsky's radio-cellulo-oscillator, and of the Multiple-Wave Oscillator (OLOM) on the cell. Serge Lakhovsky (1913–2003), then director of the "Colysa France" laboratories, also took part in this work.
>
> G. Thieux lectured on the application of oscillating circuits to plants at the Escuela Politécnica de Madrid, in the area of Advanced Agronomy.
>
> From 1986 to 2006 he developed and updated the works of Enel (Michel W. Skariatine), of G. Lakhovsky, and of Joseph Salvat (of Béziers) on the influence of natural geophysical fields on living things, combined with multifrequency polymetallic diffusers, to re-harmonize the basal metabolism of species.
>
> In practical application, these studies achieved stimulating actions on plants (vineyards, chestnuts, legumes), animals (racehorses), and humans, so that they improve their state of health and reach harmonious results in the various aspects of the living, according to their biological kingdom.

In the same prologue, Thieux names the man who introduced him to Lakhovsky's work: "Michaël Vladimirovitch Skariatine, who besides having known him well, cited — under the pseudonym of Enel — the works and realizations of 'The Secret of Life.'" He also recalls "the marvelous moments shared with Serge Lakhovsky at the Colysa laboratories he directed in Paris, and that joint work carried out at the Orsay faculty under the direction of Professor Étienne Guillé from 1975 to 1985."

## 3. The Enel legatee (ibacom.fr catalog pages + 450.fm, translated)

The three volumes of the *Carnets inédits d'Enel* — the collection Thieux directs as Enel's literary depository, from manuscripts written between 1930 and 1950 — appeared at Ibacom Éditions:

- **Tome I — *Le Monde astral et l'Occultisme*** (1 June 2016, ISBN 979-10-92733-07-5, 218 pages + 8-page photo insert). Enel's Cairo-period notebooks: the esoteric constitution of man, recounted through the experiments of the pioneering occultists (Dr. Osty, the Durville brothers, Colonel de Rochas, Leadbeater, Commandant Darget, Professor Richet, Warcollier, Crookes...), then the doors of Initiation. The photo insert reproduces esoteric paintings by Kazimir Stabrovsky, director of the Warsaw School of Fine Arts and a friend of Enel — from Thieux's private collection.
- **Tome II — *Science égyptienne et Médecine de l'Astral*** (1 February 2018, 2nd edition August 2025, ISBN 979-10-92733-56-3, 346 pages). The Cabala-decoded hieroglyphs: mummification, incantations, the secret name, the opening-of-the-mouth ceremony, and their relation to the subtle bodies Ka and Ba.
- **Tome III — *Radiesthésie thérapeutique*** (24 September 2019, ISBN 979-10-92733-20-4, 550 pages + 33 color plates). The Swiss-period notebooks: gravitational colors, radiant-form apparatus, essences of woods, radiations, ritual instruments, force fields, Paracelsus's lillium, potable gold, resonant oscillators, telluric factors, natural magnetic and electro-telluric fields — and Georges Lakhovsky's "Olom" (Multiple-Wave Oscillator), taken up by Thieux himself.

The 450.fm review (Yonnel Ghernaouti, 2022) situates him: "Little known to seekers, Guy Thieux — who wrote the preface to the present volume, specialized in geophysics (measurement of the terrestrial magnetic field in particular), took part in the collective work *Le Belvédère du labyrinthe* directed by Alfred Berthelot (Hachette, 1985), and, in his capacity as legatee of the occultist prince Michel Vladimirovich Skariatine (1883–1963), called Enel — who, like many Russian aristocrats, fled his country after the October Revolution and spent years in Egypt studying and bringing to light the sacred meaning of the hieroglyphs — has published at IBAcom Éditions, in 2016, *Le Monde astral et l'Occultisme*, then in 2018 *Science égyptienne et Médecine de l'Astral*. And finally in 2019, *Radiesthésie thérapeutique*, always in the collection 'Les Inédits d'Enel.'"

## 4. The man in person ("Guy Thieux, légataire de l'occulte," baglis.tv interview, 2019 — excerpt translated)

The interview opens in his house in the Eure-et-Loir near Châteaudun — "a place worthy of Ali Baba's cave and Hogwarts combined." Marie-Véronique Lechêne interviews; the frame: "You are a rather particular geophysicist, because you straddle esotericism, even occultism, and scientific techniques."

**Childhood.** His mother was a schoolteacher, "a bit in the style of Jaurès's school"; his father a first-class mechanic on the Northern railway network. His sister studied music entirely: "the whole childhood was rocked by the piano and other instruments, by rhythms" — and by his own constant curiosity: "why are things the way they are, whatever they may be. For example, I asked her: where does the Earth come from? I walk on it." Amiens contributed its thousand hortillonnages (the floating gardens unique in France), its extraordinary cathedral — which he crossed nearly every week with his mother, entering one side and leaving the other — and the old streets of the Vieil Amiens, the "little Venice," where each house had its protector saint.

**Early vibrational perception.** Between the ages of 4 and 8, he had a recurring dream: a giant screen displaying numbers, unlimited series, accompanied by frequency modulations and noise that became increasingly unbearable — until he woke, and "I had the impression of seeing matter, objects, as if they had become translucent. And then they solidified again." He thought everyone saw the same thing. He told his mother; she said: "Listen, I see what's happening — don't talk about it."

**The Piobb lineage.** His mother practiced geomancy. A bookseller in Amiens (named, amusingly, Léveillard) and a Dr. de Lobel directed her to the works of Count Pierre-François-Xavier-Vincent Piobb (1878–1942) — the occultist Thieux still cites as a formative influence: born in Paris, son of the chief surgeon of the Geese [Vatican] hospital in Rome, raised in the Vatican, heir from 1897 to considerable esoteric documents under the aegis of a Bonapartist Rosicrucian movement, parliamentary journalist, correspondent of Marshal Lyautey in Morocco, friend of Daladier and Préfet Chiappe. (Thieux's wife Margot Thieux is herself an author on geomancy, following Piobb's "Great Tradition" — her books at Trédaniel Vega and Cosmogone.)

**The encounters.** In the second part of the filmed interview Thieux speaks of the meetings that marked him — notably with Raymond Abellio and with the occultist prince Michel Vladimirovich Skariatine, "better known under the name of Enel" — and of the great figures he never met but who inspired him: Pierre Vincenti Piobb, Rudolf Steiner, Tesla, Georges Lakhovsky.

## 5. The Lakhovsky thread (ibacom.fr + anneauxdevie.com, translated)

The *Radiesthésie thérapeutique* catalog page notes that the book gathers "the works of Georges Lakhovsky and his 'Olom' (Oscillateur à Longueur d'Ondes Multiples) **taken up by Guy Thieux**" — and the Ibacom author catalog calls Thieux "geophysicist and geobiologist of renown," for whom this book "will constitute a great first in this domain."

The Lakhovsky connection is three-generational: Georges Lakhovsky (1870–1942, the Multiple-Wave Oscillator); his son Serge Lakhovsky (1913–2003), director of the Colysa France laboratories, with whom Thieux worked at Orsay under Prof. Étienne Guillé in the NYNAPSE years (1975–1985); and the announced Cosmogone book on Georges Lakhovsky (per baglis.tv, expected 2020). Thieux also co-prefaced François Trojani's *Une Médecine pour Demain — L'Homme électromagnétique* (with George Lakhovsky, the grandson).

Anneaux de Vie (2024): "In his latest work, *Radiesthésie thérapeutique*, third volume of the Carnets inédits d'Enel, Guy Thieux details the applications of resonant oscillators, as well as the works of Georges Lakhovsky and the 'Olom.'"

## 6. Where he sits in the lineage

Thieux is the living hinge of the French vibrational lineage this library has been tracing:

- **Enel → Thieux**: the Skariatine manuscripts (1930–1950) passed to Thieux, who spent years in conversation with Enel before his death in 1963, then forty years preparing the trilogy (1986–2006 by his own account: "he developed and updated the works of Enel... from 1986 to 2006").
- **Lakhovsky → Thieux**: the Orsay NYNAPSE decade with Guillé and Serge Lakhovsky, and the ongoing Olom work.
- **Piobb → Margot Thieux**: the geomantic strand of the "Great Tradition."
- **The Ravatin/Ark'all connection**: the *Carnets inédits* are catalogued within the Ark'all orbit (the same esoteric-scientific French milieu as Jacques Ravatin's Fondation Ark'all), and Thieux's practical geobiology continues the Chaumery–de Belizal–Enel form-wave school into the present.

The AAR (Association des Amis de la Radiesthésie — Bouly's and Mermet's society) organized the study days at Thieux's home in Montigny-le-Gannelon (Eure-et-Loir) on Tome III (16 April 2020), and Patrick Darcheville — who "took an active part in the edition of the 3 volumes of Enel" — lectured to the AAR on "The radionic equipment of the ancients: Chaumery – Belizal – Enel" (24 February 2020). The French school's chain of transmission is not a historical memory; it is an active society with a living legatee.
