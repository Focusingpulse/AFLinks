### 1. Introduction

Low Energy Nuclear Reactions (LENR), commonly known as "cold fusion," were initially proposed by Fleischmann and Pons [1], who reported observations of anomalous heat during the electrochemical absorption of deuterium in palladium lattices. Despite the intense media attention generated by their announcement, the experimental results proved difficult to reproduce consistently, leading to a heated scientific debate on the actual nature of the phenomenon.

Numerous critical analyses and reviews, including that of Storms [4], have highlighted how the low reproducibility of the experiments is tied to the complexity of the conditions necessary for triggering the reactions, particularly the inadequate control of the local geometry of nuclear spins, lattice configuration, and the interaction between protons.

In an attempt to overcome these limitations, several studies have explored the use of deuterium- or hydrogen-loaded metal lattices as support for the formation of nuclear bonds [2], while calorimetric approaches have been employed to detect any energy anomalies associated with LENR processes [3].

This work proposes an experimental configuration based on a gas phase (dry gas), with antiparallel selection of proton spins and facilitation of energy reconfiguration via neutrinos, avoiding the use of electrolytes in solution. The objective is to verify the possibility of overcoming the Coulomb barrier through fine control of the approach conditions between protons and magnetic alignment, with the intent of contributing to the understanding and validation of LENR phenomena.

### 2. Proposed Experimental Configuration

#### 2.1 Main Components

- **Solid anode (fixed target):** [2] crystal lattice (e.g., Palladium, Nickel, Zirconium) loaded with protons. Controlled orientation of the fixed protons' spin via static or oscillating magnetic fields.
- **Mobile proton source:** beam of H⁺ ions generated via cold plasma or ion source, with kinetic energy maintained in the 10–50 eV range.
- **Neutrino source:** beta-emitting isotope positioned in proximity to the interaction zone (e.g., tritium, strontium-90, yttrium-90).
- **Control magnetic fields:** static or variable, intensity between 0.1 and 5 T, for spin selection and stabilization of magnetons in the energy arcs.
- **Potential control:** potential difference applied between the mobile proton source and the anode, in the range of 1–50 V, to promote slow approach.

#### 2.2 Advantages of the Dry Gas Approach

- Absence of electrolysis and disturbances due to gas bubbles.
- Independent control of energy, spin, proton flux, and neutrino density.
- Possibility of optimizing geometry and magnetic fields to maximize the probability of energy reconfiguration.

## Translator's Notes

- **Context:** This is a 2025 experimental protocol from an Italian independent researcher, deposited on Zenodo. It proposes a dry gas (non-electrolytic) approach to LENR, addressing the reproducibility problems that have plagued cold fusion research since Fleischmann-Pons (1989).
- **Key innovation:** The protocol replaces electrolytic solutions with a gas-phase approach, allowing independent control of proton energy, spin alignment, proton flux, and neutrino density. This addresses the criticism (per Storms [4]) that poor reproducibility in LENR experiments stems from inadequate control of spin geometry and lattice conditions.
- **Antiparallel spin selection:** The protocol specifies antiparallel proton spin alignment using magnetic fields (0.1–5 T). This is notable — standard nuclear fusion requires overcoming the Coulomb barrier, and spin alignment affects the probability of nuclear interaction. The antiparallel configuration is chosen to favor the singlet state, which has different nuclear interaction cross-sections than the triplet state.
- **Neutrino facilitation:** The use of a beta-emitting isotope (tritium, Sr-90, or Y-90) as a neutrino source near the interaction zone is an unusual element. The author proposes neutrinos as "facilitators of local energy reconfiguration" — this is the most speculative element and the theoretical basis is intentionally omitted.
- **Practical applicability:** All specified components are commercially available: cold plasma ion sources, palladium/nickel/zirconium targets, beta-emitting isotopes, electromagnets up to 5 T, and standard calorimetry/radiation detection equipment. The parameter ranges are concrete and reproducible. The protocol is explicitly designed for independent verification — it is open and complete enough for any well-equipped lab to attempt.
- **Limitations:** The document is deliberately theory-free. The "energy arc reconfiguration" concept and the role of neutrinos are not explained. The protocol is a proposal — no experimental results are reported. The author is an independent researcher, not affiliated with an institution.
- **Relationship to other finds:** This complements the Italian LENR research tradition documented in Scout B (Carpinteri et al. 2026 Politecnico di Torino, Malagoli 2019, Cirillo & Iorio 2004). The dry gas approach contrasts with the electrolytic methods used in most historical LENR experiments.
