# The Kozyrev Mirror: Device, Causal-Mechanics Theory, and Consciousness Research

The Kozyrev Mirror is a device built on the theory recorded by the Soviet astronomer and astrophysicist Nikolai Alexandrovich Kozyrev. This theory concerns the link between energy, matter, time, and consciousness. Since the 1990s, researchers around the world have experimented with Kozyrev mirrors and documented experiences of expanded states of consciousness. This dossier places the Kozyrev Mirror at the intersection of device-making and consciousness research. This entry is dedicated to introducing the Kozyrev Mirror — the device, the causal-mechanics theory, and the research — and organizes the page's content thematically.

## Who Was Nikolai Kozyrev?

Nikolai Alexandrovich Kozyrev was born on 2 September 1908 in Saint Petersburg and died on 27 February 1983 in the same city. He was a Soviet astronomer and astrophysicist who studied at Leningrad University. His scientific career began at the Pulkovo Observatory, where he specialized in the atmospheres of stars. In the 1930s he was arrested during the Stalinist purges and spent ten years in the camps, until he was rehabilitated in 1946 and resumed his research. In international astronomy, Kozyrev is known in particular for his observation of possible evidence of volcanic activity on the Moon in 1958. He obtained spectra of the lunar crater Alphonsus which, by his interpretation, indicated gas emissions. This observation was disputed internationally, but it led to a reconsideration of the question of whether the Moon is geologically dead.

## The Causal-Mechanics Theory

In parallel with his astronomical work, Kozyrev developed an independent physical theory that he called "causal mechanics." This theory seeks a link between time, energy, and matter, in which time is not merely a parameter of a process but an active factor with its own energy density and specific direction of effect. From this theory Kozyrev derived the proposal that, under certain conditions, time can transmit information independently of the physical carrier medium. Causal mechanics was not accepted by mainstream Western physics during Kozyrev's lifetime. Within the Soviet scientific community there was a small group that continued working with the theory. Even after Kozyrev's death, his approaches remained present in academic discussions — often viewed critically and never established as textbook physics, but documented as a notable heterodox contribution.

## The Device

From Kozyrev's theory arose the idea of a device known today as the Kozyrev Mirror. At its core, the device consists of a spirally wound cylindrical enclosure of aluminum, intended to create a resonant space. A person enters this space and remains in it for a period of time. The device is understood as a resonating body in which information from the field of consciousness is supposed to be accessible with improved access. Structural forms vary. There are chambers for one person, larger versions for several participants, and research devices with additional measurement and recording equipment. The basic recorded design goes back to the work of Vladimir Pavlovich Kaznacheev (Wladimir Pawlowitsch Kasnatschejew) and his team at the International Scientific Research Institute of Cosmic Anthropoecology in Novosibirsk. Kaznacheev was one of the most prominent proponents of causal mechanics after Kozyrev, and from the 1990s onward he conducted systematic experiments.

## Research Since 1990

With the collapse of the Soviet Union, the results of research related to the Kozyrev Mirror became internationally accessible. Researchers from Russia, Ukraine, Germany, Italy, and the USA reported in conference papers on their experiments and observations concerning subjects who stayed inside mirror chambers. In particular, experiences of expanded states of consciousness, altered perception of time, and occasionally non-local perceptions were described. These reports were not published in established physics journals, but predominantly in journals and conference proceedings devoted to consciousness research, parapsychology research, and alternative approaches to physics. Thus the scientific standing of this research remains at the margins of established disciplines — but it is documented and repeated.
