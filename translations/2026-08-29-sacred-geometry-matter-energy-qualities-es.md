## Sustainable Geometry and the Nature of Matter

Sustainable Geometry (formerly called Sacred Geometry) explains the steps that exist between free energy and the consolidation of that same energy into matter. We can understand energy as particles in chaotic motion, while matter is that same energy only gravitationally trapped. Free energy is associated with rectilinear movements, and the zero point has the powerful characteristic of organizing those free particles around a gravity point to create matter. Energy and matter are only differences in geometric arrangement. Matter can exist because of the geometric matrices that support and promote the suction of energy in constructive wave interference—this means that given the organization of certain geometries, an ordered implosion of chaotic particles is created. This same principle is applicable to healing, the arts, and architecture.

Let us think of a universe made of a single substance. What in science is known as unified field theory, or what in religions is known as a single God. No matter how it is defined, we live in a universe made of the same substance but with different geometric arrangements and different functions. It has been said: one God, many Names. One Science, many angles. In the unified field, the first movement of consciousness that is realized to create all matter—literally creating the atoms that constitute the universe—is to fix a zero point, a focus of attention.

Fixing the point of attention is fixing a vacuum, a fractal space around which one will rotate. Accepting that vacuum is accepting the process of passage from energy to matter. From fixing attention, fixing "non-tension," the particle or event is subjected to the three mathematical constants that govern the geometric matrix that supports matter: phi, pi, and euler.

## Phi (φ): The Golden Ratio

Phi (pronounced "fi") is the harmonic relationship of the smaller to the larger and the larger to the whole. It defines the spiral movement around which one rotates at the zero point—that spiral unfolds infinitely. The first movement to generate life is spiral movement. Everything in nature—the rotation of water, tornadoes, the sexual encounter between two animals—is not linear confrontation but occurs in a spiral dance. However, the phi spiral or golden spiral does not extend unlimitedly; there is a moment when it begins to curve to create matter. In scientific terms, phi, the golden number, creates heterodynamic wave phase conjunction.

Phi is an irrational number (whose decimal is infinite and non-periodic) with a numerical value of 1.618033… We can obtain it in three ways: through the Fibonacci numerical sequence, via a mathematical formula, or through geometric tracing.

Phi has the form of a "0" crossed by an "I," symbolizing the crossing of the vacuum barred by linear masculine energy, to begin the toroidal process of implosion/explosion. The number phi has been known throughout history by different names: in Greece and Rome it was known as the golden number, in the Renaissance as the divine number or number of gold. This number has impressive characteristics, but the greatest of them is that it is the number that structures the torsion movement that interrelates different scales, dimensions, or planes of Reality. It is the first constant of life's ordering. Besides being a number, phi is a proportion. If we place a straight line and seek to divide it in such a way that it is sustainable—that is, that the smaller is found in the larger, as much as the larger in the whole—we would find that it is only possible to make one cut: at 0.618033… of the total of that line. This is the beginning of fractality, what in most philosophies and religions we find symbolized as the Trinity. In Christianity, it is said that the Father is to the Son as the Son is to the Holy Spirit; in the Egyptian tradition, Osiris the creator father, Isis the mother, and Horus the son; among North American Indians, Safari father, Ani son, and Fayo the spirit. Even in the Hindu Brahmanic tradition, Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva are spoken of as perfectly interrelated divine forces.

## Pi (π): The Circular Constant

The size of the spiral is determined by pi, which allows phi to know the dimension of the curvature—that is, the size of matter. Our arm, which unfolds in phi patterns, does not grow unlimitedly but has a limited size. The unlimited nature of the universe is not in size, but in forms. It is as if we took a spiral and wrapped it in a sphere.

Pi is an irrational number with a value of 3.141592… and defines the constant value of a circle's radius with respect to its circumference. In psychogeometric terms, it defines the size of the toroid and is used to circumscribe the energy gravitationally trapped by the spiral and the attention point or zero point.

## Euler's Number (e): The Constant of Consistency

The sphere will take consistency through the mediation of euler. It is the most important number in mathematical analysis, determines the constant of the area under the curve, and allows giving consistency to the design created by phi in the spiral and pi in the circumference. For example, euler adjusts to almost all properties required to describe the strong interactions of elementary particles.

Euler, the last constant, represented by the letter "e," has a value of 2.718281… and is the base of natural logarithms. This means it is the only number with the property that the area of the region bounded by the hyperbola of axis "x" and axis "y" is equal to 1. Euler has the property of solidifying the toroid. In biology, one of its main applications is exponential growth. This type of growth arises when there are no factors limiting growth, as occurs in certain bacterial populations, or in the recovery of a forest surface after a fire. This constant is also linked to the golden ratio and the logarithmic spiral. When a chain or cable is hung by its ends, it tends to adopt a form related to the number "e." Even in something as mundane as calculating bank interest, it is necessary to resort to euler's constant.

## The Pentaflor Process: From Chaos to Cosmos

Let us see this graphically. In the chaos of the universe (in our own sexual, emotional, intellectual universe, etc.), the first geometric movement realized is spiral movement like a tornado form; the spiral will extend until it seeks to cover the largest possible area, emerging from the chaos of the vacuum and moving toward order, toward the toroid. In this creation process, the spiral extends to the size dictated by a sphere; if it continues growing, the spiral begins to surround the sphere and turns around it to start rotating in the opposite direction, moving from the outermost toward the center. Once it reaches the center, it radiates the energy it captured from the outermost and solidifies its own structure, giving it rigidity or flexibility.

In Psychogeometry, this entire process is known as the Pentaflor process. Depending on the level at which this process is viewed—whether in plane, 3D, or 4D—it can be seen as a point in the center (the vacuum), a spiral (the golden spiral), and a circle that delimits the spiral (a sphere). In most indigenous rituals, we find a power center surrounded by a circle of fire that "protects" from the exterior, from the "spirits" or chaotic energies of the order contained in that cosmos. Cosmos means "Order"; in the cosmic process, we emerge from the vacuum and return to it, not without first knowing the experience of traversing the entire toroid. The experience of our life is summarized in this metaphor. It is the maxim of "know thyself"—when the spirit traverses its own creation, the furthest from itself to experience, and finally, returns to itself.

Psychologically, this occurs when the Soul lives on the periphery of the Being, in the personality, to understand the culture, society, family, and integrate interaction with others within itself. From the Pentaflor and the toroidal creation it entails, the pattern of the Flower of Life begins to be created, always after the spirit has traversed itself. The Flower of Life is a symbol used in many religions and beliefs of the world; it is a geometric figure composed of circles duly separated or spaced, superimposed in a coincident form, and ordered in such a way that they reflect, in a polyptych symmetrical design, a flower of form similar to the hexagon. The center of each circle is on the circumference of six more circles that surround themselves, all of the same diameter (we will see them in detail later) in Article 4.2.
