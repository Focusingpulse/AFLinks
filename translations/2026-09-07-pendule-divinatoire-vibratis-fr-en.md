# The Divinatory Pendulum: Your Complete Guide (Use, Power, Dangers...)

# Your complete guide to using the divinatory pendulum in radiesthesia

The divinatory pendulum is used to detect water and metals, much like dowsing rods. It is also an instrument of divination. There are many divinatory methods, the best known being the Tarot, but oracles also have a long history.

The divinatory pendulum is used by many psychics and radiesthesists to answer various personal, psychological, romantic, or career-related questions. It has also sometimes been used in police investigations to search for missing persons. Many businesspeople, scientists, and politicians have turned to pendulum divination to illuminate their decisions. The accounts of divinations obtained by great radiesthesists are enough to leave one perplexed, and if you did not believe in the power of the pendulum, they will certainly make you doubt! It seems that in the hands of certain individuals, the radiesthesia pendulum delivers extraordinary results.

But how can the pendulum be a tool of clairvoyance? Is it limited to the radiesthesist's gift? What are the powers and dangers of the divinatory pendulum?

## What are the powers of the pendulum?

To understand the power of the pendulum, it is useful to see the human being and the world in which they evolve as a set of vibrations. These vibrations, or energy fields, and their variations, are the key to the pendulum. The vibration of the person being sought or of the answer being sought will influence the pendulum's behavior. The radiesthesist must remain neutral in order to receive the answers without muddying them. This neutrality requires training and concentration.

### Two schools of radiesthesia

In the twentieth century, there were two schools of radiesthesia: physical radiesthesia and mental radiesthesia.

#### The physical school

According to the first, bodies emitted or received waves, which the radiesthesist received or emitted with the help of the pendulum. In this approach, the human being is a transmitter-receiver of vibrations.

#### The mental school

The second school held that it was a parapsychological phenomenon. The waves received and emitted would have been those of the brain.

### Scientific discoveries

This conflict of schools is no longer relevant, as scientific discoveries have advanced the field. The work of Professor Rocard in particular supports the idea that we are sensitive to variations in magnetic fields. The radiesthesist therefore perceives the modification of a magnetic field in a given environment. We can borrow the expression of Jocelyne Fangain, who summarizes it as follows:

"The radiesthetic act is in fact the resultant of a set of perceptions that are both mental and physical."

This statement encompasses many phenomena, and radiesthesia, while affiliated with the sciences, refuses to be reduced to a purely scientific explanation and retains its share of mystery.

In France, divinatory radiesthesia has a long history, but it is also very widespread in Russia and the United States. Nevertheless, French works are regularly cited abroad. And for good reason: many illustrious figures in French history have been linked to radiesthesia, and many books on the divinatory pendulum have been written in France.

## The great radiesthesists: lived stories of divinatory radiesthesia

### The great figures of radiesthesia

#### Abbé Bouly, or the father of radiesthesia

Radiesthesia acquired its name in France. It was Abbé Bouly (1865–1958), nicknamed the "father of radiesthesia," who gave it its name. Before him, it was called "rhabdomancy." Having discovered water sources and archaeological sites around the world, he received the Knight's Cross of the Legion of Honour in 1950. He was called upon everywhere in the world and received in hospitals where he identified microbial cultures without error, using the pendulum. After the First World War, the government called on his services to detect unexploded shells. Abbé Bouly founded the Association of the Friends of Radiesthesia in 1929, which counted many famous figures among its members, including Édouard Branly and Henry de France. For Abbé Bouly, "every body—animal, vegetable, mineral—is but an accumulation of energy whose vibrations spread throughout the entire universe."

#### Abbé Mermet and teleradiesthesia

Despite the hostility displayed by the Church toward radiesthesia, many great radiesthesists have been and are men of the Church. It was another man of the Church, a member of the Association of the Friends of Radiesthesia, Abbé Mermet, who created teleradiesthesia, or distance radiesthesia. He left us a book, "How I Operate," in which he shares his art.

#### Joseph Treyve and the discovery of water sources

Other radiesthesists have numerous discoveries to their credit. The radiesthesist Joseph Treyve claimed to have discovered eight hundred and forty water sources. It is said that he was consulted by the Nobel Prize laureate in medicine, Alexis Carrel. The latter presented him with a watch as the only object and asked him to whom it belonged. Using the divinatory pendulum, Joseph Treyve gave the exact and detailed physical description of a man and his age. The man was none other than the famous aviator Charles Lindbergh! Thus, one commonly finds radiesthesists near famous men and spheres of power. They put their divinatory talent at the service of justice or the state.

#### Jacques Aymar in the service of justice

It is known that French police services also called upon radiesthesists to assist them in their search for suspects. In the seventeenth century, in Lyon, Jacques Aymar made a name for himself by working with the police. He successfully tracked down several thieves and murderers using his pendulum. The story goes that his rod was drawn to the tomb of an murdered woman while he was searching for water. His rod was then drawn to the murderer, her husband, who fled. Thus began his adventure as a detective. His successes became known, but unfortunately, he was later persecuted for his practice, which was equated with magic. At the time, this could cost one's life.

#### Jean-Louis Crozier and photography

More recently, certain radiesthesists have made headlines, achieving success rates that can surprise. Jean-Louis Crozier is known for having found and located hundreds of people with great precision. To do this work, he primarily uses a photograph. He preferably asks for three photographs, the name, age, date, and exact location where the person was last seen. The radiesthesist enters into resonance with the person through the photograph. Results like those obtained by Jean-Louis Crozier are exceptional, and one is entitled to wonder what part is personal gift and what part is work. His case, like that of Abbé Bouly and the other radiesthesists cited here, shows the extent of the pendulum's powers.

But how do they proceed to achieve these results?

## Methods of using the divinatory pendulum

We will briefly summarize in this section how to use a divinatory pendulum. You will find more detail in our article dedicated to using the radiesthesia pendulum.

### Defining the meaning of yes and no with your pendulum

When you use a pendulum for the first time, the first step is to establish the convention with your pendulum. Concretely, this will allow you to know what the direction of "Yes," "No," and "maybe" will be with your pendulum.

Once adopted, the mental convention should not be changed, as this leads to confusion. Whether you are right-handed or left-handed, you create your own convention, the one that resonates with you and your pendulum.

Take your pendulum and ask it to show you "yes" and "no." Note its responses. If the oscillation or rotation is weak, your connection with your pendulum may not yet be well established. It is then advisable to practice preliminary exercises to master holding the pendulum. Once you obtain a clear and distinct "yes" and "no," you can move on to the next exercise.

### How to ask a question of the pendulum?

#### Precise and clear questions

It is essential to ask simple questions: the pendulum answers yes or no, so you need closed questions—precise, unambiguous questions that go straight to the point.

"There is no bad radiesthesist, only badly posed questions."

For example, the question "Is this job good?" is not precise enough and too general. Indeed, "good" is a relative term. In this case, we might prefer a question like: "Does this job suit me?"

#### Ask only one question at a time

Another golden rule: ask only one question at a time, wait for the answer, then move to the next question. We recommend a funnel-shaped question structure, starting from the general and becoming increasingly precise as the questions progress.

For example, if you are searching for your keys using the pendulum, you would proceed as follows:

- Are my car keys in the house? (answer: yes)
- Are my car keys on the first floor? (answer: no)
- Are my car keys on the second floor? (answer: yes)
- Are my car keys in my bedroom? (answer: yes)
- Are my car keys on my bed? (answer: yes)

To save time in your search, it is also possible to use radiesthesia dials or boards.

### Using radiesthesia boards

Radiesthesia boards (or dials) save time in your search by offering a wider range of answers than yes and no. Indeed, thanks to dials, you can move beyond the classic convention to pursue more precise searches.

We recommend that you have established the convention with your pendulum (defined yes and no) before beginning to use dials. The use of dials is simple and is detailed in our article on radiesthesia boards.

## Examples of using the divinatory pendulum

### Searching with a witness for missing persons

#### The photograph as witness

In teleradiesthesia (the type of practice used by Jean-Louis Crozier), one must use a "witness," that is, an object that carries the vibration of the person. The photograph is a widely used support. In the absence of a photograph, the radiesthesist can use another witness, such as a letter handwritten by the person. It can also be a piece of jewelry belonging to them that they have worn frequently. The important thing is that the object has a strong association with the person. Certain pendulums, such as the Egyptian pendulum, unscrew, and the radiesthesist can then place a small witness object inside the Egyptian pendulum. This technique was used by Abbé Mermet.

#### Essential questions when searching for a missing person

From the photograph, the first question asked when searching for a missing person is whether they are alive. If the pendulum is absolutely motionless, this can be a sign that the person is deceased. Caution, however: this type of interpretation is reserved for highly trained radiesthesists. Next, the radiesthesist determines whether the person left of their own free will or whether there was an accident, an unforeseen event, or circumstances against their will. It is possible that the person left of their own free will and does not wish to be found by the person searching for them! Such situations pose ethical problems for the radiesthesist, who may question the pendulum to determine whether they should continue.

#### Simple, logical, and structured questions

Through a series of targeted questions, whose answers are "yes" or "no," with a witness, the radiesthesist arrives at the sought answer. The series of questions are simple, logical, and structured. For example: "Is person X located in their city of residence?" The answers allow one to advance step by step. The answers provided can be more detailed with boards, but some radiesthesists do not use them at all.

### The divinatory pendulum in clairvoyance

#### The pendulum: an amplifier of our intuition

In the case of a psychic who is already exercising their gift, the pendulum acts as an amplifier of their intuition. This technique is also used to guide people in search of their talents or direction. These practices are much more accessible than searching for missing persons. The questions addressed to psychics are often the same, and one will always find the romantic question gnawing at the human mind. Thus the question "Does such-and-such person love me?" can be posed to the psychic radiesthesist, or when penduluming for oneself, taking care to be in the most emotionally neutral state possible (the exercise can prove very difficult!).

#### A tool for analyzing psychological profiles

The divinatory pendulum can also accompany character readings or psychological profiling. The pendulum is particularly well suited to this exercise since it enters into resonance with the person and their energy. The analysis provided by the pendulum can be compared to results obtained with other very useful tools such as the Myers-Briggs test or the Enneagram. By combining the results of these personality tests with the results of the pendulum, one obtains a good overview of the person's psychological profile. Knowledge of one's profile will help the individual in their educational and career orientation and in their life choices.

#### An aid to decision-making

In moments of indecision, whether personal or professional, the divinatory pendulum can be consulted. In these situations, we are often overwhelmed by emotions or fatigue, and the act of clearly addressing one's question to the divinatory pendulum can support the person in their decision-making process. For example, one might consult because one is about to enter a partnership with someone, but doubts and reservations assail us. One always proceeds by questions, for example: "Is X honest?" Thus, the divinatory pendulum can help us illuminate situations, to better understand what we are facing, in order to make good decisions.

Caution, however: this practice is reserved for experienced radiesthesists. It is indeed difficult to obtain reliable answers when penduluming for oneself.

#### A tool for entering into resonance with future probabilities

It is important to clarify that the divinatory pendulum does not read the future. It only enters into resonance with what is already present, with future probabilities. The future is not fixed and will change according to our life choices, for which we retain our free will. Using the pendulum in clairvoyance can therefore allow access to these probabilities. It is important to maintain one's personal power when using the pendulum to read the future.

## What are the dangers of the divinatory pendulum?

All great radiesthesists have trained extensively, and they all possess a form of "gift" or at least a particular sensitivity. For the beginner radiesthesist, there are many pitfalls.

### Projection, or autosuggestion

The first and best known is psychological projection. That is, making the pendulum say what we want it to say. This is also called autosuggestion. Just as we must be careful of our own suggestions—in the form of ideas, emotions, or desires—we must also filter those of others. Any outside influence in the environment where the radiesthetic act takes place can have a significant impact on the divination. The person who consults a radiesthesist surely has expectations, desires, and projections. These must be identified to prevent them from interfering. This psychological work, seemingly simple a priori, is in reality very difficult to master.

### Overuse

The second danger is overuse or addiction. That is, questioning one's pendulum for every little thing. The divinatory pendulum should not answer trivial questions that we can easily answer ourselves. One must know when to turn to the divinatory pendulum and when it is not necessary. One must also avoid giving too much power to one's pendulum and retain free will.

To remedy these two pitfalls, it is advisable to work in a well-defined environment with one's pendulum. It is also advisable to concentrate well and take a moment of listening and contemplation when using the divinatory pendulum. Excellent concentration is crucial to avoid errors. Thus, training is absolutely necessary for the answers to be taken seriously.

### Asking the wrong questions

Another danger of the divinatory pendulum is that of asking the wrong questions, or posing questions poorly. The answers can then bring confusion in interpretation. It is always recommended to think carefully about each question and its formulation before addressing the pendulum.

### Insisting without "listening"

To be receptive to changes as subtle as those the pendulum detects, one cannot be in a posture of negligence. Most radiesthesists first ask the pendulum whether their search is permitted. This is particularly recommended when penduluming for someone else and one does not know all the elements of their situation.

This opening question allows one to operate within an ethical framework. If the pendulum's answer is "no," one must trust that answer and stop there. Here lies another danger: that of wanting to insist. When an answer is given, even if it does not please us, it is proper to end the radiesthetic act. The established radiesthesia protocol—that is, the convention between oneself and one's pendulum—must be respected scrupulously. It is an agreement made with oneself.

### Be prudent

The great radiesthesists who achieved impressive results have often been persecuted, even imprisoned, and some even executed. Their art was equated with magic in the era when the Church was hunting witches. Today, fortunately, this is no longer the case. Nevertheless, the rejection of the irrational persists for some people. It is therefore sometimes better to be prudent in society. Beginners are often so amazed by what they discover that they can easily be discredited or attacked. The element of mystery in the divinatory pendulum can be frightening. In the opinion of some, radiesthesia is a form of charlatanism. This judgment leads to a series of behaviors against which it is wise to protect oneself.

Indeed, many ask whether it is a science or an occult practice. Others will tell you that it is neither! It is certain that there are forces that exceed us, as Crozier in 1961 and Jean-Claude Rostan in 1999 showed during eclipses. On those dates, during both eclipses, their pendulum remained motionless! Through this experiment, they show us that the pendulum is eminently linked to the energies of our planet.

## A powerful tool

### The multiple powers of the pendulum

The divinatory pendulum is an excellent and powerful tool in the service of clairvoyance. A receiver of energetic variations and an amplifier of intuition, it assists the psychic. Although entering into resonance with the magnetic and vibratory fields of beings may resemble communication with the invisible, the divinatory pendulum is not magic.

It can provide sometimes impressive results in the hands of trained radiesthesists who know how to spot the dangers of the divinatory pendulum and guard against them. As the accounts of the great radiesthesists attest, the powers of the pendulum are multiple.

### Different pendulums for different benefits

There are different divinatory pendulums that you can use according to your needs and your feelings. Thus, you can opt for stone pendulums such as the amethyst pendulum, rose quartz, rock crystal, tiger's eye, etc. You then benefit from all the properties of these stones during your practice with the pendulum.

You can also opt for a wooden pendulum, which is connected to the spirit of the tree, thus helping to easily capture information.

Finally, the choice of a metal pendulum is recommended for beginners, as its weight, chain, and point are ideal for obtaining precise answers during practice.

### Rigor and concentration needed for penduluming

If the practice of divination with the pendulum is simple and structured, it requires rigor, concentration, a strong ethic, and much training. When one knows that some manage thereby to find missing persons, it becomes easier to accept that others might see in you your personality, or help you find what worries you about a potential associate.

The radiesthesist enters into relation with information present, through the pendulum, which reacts to its energetic quality. Be ready to be surprised by the powers of the pendulum.
