1 Introduction


---


The adequacy of our understanding of Nature is proportional to our knowledge of the laws acting within it. The history of the development of Natural Science over at least the last hundred years testifies that the appearance of experimental results which cannot be explained within the framework of generally accepted scientific notions is a direct indication of the incompleteness of our knowledge of Nature.

Over recent decades it has been constantly asserted that all known phenomena of Nature and all experimental results are exhaustively explained by the four known interactions: electromagnetism, gravitation, and the strong and weak interactions. However, over the last fifty years some twenty experimental results have accumulated that have found no explanation within the framework of these interactions [1].

Quite independently of this dramatic — for the present stage of development of Natural Science — situation, a search for new long-range interactions had been continuing since the 1930s. It suffices to point to the works of H. Tetrode [2] and A.D. Fokker [3], and later J. Wheeler and R. Feynman [4, 5] and other authors. However, these works did not receive the development they deserved. The concepts of torsion fields were the sole exception.

The theory of torsion fields (fields of twisting, i.e. "torsion") is a traditional direction in theoretical physics, going back to works of the second half of the last century. In its modern form, however, the theory of torsion fields was formulated thanks to the ideas of Élie Cartan, who was the first to point out clearly and unambiguously the existence in Nature of fields generated by the density of the angular momentum of rotation. By now the bibliography of the world's periodical literature on torsion fields runs to about 10 thousand papers by roughly one hundred authors. More than half of these theorists work in Russia.

Despite the sufficiently well-developed theoretical apparatus, torsion fields remained merely a theoretical object until the beginning of the seventies of this century. That is why they did not become as universal a factor as electrodynamics and gravitation. Moreover, there existed a theoretical conclusion that, since the constant of spin-torsion interactions is proportional to the product G·ħ (G is the gravitational constant, ħ is Planck's constant), i.e. it is almost 30 orders of magnitude weaker than gravitational interactions, then even if torsion effects do exist in Nature, they cannot make a noticeable contribution to observable phenomena.

However, at the beginning of the 1970s, as a result of the works of F. Hehl [6-8], T. Kibble [9], D. Sciama [10] and others, it was shown that this conclusion is valid not for torsion fields in general, but only for static torsion fields generated by spinning sources without radiation.

In the following 20 years a large number of papers appeared on the theory of dynamic torsion (a spinning source with radiation). In these works it was shown that the Lagrangian of a spinning source with radiation contains up to a dozen terms with constants that do not depend on G or ħ in any way, and with respect to which the theory imposes no requirement of mandatory smallness. This fact is well known

to specialists in torsion field theory. Nevertheless, the old point of view about the smallness of the constants of spin-torsion interactions continued, for the following 15 years, to psychologically hinder a serious and comprehensive undertaking of the search for experimental manifestations of torsion effects. Only at the beginning of the 1980s, in Russia, was attention drawn to the global role of the conclusions of the dynamic theory of torsion fields. It was precisely then that attention was drawn to the existence in physics of an extensive experimental phenomenology containing many experimental results that had found no explanation from the standpoint of the four known interactions, and which constitute the experimental manifestation of torsion effects. With the creation — for the first time in the world, in Russia, in the 1980s — of torsion field generators, purposeful research was deployed and carried out in many directions on the search for manifestations of torsion fields, which yielded a large volume of practical results.

For a number of situations it is useful to interpret the fields as, in a certain sense, polarization states of the physical vacuum.

Let us make a number of preliminary remarks. We shall regard the Physical Vacuum as a material medium isotropically filling all of space (both free space and matter), possessing a quantum structure and unobservable (on average) in the unperturbed state. Such a Vacuum is described by the operator 〈0] [52]. Different vacuum states arise upon violation of the symmetry and invariance of the Vacuum [14]. In particular cases, when considering different physical processes and phenomena, the observer usually creates models of the Physical Vacuum adequate to those processes and phenomena. The use of different models of the Physical Vacuum is characteristic of modern astrophysics, in which the following are used as constructive models, for example: the Θ-vacuum, the Urn vacuum, the Boulevard vacuum, the Hartle-Hawking vacuum, the Rindler vacuum, and so on.

In the modern interpretation, the Physical Vacuum is conceived of as a complex quantum dynamical object that manifests itself through fluctuations. The theoretical approach is built on the concepts of S. Weinberg, A. Salam, and S. Glashow.

However, as will become clear from the further analysis, it was deemed expedient to return to P. Dirac's electron-positron model of the Physical Vacuum, in a somewhat modified interpretation of that model. The return to Dirac's models, despite the known shortcomings and contradictions of that model, can be considered justified — and the models themselves as not having exhausted their constructive potential — if they help formulate conclusions that do not follow directly from modern models.

At the same time, taking into account that the Vacuum is defined as a state without particles, and proceeding from the model of the classical spin as a ring wave packet [15] (in Belinfante's terminology [16] — a circulating energy flux), we shall consider the Vacuum to be a system of ring wave packets of electrons and positrons, rather than of electron-positron pairs proper.

Under the assumptions made, it is easy to see that the condition of true electroneutrality of the electron-positron Vacuum is met by a state in which the ring wave packets of the electrons and the positrons are nested within one another. If, in addition, the spins of these nested ring packets are opposite, then such a system will be self-compensated not only in charge but also in classical spin and magnetic moment. Such a system of nested ring wave packets we shall call a phyton (Fig. 1A).

A dense packing of phytons [17] will be considered a simplified model of the Physical Vacuum (Fig. 1B).

It is useful to note that in the experiments of A. Krisch [18] the observed effects are tantamount to a demonstration of the possibility of realizing — albeit dynamic — nested states in systems with opposite spins, as in the assumed

In accordance with the approach set out, one can say that the single medium — the Physical Vacuum — can exist in different phase (more precisely, polarization) states, EGS-states. This medium in a state of charge polarization manifests itself as an electromagnetic field (E). This same medium in a state of spin longitudinal polarization manifests itself as a gravitational field (G). Finally, the same medium (the Physical Vacuum) in a state of spin transverse polarization manifests itself as a spin (torsion) field (S). Thus, to the EGS-polarization states of the Physical Vacuum there correspond EGS-fields.

The concept of the phase state of the Physical Vacuum and of the polarization states of the Physical Vacuum was used in general form in many works (see, for example, [40]). It has repeatedly been noted in the past that a classical field can be considered as a state of the Vacuum [23, 25]. The polarization of the Vacuum according to A.D. Sakharov [25] is interpreted as spin longitudinal polarization (gravitational field). Torsion fields possess properties that differ substantially from the known properties in electromagnetism and in gravitation.

The most important properties of torsion fields (radiations) are:

1. Unlike electromagnetism, where like charges repel and unlike charges attract, in torsion fields like charges attract and unlike charges repel.

2. Since torsion fields are generated by classical spin, then as a result of the action of a torsion field on some object, only the spin state of that object will change.

3. Passage through physical media without interaction with those media, i.e. without losses. It is useful to note that, unconnected with torsion fields, Soviet physicists showed more than ten years ago that spin signals propagate in such a way that they cannot be screened.

4. The group velocity of torsion waves is not less than 10⁹ · c. The journal Physics-Uspekhi (UFN) published a large review analyzing astrophysical objects moving at speeds greater than the speed of light [53].

The absence of losses in the propagation of torsion waves makes possible communication over great distances using low power at the transmitter. There arises the possibility of creating underwater and underground communication. The high group velocity of torsion waves removes the problem of signal delay even within the Galaxy.

5. Since all known substances possess a nonzero collective spin, all substances possess their own torsion field. The spatial-frequency structure of a substance's own torsion field is determined by the chemical composition and the spatial structure of the molecules or the crystal lattice of that substance.

6. Torsion fields possess memory. A torsion source with a specific spatial-frequency structure of its torsion field polarizes the Physical Vacuum by classical spin in some space surrounding it. In this case the spatial spin structure that arises is preserved after the indicated torsion source is moved to another region of space.

The paradigm of torsion fields made it possible to obtain fundamentally new results in practically all scientific-technical directions.

3 Torsion propulsors


---


The new notions about fields and inertial forces set out in the work [13] made it possible to see their connection with torsion fields and to predict the existence in nature of a new class of reference frames, which were named [13] accelerated locally Lorentzian reference frames of the second kind. Unlike the accelerated locally Lorentzian frames of the first kind introduced by A. Einstein, the new frames arise in the case when compensated inertial forces act on the center of mass of an isolated system.

A simple example of an accelerated locally Lorentzian reference frame is the frame associated with the center of mass of a rotating gyroscope. Indeed, compensated centrifugal inertial forces act on the center of mass of a freely rotating gyroscope. Therefore the center of mass of such a gyroscope is at rest or moves rectilinearly and uniformly relative to an inertial frame of observation. If the equilibrium of the inertial forces in the gyroscope is disturbed in some way, then the center of mass of the gyroscope will move with acceleration under the action of internal uncompensated forces.

This conclusion does not contradict the known theorem on the conservation of the momentum of the center of mass of an isolated mechanical system. According to this theorem, the internal forces of an isolated system cannot change the momentum of its center of mass, and in the proof of the theorem the following conditions were used:

1) the internal forces satisfy Newton's third law;
2) the internal forces are all those forces that act within the internal volume bounded by the walls of the isolated system.

Most forces of classical mechanics satisfy the first condition and can be divided into external and internal according to the second. However, in mechanics there exist forces that do not satisfy Newton's third law. Such, as is known, are the inertial forces, since one cannot say from which bodies these forces are applied. Moreover, inertial forces do not fall under the second condition, since they are simultaneously both internal and external to an isolated (in the sense defined above) mechanical system.

Consequently, the motion of mechanical systems under the action of internal uncompensated inertial forces does not contradict the theorem on the conservation of the momentum of the center of mass of an isolated system of Newtonian mechanics, since inertial forces do not satisfy the conditions under which this theorem is proven.

As an example of a mechanical system whose center of mass moves under the action of uncompensated inertial forces, a device is proposed that demonstrates the connection between translational and rotational inertial forces and that can be called a four-dimensional gyroscope. It consists of a central mass M and two masses m rotating synchronously toward each other about an axis fixed to the central mass M (see Fig. 2).

If at some moment of time mechanical energy is imparted to this system (for example, by spinning up the masses m), it will be set in motion, and we have the following equations of motion [13]:

4 Torsion technologies for materials production


---


It is generally known that upon cooling of a melt, the formation of the solid phase of a substance (for example, a metal) is realized through two processes. The ions in the melt must take up positions in the potential wells corresponding to the locations of the crystal-lattice sites of the solid, and the spins of the ions (atoms) must be oriented along the edges of the lattice as prescribed by the type of crystal lattice. The latter circumstance is usually used to explain dia-, para-, and ferromagnetism. The non-fulfillment of either of these two conditions leads to the structure of the solid substance being different from the natural one prescribed by the traditional laws of solid-state physics.

As a result of the action of an external torsion field (radiation) on the melt — for example, from a torsion generator — only the spin state of the system of free atoms in the melt will change. If isotropic torsion radiation acts on a melt of a substance, then with sufficient exposure time and correctly established parameters of the melt, all the atoms of the melt will pass into a state of unidirectional spin orientation. In such a state, through spin-torsion interactions the atoms will experience mutual attraction. By virtue of this mutual torsion attraction, the melt, as a spin system, will be internally stable. As a result, the strong mutual torsion attraction, even with slow cooling, will not allow the atoms to orient their spins along the edges of the crystal lattice, and the lattice
                                                                                      10
will not be realized. The consequence of this will be an amorphous structure of the substance (metal), a quasi-glass structure.

With the fulfillment of the conditions indicated above, under the action on the melt of torsion radiation with a nonisotropic spatial-frequency structure, either crystallization will occur — but with a crystal lattice "induced" in the substance by the established structure of the external torsion field — or torsion-induced defects of the crystal lattice will arise.

All the indicated variants of the theoretically predicted results of the action of a torsion field on metal melts were experimentally confirmed at the Institute of Problems of Materials Science of the Academy of Sciences of Ukraine, in works carried out jointly with the ISTC VENT during the period 1989–1993.

5 Torsion means of communication and information transmission


---


In traditional radio communication means, the large required powers are necessary to compensate for the weakening of signals as they pass through free space in connection with their attenuation by the inverse-square law, and also to compensate for losses as the signals pass through absorbing media.

In this case the compensation must be carried out to such a degree that the transmitted signal at the receiver input has an intensity exceeding the sensitivity of that receiver.

Besides this, taking into account the propagation speed of radio signals, the signal delay already creates certain difficulties in satellite communication systems. These difficulties grow into serious problems for communication with vehicles in deep space.

The difficulties of beyond-the-horizon communication lead to the necessity of building complex global communication networks with relays.

In individual cases, radio communication can be realized not only in the domain of super-long waves but, for example, for underground communication as well; however, in this case the rate of information transmission is lost, to say nothing of the obvious technical difficulties.

A number of radio communication problems are in principle unsolvable, such as, for example, communication with spacecraft descending from orbit, since they are screened by the plasma that arises around these vehicles upon entry into the dense layers of the atmosphere.

Some questions of radio communication cannot be solved because existing systems are close to the physically limiting capabilities. Systems with throughput capacity close to the Shannon limits are known.
                                                                                     11
All the problems indicated are overcome through the use of torsion communication [43]. It suffices to point to the three properties of torsion radiations noted above: torsion radiations are not attenuated with distance and are not absorbed by natural media, and they have a group velocity not lower than 10⁹ · c.

Since torsion signals are not attenuated with distance and are not absorbed, there is no need for large transmitter powers even over long routes. By virtue of the absence of absorption by natural media, torsion signals make it possible to provide both underground and underwater communication, and communication through plasma. With such a high group velocity, the problems of communication, control, and navigation can be solved in real time even within the galaxy, and not only the solar system.

The first experiments on the transmission of binary signals over a torsion communication channel were carried out in April 1986 in Moscow. The torsion transmitter was installed on the first floor of a building and had no devices of the radio-antenna type that could be brought out onto the roof. The torsion receiver was placed on the second floor of a building at a distance of about 22 km (Fig. 6). Under these conditions the torsion signal could propagate only in a straight line from the transmitter to the receiver.

This meant that, in addition to the terrain relief, taking into account the building density in Moscow, the torsion signal had to overcome a screen equivalent to a reinforced-concrete wall more than 50 m thick. For radio communication without relays, this is a practically unsolvable problem.

In the communication sessions carried out, a binary torsion signal in the M2 start-stop telegraph code was received without error while the torsion transmitter consumed 30 mW of energy. In additional experiments the torsion transmitter was brought up to the receiver (a route of zero length). In this case the intensity of the registered signal did not change. Thus it was shown that, for torsion communication, as the theory had predicted, the torsion signal is not absorbed and is not attenuated with distance.

6 Torsion geophysics


---


At the fundamental level, as already noted, the nature of torsion fields is connected with classical spin. Two important consequences follow from this.

First, since the atoms in all molecules and in all crystals have not only a definite spatial position but also a strictly definite mutual orientation of spins, all molecules and all crystals have their own torsion field with a characteristic spatial-frequency distribution of intensity (a spatial-frequency spectrum). A large quantity of a homogeneous substance will create a collective torsion field characteristic of that substance. Taking into account that torsion fields are not absorbed by natural media and that their intensity does not decrease with distance, then a locally concentrated homogeneous substance located at an arbitrary depth within the Planet will create outside the planet the same characteristic torsion field as if that substance were located on the surface of the planet. Therefore, by registering the spatial-frequency structure of the torsion fields of Planets, one can obtain important information about their internal structure.

Second, the development of the directions concerning fields as polarization (phase) states of the Physical Vacuum [1] made it possible to define the torsion field as a state of spin transverse polarization of the Physical Vacuum. This served as the basis, alongside other theoretical factors, for the supposition that by registering the spatial-frequency structure of the torsion field of a Planet or of a part of its surface — i.e. the spatial structure of the spin polarization states — one can obtain important information about the internal macroscopic

It was possible to experimentally confirm the correctness of the supposition that when any objects are photographed, the objects' own torsion fields, arriving at the photoemulsion together with the electromagnetic (light) flux, change the orientation of the spins of the emulsion atoms in such a way that the emulsion spins reproduce the spatial structure of this external torsion field. As a result, on any photograph, alongside the visible image, there always exists an invisible torsion image.

Understanding of this fact made it possible, by analogy with the optical processing of images, to construct a procedure for extracting torsion images from photographs and processing them [44]. As indicated in Fig. 7, first a slide or photograph (3) is illuminated by a generator of isotropic broadband torsion radiation (1). In this case the spin structure of the emulsion atoms can be considered a two-dimensional spin matrix, which plays the role of a two-dimensional spin modulator (3).

After the isotropic torsion radiation (2) passes through the original photograph (3), the modulated torsion radiation (4) will reproduce the spin structure of the spatial torsion field that was perceived by the emulsion during the photographing. However, this original torsion field is a superposition of the torsion fields from all the sources within the body of the Planet. These sources may be geological formations or deposits of useful minerals. Since these structural formations have characteristic spatial-frequency spectra, if the task consists in isolating, for example, the zone of concentration of some substance (a useful mineral), then the modulated torsion radiation (4) must be subjected to the corresponding filtration. To this end, two-dimensional spin matrices — spin filters — were developed. Such spin (torsion) filters pass only those spatial frequencies which correspond to the characteristic spatial frequencies of the torsion radiations of the substance sought.

After passing through the torsion filter (5), the torsion radiation (6) will be present only in those places, relative to the original photograph (3), where the substance sought is located. This radiation, filtered for the useful torsion component, is fed onto clean special photographic material (7), subjected to a special physicochemical treatment that ensures the possibility of photoregistration of torsion radiations.

The indicated procedure was realized in the created apparatus complex for torsion image processing, which made it possible to work in various functional modes.

Besides the indicated functional capability of extracting information about the internal structure of various cosmic objects, or about the presence of particular useful minerals on Planets and their satellites, the apparatus complex for physical processing makes it possible to extract integral torsion information from photographs if the torsion filter (5) in Fig. 7 is not used.

All of modern observational astrophysics and astronomy have had the possibility of working only with visible objects ("visible" in the broad sense, including, for example, radio observations as well). Taking into account that light from distant sources sometimes travels thousands of light years, and that over this time the stars undergo significant displacements in space, it is obvious that modern astronomy is in fact not contemporary in the proper sense, but merely paleoastronomy (we study what has long since ceased to be). Let us take into account the superluminal velocity of torsion waves and consider that all stars rotate, i.e. are torsion sources. By registering their torsion radiations, one can obtain the true distribution of stars in the sky, their positions in the real time of the Universe. The first experimental results on fixing stars in their true positions were obtained by N.A. Kozyrev [45], and later by M.M. Lavrentiev, I.A. Yeganova [46], and A.F. Pugach [47].

The second important problem of astrophysics is the following contradiction. If one proceeds from the existence of only two long-range interactions — electromagnetism and gravitation, in which the wave velocity cannot exceed "c" — then the interaction time between the edges of the observable Universe will be commensurate with the lifetime of the Universe. One would then have to recognize that the majority of the far-distant objects of the Universe practically do not interact, i.e. the Universe cannot be considered as an integral system of internally interconnected objects (A.A. Silin was the first to point to this circumstance).

At the same time, it has been known for many decades that stars form a cellular structure, i.e. there is a physical interaction that holds the Universe in the form of such an integral and stable structure. It is possible that, having a velocity on the order of 10⁹·c, it is precisely the torsion fields of the stars that ensure the emergence and existence of the cellular structure in the distribution of stars in the Universe. It is not excluded that the problem of "hidden mass" is in fact a consequence of the fact that torsion interactions are not taken into account.

In this preliminary analysis, let us point to one further important circumstance. Just as, through torsion processing of cosmic photographs, one can obtain an image of the internal structure of our planet, one can, by carrying out such processing of images of stars — for example, the Sun — obtain information about their internal structure and their internal dynamics. Not only theoretical but numerous experimental results testify that torsion fields are an important reality of Nature.
