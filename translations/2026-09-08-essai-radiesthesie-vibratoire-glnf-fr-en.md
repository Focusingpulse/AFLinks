# Essay on Vibratory Radiesthesia — Review of the Chaumery & de Bélizal Classic

*Léon Chaumery & André de Bélizal. 3rd edition revised and augmented. Preface by Patrick Darcheville. Editions Dervy, 2013, 192 pages, 15€.*

Reading the 1939 original — long out of print — of Messrs. Léon Chaumery and André de Bélizal's *Essay on Vibratory Radiesthesia* is a genuine pleasure for anyone interested in this field. The 1956 edition published by Dangles is equally hard to find. The Editions Dervy have therefore done a useful service for specialists in radiesthetic practice.

The preface writer, Patrick Darcheville, is a lecturer and geobiologist, but also known for his writings: *Radiesthesia from Antiquity to the Present Day*, *The Flora of Cathedrals* (floral symbolism in religious architecture), *Druids or Monks? Celtic Monasticism*, *The Numbers of the Apocalypse*. This signals the seriousness of this revised and augmented re-edition.

This is all the more important because the original edition significantly predates the development of the New Age movement, which may have led this popular practice of radiesthesia astray, with everyone attributing to themselves some quality of seeker of "the exceptional."

Let us first define radiesthesia. It is the art of capturing the waves emitted by all bodies, visible or invisible, of identifying them, from near or very far, of determining the precise location where they are found, their nature, their composition, their quantity — in other words: seeing the invisible.

In a second step, let us attempt to enter the very heart of this work written by four hands.

Léon Chaumery, a seasoned radiesthetist of the early twentieth century, and André de Bélizal, a brilliant researcher, named the form of their pendule "Louksor" (the universal pendulum) — a receiver and emitter, it can receive and emit all the vibrations of the spectrum, both visible and invisible. They began systematic study, expanding over the years their field of research to encompass all forms: pendulums, various mobiles, metallic torsades, and so on.

They reached the conclusion that all the statuary, architecture, and even the graphic designs of certain ancient civilizations (triskel, swastika...) obeyed these laws of force emission. Lines, volumes, arrangement, orientation — everything was calculated to generate an ideal equilibrium, a perfect integration into the space of certain places and certain privileged individuals.

A lost science, or one deliberately occulted? A science or a magic — but do they not meet somewhere? — a science that might have yielded the Golden Ratio of the greatest sacred monuments of old?

Certain researchers wonder whether these forms of unknown energies do not interact with structures of the continuum other than those taken into account by our science today.

With the emissions of energy due to forms, concludes the famous Soviet parapsychologist Sergueïev, we would undoubtedly go much further, if we knew more about them, than with all our electricity and our nuclear power. There are certainly reasons why this knowledge was lost.
