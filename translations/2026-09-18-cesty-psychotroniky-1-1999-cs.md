PATHS OF PSYCHOTRONICS Volume 1 (1999), issue 1

What is psychotronics?

The phenomena that psychotronics investigates have accompanied humankind on all continents since time immemorial. Many of them share a common denominator: they are closely bound to our psyche while simultaneously being accompanied by physical manifestations. The contact between psyche and physics is perhaps one of the most complicated questions even of today's highly developed science.

We have defined psychotronics as an interdisciplinary scientific discipline investigating distant interactions between living organisms and their environment, together with the energetic and informational manifestations associated with them. As a discipline, it studies the bonds between consciousness (including unconscious processes), energy, and matter. Through the study of these relationships it contributes to the objectification and understanding of hitherto unverified human capacities and possibilities, and to a broader knowledge of living matter and of the world in general.

Z. Rejdák — 5th International Conference on Psychotronics, 1983

Motto: The Unity of Czech and Slovak Researchers in Psychotronics

Our task is to seek the natural-scientific foundations of psychotronics, to bring together researchers and all those interested in this field of human knowledge under one roof, into one community.

Paths of Psychotronics

The task of the journal this year is to carry out public education in the field of psychotronics. Over the last ten years psychotronics has been considerably devalued and in public awareness it has become a natural-scientifically worthless "mystery-mongering." And it is this state of affairs that we would like to remedy.

Editorial

The existence of psychotronics rests on a nontrivial presupposition.

The world and the events in it are intelligible and knowable — this holds for the domain of psychotronics as well. With development it has become clear that this knowability must be grounded in a foundation that is only now being formed, one standing on the informational nature of reality.

Without this presupposition, psychotronics as a natural science could not have come into being.

In psychotronics there exists no standard natural-scientific model.

A standard model generally makes it possible to unify our conceptions of nature on the basis of precisely defined building blocks and a system of interactions between them. Today we have a physical, chemical, and biological standard model of the description of nature.

We can responsibly state here that there exists no natural-scientific theory of psychotronics, and thus a genuine natural-scientific descriptive system of psychotronic phenomena occurring in nature and in natural events is entirely lacking — something we could designate as a standard model.

The unwarranted nature of the term "psychotronic."

One becomes a natural scientist not merely by interpreting one's "model" of knowable reality nicely and even making predictions according to it, but only by being prepared to humbly submit one's theory to experiment (practical observation).

Therefore, with full responsibility, we must admit for the present that there exists no natural scientist in the field of psychotronics — thus no so-called "psychotronic" in the equivalent sense of a biologist or physicist — and therefore the use of such a term is strongly misleading and unwarranted.

Researchers in the field of psychotronics therefore cannot be associated with the term "psychotronic," and at the same time, of all the people who proclaim themselves psychotronics, it must be declared that they have little in common with it. In this, we researchers advocating the natural-scientific conception of psychotronics distance ourselves from everything that in the last ten years has considerably profaned and damaged psychotronics. A similar situation has arisen in our homeland as in Western Europe in the 1970s, where, after parapsychology had managed to build positions and gain a certain respect even in academic settings, there came a period when anyone and everyone declared himself a parapsychologist in order to make money from the foolishness of ordinary people through various services founded on "allegedly guaranteed" parapsychological abilities and practices.

The current situation in psychotronics.

The 1990s represent a crisis period in the development of psychotronics. The creation of a new organization of researchers and friends should contribute to its revival.

On 9 March 1999 the foundational building stone was laid for the establishment of an organization bringing together researchers in the field of psychotronics. On 8 April 1999 the basic documents were created that make it possible to found a community of researchers in psychotronics. The result is the conception of founding the so-called Unity of Czech and Slovak Researchers in Psychotronics (JČSBP). The founding group of the Unity is Stanislav Štarman, Oldřich Válek, and Ľudovít Matula. A JČSBP Office was established: V Zahradách 24, 18000 Prague 8, and a branch in Slovakia: Kvetoslavov 32, 93041 (near Bratislava). The prospective plan was to found the Unity without legal subjectivity by the end of April 1999 — which happened — and, assuming the growth of a membership base, to realize it in the form of a legal entity by the end of 1999.

We are at the beginning of the path toward a scientific (modern) psychotronics that will create a standard model for psychotronic phenomena and will entitle us to use, in this field of knowledge of nature and man, the public designation "psychotronic."

Today we have at our disposal a series of initial speculations and countless experimental findings — but without natural-scientific credit. These facts, however, clearly testify that work on the knowledge of psychotronic phenomena and the development of psychotronics is continuously ongoing. The ranks of lay and professional researchers must be unified in this work toward common creative activity, because the time has come in which the preconditions for the rise of a genuine psychotronics as a science have already been created.

We regard the psychotronic phenomena of telepathy, telesthesia, telekinesis, and biotherapy as entirely real natural phenomena, unfortunately somewhat outside the classical scientific view of the world. We likewise regard as real the psychotronic interactions that participate in the formation of the relationships of matter, energy, and consciousness, as delimited by the initial conception of psychotronics.

Our psychotronics.

Psychotronics is unambiguously a part of the organic whole that man has created in order to better understand nature and its laws. Psychotronics is nothing outside this whole; on the other hand, within this whole it has its own specific view of the world, by which, after all, every discipline manifests itself analogously. This view always determines the place and role of the given discipline in science and in the knowledge of nature.

Within this whole psychotronics does not yet have a natural-scientific level, but nothing prevents it from attaining one — though this will not happen without persistent, systematic, and purposeful theoretical and experimental work.

The task of the Unity of Czech and Slovak Researchers in Psychotronics is to unify the efforts of all researchers and to participate in creating such a form of psychotronics as could present itself in academic settings.

In this journal, which is a "notebook on psychotronics," we will address the extensive problems of psychotronics and simultaneously, step by step, publish the path of psychotronics into natural science. It must be emphasized, however, that it will not always be possible to adhere to natural-scientific criteria, because psychotronics itself brings new views and findings that will first have to be accepted into natural science.

This journal will not be built on "mystery-mongering" or on the so-called "mystery of psychotronics," but will maintain a certain professional level. In its foundations it will strive to stand on the positions of science. Science has the disadvantage, compared to pseudoscience, that it is not entitled to dogmatically reject even the strangest claims. Taking a serious stance, however, is not always simple, and therefore we will move within the bounds of so-called healthy skepticism.

Psychotronics is not an academic science, but in this journal we will regard it as a scientific discipline.

We will build the reality of psychotronics on the analogy: "Just as energy exists against the background of matter, and together they form the base for physical reality, so information exists against the background of energy, and together they form the base for the psychotronic reality of our conception of the world."

The Origin of Psychotronics

Considerable speculation currently prevails about the origin of psychotronics. This is caused by the fact that the historical period of socialism and communist ideology has passed. And simultaneously there has passed the historical necessity of the distinctness of psychotronics from parapsychology, which was at one time the basic proclaimed slogan at the birth of psychotronics as a certain bionics of man. The bourgeois pseudoscience "parapsychology" was taken in the socialist bloc as an offspring of imperialist thinking, contradictory to communist thought, and for that reason it could never take root in the former socialist camp.

We will look at the problem of the origin of psychotronics from the perspective of several researchers, according to their published works.

1) According to O. Válek, "Psychotronika, nová věda" (Psychotronics, a New Science) (1992)

Oldřich Válek is an independent researcher in the field of psychotronics who, as one of the few, defended a diploma thesis in the field of psychotronics.

In the crisis-ridden 1960s, an effort arose in our country to legitimize that which parapsychology in the Western world had until then been engaged in. By using natural-scientific methods, the extraordinary phenomena bound to the human psyche — which until then had been inappropriately called paranormal — were to be illuminated. This task, connected with objective verification, was taken up by so-called psychotronics. It was founded by the Czechoslovak researchers Ing. Karel Drbal, PhDr. Zdeněk Rejdák, and Prof. Jaroslav Stuchlík. This happened on 18 March 1967. This date thereby became permanently inscribed in the history of psychotronics.

The term "psychotronika" was adopted from the French journal Tout la Radio, Électronique-BF-Télévision no. 192 from January 1955, in which there appeared an article headed "Psychotronique?". It recommended supplementing the technical dictionary with this new term, which had been proposed by the Frenchman Ferdinand Clerc. The term PSYCHOTRONIKA, expressing the unity of the psychic and the energetic, quickly spread and was adopted in our country as well. Special natural phenomena manifesting through extraordinary capacities of the human psyche, falling within psychotronics, we call psychotronic phenomena.

In March 1967, likewise on the initiative of Ing. Karel Drbal and Dr. Zdeněk Rejdák, several Czechoslovak scientists met at Prof. Jaroslav Stuchlík's and established a coordination group for psychotronic research, with the aim of coordinating the research activity of scientists in this area within the former Czechoslovak Socialist Republic. At the end of 1969, a section for psychotronic research was founded under the Committee of Applied Cybernetics of the Czechoslovak Scientific-Technical Society.

Psychotronics of this period is essentially conceived as a bionics of man. It sets as its goal to supplement the laws of the living and non-living world with new psychological, physical, and biological findings derived from certain extraordinary manifestations of the human psyche, and further to seek the practical consequences of this problem for man. The goal set for psychotronics could not be realized on the basis of the existing development of parapsychology; therefore it was re-evaluated and a selection was made of the phenomena it investigated, from which psychotronics took over the areas of the initial delimitation of its subject matter:

1) Telepathy — it is communication between people that often manifests under the influence of strong emotional impulses, but which can equally well be induced in the laboratory. Telepathy is the transfer of information (not of abstract thoughts, but rather of images) directly from the consciousness of one person into the consciousness of another person, without the participation of the senses. It is therefore designated as extrasensory perception (ESP).

2) Telegnosis — it concerns the cognition of objective reality and events of the external world, detected by consciousness, wherein the subject has had no contact with these facts and could not learn of them through the known senses or otherwise. The better-known designations are clairvoyance, second sight, etc.

3) Psychokinesis — it rests on the principle of a person emitting hitherto unknown energy, or of the utilization of energy in the environment by the consciousness of man — the receptor — to influence some mechanical system, movable or immovable, i.e. to perform immediate work, to achieve a measurable physical effect (e.g. action at a distance on easily rotatable systems, etc.).

4) Biotherapy — it is the capacity of a person to transfer their own energy to another person with the aim of temporarily or even permanently improving their psychosomatic state. We can say that psychotronics, as an independent interdisciplinary scientific discipline, investigates distant bonds between organisms and their environment and the energetic and informational manifestations associated with them.

The phenomena of telepathy, telegnosis, and psychokinesis are regarded as the main domain of psychotronics.

In June 1973 the section for psychotronic research under the Committee of Applied Cybernetics of the Czechoslovak Scientific-Technical Society organized the first international psychotronic conference in Prague, which became a significant milestone in the development of psychotronics on a world scale.

Psychotronics is defined as a boundary interdisciplinary scientific discipline. The main domain of psychotronics and at the same time its specific feature, however, is the rise of a theory and the investigation of distant interactions between subjects, and between subjects and objects (living and non-living). Psychotronics has from the beginning a two-polar character — the first philosophical-psychological, the second technical-physical. This is meant to help it in forming a comprehensive method of knowing reality and the objective causes of psychotronic phenomena.

"Psychotronics was born as a consequence of the historical necessity of re-evaluating parapsychology through the use of the methods of natural science and Marxist materialist dialectics. Psychotronics has deep roots in human knowledge that, from the standpoint of natural science, is pseudoscientific. Psychotronics re-evaluated the historical sources of 'spiritual knowledge' and, through its methodological approach, created the foundations of a modern potential science."

"Psychotronics has three sources: 1. parapsychology (psychotronics no longer arises in direct continuity with occult teachings), 2. metascientific conceptions (metaphysics, mesmerism, metapsychics or metapsychology), and 3. natural philosophy (the teachings of ancient cultures, which perceive nature as a harmonious indivisible whole not only of the material but also of the spiritual level)."

Fig. 1) The continuity of psychotronics with historical teachings and period conceptions

Psychotronics advocates the interdisciplinary scientific investigation of interactions between living organisms, their internal and external environment, at the energetic and informational level; it further seeks connections with the so-called biological plasma of the organism and with distant interaction, which is the hypothetical presupposition in the realization of a psychotronic phenomenon. Psychotronics recognizes the connection of matter, energy, and consciousness. The integrated study of these internal bonds contributes to a new understanding of the energetic and informational properties of man and other living organisms, of life processes, and of matter as such.

It defines psychotronics as the science addressing the relationship of matter, energy, consciousness, and distant interaction.

2) According to M. Nakonečný, "Lexikon hermetismu" (Lexicon of Hermeticism) (1993)

Milan Nakonečný is a university professor at the Faculty of Arts of Charles University, a recognized expert in the fields of psychology and hermeticism.

In its essence, psychotronics is merely a new name for parapsychology, introduced by a group of scientists striving to distance themselves from certain unscientific parapsychological methods. The term psychotronics was proposed by Ing. F. Clerc for "phenomena of the use of energy equipped in the process of thinking and energy equipped by the impulse of the human will." The term was then promoted by two Czech parapsychologists, or psychotronics: K. Drbal and Z. Rejdák. In March 1967, in Prague, under the chairmanship of Prof. J. Stuchlík, then the doyen of Czech psychiatry, the Coordination Group for Research on Questions of Psychotronics was established; it later also organized the first international conference.

At the first international congress of researchers in psychotronics, the International Association for Psychotronic Research was established under the presidency of Z. Rejdák. The leading representative of psychotronics, Z. Rejdák, understands this new scientific discipline as an interdisciplinary field (psychology, biology, physics) that investigates "distant interactions between living organisms and their environment and the energetic and informational manifestations associated with them." The phenomena that psychotronics investigates have a psychobiological and psychophysical character.

Psychotronics develops and delimits so-called "distant interactions" within living matter (e.g. telepathy, biotherapy), further distant interactions between living and non-living matter (psychokinesis and psychotronic activation of matter, dermo-optical perception, dowsing), and distant interactions between living matter and the information field of the external environment (telegnosis, which has the forms of retrocognition, i.e. cognition of the past; precognition, cognition of the future; and current telegnosis, i.e. in fact cryptesthesia, clairvoyance in the present).

In essence, the subject matter of psychotronics does not differ from the subject matter of contemporary parapsychology. The two disciplines differ, however, in their basic methodological conception (in psychotronics, emphasis is placed on the objective study of psychotronic phenomena, and research on mediumistic phenomena, "poltergeists," and other phenomena "reeking of occultism" is set aside), while the philosophical foundation of psychotronics, as it was cultivated in our country and in some neighboring countries, was originally dialectical materialism.

3) According to M. Rýzl, "Úvod do parapsychologie" (Introduction to Parapsychology) (1991)

Milan Rýzl is an American parapsychologist who emigrated in 1967 from Czechoslovakia to the United States, where he lectures in parapsychology. When he was leaving Czechoslovakia, he believed that parapsychology in the Western world was already a fully recognized scientific discipline, respected almost as much as, say, chemistry or biology. He was surprised that even at Duke University — where J. B. Rhine's laboratory was, the founders of scientific parapsychology, which represented the pinnacle of American parapsychological research — parapsychology was received with considerable skepticism.

At the beginning of the 1990s, after a further 24 years had passed since his time in the USA, Rýzl is forced to state that nothing factual has changed in parapsychology: apart from the rise of the East European phenomenon "psychotronics" and a generally better-informed public, no radical discovery has yet been found, and parapsychology is thereby still in the stage of pioneering research.

According to Rýzl, psychotronics made the mistake of ignoring several decades of development of parapsychology, which had drawn its precise boundaries. Parapsychology, from its original extensive old conception including "all mysterious phenomena of the human psyche," had narrowed and devoted attention to so-called extrasensory perception (ESP) and psychokinesis. These two groups of phenomena are connected by similar behavior and related laws. Psychotronics annulled this entire development. It again took into its scope the marginal topics that had earlier been excluded from parapsychology, and thus thematically set research back by centuries. For psychotronics strove for a natural-scientific and especially physical explanation of parapsychological (psychotronic) phenomena through distant energetic interactions. The special laws of extrasensory perception and psychokinesis (e.g. independence from material obstacles, from physical energy systems, from spatial distance, and even the possibility of bridging the time barrier for scrying) show that these are phenomena of a "non-physical" character (understood from the standpoint of the current state of physics). For their explanation it is necessary to find new principles that go beyond our existing knowledge in physics.

Psychotronics has its historical justification. At the time when the philosophy of dialectical materialism was in the communist states the main criterion of scientific truth, parapsychological research was for Marxist philosophers suspicious and philosophically wayward. In this situation it could be tactically useful to erase the sharp boundaries of parapsychology, to include in it also the research of marginal topics, and to say that all this belongs to parapsychology renamed "psychotronics," that the investigated phenomena are fully repeatable, and to proclaim that everything is completely explainable by known physical laws — radiations, energies, force fields. Such an approach could bring recognition, financial support, and thereby hope for psychotronic research. L. L. Vasiliev himself used a variant of this tactic in his effort to push through telepathic research at Leningrad University. But times are different now. Politically inspired tactics are not needed; rather, it is necessary to look the truth in the face. Unfortunately, the research stance that underlies psychotronics leads, in my opinion, research on extrasensory perception and psychokinesis astray.

4) According to K. Drbal and Z. Rejdák, "Perspektivy telepatie" (Perspectives of Telepathy) (1970)

K. Drbal and Z. Rejdák, together with J. Stuchlík, were the founders of psychotronics as a scientific discipline.

At the end of the said book is a chapter "VII: Czech Research on Psychotronics." This chapter treats the basic historical dates of the development of pre-psychotronic research in the field of extraordinary psychic capacities of man. Some great researchers are named there as well, e.g. J. E. Purkyně, Prof. E. Bobák.

In 1922 the Scientific Metapsychical Society was founded in Prague, in which a number of significant researchers worked: V. Mikuška, Dr. K. Kuchyňka, Prof. O. Fischer, and others. A separate chapter is formed by B. Kafka, who is an exceptional personality in experimental hypnosis. At the turn of the 1950s and 1960s, RNDr. M. Rýzl published the first informative articles, whose aim was to rehabilitate the field of parapsychology and metapsychics. He pointed in them to research in the West and in the Soviet Union.

A certain qualitative turn, both in the approach to the whole problem and in the manner of experimentation, came on 18 March 1967, when, on the initiative and under the chairmanship of Prof. J. Stuchlík, the Coordination Group for Psychotronic Research was established. Dr. Z. Rejdák was appointed its scientific secretary. Psychotronics was defined as an interdisciplinary discipline in which the following scientific disciplines participate: physics, communications engineering, mathematics, cybernetics, psychology, psychiatry, medicine, neurophysiology, physiology, anthropology, geology, cosmology, sociology, philosophy, and bionics (for each scientific discipline its connection with psychotronics is simultaneously delimited).

The members of the Coordination Group became scientific workers from the relevant disciplines. They did not set as their goal to defend at any cost the existence of telepathy, telegnosis, and psychokinesis; they believed, however, that today, when sensitive recording instruments and precise methods elaborated in many fields are available, a suitable time had come for a serious examination of all the disputed and less disputed questions that have above all excited people, and on whose research leading scientists of all epochs have participated, and in this century above all Nobel laureates. The workers of the Coordination Group understand psychotronics for the present more or less as an "artificial model" and are convinced that by studying this "artificial model" we can in no way harm science — on the contrary, we can benefit it and set before it new questions. ... At present we can say, even though we work under very "specific conditions," without subsidy, that we have research coverage of all three main areas of psychotronics: telepathy, telegnosis with dowsing, and psychokinesis — this above all thanks to the self-sacrifice of the experimenters and test subjects who do not spare their free time and often their own financial means for this research.

5) According to Z. Rejdák, "Průvodce po psychotronice" (A Guide to Psychotronics) (1991)

Zdeněk Rejdák is a founder of psychotronics and a world-renowned researcher in the field; his works create the basic theses of the structure of psychotronics.

In the said work, Z. Rejdák does not analyze the immediate circumstances of the origin of psychotronics, but rather delimits here the problem of the specialization of the individual disciplines, their growing distance from one another, and figuratively the rise of a certain vacuum between them. This vacuum is not empty; everything abnormal falls into it, everything that escapes ordinary knowledge and everyday plain practice. Everything we would today designate as paranormal is found in this vacuum. These are phenomena that are perhaps physical, perhaps psychological, perhaps medical, perhaps philosophical, perhaps biological, perhaps astronomical, etc. Many of them share a common denominator: they are closely bound to our psyche and at the same time are accompanied by physical manifestations; they are psychophysical — psychotronic — phenomena.

The origin of psychotronics has historical continuity, but the word psychotronics was not chosen with the aim of distinguishing itself from parapsychology or metapsychics, but for entirely factual reasons. The phenomena that psychotronics and parapsychology investigate lie not only alongside or beyond psychology; they should also lie alongside, outside, or beyond medicine, physics, geology, philosophy, etc. Then we would have to speak of paramedicine, paraphysics, parageology, etc. But the general question arises whether it makes sense, for the named discipline, to emphasize that it investigates, in effect, second-order phenomena. Thereby it itself places itself to a certain degree outside the existing scientific disciplines. If we proceed from the presupposition that these are entirely real phenomena, even if for now rare or occasional in their manifestation, then there is no reason at all to use the prefix "para." The common denominator of the phenomena we investigate is that they are closely connected with our psyche and at the same time accompanied by energetic manifestations. Therefore we decided to adopt for their collective designation the already-proposed term psychotronics, which we considered the more advantageous, because it also captures the mutual bond of the psychic process over the energetic manifestations that accompany this process. Its author is Ferdinand Clerc. He first used it in the French radiotechnical journal Tout la Radio (No. 192, p. 12, 1955)."

Psychotronics creates a unified terminology proceeding from the fact of the bond of psychotronic phenomena to living organisms; therefore Z. Rejdák introduces the word-element "bio" into all terms. This creates the concepts bioindication, biotelegnosis, biocommunication, biodiagnostics, biotherapy, biotelekinesis, bioactivation, biolocation. Out of this series stand the concepts dermocognition and discontinuity zones.

Important is the definitional delimitation of psychotronics: "Psychotronics is an interdisciplinary scientific discipline studying distant interactions between organisms and the external environment, which are conditioned by the potencies of the soul, by conscious and perceptual processes. Psychotronics investigates the energetic and informational properties of these interactions, thereby helping to objectify and come to know the hitherto unrevealed reserve capacities of man and of living matter, and thus contributing to the knowledge of the world in general."

With his definition of psychotronics, Z. Rejdák introduces into psychotronics the key concept of the soul; from its potency — that is, from certain properties and capacities of the living human organism connected with it — the existence of the psychotronic phenomena that are bound to it, or that it is capable of inducing, is then derived. This stance expresses the effort to return to research in psychotronics, and to human life in general, certain moral and ethical qualities that must be respected especially in psychotronics, and thereby to prevent also the threat of its misuse against man.

Conclusion

We can say that the initial delimitation of psychotronics by its founders was entirely clear; with development, however, marginal topics were added to it that somewhat dispersed its focus on special natural phenomena and the extraordinary capacities of man. The development of psychotronics stabilized on the model of distant interactions; later, however, the concept of the soul was introduced into it by Z. Rejdák. Thus there arises the area of so-called "psychotronics with a soul," as O. Válek designated it.

The concept of psychotronics was at one time introduced as a name for parapsychology promoted in the East, even though it officially distanced itself from it. The necessity of distancing itself from parapsychology had several causes; beyond the ideological ones, one of the more important was the fact that in the awareness of the scientific public, only "the better occultists" engage in parapsychology. The fact of the close connection of parapsychology and psychotronics is confirmed by their similar delimitation in the study of natural phenomena. Parapsychology studies extrasensory perception — telepathy and telegnosis — further dowsing (radiesthesia), animal magnetism, and poltergeist phenomena. Psychotronics, according to its initial delimitation, studies the phenomena of telepathy, telegnosis, dowsing, psychokinesis, biological plasma, and biotherapy.

In conclusion we can say, however, that psychotronics managed, through its methodological and philosophical approach, to lay the foundations of a new potential science of the future. At the same time it must be realized that the fundamental discovery that would kick off official psychotronic research has not yet been made, and even the Sisyphean labor in psychotronics still lies before us. A share in this is also the fact that the manner of work of researchers in psychotronics is the same as in the past — it is still work "on the knee," without the general trust of scientists and without financial means.

With pride, however, we can state that psychotronics is a Czech scientific phenomenon, which is today already known throughout the whole world, even though its current state is not very encouraging.

Jan Fárek

The Place and Role of Psychotronics in the System of Science

The place of psychotronics is at the boundary of the scientific disciplines studying non-living and living matter.

The role of psychotronics is to investigate the essence of the existence of living matter and the psychotronic phenomena connected with it.

The stated delimitation of the place and role of psychotronics in the system of science is a new doctrine, arising from the knowledge and development of modern psychotronics.

This doctrine arises in the third developmental stage of psychotronics, and its elaboration could enable the entry of psychotronics into academic settings.

Delimitation of the individual stages of the development of psychotronics

1st stage — was connected with the initial delimitation of psychotronics, in which it had to satisfy the criteria of materialist science, founded in our country in the 1960s on Marxist-Leninist philosophy (the first ten years of its existence, 1967–1976); it was therefore conceived only as a working model enabling the investigation and verification of psychotronic phenomena from various angles of view.

2nd stage — was connected with the dispersal of the initial delimitation of psychotronics and the adoption of so-called marginal topics (e.g. dermocognition, acupuncture...). In this stage, psychotronic phenomena are classified in dependence on the so-called distant interaction of the organism of living matter with the environment and with the subjects and objects in it (1978–1986).

3rd stage — is connected with the necessity of incorporating the principial essence of the existence of living matter into psychotronics. Here an inner split of psychotronics occurs into two philosophically different parts:

- the first part is a continuation of the original ideas of psychotronics, where the foundation is built on the generalization of the concept of consciousness

- the second part is a branch that deviates from the original ideas and introduces into psychotronics the concept of the soul. The soul becomes the basic causality in the background of psychotronic phenomena. The propagator of this direction is Z. Rejdák. It seems to me that by this step psychotronics was brought into crisis in the 1990s. Psychotronics with a soul, however, plays its role and in its own way points to the problem of the present day, into which the "spiritual dimension," the moral and ethical aspect, should return.

A new conception of psychotronics

Psychotronics has adopted a relationship to the essence of living matter, and thereby it has become sharply and permanently separated from parapsychology. Psychotronics and parapsychology can no longer be connected in their basic theses. The connecting link of both fields remains, however, the methodological apparatus of the natural sciences and the natural phenomena of extrasensory perception (telepathy, telegnosis) and psychokinesis, which psychotronics and parapsychology assess from their own standpoints.

In its thirty-year development, psychotronics has unambiguously taken its place at the boundary between the disciplines studying non-living and living matter. Connected with this are its content and the necessity of joining the knowledge of the continuity of psychotronic phenomena to the internal organizational and causal essence of the organism of living matter.

This position sets psychotronics in a key role in knowing the essence of the difference between living and non-living matter — a role in which we touch the load-bearing principle of the causal essence of life. It is not appropriate here to speak directly of an animating principle of matter, but of its organizational factor, which gives the internal motion of matter the form of an evolving living structure of material substance — the matter of the organism. We can speak of an evolutionary principle of living matter.

In the development of psychotronics there arises a state in which, with the growth of its knowledge, it will have to adjust and suitably generalize its definition. Psychotronic phenomena, it seems, are only the foam on the crests of the waves that carry them. These notional waves symbolizing the essence of life in the organism of living matter are its "organizational factor." Psychotronic phenomena are thereby placed in immediate relation with the mechanisms of the microcosm of living matter.

Psychotronics can be defined as the science addressing the relationship of matter, energy, consciousness, and psychotronic interaction. In this definition, the organizational principle of living matter is expressed by the concept of consciousness. In this context, however, it is no longer a matter of consciousness understood as a property of man, but of consciousness in a considerably more general sense. This problem is addressed by the so-called broader conception of consciousness in the theory of the "Broader Conception of the Human Psyche."

The concept of consciousness in psychotronics must be understood as an informational process acting in living matter as an organizational factor. In this context we must then speak of an energetic-informational process. Quite evidently, therefore, it is not only a matter of informational processes that carry psychic and psychological properties.

Z. Rejdák, in my opinion, precisely sensed the direction in which psychotronics was heading, and because he did not generalize the concept of consciousness, he had to introduce the concept of the soul into it in 1988. The soul here then plays the role of mediator between psychotronic phenomena and the essence of life. On the other hand, however, we must say that the introduction of the concept of the soul is also connected with the effort to prevent the misuse of psychotronics against man.

A possible historical continuity

Psychotronics arises in historical continuity with such teachings as magic and hermeticism. The subject matter of these at-one-time "scientific" teachings — or rather of the period knowledge — was always, among other things, the search for the connections of man with nature and with the forces that govern it. These conceptions were always elaborations of ideas concerning the essence of the origin, development, and functioning of life in nature. The ceremonies and ritual practices of magic were then meant to serve man in mastering the "spiritual forces" of nature standing on this essence. The scientific-technological revolution and the enormous human knowledge of nature connected with special technical instruments — apparatuses — affected also the field of these "pre-scientific teachings" — magic and hermeticism. Today there exist two points of support which, in these new social and technological conditions, continue the elaboration of what these teachings formerly began. These are psychotronics and parapsychology.

In historical context, psychotronics can appear as an elaboration of hermeticism, which in the Middle Ages represented a synthetic teaching of multiple disciplines.

Nothing changes the fact that psychotronics arose as an East European analogue of Western parapsychology. For psychotronics arose in Prague, which in its traditions was a center of world hermeticism. All the Czech researchers in psychotronics were influenced by Czech hermeticism, which flourished in our country at the beginning of the century, and they take over from it the striving for a synthetic perception of reality. If they have a serious approach, they must also have passed through the study of the historical sources of psychotronics, of which Czech hermeticism is a part.

The multidimensional conception of the perception of the world contained in hermeticism then manifests itself right at the birth of psychotronics and is wrapped in its interdisciplinary conception and in the necessity of cooperation with fully fifteen already-existing scientific disciplines. So what kind of discipline is "that psychotronics," when in order to arise at the end of the 1960s and study psychotronic phenomena it needs to absorb and utilize the knowledge of practically the whole of official science? Psychotronics is, after all, no omniscience. The problem is that it is not known where to begin. It was not clear where the place of psychotronics in the system of science was. Today, after 30 years, its place and role are given by development.

The founders of psychotronics, however, did not even suspect this "place and role" of it.

Psychotronics and parapsychology

Parapsychology and psychotronics are related disciplines, but they are not one and the same. It would truly be senseless to promote psychotronics if it were engaged in what parapsychology is, and thereby were parapsychology under the name psychotronics. Such a conception is indeed promoted by some people.

In the atmosphere of the development of psychotronics as a science, tendencies assert themselves to preserve psychotronics and not to merge it with parapsychology. The key role in enabling psychotronics to be a genuinely independent science, freed from parapsychology — for it to have any meaning at all — lies precisely in the "place and role of psychotronics in the system of science," which we address here.

And indeed psychotronics has its place and its role; but in this first article on this topic I will not yet manage to express this fact absolutely precisely and intelligibly. On the pages of this journal we will return to this problem, and so I call on everyone for patience.

The solution of the place and role of psychotronics in the socio-scientific system of views of the world is connected with the birth of a new science that not everyone will be willing to accept at the start. This science will be called psychotronics, and it will arise from what is today designated as the pseudoscience psychotronics.

"The place and role of psychotronics is connected with the assignment of psychotronics to the space of integration of physics, chemistry, biology, and psychology for the purpose of solving the problem of the essence of living matter, to which the existence of psychotronic phenomena is bound (the integration of physics, chemistry, biology, and psychology is not conceived here generally, but selectively, i.e. it is realized only for the purpose of focusing on the solution of the stated concrete problem, thereby connecting only the partial boundary areas of the named disciplines)."

Fig. 2) Delimitation of the space of existence of psychotronics

How, then, can we imagine the place and role of psychotronics in science?

Let us take four basic disciplines forming the core of scientific knowledge — physics, chemistry, biology, and psychology. Around this core gather all the further disciplines, including philosophy, sociology, and physiology, which are likewise significant for psychotronics.

Fig. 3) The place of psychotronics in the system of science, for the second time

The space between the disciplines depicted in Fig. 3 can be filled by the boundary areas of physics, chemistry, biology, and psychology. Psychotronics can then be imagined as a scientific discipline synthesizing the problems of the marginal topics of these disciplines into one whole, which is connected with the true principial subject matter of psychotronics.

Psychotronics, soon after its origin, was conceived as an interdisciplinary discipline addressing the relationship matter – energy – consciousness, but despite this breadth of scope it concentrated, in its 1st developmental stage, only on the phenomena of telepathy, telegnosis, psychokinesis, and biotherapy. And in the 2nd stage it extends its scope into the broader contexts described by the model of distant interactions. Only in the 3rd stage does the spectrum of the content of its definitional delimitation begin to be fulfilled.

The problem of psychotronics and its basic idea rests on this: what spreads out in the boundary area of physics, chemistry, biology, and psychology has one common element, which is viewed once from the position of physics, a second time from the position of psychology, a third time from the position of chemistry, and a fourth time from the position of biology. This common element is the core on which it is possible to build psychotronics as an independent science. Psychotronics thereby acquires its principial subject of investigation and can create its own specific apparatus, which will have its precisely defined character, just as physics, chemistry, biology, and psychology have.

The psychotronic phenomenon, in such a scientific psychotronics, will then be only a small area of its content, which from the experimental standpoint — thanks to the properties of the human psyche — is unstable and has a probabilistic character. Psychotronics will form itself as a discipline investigating informational processes bound to energetic states in living matter, which have their internal and external manifestations, of which only a part are those we today designate as psychotronic phenomena.

The informational-energetic process as the essence of living matter has:

- informational manifestations, connected with the organization of biological matter

- energetic manifestations, connected with physical interactions

- material manifestations, connected with the arrangement of the matter to which it is bound and with the chemical reactions that accompany it (instead of "reaction" one can assign "construction")

- manifestations of consciousness, connected with the knowledge necessary for preserving one's own existence in a measure adequate to the developmental degree of living matter

Fig. 4) Delimitation of the basic problem of psychotronics

The central problem of psychotronics is the informational-energetic process in the microcosm of matter, which from the standpoint of biology is connected with the organism of living matter and its substantial morphological carrier; from the standpoint of psychology it is connected with the consciousness and psychic capacities of every living matter; from the standpoint of chemistry it is connected with the atomic arrangement into molecular structures and its role in the material system of living matter; and from the standpoint of physics it is connected with the external manifestations (which we are only learning to detect), provisionally designated as psychotronic (distant) interaction, and with the fourth state of matter existing at low temperatures (cold plasma, so-called bioplasma). Here we then clearly see the connection with the delimitation of psychotronics as a discipline studying the relationship matter – energy – consciousness in organized matter, i.e. the organisms of living matter including man.

By elaborating these ideas, psychotronics will become an autonomous science. The specific peculiarity of this scientific discipline will be its two-polar natural-scientific and socio-scientific character, which is in accord with the place and role of psychotronics in the system of science.

Conclusion

Psychotronics seeks the organizational principle of living matter, which participates fundamentally in the existence of psychotronic phenomena and probably has a decisive role in the evolution of living matter. It is connected:

1) — from the standpoint of psychology, with consciousness as an informational process capable of organizing living matter, and at the same time with "thinking," i.e. the processing of information, fulfilling a primary function in preserving one's own existence for the given developmental degree of living matter

2) — from the standpoint of biology, with the material structure ("organ" — morphological substratum) of the organism of living matter, which is the carrier of the informational process (the stated capacity /1/) of the organism of the given developmental degree

3) — from the standpoint of chemistry, with the atomic and molecular arrangement of the basic material element (structure), which is the common element in the construction of the carrier of the informational-energetic processes in living matter (the material structure /2/), for all developmental degrees of living matter

4) — from the standpoint of physics, with the external and internal objectively detected manifestations of the informational-energetic process /1/ in our world, which manifests itself through psychotronic (distant) interaction and the psychotronic phenomena connected with it

With regret it must be stated that the development of psychotronics so far turned mainly to point /4/, and specifically to the psychotronic phenomena connected with the so-called distant interaction. From such a narrow conception psychotronics as a science could not arise, because its whole conception was torn out of context. The deliverance of psychotronics from crisis is connected with a conception in which it takes on the study of the surroundings of the essence of living matter from which psychotronic phenomena derive.

In conclusion we must therefore say that what holds is what we said about psychotronics at the very beginning: "The place of psychotronics in the system of science is at the boundary of the scientific disciplines studying living and non-living matter, and thereby psychotronics in fact investigates the essence of living matter and the psychotronic phenomena connected with it." This problem loomed before psychotronics earlier as well; it was constantly in its background, but it was not reflected.

The purpose of these reflections was to show not only what the role and place of psychotronics in the system of science is, but also in what developmental stage it currently finds itself. Psychotronics in its 3rd developmental stage is heading into natural science. Psychotronic phenomena in this context become, for it, a part of the study of the essence of our existence. The separation of research on psychotronic phenomena from their essence will not allow the rise of psychotronics as a science. Therefore the conception here established is the only real conception, which must become the basic doctrine in its further development.

Personality: BRETISLAV KAFKA

The personality of B. Kafka is treated very beautifully by his son in the article "Who Was Břetislav Kafka" in the book Parapsychologie; in the said article he relates:

"Father was born on 14 May 1891 into the blacksmith family of Rudolf Kafka, as the eldest of seven children. In his birthplace of Horní Kostelec he attended school and then briefly stayed at the sculpture school in Hořice. Because of illness, however, he interrupted his studies and worked as a carver's laborer for the Červinka firm in Bílá Třemešná. There he met his wife Marie, whom he married in 1912. During his time in Bílá Třemešná he was invited to carve the heads of the stone gargoyles during the construction of the Těšnov dam.

At that time he also discovered, by chance, strong hypnotic capacities in himself. It happened thanks to a spiritualist, the local school director, who was a witness of how father unknowingly put his two friends from the construction site into hypnotic sleep. Father then met regularly with the director, and the latter initiated him into the mysteries of phenomena hitherto unknown to him. He soon found that many people succumbed to his hypnotic influence, and so he began doing experiments in the field of suggestion and telepathy. Since he constantly had health problems, he also began at that time to take an interest in natural healing. He joined this interest with an ever-deepening interest in the occult sciences, and he intensively developed the unusual capacities of his inner being.

Over time he acquired capable subjects — sensitives — with whose help he entered into people's subconscious and determined the course of treatment. Father's popularity grew, and attendance at that time was so great that it was beyond human strength to manage everything. He devoted himself fully to patients, sometimes to the point of exhaustion, which he describes in the book Experimentální psychologie.

He provided his help as a matter of principle free of charge; on the contrary, out of the proceeds of his books he supported students and contributed to the purchase of the property at Staré Hamry — Oruna.

Not only the sick came to him, but also people asking about phenomena they could not explain themselves. Father carefully recorded everything, and over the years wrote the book Parapsychologie, which has not yet appeared in print.

The name Kafka often meant the last hope and deliverance from affliction. There was not a day that he did not help someone; he was unable to refuse anyone, for his Christian conviction and humanity did not permit otherwise. His love of his neighbor, however, never met with understanding from the authorities. In 1926 he was even fined two hundred crowns, and in case of non-payment he was threatened with twenty days in prison. The whole affair was finally settled with a hundred-crown fine. From earliest youth father went through a multitude of illnesses and injuries — from fractures and spinal damage, inflammation of the pleura and joints, to a heart defect; later one of his kidneys was even removed. Through exercise and firm will, however, he managed to overcome all the tribulations plaguing his physical and mental life. Perhaps precisely these life vicissitudes and deep faith in God led him to his worldview orientation toward helping his neighbors.

The center of gravity of his research work was the neglected domain — the soul and the mental life of man and their development. He advanced the knowledge of the time in psychology, parapsychology, philosophy, and folk healing by a considerable stretch. He demonstrated that the mental properties and capacities of man are incomparably higher than anyone can imagine. He also showed the direction in which to come to know these hidden forces of man and nature, and how to use them for the good and health of humanity.

Scientific authorities recognize him as a co-founder of world psychotronics. This is documented, for example, by a quotation from the book Telepatie a jasnovidnost, where he is ranked among such capacities as Bekhterev, Sinclair, Purkyně, and even Einstein. Today's science builds on the results of his research in many respects. His activity also brought many useful findings for practical life.

The book Nové základy experimentální psychologie (New Foundations of Experimental Psychology) was preceded by a book that appeared in 1925 under the title Svítání v duši (Dawn in the Soul). Two further books followed — Člověk zítřka (Man of Tomorrow) and kultura rozumu a vůle (the culture of reason and will).

At present a serious re-evaluation of the results of father's research is taking place. In professional circles it is being confirmed that the results of his investigations were decades ahead of their time, and that much of what he arrived at awaits further recognition.

Given father's principled character, no wider promotion of his work occurred. He also did not permit any tendentious interventions into his work. For the past forty years his books were on the index; they were removed from all libraries and their publication was not permitted.

The books are written in intelligible language and are intended not only for the suffering but for all who desire their own self-improvement.

Father was ahead of his time also in questions of lifestyle. Already more than sixty years ago he was determining, with the help of subjects, what energy food emits. On the basis of these experiments he recommended to people oat flakes, sprouted grain, and as much raw food as possible, especially vegetables, which emits the most life-giving energy.

After the Second World War he was invited to the United States for cooperation with the experts there, and to Australia, to the university in Melbourne. He never took advantage of these invitations, and after 1948 he regretted it. At that time all his research activity was forbidden, and a time of persecutions set in: expropriation of the laboriously founded enterprise, interrogations, mental extortion, house searches.

Despite the severe trials of life, father did not become embittered and continued to help his neighbors as he could. Scarcely a day passed without some visitor. Some asked for advice; others wanted to meet him personally. Most of the debates were on a high level until the end of his life. He was a good storyteller and an attentive listener. He enthusiastically took part in endless debates about politics, but he was utterly trusting and often paid for his trustingness. He rejoiced and hoped; unfortunately he did not live to see the year 1968. On 27 August 1967 he died of a stroke.

Terminological Glossary: BIOCOMMUNICATION

Biocommunication is the capacity of living organisms to communicate with one another, to transmit and receive information at a distance by means of their own mechanisms, which apply also beyond the range of the capacities of the senses, of perceptual and receptor activity. The quality of the transmitted information corresponds to the degree of organization of the living matter.

The terms most often used in the literature for biocommunication are: telepathy, telepathic transmission, Extra Sensory Perception, telesthesia, Mental Radio (U. Sinclair).

In biocommunication there occur distant interactions between people mutually, between people and other living organisms, and between living organisms mutually.

Biocommunication has a spontaneous, experimental, or purposeful character.

The transmission of information can bridge a distance of several meters as well as several thousand kilometers.

The cases of spontaneous telepathy that first attracted attention are bound to intense emotional experiences, connected mostly with an accident, illness, or death. A person close to the one to whom something happened experiences, in the form of a clear hallucination, simultaneously with him the experiences, as if she herself were present at the course of the events. The reception of the information can also have a weaker intensity; the percipient may feel only a certain disquiet with an unconscious orientation toward a certain person.

In experimental telepathy, the inductor concentrates on drawings or card symbols that the distant percipient is to draw or guess. In qualitative experiments, each experiment is in itself already proof of the transmission of information, e.g. if an approximately identical figure is received. In quantitative experiments, a limited number of symbols, known in advance to both the percipient and the inductor, is transmitted many times. The most frequently used are the so-called Zener cards with five symbols — cross, square, star, wavy lines, circle. Whether telepathic transmission occurred or not is decided by statistical evaluation. One of the best-known test subjects in the world for quantitative testing is the Czech, Mr. P. Štěpánek.

Prof. S. Manczarski, Prof. I. M. Kogan, and Prof. A. S. Pressman strive for an explanation of the distant transmission between people on the basis of electromagnetic waves. Prof. S. Manczarski found that the image of a figure placed in an impulse generator producing electromagnetic waves is transmitted better than without it. Prof. L. L. Vasiliev held a non-electromagnetic hypothesis: the transmission takes place with the help of a hitherto unknown energy. Prof. J. B. Rhine excluded any energetic agent.

Spontaneous telepathy prompted scientists toward systematic investigation and research of psychotronic phenomena. The distant transmission of information was observed predominantly between people. The latest research, however, has shown that the capacity for the distant transmission of information is a natural and ordinary property of living matter.

According to the distant interaction within living matter, we divide biocommunication into kinds:

- distant interaction human – animal

- distant interaction animal – animal

- distant interaction human – plant

- distant interaction animal – plant

- distant interaction plant – plant

- distant interaction at the cellular level and in lower-organized living matter

Editor's note:

The percipient is the receiver of information by way of biocommunication (telepathy).

The inductor is the transmitter of information by way of biocommunication (telepathy).

An agent is here understood as the transmission medium of information in biocommunication (telepathy).

The delimitation of the concept of biocommunication is according to Z. Rejdák.

Story: The Sun Is Closing

One autumn day of 1993 I entered the children's room and observed with astonishment how my son was huddled under the little children's table, trembling all over. I was surprised and asked him: "What happened to you?" He kept trembling; only after a while did he answer: "The Sun is closing."

I was genuinely confused. How can the Sun be closing? I looked out the window and saw that the sky was covered with heavy gray-black clouds. It was shortly before noon, around eleven o'clock.

I waited patiently until my little boy calmed down. When he saw how tensely I awaited his answer, he suddenly grew serious and quieted. He looked at me, and with his little eyes he let me know that my presence gave him strength and restored his lost courage.

Somewhat more calmly he repeated: "The Sun is closing." Even now I did not grasp the meaning of his words.

"How — the Sun is closing? What do you mean by that?"

Silence spread again. I felt that something important had happened, and so I remained patient and full of expectation. I realized in my mind how he had grown — five years had passed like water. Finally the boy said: "There was a voice here, and it told me: 'The Sun is closing.'"

"What voice? Where did it come from?"

"Well, here — from Vendulka."

Only then did I notice that she was sleeping contentedly on Martin's bed, just like that, without a blanket. She must have fallen asleep while playing with her favorite doll, Lenička.

Until lunch I then listened to Martin's account.

* * *

I came home from outside for Vendulka, so she would come play with me. I found her sleeping. I wanted to wake her, but some force held me and I could not move. The little room filled with a light that did not so much shine as burn. Yes, the room filled with a heat that was all around me and filled me with terror. With the coming of fear, however, my paralysis vanished, and I hid under the table.

The heat in the room slowly subsided — or rather, no, it only contracted into the shape of an enormous sphere hanging in the air, almost within my reach. The sphere began to color, from a faint red to a truly crimson, and gradually turned golden. Then it spoke, and said several times: "The Sun is closing, the Sun is closing." The voice sounded from everywhere; the whole room was full of it, it was in me too.

The sphere grew smaller, and then, as when an eye hides itself behind its lid, it began to vanish. Meanwhile I kept hearing, ever more faintly, the repetition: "The Sun is closing."

* * *

I would not take the whole story seriously and would have forgotten it, if three days later Martin had not come to me and, out of nowhere, told me of his own accord that he had already seen and heard that sphere and that voice a second time. He did not remember exactly when — roughly a bit over a year back — but he knew with certainty that it was at night, and that Mom was not home. It had woken him, and when he called for me, I was not coming, and that is why he had been so afraid. And so I wrote this story down in my notes.

* * *

Both cases happened after the third year of the children's age; the first time Martin was three, and the second time Vendulka. These stories symbolize the separation of man from the womb of nature, as the philosophical teachings of ancient cultures depict it. In the kabbalistic teaching, for example, there is talk of the visit of the angel of death, who visits a person several times during his life, and on the last visit leads him away to the other world. There is no room here, however, for a discussion of these teachings.

With the third year of a person's age, his bond to nature changes into a bond to society. Man begins to be dependent on verbal communication, and his nonverbal capacities slowly fall asleep. Many people in their lives long for a return to nature in the spiritual sense, and in the striving for this kinship they go astray at the turning point. Only an insignificant number, however, find what they were looking for. From this small group are recruited the individuals who have exceptional capacities connected with psychotronic phenomena.

Jan Fárek

Publication of the Basic Part of the Statutes of the JČSBP

Head. I BASIC PROVISIONS

1. The Unity of Czech and Slovak Researchers in Psychotronics (hereafter JČSBP) is a voluntary, independent interest, non-political, and non-profit organization in the form of a closed community, operating on the territory of the Czech and Slovak Republics.

2. The JČSBP is not yet an independent legal entity.

3. The intention of the JČSBP is to become an independent legal entity this year, and within two to three years to found a Foundation for Psychotronic Research.

4. The JČSBP does not exist so that its members make themselves visible in public, but so that they fulfill the mission of this Unity and of psychotronics itself.

5. The validity of these statutes is from 8 April 1999 to 31 December 1999; for the year 2000 new statutes will be worked out on the basis of experience.

Head. II MISSION AND GOAL OF THE JČSBP

1. The basic mission is to unify the efforts of researchers and friends in the field of psychotronics toward common creative activity, to realize some elementary (basic) research projects in this still little-known field of human knowledge, and thereby to create the preconditions for the entry of psychotronics into academic settings.

2. Delimitation of the tasks of the JČSBP:

- to bring researchers in the field of psychotronics and its friends under a common roof

- to create a space of self-realization for all JČSBP members in the field of psychotronics

- to gather and disseminate the newest findings from psychotronics

- to create conditions for open professional discussion on solving the questions of the development of psychotronics

- to publish the journal PATHS OF PSYCHOTRONICS, in which the JČSBP, the results of the work of its members, and psychotronics itself will be presented

- to educate JČSBP members and other interested persons in psychotronic questions

- to cooperate with organizations, institutions, and persons whose activity touches psychotronics

- to create the foundations of psychotronics as a natural science

Address to Researchers and Friends of Psychotronics

The founding of the community

Dear friends: on 9 March 1999 the Unity of Czech and Slovak Researchers in Psychotronics (JČSBP) was founded. We offer you the possibility of membership in our Unity. In the case of your deeper interest in work in the field of psychotronics, we will enable you to take part in the activity and events organized by our Unity.

Some of you have been engaged in psychotronics for a longer time, or even your whole lives; it would truly be a pity if you did not become members of the emerging community of researchers in the field of psychotronics and did not put your experience and knowledge to use.

We are building the Unity of Czech and Slovak Researchers in Psychotronics from the conviction that by common effort we will manage to create such a credit for psychotronics as will be acceptable even in academic settings. Then genuinely new possibilities and official paths of knowledge will open up for us.

All further information you will learn at the first JČSBP Conference, to which you will be invited if you express the willingness to become our members — whether as researchers, correspondents, critics, or friends of psychotronics.

The task of the researchers is to take part in research and to lecture in the field of psychotronics, to write professional articles for the journal "Paths of Psychotronics," to promote psychotronics in public, etc. A researcher in the field of psychotronics can be delimited as a scientist, a specialized expert, or an amateur engaged above all in the boundary area of physics, chemistry, biology, psychology, and sociology, in which non-causal phenomena appear with regard to the named disciplines — phenomena that have psychotronic causality — psychotronic phenomena, etc.

The task of the correspondents is to write professional articles and studies. Here belong also those who either do not wish to or cannot lecture, travel, etc.

The task of the critics is to provide serious criticism of everything concerning the field of psychotronics.

The friends have no special tasks; they are all the readers of this journal and those interested in psychotronics who gladly attend lectures or other professional events in the field. These have the right to be informed of JČSBP events, so that they can take part in them according to their own judgment.

A special group is formed by the youth, i.e. those interested in psychotronics from 15 years of age.

Acceptance of the offer of membership will be confirmed by your application to the community. You become valid members of the community only after fulfilling the criteria of the JČSBP statutes, which precisely determine the rules of the functioning of the community and the rights of every member.

Whom we are looking for

At one gathering, Mr. Žert related his experiences from lectures in the field of psychotronics. Among other things he assessed the quality of his listeners: "Half of the attendees at some of my courses were under the care of a psychiatrist." Yes — some people seek help from those who promote psychotronics or proclaim themselves psychotronics; we believe, however, that it would be better for them if they sought it from a professional. If not from a psychiatrist, then from a psychotherapist or psychologist. On the other hand, we believe that a person should not entrust his fate into strange hands and should decide about his own life himself. To such people, who seek help for themselves and their loved ones, we recommend this journal, that they look through it and not expect miracles from psychotronics.

We are not looking for people who would want to train in psychotronics and then misuse the knowledge thereby acquired, nor such people as build psychotronics on mysteries and enigmas, or who dishonestly enrich themselves under its name.

For the JČSBP community we are looking for serious researchers, pioneers, scientists, specialists, teachers, amateurs, enthusiasts, sponsors, etc., who by their expertise, knowledge, or various means and gifts are willing to contribute to the development of scientific psychotronics. We are especially looking for physicists, biophysicists, biologists, physiologists, psychologists, sociologists, philosophers, and translators who would be willing to take part in the gathering and evaluation of materials concerning the boundary area of the named disciplines and psychotronics.

The problems of psychotronics can today, after thirty years of its development, be delimited natural-scientifically with relative precision, which creates the conditions for the serious investigation of its individual partial problems.

All who are interested in membership in the Unity of Czech and Slovak Researchers in Psychotronics can apply by filling in the application and sending it to the JČSBP Office. Whoever is interested only in receiving the journal Paths of Psychotronics likewise applies with the enclosed application, in which he checks the column "friend" and "receiving the journal" A = yes. A friend who receives the journal pays no membership fees.
