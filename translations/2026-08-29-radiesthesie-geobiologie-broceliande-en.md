# Radiesthesia and Geobiology in the Brocéliande Forest

*Encyclopédie de Brocéliande. Translated from the French by Forge.*

This article's purpose is not to deny the existence of the "energetic" sensory perceptions claimed by dowsers, radiesthetists, magnetizers, and geobiologists. It proposes to show:

- How, during the 20th century, neo-druidism appropriated the discourse on "cosmotellurism" and "geobiology" associated with megaliths,
- How this current produced a discourse on the Brocéliande forest,
- What place this discourse currently occupies on the Paimpont forest massif.

## Cosmotellurism and megaliths

### The invention of radiesthesia

"Radiesthesia" is a term invented by Abbé Bouly (1865–1958) and first used in 1926. — BOTROT, Jean, "La radiesthésie, ou l'art peu commun de découvrir les filons", Le Journal, 30 October 1926, p. 1.

Composed of the Latin *radius* meaning "rod" and the Greek suffix *-esthesia* meaning "sensation," "perception," radiesthesia is the faculty of perceiving the radiations that certain bodies would emit, as well as the detection process founded on this faculty. — CNRTL, "Radiesthésie", 2012.

Presented by its inventor as a continuity of the art of the dowser, radiesthesia was at its creation considered an object of study by important members of the scientific community. — ARCHIVES DU PAS DE CALAIS, "Naissance de l'abbé Bouly, radiesthésiste, à Condette", 2023.

### Precursors of cosmotellurism

In 1933, Louis Merle (1890–1973), a dowser passionate about archaeology, published a work in which he sought to lay the foundations of a correspondence between the existence of underground currents and the location of megaliths.

> I believe I am the first to have had this revelation of the relationships that exist between megalithic monuments and underground influences. This fact constitutes a discovery whose importance no one will deny from the prehistoric point of view. But since these are unpublished data, I have few or no technical terms at my disposal, or only those consecrated by usage. I will therefore try to explain as best I can and in my own way the result of my observations. By telluric current or underground influence I mean not only water, but any matter giving off radiations; and by telluric line any line of influence that an underground current projects. [...]

Louis Merle's theories on the links between underground currents and the archaeology of megaliths met with an immediate echo in the world of neo-druidism, then from the end of the 1950s in that of geobiology. — BONVIN, Jacques, Mégalithes: lieux d'énergie, Mosaïque éditions, 1995, 284 p. [pp. 133–149].

### Atlanteans, druids, and cosmotellurism

In 1942, Paul Bouchet (1897–1979), created the College of Druids, Eubages, Bards, and Ovates of the Gauls, of which he made himself "Grand Druid" under the name Bod Koad. Author of theories marked by various esoteric currents, he became an important popularizer of the associations between druidism, megalithism, and cosmotellurism in the 1960s.

The Grand Druid Paul Bouchet wrote several works popularizing the knowledge of the "Druidic College of the Gauls," and it is no doubt in doing so that he was most anti-traditional, since knowledge was supposed to be transmitted only orally. He insisted heavily on the notion of telluric currents which, according to him, line the dolmens and various ancient religious buildings.

In *Les derniers atlantes* — a novel published in 1959 — as well as in *Science et philosophie des druides* — an essay published in 1968 — he develops a theory centered on the continuity between the civilization of Atlantis and the Celtic civilization. He there affirms in particular that the druids, heirs of the parapsychic powers of the Atlanteans, would have erected the megaliths on telluric currents. — BOUCHET, Paul and DRUIDE BOD KOAD, Science et philosophie des druides, Blainville-sur-Mer (Manche), L'amitié par le livre, 1968, 190 p.

Paul Bouchet's theories, long kept in relative anonymity, met with success during the 1970s. His most important work, *Les Druides Science et Philosophie*, republished by Laffont in 1976 — sold 100,000 copies — exerted its influence on many authors of the 1980s–1990s. — BOUCHET, Paul and BOUCHET, René, Science et philosophie des druides, 1968, Paris, Laffont, 1976, 281 p.

### 1937–1961 — Birth of geobiology

Geobiology, a discipline arising from radiesthesia, can be defined as the study of the whole set of environmental influences on the living, and notably of the waves linked to magnetic and electric fields, underground water currents, metallic networks, geological faults, etc.

This discipline, without scientific foundation, postulates the existence of telluric currents covering the entire surface of the terrestrial globe.

In 1947, one of its precursors, Doctor Peyré of Bagnoles-de-l'Orne, evoked the existence of a magnetic network crossing and distributing itself symmetrically over the surface of the planet. — PEYRÉ, doctor, Radiations Cosmotelluriques. (Rayons Peyré): Leur topographie sur toute la planète, leur rapport possible avec la pathologie humaine, animale, végétale et notamment avec le cancer., R. Martinet, 1947, 112 p.

In the 1950s, Doctor Ernst Hartmann (1915–1992) — founder of geobiology — postulated the existence of a network made of crossings of telluric radiations, present on all continents and bearing the name Hartmann network or H network. From 1951 he disseminated his theses during colloquia and seminars, and in 1961 founded the "Cercle de recherche en géobiologie" (Geobiology Research Circle), which he chaired until his death in 1992. — BONVIN, Jacques, Mégalithes: lieux d'énergie, Mosaïque éditions, 1995, 284 p. [pp. 121–131].

Doctor Hartmann's theses won many adherents, making geobiology a particularly influential New Age current. Not only do geobiologists unanimously recognize that our world is crossed by telluric or cosmic energy waves whose influence is positive or negative; they also set out theories on the distribution of telluric currents. They consider that there exists a particular grid of these currents (Hartmann network and Curry network). The crossing points of this grid can cause problems for living beings who stay too long in these places.

## Cosmotellurism and neo-druidism in Brocéliande

### 1981–1985 — First writings

The first writings evoking the existence of telluric currents in the Paimpont forest date from the early 1980s. They are the work of authors of marginal audience. Linked to the current of thought initiated by Paul Bouchet, these writings attribute to the druids of antiquity, or even to the Atlanteans, the faculty of perceiving and using the energies of telluric currents.

#### 1981–1983 — Alan-Morvan Chesneau

In 1981, in an article in the journal *Armor* dedicated to the storyteller Patrick Lebrun, Alan-Morvan Chesneau is the first author to evoke the ambient tellurism of the Celte Sylve de Brec'hallean.

> If you know how to hear and keep preciously what must be heard, within the messenger and transmitting tale, then with Patrick, or any other "necessary intermediary," you will be able to participate in this "ambient tellurism," issuing straight from the nocturnal mounds of the forest.

In 1983, he participated in a collective work devoted to the Breton abbeys published by Fayard. In charge of the chapter devoted to the Paimpont abbey, Alan-Morvan Chesneau advances references to druidism, esotericism, the New Age, and tellurism to narrate the origin he supposes of the abbey.

> The abbey of Notre-Dame de Paimpont, situated on a formerly druidic site, certainly enjoys the magical protections of the place. The four elements plus the mysterious "Flour of the air"! And here is the osmosis dear to our Bards, thus realized in venerable Celtia. Drawing its energies in the mother-earth, warmed without respite by the underlying tellurism of its promontory, at its cult mound, reassured by its massive fronds of the nearby high-forest, crowned by a clement sky, the abbey awaits its transhumance hour. [...] The abbey is built at the crossing of many underground passages and, at the same time, at the exact intersection of many telluric currents. Certainly, the aestheticism, the beauty of the place where it rests, does not suffice to untie the "Gordian Knot" concretized by the mystery of its installation. The answer can only come from Brocéliande... One can nevertheless affirm that the monastery is fixed on a "passage" leading to the Celte Gnosis formerly taught by our fathers... At Paimpont, around the abbey as in the very depths of its rocky support, resides a door toward an "Elsewhere," toward "Something Else."

#### 1985 — Jacques Pegeaud

Jacques Pegeaud is the author of a work on Brocéliande published in 1985. He takes up in this publication the theme of the Atlantean origin of the powers of the druids and their use through megaliths.

> One can think that the Atlanteans had not, like us, lost the memory of the original knowledge and that they fully used the possibilities of their brain. [...] In short, the megaliths are there and they did not fall from the sky! [...] The Paimpont site and the Brocéliande forest would hold the record both in number and quality, were it not for the many acts of vandalism [...] The Atlanteans populated the forest with a predilection for its geographic and cultural center: Paimpont. The menhirs raised toward the sky like monstrous megalithic question marks, the dolmens offered as altars to the mother goddess, the covered alleys necessary for the ritual of death and passage to the kingdom of Ouranos, the tumuli stripped in winter and flowered in summer, were so many markers placed at the limits of their knowledge. They knew geometry, trigonometry, units of length, angle measurements, the four cardinal points, and many constellations of the sky. As much at ease in time as in space, they kept a perpetual calendar, knew the beneficial effects of telluric currents, channeled them and perhaps used them...

— PEGEAUD, Jacques, Brocéliande d'hier et d'aujourd'hui!, Saint-Méen-le-Grand, Editions de Communications et de rencontres, 1985, 135 p.

### 1987–1999 — First discovery circuits

From the mid-1980s, the theories on cosmotellurism left their relative confidentiality. Large-circulation works, carried by publishing houses specialized in mainstream esoteric popularization — Robert Laffont or Guy Trédaniel — as well as by authors of national audience like Jean Markale (1928–2008), proposed to give access to the hidden science of the ancients. Integrating geobiology into neo-druidic theories, these works "reveal" to the general public discovery circuits of the Brocéliande forest, one of the most fascinating cosmo-telluric high places.

### 1986 — The opera of the waters at the Val sans Retour

The opera of the waters is a project by Jean-Charles Fabre marrying architecture and geobiology at the Val sans Retour. This project — subject of a thesis submitted in 1982 — is conceived as a regeneration center where the sun, the earth, and the water reunite in forms of life.

> The sensitive architectural project is underlaid by the discovery of a network of close interconnections of the place with its global environment: human, bioclimatic, cosmotelluric environment... The creator crystallizes on this backdrop a dwelling issuing from a fine and complex archi-texture. The appellations as well as the coats of arms maintain the force of the spirit of the place, its energetic climate in a way.

— FABRE, Jean-Charles, Maison entre Terre et Ciel: Les vertus thérapeutiques de l'habitat, 1986, Editions Véga, 2004, 193 p.

This architectural project inspired by geobiology prefigures the New Age fusions — a marriage of shamanism, geobiology, and neo-druidism — that would develop in the Paimpont forest from the 2010s.

#### 1987 — Gilbert Altenbach and Boune Legrais

The first publication proposing the discovery of cosmo-telluric high places located in the Paimpont forest appeared in 1987 in the collection *Les énigmes de l'univers* at Robert Laffont. — ALTENBACH, Gilbert and LEGRAIS, Boune, Lieux magiques et sacrés de la France, Robert Laffont, 1987, 301 p.

The authors — Gilbert Altenbach and Boune Legrais — propose to draw up a cartography of the magical and sacred places of France, these healing centers of the body, the soul, and the spirit, where the sky and the Earth meet, these high places whose knowledge has until now remained secret and reserved only for initiates.

This work, which claims the science called geobiology and habitat biology, takes up the broad lines of Paul Bouchet's theory according to which religious buildings were erected on ancient megalithic and druidic sites initially raised on telluric high points.

> The Ancients, builders of megaliths and pyramids, the builders of cathedrals, had the knowledge of cosmo-telluric subtleties and used them to access superior states of consciousness.

The cosmo-telluric sites of the Brocéliande forest are addressed in the chapter entitled *La Bretagne des Druides*.

> Therapeutic high place, magical high place, sacred high place, Brocéliande remains one of the most fascinating cosmo-telluric high places.

The authors begin by locating the two main telluric currents that cross the forest and cut each other perpendicularly in the Paimpont abbey.

> The ridge of the telluric current that crosses Brocéliande from east to west is cut perpendicularly by a north-south current. This intersection takes place precisely at the edge of the Paimpont pond, at the location of the abbey. The latitudinal current links Tréhorenteuc, the Val sans Retour, the chapels of Beauvais and Saint-Jean to those of Saint-Péran and of Coganne, crossing the Paimpont abbey. The longitudinal axis passes not far from Comper, which would have been the residence of Viviane, later that of Lancelot du Lac, and of the village of Beignon.

The cosmo-telluric high places of Brocéliande are presented as an initiatory journey beginning at the church of Tréhorenteuc and ending in the chapel of the Holy Sacrament of the Paimpont abbey.

> The church of Tréhorenteuc seems to us to be the entrance door, the key allowing access to this enchanted forest. The geodynamiter attests to a life wave of 25 positive units inside this high spiritual place.

The Arthurian sites of the Val sans Retour and the Fountain of Barenton are for the first time mentioned in their telluric dimension.

> If the Val sans Retour undeniably possesses an interesting energetic potential by virtue of its situation on the telluric current, the Fountain of Barenton has reserved many surprises for us. [...] The life wave of the place is very weak and barely exceeds neutral (zero). The vital energy of this "ice-cold boiling water" is limited to five positive units. However, and this is perhaps the explanation of the enigma, a heavy atmosphere hangs over the place. An energy similar to that encountered in places of occult practices. So caution, the apprentice sorcerers are not far away.

The energetic visit of Brocéliande ends at the abbatial church of Paimpont, oddly implanted at the intersection of two telluric currents.

> Two powerful cosmo-telluric points: — The chapel of the Holy Sacrament, a small intimate and severe sanctuary recalling the austerity of the monks of Saint Colomban. The life wave there exceeds 32 positive units; — A zone in the choir that we will leave for the pilgrim-researcher to discover.

#### 1992 — Le Crapouillot

A 1992 article in *Le Crapouillot* shows the influence of the work of Gilbert Altenbach and Boune Legrais on the tourist appeal of the Paimpont forest.

> It is near the hamlet of Folle-Pensée that one meets quite singular visitors, attracted by the therapeutic reputation of the fountain. A handful of radiesthetists, pendulum in hand, pace this sacred clearing that one discovers with some difficulty, just like these megaliths, true energy centers scattered through the woods.

#### 1996 — Jean Markale

In 1996, Jean Markale evoked four megalithic sites — Les Buttes aux Tombes, the Jardin aux Moines, the Hôstié de Viviane, and the Tombeau de Merlin — in a work devoted to a spiritual approach to the Paimpont forest. One finds in this publication the first mentions by Jean Markale of the existence of telluric currents in the Brocéliande forest. — MARKALE, Jean, Guide spirituel de la forêt de Brocéliande, Monaco, Éditions du Rocher, 1996.

The Buttes aux Tombes are there presented — despite all archaeological plausibility — as Celtic tumuli of the Iron Age intuitively erected on the location of telluric currents by the druids. — Markale, Jean (1996), pp. 21–23.

> [...] This is here a meeting point between cosmic energies coming from elsewhere, that is, from the external universe, and telluric energies surging from the center of the earth [...]. In those ancient times, one did not bury the deceased just anywhere: it had to be a chosen location — by intuition or by knowledge — according to certain virtues, in particular, at the meeting of telluric currents and cosmic currents.

#### 1999 — Adolphe Landspurg and Norbert L'Hostis

In 1999, a second discovery circuit of the cosmotelluric sites of the Paimpont forest was proposed to the readers of *Bretagne magique et vibratoire* by Adolphe Landspurg and Norbert L'Hostis. The two authors begin their energetic visit at the chapel of the Holy Sacrament of the abbatial church of Paimpont.

> The chapel is crossed by a fault and an underground watercourse oriented toward azimuth 77; the choir of the chapel is placed on a second perpendicular underground watercourse and on a geological fault, which proves that the location was chosen by builders who mastered the art of the dowser. [...] The choir of the church vibrates at 375 H.L.V. units on plane III (spiritual 38). We measure the "magnetic" green color vibration, a cosmo-telluric ratio of 3/3 and a magnetism of 100%.

Notwithstanding the abundance of "scientific" vocabulary, the measurement of cosmotelluric waves is carried out with the pendulum and the geodynamiter. — LANDSPURG, Adolphe and L'HOSTIS, Norbert, Bretagne magique et vibratoire: Circuits insolites et mesures sacrées, Guy Trédaniel éditeur, 1999, 270 p.

The circuit continues with the traditional sites of the Paimpont forest, mixing legendary and megalithism. Walking in the footsteps of Jean Markale, Adolphe Landspurg and Norbert L'Hostis first go to the Jardin aux Moines.

> We measure at the center of the quadrilateral a vibration of 250 H.L.V. units on plane II (mental 25), a magnetism of 100% and a [cosmo-telluric] ratio of 3/3. The electric black color vibration corresponding to the radiesthetic dimension of death indicates that this monument must have served as a funerary place. We mark the first significant cult of the place at −2524.

At the Hôtié de Viviane as at the Jardin aux Moines, the authors indicate the dates of first cult without giving any explanation on the origin of their assertion.

> We climb to the top of the ridge that dominates the Hôtié de Viviane. On this small east-west oriented ridge, we measure a vibration of 2500 H.L.V. on plane III (divine 49). The cosmo-telluric ratio is 3/3 and the magnetism 100%. The electric green color vibration indicates that we are on an ancient place of sacrifice. Given the orientation of the ridge, I think we are on an ancient place of solar worship dedicated to Bélénos. We date the first significant cult of the place at −3140.

The cosmotelluric circuit of the Paimpont forest concludes at the Tombeau de Merlin, where the authors, to their great astonishment, feel a heat that comes from the ground and traverses our body from the feet to the head.

> No doubt and our radiesthetic measurements confirm it, this privileged place of the Brocéliande forest is a cosmo-telluric vibratory high place that must have been known and used by the druids, great connoisseurs of cosmo-telluric forces and energy high places.

Buoyed by its commercial success, the work was reprinted in 2015. — LANDSPURG, Adolphe and L'HOSTIS, Norbert, Bretagne magique et vibratoire: Circuits insolites et mesures sacrées, 1999, Véga Editions, 2015, 345 p.

## Cosmotellurism in the 21st century in the Paimpont forest

Confined to specialized publishers in the 80s and 90s, cosmotellurism and energetic approaches became in the 2000s commonplaces taken up by major publishing houses and sold in the shops and tourist offices of the forest massif.

In works published from 2010, the vocabulary of radiesthesia and geobiology fuses with numerous esoteric references, traditional or New Age beliefs, among which the Marseille tarot, astrology, the existence of the little people (elves, korrigans), neo-shamanism, etc.

### 2011 — Brocéliande secrète

*Brocéliande secrète* is a publication devoted to the Paimpont forest massif in which the "New Age" references strongly associate with the vocabulary of radiesthesia. — BOCHER, Alain and ROPARZ, Klaod, Brocéliande secrète, Saint-Malo, Pascal Galodé éditeurs, 2011, 84 p.

The author uses the Mermet pendulum, the Lecher antenna, or the Bovis scale to account for the characteristics of the vibratory high places of the forest. According to him, the Brocéliande forest is riddled with high places that are either places of contact with our divinities, or places of passage from one to the other. — BOCHER, Alain and ROPARZ, Klaod, Brocéliande secrète, 2011, p. 38.

The work catalogs numerous sacred sites with extraordinarily vibratory characteristics, among which the "traditional" Jardin aux Moines, Fountain of Barenton, or Hôtié de Viviane.

> [The Jardin aux Moines is] indeed a sacred place [...] All the more sacred because its vibration rate on the Bovis scale is very high. We are in the presence of an authentic vortex. Be certain of it. Vortex as are many churches whose priests have forgotten the key (when they have not forgotten the function); that is, a passage from one world to another and in the present case to help us pass into that of the fairies and the korrigans.

The high places of the Paimpont forest, linked together by the author of *Brocéliande Secrète*, take the form of the constellation of the Dragon. — BOCHER, Alain and ROPARZ, Klaod, Brocéliande secrète, 2011, p. 48.

The Hôtié de Viviane is described as a temporal vortex like few, even in Brocéliande. — Bocher & Roparz (2011), p. 36. As for the Fountain of Barenton, its marvelous characteristics mentioned since the 12th century find their explanation in cosmotellurism.

> It would be an energy channel between the subsoil, very charged with telluric energy, and the sky, itself charged with cosmic energy. An enormous potential difference which, when one adds water (probably a good conductor), discharges violently.

Two new sites enrich the energetic itineraries of the Brocéliande forest: the old road leading to the Château de Trécesson and the beech tree of the Gelée.

> It was a very strange tree since, for a millennium, it was half dead, and half alive and of astonishing vibratory power for the living half (more than 60,000 Bovis Units, which is exceptional).

### 2022 — Aurélie Aimé

The legendary Brocéliande forest is one of the 50 energetic high places in France proposed for discovery by Aurélie Aimé in *Lieux sacrés et mystérieux en France*. This work, inscribed in the personal development current, proposes to link the energetic approach and the discovery of spirituality. — AIMÉ, Aurélie, Lieux sacrés et mystérieux en France: Connectez-vous à l'énergie de 50 sites d'exception (Spiritualité & intuition), Le lotus et l'éléphant, 2022, 280 p.

## Therapists and cosmotellurism

From the 2010s, the interest of a broadened public in cosmotellurism allowed new economic actors of the forest massif — therapists, magnetizers, shamans, etc. — to propose workshops, trainings, or stays in the Paimpont forest based on approaches integrating the perception of telluric currents.

Particularly present on the web, these commercial therapeutic proposals show connections to many New Age currents, the main ones of which anchor in shamanism, geobiology, or neo-druidism. All these beliefs find variations and marry each other on occasion.

A report from the 19/20 of France 3 dated 2015, for example, proposes following a shamanism initiation group in search of "energetic" contacts with the little people of the megalithic site of the Tombeau du Géant. — FRANCE 3 -19/20 NATIONAL-, "Il heurte une colonne de puissance à Brocéliande", Dailymotion, 2015.

A storyteller druid proposes a circuit in the Paimpont forest integrating a verification of form waves by the spectrum of colors and individual therapeutic use of the cosmo-telluric energies that result, all this according to the Tradition of the druids. — DWRDAN, "Dwrdan, le Druide accompagnateur, géobiologue et conteur à la fois", Bro Kelt Lan - association culturelle celtique, 2018.

The interest in cosmotellurism in the Paimpont forest is not limited to its commercial exploitation. Many inhabitants of the forest massif also integrate radiesthesia and the existence of telluric currents into their personal beliefs, like this inhabitant of Concoret, interviewed in an article of Bretagne Magazine of September 2023.

> Here, he explains to me, the forest is rocky, seated on purple schist very rich in iron. The region receives a lot of water. This water and the underlying metal create a magnetic field. This could partly explain the attraction of men for Brocéliande, because we are sensitive to magnetic fields, even if we are not necessarily aware of it.

Forty years after the first writings evoking the ambient tellurism of the Celte Sylve de Brec'hallean, it must be noted that the belief in the existence of cosmotelluric currents in the Paimpont forest has spread among many tourists and inhabitants of the forest massif.

Regardless of the criticisms formulated against Paul Bouchet, he worked with force and conviction for the rebirth of druidism; he oriented the Druidic College of the Gauls in a paganizing direction, without false concessions to the established Church. [...] The Druidic College of the Gauls strove to maintain fraternal links with the other druidic organizations [...] The Grand Druid Paul Bouchet wrote several works popularizing the knowledge of the Druidic College of the Gauls, and it is no doubt in doing so that he was most anti-traditional, since knowledge was supposed to be transmitted only orally. He insisted heavily on the notion of telluric currents which, according to him, line the dolmens and various ancient religious buildings.

> Legend and the hypothesis of the possible meet at Atlantis. It is in any case the last of the great vanished civilizations. After its collapse, the surviving initiates would have had their science expressed by architectural ensembles like the pyramids of Egypt and Central America, the Chaldean temples, and the megaliths. It seems indeed that we touch here on the origin of druidism (which has never been found). The druids would be the heirs of the cosmic and geonomic sciences of the Atlanteans, but also of their very modern conception of metaphysics. All this knowledge is therefore in place and within reach. It awaits, to be revealed, that the times have come.
>
> They clearly pre-exist the all-fortuitous discontinuities of the soil and subsoil, such as faults and underground networks, so often invoked as factors of harmful waves. They pre-exist the mineral deposits whose veins and deposits could only settle at a relatively recent epoch of the cooling of the planet. To conceive their far more distant origin, one must no doubt go back into the night of time, before even the solidification of the continents and to the most remote beginnings, in the very nebula, of the formation of the terrestrial core. Thus, the cosmotelluric radiations, limited and principal object of this work, must have appeared from the formation of the globe, at the first contact, at the first interactions of cosmic and telluric forces. In the manner of a crystallization, or rather, under the action of a law analogous to those of Oerstedt and Ampère on electric and magnetic currents, these forces reacted and still react on each other, crossing and distributing themselves symmetrically over the surface of the planet. They thus gave birth to a practically orthogonal new radiation composed of North-South radiations of magnetic appearance, parallel to the magnetic meridian and merging with it, and East-West radiations of electric appearance, very approximately perpendicular to the former.

Rejecting certain technologies, the geobiologist nevertheless accepts improving his perceptions by using measuring instruments, which immediately evoke the physicist. He thus brings a scientific image to his intervention.

Geobiology becomes the master piece of the renewal of rituals around stones. It proceeds from a desacralization of practices and positions the officiant between the image of a magical healer and that of an energetic scientist.

> This absolutely straight line links the extraordinarily vibratory high places and consequently it is not innocent, and the fact that it ends at the Château de Trécesson has had (and perhaps still has) importance on the vibratory plane.

> Thus, a High Place of cosmo-telluric vibratory energy is a privileged place where the human being can capture the powerful beneficial vibrations, both cosmic and terrestrial. These High Places of energy, often placed on the ancient privileged sites of the Primordial Tradition, generally increase the amplitude of the subtle bodies, and by this fact, amplify the aura of the people who frequent them. Some of these places also offer an exposure to vibratory energies proper to improving and increasing the psychic faculties, thereby raising the level of consciousness.
