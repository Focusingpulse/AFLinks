# Wave Universe Theory (TUO): From the Electromagnetic Origin of the Vacuum to the Covariant Reformulation of Gravity and Cosmology

**Author:** François Maillot

**Published:** 2025 | Version v2 | Zenodo

**DOI:** 10.5281/zenodo.17311989

## Description

This deposit presents two fundamental works establishing the theoretical framework of the Wave Universe Theory (TUO), a new unified approach to vacuum physics and gravitation.

### I. Classical formalism and tension field T_v

This first article (*Classical formalisms and correspondence with TUO: From Maxwell and Einstein to the vacuum tension field T_v*) establishes the mathematical structure of TUO by introducing the scalar Vacuum Tension field (T_v). The dynamics of T_v are derived from a covariant Lagrangian (L_TUO), allowing the field equations to be deduced via the principle of least action. The formal correspondence with classical equations (Maxwell and Einstein) is explicit, showing that the Vacuum Energy-Momentum Tensor (T_μν(v)) replaces the cosmological constant (Λg_μν) with a dynamic and local term. The enriched mathematical demonstrations (Friedmann-TUO equations, linear perturbations, Noether current) prove the complete integration of TUO within the framework of General Relativity and provide the tools necessary for the cosmological modeling of dark energy.

### II. Conceptual bridge and origin of T_v

The second article (*From confined electromagnetic fields to cosmic vacuum tension: Bridge between Michaud's theory and TUO?*) provides the physical foundation of the T_v field. It proposes that vacuum tension is not an arbitrary fundamental field, but the macroscopic and homogenized result of the internal electromagnetic (EM) energy of elementary particles (Michaud model). This conceptual bridge allows the gravitational field (g) to be derived locally as the negative gradient of vacuum tension (g = −∇T_v). The article thus demonstrates the transition from confined electromagnetism to gravitation, and justifies the physical interpretation of T_v as a geometric property of the state of the medium, fundamental for unification.
