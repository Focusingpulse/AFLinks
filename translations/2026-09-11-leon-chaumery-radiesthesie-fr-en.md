# Léon Chaumery (1880–1957): The Radiesthesist Who Died of His Own Ray

*English translation and biographical study (composite of French and English sources: hatch.kookscience.com, rexresearch1.com [de Belizal, *Physique Micro-Vibratoire et Forces Invisibles*], dervy-almora.fr, editions-tredaniel.com, eyrolles.com, glnf.fr, franco.wiki, formationmagnetisme.fr, ebookesoterique.com, elishean777.com, e-librairie.leclerc) · translated by Forge (translation-qc agent) · 2026-09-11*

## Introduction

In the French school of physical radiesthesia, every figure has a role: Abbé Bouly coined the word, Abbé Mermet built the method, Armand Viré supplied the scientific respectability, and André de Belizal constructed the grand theory. Léon Julien Marie Chaumery (December 13, 1880 – February 27, 1957) was the school's co-founder and its martyr. With de Belizal he named the Universal Pendulum, patented the negative green ray, coined the expression *ondes de formes* ("form waves") that gave the whole tradition its vocabulary — and then, in the story his own collaborator tells, became the victim of the very radiation he had spent thirty years mapping: de Belizal reports that Chaumery died "completely dehydrated by our negative green ray (V−)."

No other figure in the tradition so completely embodies its central claim and its central danger. This study assembles his life and work from publisher biographies, the record of his patents and books, and the testimony of his collaborator.

## Life

Léon Julien Marie Chaumery was born in France on December 13, 1880. Publisher and encyclopedia accounts describe him as *un radiesthésiste chevronné du début du XXe siècle* — a seasoned, practiced radiesthesist of the early twentieth century; one retail biography goes further, calling him "one of the most important researchers in the field of subtle energies and one of the greatest pioneers who sought to explain the phenomenon of 'form waves' relative to vibrational radiesthesia."

His research partnership with André de Belizal (1896–1975) began around the late 1920s — de Belizal writes that "it was with Léon Chaumery, some thirty years ago, that we began our research," counted from the 1950s. The partnership was the productive core of the physical school: Chaumery was the practiced dowser and systematizer, de Belizal the theorist. Together they worked from de Belizal's manor in Brittany, where much of the pyramid and form-wave research was conducted.

Chaumery died on February 27, 1957. (A retail biography erroneously gives 1958; the date of February 27, 1957 is confirmed both by the Kook Science encyclopedia entry and by de Belizal's own preface, which mourns his collaborator "taken, alas, prematurely from our affection on 27 February 1957.")

## The death — de Belizal's testimony

The most extraordinary primary document about Chaumery is the preface his collaborator wrote to *Physique Micro-Vibratoire et Forces Invisibles* (de Belizal & Morel). Translated from the French:

> "This work is the fruit of 30 years of patient study... of the volume already published in 1939, then re-edited in 1956 under the title *Essai de radiesthésie vibratoire*, and this in collaboration with Léon Chaumery, taken, alas, prematurely from our affection on 27 February 1957. Our collaborator and friend was indeed the victim of his experiments, and completely dehydrated by our negative green ray (V−). At that time we had not yet found the antidote vibration, discovered since with M. P. A. Morel, who kindly turned his attention to the problem."

This is the tradition's own account, given without hedging: the co-discoverer of the negative green ray died of exposure to it — desiccated, in the manner the ray was itself said to desiccate. Within the belief system this is internally consistent: V− was defined as the vibration with "power over matter: it burns, destroys... but can also preserve Life in potentiality, outside space and time," the ray the Egyptians allegedly used "to dehydrate their dead and withdraw the bodies from decomposition." Chaumery's death was read as proof of the ray's reality — the founder killed by his own discovery, before the antidote was found.

Outside the belief system, of course, the claim cannot be verified, and no medical record supports a death by "radiation" of this kind. The account is valuable precisely as testimony: it shows how the school understood the stakes of its own practice — radiesthesia as a physically dangerous craft, not a parlor trick.

## The Universal Pendulum and the 1936 patent

Chaumery and de Belizal's central instrument was the *Pendule Universel* (Universal Pendulum), a spherical detector they named **Louksor**, after the Luxor obelisk form. Publisher Dervy's description:

> "L. Chaumery and A. de Belizal baptized the form of their pendulum with the name Louksor (the Universal Pendulum), receiver and emitter: it allows one to receive and to emit all the vibrations of the spectrum, visible as well as invisible. They began the systematic study, widening over the years their field of research to the whole of forms: pendulums, various mobiles, metallic torsades, etc."

The instrument was patented on April 10, 1936 (published July 30, 1937) as **FR 816,132 — "Procédé et appareillage radiesthésiques"** (A radiesthetic method and apparatus), filed by L. Chaumery, J. Chaumery, and A. de Bélizal. The patent text, translated:

> "The present invention relates to a dowsing method and apparatus based on terrestrial magnetism. Until now, pendulums used in dowsing have been bodies of any shape, suspended by a flexible link. The most perfected agree only in wavelength. With all these devices, and in particular with disks, it is impossible to explore the region comprised between infra-black and ultra-white, these two rays being welded along an axis which itself possesses another ray not yet qualified. This ray is the antipode of the green of the spectrum: it is the shortest wave and consequently the most powerful of the invisible spectrum. In what follows, this ray is referred to as negative green or special green."

This patent is the founding document of the negative green concept — the "antipode of green," the shortest and most powerful wave of the invisible spectrum, positioned between infra-black and ultra-white on the sphere of the pendulum's graduations. De Belizal later called it "the cornerstone of our method, without which it would have been impossible to construct a valid theory."

## The books

Two volumes carry Chaumery's name, both written with de Belizal:

1. **Traité Expérimental de Physique Radiesthésique** (1939, Éditions Dangles, Paris) — the earlier systematic treatise of the physical school.
2. **Essai de Radiesthésie Vibratoire** (first published 1939; re-edited 1956 by Dangles; re-issued 2013 by Dervy, 191 pages) — the school's summary statement, the fruit of "more than thirty years of research." The Eyrolles bookstore abstract gives its program:

> "Radiesthesia is currently divided between two tendencies: one which proceeds from the mental, the other which seeks to rest on the principles of physics, with which it appears to be related. To add to the general confusion, these two tendencies, which at first sight seem rivals, nevertheless have one point in common: the transformation and amplification of the radiation passing through the body of man by way of the vital fluid, whose mechanism is translated by the movement of a detector (pendulum or rod). These two theories, mental and physical, assure positive results which may be considered equivalent. The authors estimate that the true radiesthesist has every interest in orienting himself toward this latter path, where he will benefit from the recent discoveries of physics, which will allow him to carry his art more surely to the level of a science. This book is the fruit of more than thirty years of research which allowed them to discover that every object, according to its specificity and particularity, influences the energetic body of man, and that every object emits 'form waves.'"

The GLNF review, reviewing the rare 1939 and 1956 editions and welcoming the Dervy re-edition, notes a preface by geobiologist Patrick Darcheville and describes the book's scope: Chaumery and de Belizal "arrived at the conclusion that all the statuary, architecture, and even the graphics of certain civilizations (triskel, swastika...) of Antiquity obeyed these laws of force emissions. Lines, volumes, disposition, orientation — everything was calculated to generate an ideal equilibrium, a perfect integration into space of certain places and certain privileged men."

## The vocabulary: "ondes de formes"

Chaumery's most durable contribution to the tradition is a phrase. The French encyclopedia at franco.wiki records:

> "The expression 'onde de forme' [form wave] was created for the first time by Léon Chaumery and André de Belizal in the 1930s. Their work and that of Enel are a reference in the world of so-called scientific radiesthesia."

Every later development — Enel's Omega ray, the Servranx brothers' radionics, Ibrahim Karim's BioGeometry — is built on the vocabulary Chaumery coined. The claim that every object, by its form alone, emits a characteristic vibration, is the foundation-stone of the entire physical school, and it is Chaumery's.

## The pendulum of Thoth

A further Chaumery–de Belizal contribution, recorded by the formationmagnetisme.fr school: in the 1930s the two men studied the *Ouadj* — the papyrus-scepter amulet of ancient Egypt, mentioned in chapter 160 of the Book of the Dead, placed on the throat of the dead "to restore the breath of life" — and found that its shape possessed particular geometric properties. They renamed it the **Pendule de Thot** (Pendulum of Thoth), in homage to the god of writing and knowledge. The related "Egyptian" pendulum, made of ironwood weighted with lead, was de Belizal's creation, designed specifically for physical radiesthesia in emission of negative green — a fact the Servranx laboratories later had to explain repeatedly to buyers who confused generic "Thoth" pendulums with the licensed model described in Marc Roquart's *Le pendule de Thoth et ses mystères*.

## The afterlife of the ray

Chaumery's negative green outlived him along three lines:

- **Enel** (1940s–50s) used the Chaumery pendulum principle to study the tombs of Christian saints in Egypt, reporting that all of them emitted a vibration quality in the negative green zone — which he designated Omega, a marker of spiritual energy.
- **De Belizal and P. A. Morel**, after Chaumery's death, found the "antidote vibration" to the vertical component of V− and consolidated the whole system in *Physique Micro-Vibratoire et Forces Invisibles* — the book whose preface records Chaumery's death.
- **Ibrahim Karim** (BioGeometry) determined that sacred monuments use horizontal negative green as a carrier wave, alongside two other energies — the BG3 quality — building directly on the Chaumery–de Belizal spectrum.

The tradition also preserves an internal critique worth quoting (via the Elishean compilation): the Chaumery–de Belizal spectrum of the 1930s places black, white, and negative green between scarlet red and violet purple; "they drew and named these colors for convenience, but never said that 'negative green' was actually green in color. These pseudo-colors serve simply to differentiate what happens between magenta and purple. The electromagnetic spectrum contains green only once; introducing it twice into a subtle spectrum is an absurdity." The pseudo-colors are labels for positions on the sphere — a convention, not a claim about optics.

## Assessment

Chaumery occupies a unique position in the French school: he is at once its most-cited founder (the vocabulary, the pendulum, the patent, the two books) and its cautionary tale. The tradition's own history of him is a moral one — the researcher killed by the force he mapped, before the antidote was found — and it encodes the school's deepest conviction: that form-wave radiesthesia is a physical craft with physical consequences, to be practiced, as the *Essai* itself insists, "with a pure and upright conscience... only for good and for noble causes."

Mainstream science does not accept the physical claims: no instrument has ever detected negative green, and the historical effects attributed to it (mummification by pyramid form, desiccation at a distance) have no verified instances. But as a chapter in the history of French physical radiesthesia — the tradition Sandra's library documents — Chaumery is the load-bearing figure: the man who gave the school its words, its instrument, its patent, and its legend.
