"Objective immersion in the observed objects causes the spirit to dissolve completely into them; so that Goethe's theories do not appear to us as if a spirit were abstracting them from objects, but rather as if they formed the objects themselves in a spirit that, during observation, forgot itself. This extremely rigorous objectivity would have made Goethe's work the most perfect work of natural science; it would be an ideal to which every natural scientist should aspire; it would be for the philosopher a typical example of research into the laws of objective observation of the world. One may suppose that the theory of knowledge that now arises everywhere as a fundamental philosophical science will be fruitful only when it takes its starting point from the Goethean manner of thinking and observing" (pp. 36-37).
