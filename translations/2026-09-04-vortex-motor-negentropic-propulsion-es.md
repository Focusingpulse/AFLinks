Abstract
This work presents the theoretical framework for the development of a "Vortex Motor," a propulsion and energy generation device based not on the entropic principle of explosion (combustion), but on the negentropic principle of implosion (vortex). Drawing on the pioneering work of Viktor Schauberger and the physics of the Ether, we postulate that it is possible to generate a net traction force by creating a localized pressure gradient in the Ether. The theoretical architecture of the motor is described: a resonant cavity of ovoid/spiral geometry that induces a centripetal vortex in a working fluid (air or water). It is argued that, by applying an electromagnetic resonance frequency, the viscosity of the Ether can be reduced, allowing the vortex to accelerate to a point of "implosion," where a dynamic vacuum is generated. The pressure of the surrounding Ether, in attempting to collapse this vacuum, produces a traction force ("anti-gravity"). Finally, a roadmap is established for the numerical validation of this principle through Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations, inviting the community to verify this model.

1 Introduction: The Paradigm of Implosion vs. Explosion
The technology of the 20th century has been founded on the principle of explosion: centrifugal, expansive, and high-temperature processes (e.g., internal combustion engines, jet propulsion, nuclear fission). These processes are inherently entropic, generating heat, noise, and disorder as byproducts. Nature, however, operates primarily through implosion: centripetal, contractive, and low-temperature processes (e.g., vortices in water and air). These processes are negentropic, generating order, structure, and concentration of energy. Inspired by the work of Viktor Schauberger, this work explores the application of this latter principle to propulsion technology.

2 Theoretical Architecture of the Vortex Motor
The proposed model is based on a device that generates a net traction force by creating a localized pressure gradient in the Ether. Its architecture consists of four synergistic components.

2.1 The Resonant Cavity
The motor chamber is not cylindrical. It must have an ovoidal geometry or follow a logarithmic spiral (golden section). This shape is essential for guiding a working fluid (air, water) into a stable centripetal vortex, minimizing turbulence and maximizing acceleration.

2.2 The Working Fluid and the Ether
The motor operates using the surrounding medium as the working fluid. Its true source of energy, however, is the Ether, the dielectric medium that permeates space. The working fluid acts as the vehicle through which the motor interacts with the Ether.

2.3 The Vortex Initiator
An internal rotor with a specific hydrodynamic design (based on Schauberger's "trout blades") initiates the rotational movement of the fluid. Simultaneously, the application of an oscillating electromagnetic field at a resonance frequency ω_res in the cavity is postulated. The hypothesis is that this frequency can modulate the properties of the medium, such as effective viscosity, allowing the formation of a high-velocity, low-friction vortex.

2.4 The Propulsion Mechanism: Traction by Pressure Gradient
The acceleration of the vortex toward the focus of the ovoidal cavity creates a region of extremely low density and pressure, a dynamic vacuum in the Ether. The pressure of the surrounding Ether, P_ether, in attempting to collapse this vacuum, exerts a net force on the device:

F_traction = −∮ S P_ether dS    (1)

where the integral is taken over the outer surface of the motor. The propulsion is not a "push" against a reaction mass, but a "traction" generated by a pressure gradient in the very fabric of space. This effect has been colloquially termed "anti-gravity."

3 Roadmap for Numerical Validation
The validation of this principle requires a Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulation. The following simulation protocol is proposed as an open challenge to the community of computational engineers and physicists:

1. Domain Design: Model a 2D axisymmetric cavity with an ovoidal geometry based on the equation x²/a² + y²/b² = 1, with a tangential inlet.
2. Fluid Model: Use the Navier-Stokes equations for an incompressible fluid, introducing an effective viscosity term, μ_effective, that can be modulated to simulate the interaction with the Ether under resonance conditions.
3. Simulation Objective: The objective is to demonstrate that, under certain flow and viscosity modulation conditions, the system converges to a stable state where a centripetal vortex forms that generates a significant negative pressure gradient (ΔP ≪ 0) at its focus, resulting in a net traction force on the body.

A set of suggested parameters and geometries will be released in a public repository to facilitate independent replication.

4 Conclusion
The Vortex Motor represents a paradigm shift from brute-force physics to harmonic flow physics. This document establishes the fundamental theoretical framework and the roadmap for numerical validation of a potentially revolutionary propulsion system. The scientific and engineering community is invited to take on this computational challenge, whose results could open the door to a new era of negentropic technology.
