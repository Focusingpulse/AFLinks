# A Brief History of the Theory of Form Waves

*February 17, 2024 — by Maude — GaiaMamart*

Hello everyone!

In this new article on form waves, I wanted to focus on the origins of this theory.

As a reminder, certain researchers, thinkers, essayists (and ordinary people like me) believe that objects and living beings emit a vibration. These vibrations, or form waves, interact with one another and with their environment. This is how certain shapes like the Flower of Life can be said to be "harmonizing" through the positive action they exert.

In this article, you will see that the concept of form waves is very old. Our ancestors, without ever using the term "form wave," already knew that links exist between form, matter, and intention.

This concept was later theorized by Westerners — notably radiesthesists, but also the biochemist and parapsychologist Rupert Sheldrake. The latter worked on morphogenetic fields (don't worry, I explain what that is further on).

Shall we begin?

## 1 — Form Waves and Architecture in Traditional Societies

For millennia, humans have built palaces, temples, monuments, public spaces, and dwellings. The study of certain ancient societies reveals that our ancestors did not build at random. Sites were carefully chosen, as were the orientation and the materials.

According to Georges Prat, the Indians created a science of architecture over 5,000 years ago: the Vastushastra (vāstu śāstra). Like Feng Shui, this science studies the energies of the earth and their effects on human beings, in order to determine where and how to build a dwelling or any other structure.

Hélène Bernet, in her work on form waves, recounts an anecdote: "Thus, in Tibet and Bhutan, I watched the creation of icons, tankas, mandalas, and even the finishing of a temple roof. Appropriate attire, meditation, prayer, song — everything was brought together to achieve the desired result: energetic completeness and plenitude, that of the sacred."

Closer to us, in the West, builders left nothing to chance. A church steeple, for instance, is an antenna for an entire village. It is designed to broadcast the good frequencies of a high-energy site, amplified by the forms and materials of the church.

The same logic — the quest for improved energetic atmosphere through the arrangement of standing stones — is found in megalithic sites.

⇒ Geobiology (and Vastushastra) explores the interactions between humans, their environment, and ambient energies such as electromagnetic disturbances.

This is a theory that validates the theory of form waves: everything is vibration, and it is up to humans to adapt their living space to ambient energies.

## 2 — Form Waves in Art

The artist seeks to convey a message. They want to be striking, impactful, applauded for the quality of their work. To be recognized by their peers, they must make them vibrate, and spend a good part of their life seeking to transmit Harmony in the finest detail. This is why artists are often sensitive beings, attuned to energies.

For millennia, humans have used art to harmonize their living spaces.

Thus, both Hindus and Buddhists create yantras to balance certain aspects of their lives. The shapes and symbols contained in yantras were not chosen at random, but for their symbolic reach and their vibration.

One also thinks of the creation of mandalas, an ancient Buddhist tradition. These circular, symmetrical representations soothe their creators. Carl Gustav Jung, the psychiatrist, frequently used mandalas to reconnect with himself, following the example of the Buddhists.

⇒ Artworks combine shapes and colors: they have a high vibratory potential, and artists have known this for millennia.

They are the guardians of a kind of connection between the divine, the spiritual, and the material world. They transcribe the subtle waves they perceive into their creative work.

## 3 — The Work of Lakhovsky: Vibrations Before Form Waves

One of the first to have supported the theory of form waves was G. Lakhovsky.

Georges Lakhovsky (1873–1942) was a Russian-born writer, inventor, and engineer (who became French in 1909). He is famous for his hypotheses concerning a possible communication between living cells through high-frequency electromagnetic radiation.

He is credited with inventing alternative medicine devices purportedly able to cure cancer through the form waves they emit.

He argued, in various works, that the state of health of living beings depends on the quality of the vibrations of their cells.

His theories can be summarized by the following three principles:

- Life is born from radiation
- Is sustained by radiation
- Is suppressed by any oscillatory imbalance

⇒ Without speaking of form waves, G. Lakhovsky was one of the first (to my knowledge) to theorize a vibratory vision of the Universe.

## 4 — André de Bélizal and Paul-André Morel, Fathers of Form Waves

André de Bélizal and Paul-André Morel are pioneers in the research of "form waves." They were the first to define and name them:

> "Forms capture terrestrial magnetism, become saturated with it, and re-emit the captured energy as a form wave."

They are the authors of several successful books on radiesthesia — a science that studies the relationships between humans and the various radiations emitted by different bodies.

They experimented for over thirty years to bring their research to fruition. The two men also revealed the use of pendulums in ancient Egypt.

André de Bélizal and Paul-André Morel are among the fathers of radiesthesia.

Among their research themes, we find:

- The design of various research and measurement tools: the Universal Pendulum, the Egyptian pendulum, the Snail-Selector, the Micrometer, etc.
- The physical study of the influence of vibrations emanating from shapes and colors
- The history of the use of form waves in ancient societies

⇒ As inventors of the concept of form waves, André de Bélizal and Paul-André Morel published works that remain, even today, essential references for anyone seeking to understand radiesthesia and radionics.

## 5 — Focus on Morphic Fields

We continue our exploration of the theory of form waves by discussing morphic fields and morphic resonance, dear to the researcher Rupert Sheldrake.

According to him, everything that exists — living beings, snowflakes, thoughts, etc. — leaves a trace in the universe after its physical disappearance.

If an organizing field has disappeared physically, its morphic field does not vanish and extends through space and time. It can manifest again "at other times, in other places, wherever (and whenever) the physical conditions are appropriate" (R. Sheldrake).

This theory echoes the theses of Carl Gustav Jung on the collective unconscious: there is something that hangs in the air and composes a kind of collective memory (or an egregore).

It is somewhat complex. To understand it well, I can only recommend this excellent blog article on the concept of morphogenetic fields: *Morphic Resonance*, Michel Ricquier.

⇒ The theory of morphogenetic fields supports the thesis of form waves: every object or person emits vibrations when physically present, and even after.

## Conclusion: Key Takeaways on the Origins of the Theory of Form Waves

1. Some of our ancestors were more aware than we are of cosmo-telluric energies, and this knowledge was used to harmonize their living spaces.
2. It was in the 20th century that the theory of "form waves" was conceptualized.
3. As long as their existence cannot be proven by tangible means, form waves will remain a theory that cannot be validated by Cartesian scientific methods.
4. Certain researchers believe that everything is vibration, and that vibrations leave an imprint in the world even after the physical body has disappeared.
