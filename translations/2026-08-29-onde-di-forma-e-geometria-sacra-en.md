# Form Waves and Sacred Geometry

## The hidden energy behind perfect forms

You may never have heard of form waves — or you may have come across them by chance without ever exploring their meaning. Yet behind this definition lies a fascinating concept, capable of uniting science, spirituality, and natural philosophy. It might represent a key to subtle realities, where matter, vibration, and consciousness merge into a single universal language.

Matter, as is well known, emits radiations of various kinds. Some are well understood — electromagnetic or sound waves, for example — while others remain mysterious, difficult to measure, yet perceptible in their effects. Among these are the so-called *form waves*, created by the geometrical configuration of matter. As the name suggests, it is not the substance itself that generates the phenomenon, but the form it assumes in space.

The wavelength of these emissions is infinitesimal, and yet, according to various researchers, extremely influential on the surrounding environment. Everything that exists vibrates; everything is frequency. Every object, every living being, every thought is a manifestation of energy organized according to a specific harmonic structure.

At the atomic level, everything possesses a specific form determined by the molecular arrangement of its elements. And since form and energy are inseparable, the vibration emitted by a body also varies according to its geometry.

### From French radiesthesia to subtle physics

The modern interest in form waves was born in France in the first half of the twentieth century thanks to the scholars Léon Chaumery and André de Belizal, authors of *Physique micro-vibratoire et forces invisibles* (1959). Both researchers, experts in radiesthesia, theorized that every geometrical object is able to absorb, transform, and re-emit subtle energies coming from Earth's magnetic field.

Their research led to a three-part classification of energies: electrical, magnetic, and neutral. The latter would be closely tied to human thought and capable of amplifying its effects. In other words, a geometric form can become a true mental catalyst, able to interact with the will and consciousness of the observer.

Later, scholars such as P.A. Morel and Jean de La Foye attempted to place the phenomenon in a broader scientific context, linking it to the morphogenetic field theory proposed by the Cambridge biologist Rupert Sheldrake. According to Sheldrake, nature possesses a sort of collective memory capable of guiding the form and behavior of living structures. In this view, form waves could represent the physical expression of these invisible informational fields.

## Sacred geometry: the language of the gods

The idea that the universe is governed by harmonic proportions is extremely ancient. Pythagoras and his disciples spoke of a "harmony of the spheres," a cosmic music produced by the movement of celestial bodies according to perfect mathematical ratios. In the Tetraktys, sacred to the Pythagoreans, the sum of the first four numbers (1+2+3+4=10) represented the totality of the universe.

Plato, in the *Timaeus*, associated the five Platonic solids with the fundamental elements of matter: earth, air, fire, water, and ether. Centuries later, Johannes Kepler, in his *Mysterium Cosmographicum* (1596), took up this geometrical vision of the cosmos, attempting to explain planetary orbits as an expression of divine order.

Sacred geometry is born precisely from this awareness: the universe has a harmonic structure, and perfect forms are its visible manifestation. It is no coincidence that Egyptian temples, pyramids, Gothic cathedrals, and Islamic mosques share proportions based on the Golden Number (1.618), the symbol of the ideal balance between parts.

## The Flower of Life and primordial knowledge

Among the best-known symbols of sacred geometry we find the Flower of Life, present in extremely ancient cultures and even carved into the granite of the Temple of Osiris at Abydos, in Egypt. The symbol is composed of perfectly interwoven circles, from which fundamental figures originate, such as the Vesica Piscis, Metatron's Cube, and the Kabbalistic Tree of Life.

In early Christianity, the Flower of Life was interpreted as a symbol of the divine Sun, a representation of God the Father and of the resurrection of Christ. In Celtic traditions, by contrast, it was considered a solar chariot, an emblem of the vital force that renews creation.

The center of the Flower, formed by the perfect intersection of the circumferences, represents the point of absolute balance: the source from which everything comes and to which everything returns. It is the synthesis of the divine, the supreme knowledge that leads to enlightenment.

In Jewish esotericism, the structure of the Flower of Life is connected to the Sephirotic Tree, since from its geometry can be derived the ten Sephiroth, symbols of the divine emanations.

### Vesica Piscis, the Golden Number, and the form of divinity

Another figure of great importance is the Vesica Piscis ("fish bladder"), obtained from the intersection of two circles of equal radius, whose centers lie on each other's circumference. The resulting shape, called the mandorla, is recurring in sacred art: many Byzantine icons show Christ inscribed within a luminous mandorla, symbol of his dual human and divine nature.

The Vesica Piscis, beyond its symbolic value, also possesses a mathematical meaning: from its construction the golden ratio is derived — the proportion that confers harmony and beauty upon forms. This number, found in the spirals of seashells, in the arrangement of flower petals, and even in galaxies, was studied in 1509 by the mathematician Luca Pacioli in his *De Divina Proportione*, illustrated by Leonardo da Vinci, who used it in his most famous works.

### The echo of perfection: energy, architecture, and spirit

Observing the great Gothic cathedrals, one cannot help but perceive a force that transcends matter. Medieval architects and master builders knew and applied sacred geometry as a spiritual language: proportions, angles, arches, and rose windows were designed to channel subtle energies, amplified by the thought and faith of the worshipers.

Authors such as René Guénon and Fulcanelli have suggested that these architectures functioned as spiritual machines, capable of converting cosmic energy into inner vibration. Today, some scholars find analogies between these ancient intuitions and modern informational-field physics, such as that proposed by David Bohm or Ervin Laszlo, according to which the universe is an immense field of unified consciousness in which information (form) precedes matter.

## Sacred geometry is the bridge between science and the divine

In light of all this, we might say that sacred geometry is not merely a set of aesthetic forms, but a symbolic language connecting man to the cosmos. Every form is a vibration, every vibration is information, and every piece of information is a fragment of divine thought.

Perfection belongs to God, but man, though imperfect, can aspire to it through knowledge, contemplation, and inner harmony. Perhaps, as the ancient Hermetic principle taught:

"As above, so below; as below, so above, to accomplish the miracles of the one thing."

And so, from the invisible waves of matter to the perfection of sacred geometries, a single truth manifests: the universe is form that vibrates, and vibration that prays.

In the end, every temple, every symbol, every proportion is a way in which man attempts to draw near to the divine mind. Form waves are nothing but the reflection of a greater principle: the idea that all creation, in every detail, is a perfect symphony.

I do not presume to convince anyone, but I invite you to observe reality with new eyes. Perhaps, behind the simplest forms, the secret of the connection between God, matter, and consciousness truly lies hidden.
