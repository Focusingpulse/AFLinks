# Emilio Del Giudice Explains Gerald Pollack's Discovery of the Fourth Phase of Water

The Italian physicist Emilio Del Giudice, in this lecture, speaks about a property of water discovered by Gerald Pollack. A discovery that clarifies one of the greatest mysteries of biology, already highlighted in the 1950s by the Nobel Prize winner for medicine Albert Szent-Györgyi.

## The Mystery of Electrons in Living Matter

Let us give a brief summary. In living matter, an enormous quantity of chemical reactions take place. These are possible only if some substance is capable of releasing electrons. But which of the substances present in living matter is capable of releasing them? That is the mystery. Györgyi thinks it is water. Yet it takes 12.6 electronvolts to release an electron from a water molecule. This requires a level of energy (nuclear or thermal) incompatible with life. Where do the electrons come from, then, that are needed for the chemical reactions that constantly occur in the body?

As Albert Szent-Györgyi intuited, they come from water itself.

## Pollack's Discovery

Here is Emilio Del Giudice's account. In the early 2000s, the American physicist Gerald Pollack of the University of Seattle experimentally observed that water near surfaces, to a depth of several microns, is water that has different properties from bulk water and is capable of easily yielding electrons. The water molecule, when isolated, requires 12.6 electronvolts to release an electron, but the same is not true when this same molecule is together with other water molecules and when it is near a surface.

We must remember that in living matter everything is organized through membranes and that there is no point in our organism that is more than one millionth of a centimeter from some membrane. This means that there is no water molecule (99% of all molecules present in the body are water) that is more than one millionth of a centimeter from a membrane.

Pollack thus demonstrates that water near a membrane has a different nature and that it is sufficient to place the end of an electric wire in this water to obtain a current. This current, according to Pollack, is the expression of what was once called the vital force. A specific form of energy that is at work in biological organisms.

## The Exclusion Zone Experiment

Del Giudice describes one of Pollack's experiments:

> "He fills a container with water whose walls are made of hydrophilic protein substances, then adds traces of dye, observes this colored liquid with a powerful microscope, and what does he discover? The dye does not dissolve uniformly but leaves dark zones near the walls. He calls this layer the exclusion zone. In other words, he demonstrates that solutes cannot penetrate this zone. Not only that, Pollack discovers that in this zone it is easy for water to yield electrons. In water that has these particular characteristics, nothing dissolves and solutes remain on the surfaces and organize themselves on the surfaces. This is the mechanism that gives rise to biological morphologies. If we have these regions of water into which nothing penetrates, solutes concentrate in the interstices between them. And so architectures form, structures that have their own morphology and that are the X-ray of the coherence domains of water. In other words, these structures trace the structure of the organized regions of water — those that Pollack calls exclusion zones."
