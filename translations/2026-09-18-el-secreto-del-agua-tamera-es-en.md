# THE SECRET OF WATER AS THE BASIS FOR A NEW EARTH
Healing the water cycle through the creation of "Water Retention Landscapes"
Talk by Bernd Walter Mueller, 2011 (2nd corrected edition, August 2013)
Translated from German by Mariló Herrero (Spanish version); English translation from the Spanish

"Water, energy and food will be freely available to all of humanity when we no longer follow the laws of capital, but the logic of nature."
Dieter Duhm, from the Tamera Manifesto for a New Generation on planet Earth.

I choose this sentence to open my talk because I want to ask you to see this vision of a healed world as frequently and vividly as you can. We must not grow accustomed to a state in which something that is actually self-evident seems to us an unrealistic utopia. An Earth on which all people have free access to sufficient water, energy and food is an entirely feasible vision. More than 80 years ago, similar ideas were described by the Austrian Viktor Schauberger, a brilliant water researcher, a pioneer, visionary and leading thinker of the highest order. Already then he could foresee the global problems we face today, and he showed how they could be solved. A key point in the solution is the proper treatment of water. That is why I would like to address the subject of water in this talk.

Water is life. And where there is life, there is also food and energy.

The years 2010 to 2020 were declared by the United Nations the "Decade of Deserts and the Fight Against Desertification." Progressive desertification is today one of the greatest global problems. More than 40% of the Earth's land mass is now considered arid zone. Even in Europe — for example here on the Iberian Peninsula — the desertification process is dramatic. One third of Spain's surface has already turned into arid land. But most of the arid zones of our Earth are located in even poorer countries. Billions of people today no longer have access to good, fresh water. Even if we still try to stand apart, we know that this is due, among other things, to our way of life here in the industrialized countries. Day by day, hour by hour and minute by minute, it leads to a situation in other regions of the Earth where children fall sick and die from contaminated water, where humans fight over what water remains, and animals die of thirst. Water — in truth a source of life — is today a cause of war, power struggles, disease, and endless suffering.

That is why the Bolivian president Evo Morales called in 2008, in his "Ten Commandments to Save the Planet, Humanity and Life," for us to confront this issue of the "global water crisis" and declare access to water a human right. I join in this request. I give this talk so that all people and all animals may once again have free access to good-quality drinking water. For this purpose, the idea of the Water Retention Landscapes and of the Terra Nova School has been developed.


---


Desertification resulting from incorrect water management

We humans have the knowledge of how to transform the deserts and semi-deserts back into living landscapes, traversed by streams whose source is fresh spring water. Desertification is, in most cases, not a natural phenomenon but the result of incorrect water management on a global scale. Deserts do not form from a lack of rainfall, but because humans treat water in an incorrect manner.

An example: our landscape in the Alentejo is considered an arid zone. But in the last week there were heavy rains here. The quantity of water that fell in a few days would have been enough to supply the population of this entire region with drinking and household water for the whole year; instead, water runoffs were created that went unused and moreover caused damage: they eroded fertile soil, undermined bridge foundations, flooded streets, towns and villages. Humans are now occupied repairing the damage caused. This is laborious and costly, and with the next rain the same thing happens again. Thus they have no time to think about what they can do to invest in new systems with which they could have clean water all year and, at the same time, prevent floods.

In Portugal we have much rain in winter and the summer is dry. Only a few decades ago, southern Portugal was a region where even in summer the streams flowed with water all year round. Today the streams fill up only during the rainy season and then dry out again. The system is completely out of balance.

This situation is found all over the world, in the corresponding form in all climate zones. Almost everywhere we witness, today, floods and landslides with catastrophic consequences for humans, infrastructure, animals and nature. Then people speak of natural disasters, although in reality they are disasters caused by man.

The average water cycle

[Note: from here this section of the source text switches to Portuguese in the original PDF.]

How can we change this situation, both at a local and a global level? What does a system change mean in terms of water management, and how can we initiate this process? To find answers to these questions we will have to analyze once again the situation that we find today all over the globe. This situation corresponds to the incomplete hydrological cycle, as described by Viktor Schauberger: Water evaporates, forms clouds, and falls as rain. The rain reaches a soil that, as a consequence of intensive grazing and deforestation, is arid, and is therefore now incapable of absorbing the water. Previously, the planet was protected by a dense variety of vegetation. In this way, the valuable humus (fertile soil) could form, and soak up water like a sponge.


---


Nowadays, however, this vegetation rich in species variety has been destroyed on a large scale, forests have been felled, meadows have been misused through overgrazing or lack of grazing, and large areas have been "sealed" through urban development or exploitative use. The soil left unprotected in this way heats up, and since its temperature becomes higher than the temperature of the rain, the soil closes up, becomes hard, and loses the capacity to absorb the rainwater, which then runs off across the surface. The water then accumulates in large, fast-flowing torrents. Wherever a layer of fertile soil or loose, rich earth still exists, this current of water will drag away the humus particles present. In this process, any remaining layer of surface fertile earth is carried away together with the water, causing the fatal problem of erosion.

In the incomplete hydrological cycle, the accelerated water rapidly fills the rivers and streams. With heavy rains, these watercourses swell greatly in volume and carry along with them large quantities of soil and other materials, which they can then no longer deposit on the next bend of the river's meanders, because the water is no longer allowed to move in a sinuous winding course, because the rivers have been straightened and their banks artificially reinforced. The precious soil, so urgently needed in the fields, now settles downstream, causing the riverbeds to widen. The watercourses thus become progressively shallower, breaking through their banks and causing countless damages, especially in the cities located near river mouths. In the incomplete hydrological cycle, instead of carrying clean spring water, the rivers now carry torrents of polluted, silt-laden rainwater. There are no longer any places where the water has time to stop, rest, mature, and enrich itself with minerals and information. I think few young people in this world still know streams that carry flows of clean spring water.

The falling water table

[Note: the source text switches back to Spanish here.]

If water cannot infiltrate into the body of the earth, there is scarcity. Due to the resulting dryness, the soil biota suffers, microorganisms withdraw, the fertility of the land decreases significantly, and fewer and fewer plant and animal species can survive. The dryness of the land and the loss of biodiversity are the most important indicators of desertification. The water table is falling all over the world, and dramatically so. Globally, the drinking water reserve is shrinking.


---


Here we stand before a fact that leads us directly into apocalyptic scenarios if we do not manage to stop this process. Through the falling water table, the balance between the fresh water of the aquifer and the salt water of the sea can no longer be maintained; seawater infiltrates into the interior of the earth without hindrance, and the soils and the fresh water of the deeper aquifer layers become salinized. The ecosystem collapses — a nearly irreversible situation. In many coastal areas around the world this process is already happening. Here on the Iberian Peninsula too, the water of the aquifers near the coast is beginning to become salinized. But what times await humanity if there is no more drinking water of natural origin? We cannot look away and allow something to happen that can be prevented. The knowledge for this is available; now it is a matter of applying it. We know it: this is not how planet Earth is meant to be. This is not the way the coexistence of humans, animals and the Earth is meant to be. This is not how life is meant to be.

The complete water cycle

Let us look once more at the healed image. It is the image of the complete water cycle: the rain that falls on the earth is absorbed by the humus layer as if by a sponge. Not long ago, in the area of Tamera there was a layer of fertile earth up to half a meter deep. It was more or less like this throughout Portugal and, in principle, throughout Europe. This layer of humus soil, shaded and rooted by plants, absorbed the rainwater, and thus the water had time to filter down into the deeper layers of the earth and replenish the aquifers of the body of the earth.

In this way the saturated body of the earth acted as a storage organ. There, beneath the earth, the water rests at various depths, sometimes for long periods of time. We still know little about what really happens with the water down there in the darkness. I perceive this as the feminine part, or soul, of the water cycle. What we can say is that the water matures there, becoming mineralized and absorbing information. This ability to absorb and store information is one of the most essential and mysterious qualities of water. In the saturated earth, the water cools on its way to deeper layers of the earth. Where the complete water cycle is intact, the water emerges again at the surface as mature spring water at a temperature of 4 degrees Celsius. This spring water has an immense healing power for the earth and all its creatures.


---


Rivers and streams that carry spring water and can meander according to their nature have healing power for the earth. The water revitalizes itself as it advances along its course. On the banks of such rivers and streams, diverse biotopes develop, where life unfolds. Water, in its complete cycle, flows continuously and steadily. The earth acts as a buffer. It can also absorb a large quantity of water all at once, but releases it slowly. In this way floods are prevented. And at the same time the rivers carry clear, clean water all year round.

Balance is achieved between the rainy months and the dry season of the year. This applies in principle to all climate zones. A complete water cycle in which the body of the earth once again carries out its function fully creates stability and equilibrium everywhere.


---


Healing nature through Water Retention Landscapes

Today, this body of the earth — the humus of the fertile layer — has disappeared from a large part of the Earth's surface. The process of erosion, especially in these last decades, has progressed so rapidly and extensively that we can speak of a global disaster. This is why we must not busy ourselves developing ecosystems that only create a thin layer of humus after 30, 40 or even more years. We need that balancing sponge effect first. To be able to complete the water cycle again, we had to find a way for water to be absorbed by the earth despite the lack of the fertile layer. This is how the idea of Water Retention Landscapes developed.

Water Retention Landscapes are systems for restoring the complete water cycle by retaining water wherever the rain falls. There are abundant methods for retaining rainwater in the earth; these can be used in combination with one another — for example the application of retention spaces, from checkdams, swales (ditches), terraces, deep plowing along keylines, or through special forms of land use such as reforestation, ecological agriculture, or through special pasture management (Holistic Management*).

The goal of the work is that no rainwater or wastewater leaves the terrain. Then we will have transformed a landscape into a Water Retention Landscape. All outflowing water should once again be spring water.

At Tamera we have created a series of retention spaces connected with one another (in size from a pond up to the size of a lake), in which the rainwater is collected behind a dam built with natural materials. The retention spaces themselves are not sealed with cement or an artificial liner, so that the water can diffuse slowly and steadily into the body of the earth.

The term "Water Retention Landscape" is always connected with the concept of the healing of nature. The construction of Water Retention Landscapes is an active and effective response to the destruction of nature, which has been carried out at Tamera in intensive collaboration with the permaculture specialist Sepp Holzer of Austria and various visionaries and ecologists from around the world.

There are no regions inhabited by humans that are unsuitable for the construction of Water Retention Landscapes. Everywhere that destroyed or degraded ecosystems exist today, Water Retention Landscapes can and should be created — on every type of soil, in every climate zone, on every hillside, and especially in areas with little rainfall. There they are of particular importance. The less precipitation an area has, and the longer the time between rainy periods, the more urgent the construction of retention landscapes becomes. But in tropical regions rich in rain as well, Water Retention Landscapes are a great step toward healing. The retention spaces replace the delicate humus layer which, after the felling of the tropical forests, is sometimes eroded away completely during a single rainy season. And through their high capacity to absorb water, they also help prevent fatal landslides, which today are caused more and more often by heavy rains. Thus they also save human lives in a direct way.

Perhaps some forested areas still exist on Earth where corrective intervention is not yet necessary, because enough humus is still available. But unfortunately these are today only exceptional cases. A Water Retention Landscape is the healing impulse required by the Earth and all its creatures. And they must and can arise everywhere that people have the courage and the strength, and naturally recover the knowledge, to create them.

For this we now need a certain common force and direction. To be able to create Water Retention Landscapes all over the world, special education centers are required. We have convened the Terra Nova School to spread the information through the Internet and to support groups and initiatives that want to apply this knowledge in their countries. In our vision, so-called "model universities" could be organized autonomously everywhere, where one learns theoretically and practically how to build Water Retention Landscapes. In this way a change in the thinking process begins, which naturally also includes all other aspects of human life. A Water Retention Landscape is only sustainable if individual and social life is once again included in nature and in the higher orders of creation. How this integration functions in modern times, and what technological and social knowledge is needed for it — all of this must be researched and taught in the models, and must be available to all people who seek this knowledge.

The process of change in thinking will finally be complete when there is no longer a single living being on Earth deprived of sufficient water, food, and human compassion.

Knowing the being of water

The first step in the change of thinking begins with water itself. A water retention space must be understood not only in a technical way; it also exists to bring the being of water closer to a new type of engineer. A retention space must be shaped so that the water does not stagnate, but rather is able to move according to its being. Water is not merely a physical or chemical substance that humans can handle according to their convenience, or merely according to industrial norms. Water is a living being. We modern humans must learn to understand this once again in full.

The shaping of water retention spaces is therefore not arbitrary. We observe the water: how does it want to move? What forms does it like on the banks? What temperature, and what temperature difference, does it like? Does it like to form waves or not? All of these aspects are incorporated into our work.

Like all living beings, water also needs the freedom to move according to its being. Water wants to turn, spin, curve and meander. This is how it stays vital and fresh. Through such movements it purifies itself. At the same time it can also calm down and has time to filter into the body of the earth. There are three important principles for shaping this type of retention space:


---


• The longest side of the retention space is, if possible, placed in the same direction as the prevailing wind. The wind then blows across a long surface. Thus it creates waves that oxygenate the water: oxygen is an important element for the purification of water. The wind and waves carry waste particles toward the banks, where they are trapped by aquatic plants, which will ultimately absorb them.

• The banks are never built straight or reinforced, but are created in meandering forms, with steep zones and flat zones, so that the water can turn and spin. At least one part of the bank will be planted with aquatic plants and bank plants. Deep and shallow zones are created. In this way different temperature zones emerge, which provide healthy thermodynamics in the water. Shaded areas at the bank also support this process. Therefore a diversity of aquatic organisms finds suitable habitats.

• The dam of the water retention space is composed entirely of natural materials; no plastic liner or cement is used. The vertical sealing layer of the dam consists of a material as fine as possible — ideally clay. It would be perfect if the material we use is that excavated from the deep zones. It is connected with the impermeable layer of the subsoil, which sometimes lies only a few meters below the surface. The sealing layer is compacted and built layer by layer with fine material of moist earth. Then mixed earth is piled up on both sides, covered with humus or a fertile earth layer, and then designed and used for planting.

Through this natural construction method, the water retention spaces fit into the landscape and are not strangers to their surroundings. A short time later, life reappears on the banks again. Finally the plants, especially the trees, find themselves once more supplied with water from the soil, as is appropriate to their nature. Then we need less and less artificial irrigation from the surface, and finally none at all.

The helping forces

In the construction of Water Retention Landscapes there is an abundance of helping forces from the realm of nature at our side. Knowing this, the new engineers get in contact with these forces and ask for their cooperation.

There are millions and billions of microorganisms that immediately begin their work the moment they realize there is water — even after a


---


rainy season. They are our best allies. Most of them live invisibly in the earth. These beings sense that a sustainable healing process has been initiated, from which everyone benefits. Perhaps for a long time we cannot see their effect, but we must know that they exist and that they quickly begin their work.

Eike Baunroth, an expert in the area of cooperation with nature, describes impressively in his book "Harmonie mit den Natur-wesen" [Harmony with the Beings of Nature] what happens when animals previously considered pests or vermin — and against whom we fought accordingly — are finally considered as allies in cooperation. He describes the example of slugs, aphids, field mice, potato beetles and ticks: "Their abundant presence, their unbridled reproduction, their unstoppable feasts in my garden and their resistance against my tricks opened my senses to a different consciousness of life ... Today they all live an existence free of obstacles in my garden. They have shown me what nature is capable of: unconditional friendship!"

In our ecological work at Tamera, this aspect of cooperation is strongly incorporated. Birds, for example, are necessary collaborators for reforestation, for some seeds must pass through the stomachs of birds in order to be able to germinate. Here lies a fascinating area of work and research.

There are also helping forces that are still little known to us. Through Dhyani Ywahoo, a Cherokee spiritual teacher, we learned that lightning is an important factor in the revitalization of weakened land, once that land is moist again. In her book "Voices of Our Ancestors: Teachings of the Cherokee People," gathered from the Fire of Wisdom, she writes: "When those underground aquifers have been depleted, they will no longer attract the electrical energy of lightning. The activity of lightning is the pulse — just as the nervous system is the pulse that animates your body. So, as those aquifers empty out, there is less and less energy for growing, for life. There are also other, more subtle effects of lightning."

Sepp Holzer has discovered that thunder is also a helping force for the growth of several species of edible mushrooms.

With these examples, we see before us how much fascinating research work is still to be done.

With the construction of Water Retention Landscapes, humanity re-enters into cooperation with the spirit of the Earth, and with the spirit of the plants, animals and human beings who live, or are destined to live, in that space. In creating these systems, it is not only a matter of engineering but of the art of contacting the living, and of recognizing that we humans are not the only beings living on this planet. Creation has been entrusted to us, to perceive it and to care for it. It is the original role of the human being on Earth. Here the ancient knowledge that all indigenous peoples possessed is reawakened and transferred into modern life.

Tamera's Water Retention Landscape

At Tamera, we began in 2007 with the construction of the first water retention space. The proposal came from Sepp Holzer, who had long supported us in the renaturalization and ecological healing of the Tamera terrain. Until then we believed we lived in a dry country. When he showed us the dimensions of the first planned retention space, the question arose of how long it would take to fill that large hole with water. "Lake 1," as it is known today, is located in the center of our terrain. The idea of having to look after a muddy, half-empty pond for years did not motivate us to take this first step toward the planned Water Retention Landscape.

So, to make things clear for ourselves, we had the idea of calculating the annual average precipitation that fell in the catchment area of the retention space. In our minds we filled containers with this water, each with a capacity of one cubic meter, and placed them one after another in a row reaching a length of nearly a thousand kilometers, from Tamera to Barcelona.

That was enough to shake us out of our scarcity mindset.


---


In the same year we began construction. In the first winter, the lake and the adjacent body of earth filled to two-thirds with water. After the second rainy season, which had below-average rainfall, only a few centimeters were missing to reach the high-water level. In the third winter there was so much rain that we could have filled the retention spaces several times over. Today, only four years after beginning the first construction, it is as if there had never been anything but the water retention spaces. Many people who visit Tamera for the first time cannot believe that it is not a natural lake. On the terraces beside the bank, we have created an "edible landscape" where we have planted thousands of fruit trees and shrubs. Some wild animals, such as otters, have settled there. And the birds have returned: we now know 93 different species of birds at Tamera, some of which are very rare species found only in water-rich areas.

Already in the first year a new seepage spring emerged, which since then has been flowing continuously all year round.

The construction of "Lake 1" was only the beginning. Since then we have created a number of additional retention spaces. In 2011 we built a water retention space with approximately three times the capacity of Lake 1. With this construction we have achieved in this valley a radical transformation from a landscape with much water to a Water Retention Landscape: this area is now able to absorb all the precipitation of an average winter.

This large retention space is located at the highest part of the valley. The water pressure is sufficient to irrigate the entire land (for as long as that is still necessary) without having to provide additional energy for pumping. With the water from this retention space situated up high, the water level of the retention spaces downstream remains nearly stable throughout the year.

Here at Tamera we want to show a model of how it could be throughout the Alentejo and, fundamentally, throughout the whole world. Without water there is no life. Said positively: with water there is life. We are increasingly able to see and hold this image, which arises before our eyes, when we ask ourselves: what would it be like if we lived with water, and not without water? How quickly we come to have visions of paradise, and how quickly we can leave the scarcity mindset on all levels!

I would like to conclude with a sentence from Viktor Schauberger. It comes from an essay he wrote in 1934, from the book "Das Wesen des Wasser" (The Being of Water): "Everything originates from water. Therefore water is itself the universal raw material of all culture, or the foundation for all physical or mental development. The discovery of the secret of water is the end of all forms of speculation or intrigue and their excesses, to which belong war, hatred, envy, intolerance and discord of every kind. Deep research into water therefore truly means the end of all monopolies, the end of all domination and


---


the beginning of the emergence of socialism based on the development of individualism in its highest form. If we manage to discover the secret of water, by understanding how water can come into being, then it will be possible to produce water of all qualities in any place, and then we will be able to make vast desert areas fertile; then the sale value of food and also of machinery will fall, since it will no longer be worth speculating with them any longer."

I ask all of you to share this vision. I ask all of you to see how the human being is meant to be, to see what the true position of the human being is, and the role that the creation of models plays in this. A person who takes their human rights into their own hands also takes a stand for the rights of water, as Evo Morales calls for, and will enter into cooperation with nature and its being. When we have found again the inner image of reconnection with nature, then we will begin to understand the sentence:

"Water, energy and food will be freely available to all of humanity when we no longer follow the laws of capital, but the logic of nature."

This is how life is meant to be.

About the author: Bernd Walter Mueller

Born in 1962 in Cologne, Germany. Nature researcher, specialist in the construction of Water Retention Landscapes, permaculturist. Since 2007, co-worker at the Tamera Peace Research Center in Portugal, in close cooperation with Sepp Holzer. Today, Bernd Müller is the director of Tamera's Global Ecology Institute and a teacher at the Global Campus, an international training center for peace workers. In 1986 he abandoned his engineering studies in the traditional university system because he did not find the answers he was seeking. He started his own business, running a health-food store, working in landscape gardening and later as an arborist. In 1989 he emigrated to Spain and managed an organic farm in the Sierra Nevada. There he found the calm necessary to study natural processes through intense observation. He discovered a new, more subtle possibility of cooperation between man and nature. Today he passes on the findings from this process of self-education in the practical development of ecological models for the healing of the landscape and the restoration of the Earth.
