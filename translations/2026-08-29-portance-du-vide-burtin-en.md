## 1. General introduction

### 1.1 Origin of the project

Modern cosmology is crossed by two major enigmas: **dark matter**, invoked to explain galactic rotation curves and structure formation, and **dark energy**, supposed responsible for the acceleration of the expansion of the universe. Although the ΛCDM model succeeds in accounting for a large number of observations, it rests on two hypothetical components that have, to this day, never been directly detected.

This situation, paradoxically stable, is accompanied by growing tensions: the Hubble constant H₀ presents a significant disagreement between local and cosmological measurements, the density profile of dark matter halos is incompatible with observations of spiral galaxies, and the very nature of Λ (cosmological constant) remains philosophically and physically problematic.

It is in this context that we developed the **Φκ model**, a radically different approach based not on the addition of new particles, but on an inertial rereading of the vacuum itself. The founding intuition is simple: **the vacuum possesses a measurable inertial density, and this density, when it interacts with matter in motion, generates a scalar field that modifies the observed dynamics without it being necessary to introduce an invisible mass.**

### 1.2 Objectives of the Φκ model

The Φκ model was designed to meet three major scientific objectives, with a strong falsifiability constraint:
1. **Replace dark matter** with an inertial reaction of the vacuum, induced by the presence of moving baryonic matter, and modeled by a dynamic scalar field Φκ.
2. **Provide an explanation of dark energy** in the form of an emergent quintessence field, whose behavior depends on the spatio-temporal evolution of velocities and baryonic densities in the universe.
3. **Unify galactic and cosmological dynamical effects** within a single fundamental mechanism, without recourse to two distinct theoretical entities.

### 1.3 Methodology adopted

The approach rests on four methodological pillars:
- A strong, testable physical hypothesis: the vacuum possesses an effective inertial density, a function of the presence of moving matter.
- A complete Lagrangian formulation, ensuring compatibility with relativistic covariance principles and allowing integration into the Friedmann equations.
- A rigorous dimensional analysis, to guarantee the naturalness of the couplings and constants introduced.
- A priority given to falsifiable prediction, in particular on galactic rotation curves, the Tully-Fisher relation, the equation of state w(z), and laboratory tests of the atomic interferometry type.

The model was designed with a modular architecture, allowing both local tests (spiral galaxies), cosmological extensions (modified Friedmann equation), and connections to quantum vacuum physics.

### 1.4 Positioning with respect to existing paradigms

Unlike MOND, which modifies dynamics without a universal Lagrangian foundation, and ΛCDM, which postulates two undetected entities, Φκ proposes an alternative anchored in a revisited physics of the vacuum.

The Φκ field is a classical scalar field, but with a non-direct gravitational origin: it emerges from the kinetic structure of baryonic matter in a vacuum endowed with inertially reactive properties. This posture makes it compatible with:
- general relativity (invariant formalism),
- the structure of the quantum vacuum (Casimir effects, fluctuations),
- and galactic and cosmological tests.

Finally, the Φκ model distinguishes itself by a strong will not to add arbitrary degrees of freedom: no new particle, no dark mass, only a dynamics of the vacuum that we had neglected until now.

## 2. Physical foundation: the vacuum as an inertial medium

### 2.1 Hypothesis of vacuum inertial density

We posit here a central hypothesis, both conceptual and physical:

**The vacuum possesses an effective inertial density, variable according to local conditions of velocity and baryonic density.**

Unlike the classical vision of the vacuum as an "absence of matter," we adopt here an active conception: the vacuum is a reactive medium, capable of responding dynamically to the acceleration of matter in its volume.

This inertial density of the vacuum, denoted ρ_eff_vacuum, is not constant but emerges from a kinetic coupling with local baryonic matter, of the form:

**ρ_eff_vacuum ∝ ρ·v²**

where:
- ρ is the local baryonic density,
- v is the local orbital velocity (galaxy),
- the proportionality constant α will be formally introduced in the next section.

### 2.2 Experimental results — the vacuum has a weight

The precise cosmological measurements from the Planck, DES, and BOSS collaborations (2021) give for the energy density of the cosmological vacuum:

**ρΛ = (60.3 ± 1.3) × 10⁻³¹ g/cm³**

This value, associated with dark energy, represents approximately 68% of the total energy content of the universe.

**Conceptual distinction:** the dark energy density ρΛ observed cosmologically differs from the hypothesis of "local inertial density of the vacuum" proposed in the Φκ model. The first is a global property of spacetime, while the second would be a local reaction to matter in motion.

Another well-established experimental datum: the **Casimir effect**, observed since 1948, demonstrates that the vacuum exerts a measurable force between two neutral metal plates, solely from the local reorganization of allowed quantum modes. These results suggest that the vacuum can be treated as a compressible physical medium, endowed with a dynamic topology, sensitive to:
- the geometric configuration,
- the local energy density,
- and the motion of matter.

### 2.3 Dynamic interpretation: compressible fluid analogy

The behavior of the vacuum is here modeled as that of a weakly compressible anisotropic fluid, whose inertial response to a moving baryonic source gives birth to a scalar field Φκ, analogous to a distributed buoyancy.

A galaxy rotating in the vacuum acts like a moving wing in a fluid: it creates an inertial gradient in its environment, perceptible as an additional force.

This force is the macroscopic manifestation of the field Φκ, defined as:

**Φκ ∝ ρ·v²**

This field modifies orbital trajectories as if an invisible force (dark matter type) were present, when it is in fact a deferred inertial reaction of the vacuum.

### 2.4 Positioning within general relativity

The definition of a field Φκ does not contravene the foundations of general relativity:
- It is a classical, non-tensorial scalar field, not modifying the Einstein tensor directly.
- Its coupling is external to geometry, introduced via an additive Lagrangian, and not as a modification of the fundamental equations of GR.
- It can therefore be superposed on a ΛCDM model, or replace it partially or totally, according to the parameters α and µ.

This field is invariant under change of local inertial reference frame (covariant formalism), and its effects are zero in zones of low velocity or negligible density — which makes it compatible with precise observations of the solar system.

**L = ½ ∂^μΦκ ∂_μΦκ − V(Φκ) + L_int**

**L_int = α Φκ·ρ·v²**

This term represents the injection of baryonic kinetic energy into the inertial field of the vacuum, without mass transfer.

Consistent with volumetric energy → the Lagrangian is physically dimensionally valid if α is dimensionless.

The scalar field Φκ introduced in this model aims to capture the inertial buoyancy of the vacuum through a direct coupling to the local kinetic energy density. This hypothesis naturally modifies the expansion dynamics of the universe.

**L_total = L_EH + L_m + L_Φκ + L_int**

**Φ̈κ + 3H Φ̇κ + dV/dΦκ = αρv²**

**H² = (8πG/3)(ρ_m + ρ_r + ρ_Φκ)**

where ρ_m is the matter density, ρ_r the radiation density, and a(t) the scale factor.
