Water — Storage and Transmitter of Information

The distinctive physical properties of water and the numerous short-lived hydrogen bonds between neighboring hydrogen and oxygen atoms in the water molecule create favorable conditions for the formation of special associative structures (clusters) that receive, store, and transmit a wide variety of information. Homeopathy, which has two centuries of experience and is currently experiencing a new stage of development, is based on this property of water. Water, consisting of many clusters of various types, forms a hierarchical spatial liquid-crystalline structure that can receive and store enormous volumes of information. The figure (V.L. Voeikov) shows, as an example, diagrams of several of the simplest cluster structures.

Information carriers can be physical fields of the most diverse nature. Thus, the possibility of remote informational interaction between the liquid-crystalline structure of water and objects of various natures has been established through electromagnetic, acoustic, and other fields. The acting object can also be a human being.

In 1999, S.V. Zenin defended his doctoral dissertation at the Institute of Medical and Biological Problems of the Russian Academy of Sciences, devoted to the memory of water, which constituted a significant milestone in the advancement of this field of research, the complexity of which is heightened by the fact that it lies at the intersection of three sciences: physics, chemistry, and biology.

Based on data obtained through three physicochemical methods — refractometry (S.V. Zenin, B.V. Tyaglov, 1994), high-performance liquid chromatography (S.V. Zenin et al., 1998), and proton magnetic resonance (S.V. Zenin, 1993) — S.V. Zenin constructed and proved a geometric model of the fundamental stable structural formation of water molecules (structured water), and subsequently (S.V. Zenin, 2004) obtained images of these structures using a contrast-phase microscope. The structural unit of such water is a cluster consisting of clathrates, whose nature is determined by long-range Coulomb forces. The structure of the clusters encodes information about the interactions that have occurred with the given water molecules. In water clusters, through the interaction between covalent and hydrogen bonds between oxygen atoms and hydrogen atoms, proton (H+) migration can occur via a relay mechanism, leading to delocalization of the proton within the cluster.

Water is a source of ultra-weak and weak variable electromagnetic radiation. The least chaotic electromagnetic radiation is produced by structured water. In such a case, induction of a corresponding electromagnetic field may occur, altering the structural-informational characteristics of biological objects.

Any system whose level of order exceeds the minimally acceptable 60% begins self-regulatory maintenance of ordered interactions. The higher the cluster content in water, the more ordered its structure, the more capable it is of self-reproduction — which is precisely what is observed in living systems. This indicates that the water of the human organism can perform a system-forming role on the one hand, and a regulatory role on the other.

In S.V. Zenin's laboratory, the effects of human beings on the properties of water were studied. Monitoring was conducted both through changes in physical parameters, primarily changes in the electrical conductivity of water, and through the use of test microorganisms. The studies showed that the sensitivity of the information system of water turned out to be so high that it is capable of sensing the influence not only of various field effects, but also of the shapes of surrounding objects, and the effects of human emotions and thoughts.

The Japanese researcher Masaru Emoto provides even more astonishing evidence of the informational properties of water. He established that no two samples of water form completely identical crystals upon freezing, and that their shape reflects the properties of the water, carrying information about the effects exerted upon it. The technology for obtaining photographs is as follows. Water whose crystals are to be obtained is poured into fifty Petri dishes and placed in a freezer at –25 degrees Celsius. The crystals are then photographed under a microscope in a room where the temperature is constantly maintained at –5 degrees Celsius. A water crystal "lives" under the microscope for an average of no more than two minutes. Since no two crystals are identical among the fifty photographs obtained, the photograph reflecting the most frequently occurring shape is selected.

In Dr. Emoto's laboratory, water samples from various water sources around the world were studied. The water was subjected to various types of influence, such as music, images, electromagnetic radiation from a television or mobile phone, the thoughts of an individual and of groups of people, prayers, and printed and spoken words in different languages. More than fifty thousand such photographs were taken. The photographs presented show examples of such images obtained by Masaru Emoto.

The starting point for Masaru Emoto's research was the work of the American biochemist Lee Lorenzen, who in the 1980s demonstrated that water receives, accumulates, and stores information communicated to it. Emoto began collaborating with Lorenzen. His fundamental idea was the search for ways to visualize the effects obtained. He developed an effective method for producing crystals from water onto which various information had been previously imparted in liquid form through speech, inscriptions on the vessel, music, or through mental intention. Masaru Emoto reported on these findings on March 16, 2004, at a meeting with Polish researchers and journalists at the Institute of Geology in Warsaw. The results caused a sensation. Numerous and diverse experiments, many thousands of photographs, demonstrated that information received by water is perceived and reflected in the geometric structure of the crystals, which serve as its images. Water reacts to the thoughts and emotions of the people around it, to events occurring with the population, and so forth. Crystals formed from freshly obtained distilled water have the simple shape of the well-known hexagonal snowflakes. The accumulation of information changes their structure, complicating and enhancing their beauty if the information is kind, and, conversely, distorting or even destroying the original forms if the information is evil or offensive. Water encodes the information it receives in a nontrivial manner. We must still learn to decode it. But sometimes "curiosities" arise: crystals formed from water that had been placed next to a flower replicated its shape.

Science begins with measurement, with the acquisition of quantitative comparable data. In this sense, the work devoted to studying the memory of water is at its earliest stage. Numerical values that would allow quantitative conclusions are practically impossible to obtain, since the principle of encoding information in the form of geometric images is unknown. Nevertheless, despite this, the results obtained are of significant importance, as they open a new path toward studying one of the most important properties of nature — life itself. The discovery of the Japanese researcher is, in the opinion of many scientists, one of the most sensational made at the turn of the millennium. The first book by Masaru Emoto, "Messages from Water," was published in 2002. It has been translated into many languages of the world, including Russian.

From the work of other authors, certain "threads" also become clearer, which, when pulled, can lead to an understanding of the process by which water perceives and remembers information from its surroundings. These may be, for example, groups of molecules separated from each other by distances of no more than 33 nanometers, which form so-called "bifurcated bonds" in an unstable state. These groups determine the pathways for the development of crystalline structures in water under the influence of external effects. The figure (G.G. These drawings very much resemble holographic structures. And if this is so, then here they are — the receiving antennas that convert incoming information into the structure of liquid and solid crystals of water. Of course, this is only one of the possible hypotheses.

The role of water in nature is enormous, and its properties are unusual and very interesting. It is not surprising that this remarkable substance is the subject of not just a few dozen thousand scientific publications. However, the properties of water, like life itself, are inexhaustible, and many surprises await us yet.

This compels us to once again address the question: what water do we drink? Unfortunately, ordinary tap water contains approximately 12 thousand toxic elements, among which are pesticides, heavy metals, and pathogenic bacteria. According to statistics from the World Health Organization, up to 80% of all diseases are caused by low-quality, and often simply dirty, water that cannot be called drinking water. This compels people to buy drinking water in plastic bottles. But prices for it are rising, and so is the risk of counterfeits. Moreover, water — the most powerful solvent — after standing in a plastic bottle, becomes quite different from what it was when poured.

A necessary household item today has become drinking water filters for purifying tap water. Household filters used for obtaining drinking water can be conventionally divided into three categories — basic, medium-grade, and high-grade purification. The best (high-grade) purification category includes purification by reverse osmosis filters (reverse osmosis systems) — the most advanced and highest-quality technology available today. Basic filters include pitchers and faucet attachments.

But even a very good filter produces water that differs from the water of a good natural spring. No matter how well tap water is filtered, no matter how beautiful the label on the bottle of commercially sold drinking water, if you conduct a simple experiment: take identical bowls, pour this water into one of them, and pour water from a natural good spring into the other — a cat or dog experiencing thirst will invariably drink the spring water.

Therefore, in recent times, work has been underway to imitate spring water as accurately as possible by allowing filtered water to settle on a set of the most beneficial minerals. An example of this is the "Istok BIO" [Source BIO]. The Istok BIO is:
- four levels of filtration;
- a layer of activated carbon, treated with silver, purifies water from chlorine, bacteria, nitrates, petroleum products, phenols, and other organic compounds. It eliminates unpleasant odor and taste.
- a layer of ion-exchange resins removes heavy metal ions from water (lead by 96%, mercury by 98%), and other metals (cadmium by 98%, aluminum by 90%, copper by 98%). It prevents the deposition of hardness salts on heating elements and the walls of water-heating devices (electric kettles, electric coffee makers, electric samovars).
- a layer of silica sand removes acids from the water and improves taste.
- a layer of mineral and coral sand enriches the water with mineral salts and oxygen. It normalizes the pH (acidity) level of the water.
- enrichment of water with beneficial minerals and oxygen;
- normalization of pH acidity level;
- replacement elements with a long service life and replacement every 1-2 years;
- autonomy from water supply and electrical networks;
- storage capacity of 5-10-20 liters of purified water.

Just as activated carbon absorbs all the chemical elements present in its surrounding space until it is fully saturated, so too does water absorb the most diverse information from its surrounding space. In the process, by the time water travels from its source to the consumer, the accumulated information — unfortunately, predominantly negative — turns out to be very substantial. Therefore, the next stage in the development of water sources should presumably be, in addition to chemical purification — informational purification, and the subsequent enrichment with beneficial information — an analog of homeopathy. Just as computers require an antivirus program to ensure safe operation, so too, when drinking water is consumed, devices are needed to detect and destroy harmful information.

The informational health of the biosphere is ensured by the natural water cycle. Recently, due to the pollution of the atmosphere, this function of the water cycle in nature has reversed. The change is exacerbated not only by chemical pollution of the atmosphere, but also by "emotional" informational pollution from a dying biosphere. As a result, it is not life-giving moisture that falls to the earth, but something poisonous requiring purification.

Every drop of water in our body has participated and continues to participate in the cycle of matter. Water is the cradle of life. Let us respect, value, and protect water — one of the chief foundations of our lives.
