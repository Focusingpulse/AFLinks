*Translated from the French of Editions Servranx (servranx.com) — the "Qui sommes-nous" page and the "Origins of Radionics" section of the radionics page. The Servranx brothers were Belgian pioneers of radiesthesia and radionics whose journal, books, and EXDOCIN dossiers shaped the French-language radiesthesia tradition from 1946 to 1967 and beyond.*

## Who Were Félix and William Servranx?

Jean-Louis-Félix, known as Félix, and his brother Guillaume-Jean, known as Willy Servranx, were Belgian and lived in Brussels. Willy, the elder, of fragile health, assisted Félix in all his work. Félix was director of advertising at Fabelta. Together, the two brothers established the SERVRANX BUREAU, located at 11, rue Fossé aux Loups in Brussels, and later at 59, boulevard Anspach from November 1965. It was an advertising agency: market studies, commercial advertising, sales correspondence courses, personnel selection, information on business transfers or sales, and so on. Such were the activities of this bureau.

## The Servranx Brothers and Radiesthesia

The Servranx brothers, passionate about radiesthesia, also launched a specialized journal in this field: *La Radiesthésie pour tous* (Radiesthesia for Everyone), or L.R.P.T. The journal was born in January 1946 and the final issue dates from October 1967. To their credit: consultations, organized meetings, conferences, and numerous books and dossiers:

- *Your Beginnings in Radiesthesia* (Vos débuts en radiesthésie)
- *Reading Character with the Pendulum* (Lecture du caractère au pendule)
- *Express Method of Personal Magnetism*, signed under the pseudonym Apollonius
- *Introduction to Medical Radiesthesia*, signed W. Herrinckx, another pseudonym
- *Radiesthesia Applied to Business* (La radiesthésie appliquée aux affaires)
- *Radiesthetic Materializations* (Matérialisations radiesthésiques)

## Introduction to Radiesthesia

There were also the courses: *Introduction to Radiesthesia in 12 Lessons* and *Practical Course in Radionics and Distant Action* — two complete courses taught by correspondence, now available as albums.

Finally, the EXDOCIN dossiers: EX-periences, DOC-umentation, and IN-structions on the new sciences of interest to radiesthetists — this was the full title given by F. and W. Servranx to this series of monthly dossiers, published from June 1957 to September 1966. The Servranx brothers called upon the best-known radiesthetists in Belgium and beyond to form radiesthesia research groups. The EXDOCIN dossiers are therefore the product of these compiled studies, completed by the Servranx brothers, who wrote them in their entirety.

Among the subjects covered: medical astrology, radiesthetic graphology, how to magnetize yourself, commercial applications of radiesthesia, luck and its techniques, how to manufacture artificial remedies, how to influence people, the Great Pyramid and its influences, the keys of magic, how to increase your radiesthetic sensitivity, words that heal, and so on.

In 60 years, the reputation of the EXDOCINs has only grown. Indeed, they are clear, complete, easy to read, and can be put into practice immediately. It goes without saying that the practical exercises were tested and duly verified by the Servranx brothers. This is documentation of great value, which has its equivalent only in the secret initiations of certain very closed groups.

Of tireless activity, the Servranx brothers, confirmed bachelors, devoted all their time to radiesthesia. The younger, Félix, died on October 30, 1966, after a short illness. His brother, then suffering from paraplegia, survived him by one year; he died on November 13, 1967.

## What Is Radionics?

If you already know radiesthesia, perhaps you ask yourself this question. Perhaps, too, you are poorly informed about this sister discipline of radiesthesia, and why not, you are a bit afraid to use it, or sometimes even to speak of it.

### Radionics: A Bright Future

What is radionics, you may ask? We have explained it often in our works, but for those of our readers who have not yet had the opportunity to take an interest, we will say that radionics — Americans say Psionics or Radionics — is the detection or transmission of influences at a distance, often at very great distances (even to the antipodes), which allows both the diagnosis and treatment of diseases of humans, animals, plants, or soils, as well as remote chemical analyses and, under certain still-experimental conditions, the photography of hidden things (internal elements) or things in evolution.

Radionics is also interested in more or less automatic instruments for prospecting the soil and the environment, in the detection of telluric and cosmic waves, joining electronics on certain points.

Already widely practiced in English-speaking countries, it is poorly known and little widespread in our countries. We have worked to make it known and to provide the construction schematics and operation of the instruments in our works, and we would like to insist here on the very great future of radionics, notably in agriculture. One can predict that within a few years radionics will have its place among normal activation techniques, in agriculture and veterinary art as well as in human medicine.

The construction of radionic instruments has slowly but surely developed and is beginning to become a tool of choice for radionic practitioners who wish to combine them with the use of classic charts.

We are still somewhat in the heroic era of pioneers: all progress, all achievements are the fruit of the research of scattered operators. But what happened with radio and television will undoubtedly happen within a few years for radionics.

However, even when the university professors, who today pull disgusted faces, have seen the profits they can draw from it, even when they have laid their hands on this avant-garde technique, there will always remain a vast field open to the construction of equipment and devices, to the handling of instruments, and to personal experimentation.

Thus far, radionic devices fall into four types:

1. Drawn circuits, a common example of which is the "Herrinckx 4-diamond Emitter chart";
2. Radio-type electronic amplifier devices, most of which are inspired by the research of Boyd, Abrams, and Dr. Drown;
3. Pure radionics devices, consisting of assemblies derived from solenoids (potentiometers, cavities...) operating without any external energy input;
4. Other devices — with prisms, with points, with simple dials — which will likely fall into the previous category or eliminate the other categories when we better understand what radionics is and how it acts.

It is because we believe that this radionics represents one of the aspects of radiesthesia that we speak of it often. It is because we are convinced of the future reserved for this radionics that we disseminate without reservation these principles and teachings.

Not in the form of a dogmatic exposition, but by publishing as often as possible applications and clearly described devices: you will find in our works very simple assemblies, directly usable.

## Origins of Radionics

Perhaps you have heard of Abrams' experiments?

Dr. Abrams (1863–1924), after excellent studies in the United States, came to continue his training in Europe, at the University of Heidelberg in Germany, from which he graduated with all honors and the gold medal. He was one of the most learned physicians of his generation.

By pure chance, as often happens with great discoveries, he noticed that it was possible to diagnose the diseases of one person by auscultating another, under a certain orientation. This transfer, even at such a short distance and in the presence of the patient, struck him vividly. This was the heroic age of radio, and in Glasgow, Boyd maintained that all diseases originated from an electrical imbalance.

While multiplying examinations of patients through a healthy subject, whom he called the "reagent," Abrams was relentless in constructing instruments allowing diagnosis and soon treatment of diseases at a distance, a sort of medical radio that quickly took the name "broadcast treatment."

It was in 1916 that Abrams published the results of his work, which then spread among avant-garde physicians and researchers worldwide. Boyd enthusiastically adopted Abrams' methods; Mac Manis refined them. Then Starr-White, Mac Roberts (to whom we owe the tactile detector), Brunori Guttierez, Simon and O'Connor, W. H. Seymour, and in Moscow, Lazarew — how many others — experimented and created new devices for diagnosis, treatment of diseases, or detection of human waves.

A few years before the last war, Dr. Ruth Drown in the United States and Dr. Guyon Richards in Great Britain developed true instruments for distant diagnosis and treatment, tuning to patients as one tunes a radio set to a distant broadcast.

At very great distances, they recorded spectacular effects, and their clientele extended to the antipodes! It is Ruth Drown's apparatus that, with slight modifications, seems to have prevailed in medical practice and that inspired the devices for all other applications, whether medical or agricultural.

For this technique was not confined to medical practice; it was used to dope racehorses as well as to influence crops, and this sometimes made noise in the English and American press. At the congress of specialists in these questions, held in London in 1950, communications detailed the various applications and let it be foreseen that this would continue to expand.

## Radionics Has Followed Four Paths

Abrams' work has been pursued in four very different orientations:

1. **Electrical devices**, whether for the measurement of human radiations or for treatments by electrical negation. Dr. Leprince devoted fine work to this path of radionics.

2. **Pure adjustment devices**, operating without external energy input. This path was followed by Dr. Drown and the English specialists.

3. **Device schematics and drawings**, of which Bovis and Turenne appear to have been the precursors. The Americans discovered this possibility later and with great astonishment.

4. **Beeswax devices**, of which Bignand developed several applications, and which the engineer Violet also used in his apparatus.

On our side, at Editions Servranx, we have broadly explored all these questions since 1946, in all four orientations. Since 1960, we have focused on making known the possibilities of radionics by adjustment, without energy input. And we have devoted several entire works to specific questions of radionics; the success of these works demands regular re-editions.

The documentation is also rich in English, but many works are out of print, beginning with those of Abrams and Drown, unobtainable today.

## A Definition of Radionics

Among all the definitions sketched by different authors, we propose this one:

> "Radionics is the technique of distant action using devices or other apparatus that do not require psychic influence from the operator."

We have seen that this distant action can operate through true "transmission stations" with adjustable dials, or through stones or water impregnated with certain influences, with instruments of an electrical appearance, or again through forms, drawings...

The word radionics comes to us from Great Britain (Radionic), the technique seeming to have been born in the United States where it is known under the name "Psionics."

### What Force Is at Work?

English specialists have hypothesized a transmission by a primal — primary, if you prefer — force, which would belong to what occultists had called the etheric plane, the etheric bodies.

Americans would rather see it as a mobilization of the "Psi" effect studied by Professor Rhine and his assistants at Duke University.

We would say that several forces make distant action possible: form waves, vital magnetism, Hertzian waves, vertical waves (of Turenne), planetary or zodiacal influences, and even the influence of numbers, as well as thought, nervous force, etc.

There does not seem to be one force at work, but several forces in the action of any given device.

### The Influence of the Operator

Although by definition radionics is an automatic emission, independent of the operator, we increasingly arrive at the conclusion that the (unconscious) action of the operator is not indifferent.

If, very generally, a radionic instrument works regardless of who uses it, the results nevertheless differ and seem to depend partly on the training and the exteriorization capabilities of the operator. In other words, a radionic transmitter will produce a certain influence no matter who sets it in motion; but the influence will be more or less clear, more or less powerful...
