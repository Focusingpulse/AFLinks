Kozyrev's theory rested not on philosophy but on experiment. The scientist conducted laboratory experiments in which he recorded the directionality, density, and active properties of Time. These experiments became the foundation for further research.

Scientific publications by V. L. Pravdivtsev.

Section: The Theory of Time

Time is one of the fundamental concepts of physics and, at the same time, remains one of the greatest mysteries of nature to this day. Back in the 1930s, one of the greatest scientists of his era, Academician V. I. Vernadsky, wrote:

"The science of the 20th century has reached a stage where the moment has come to study time in the same way that matter and energy, which fill space, are studied."

This series of articles examines and analyzes the current state of N. A. Kozyrev's theory of Time, which proposed a fundamentally new approach for science to understanding Time as an active factor of reality.

Particular attention is given to identifying the interconnection between the ideas of the famous astrophysicist and the notions of ancient philosophical and scientific schools regarding the essence and properties of Time.

The material is prepared on the basis of the publication:

"The Kozyrev Theory of Time and Ancient Philosophy" (Part 1)

Author: V. L. Pravdivtsev, Candidate of Technical Sciences, Scientific Director of the Laboratory of Integral Bioinformation Technologies of the science and production company "SFERA"

Laboratory experiments

"Only with the help of simple experiments, which allow visual representation, can one truly understand the essence of Time."

N. A. Kozyrev

N. A. Kozyrev in the laboratory

Kozyrev considered the search for external sources of stellar energy to be one of the main endeavors of his life. He devoted several decades to experiments that were meant to prove his theory. He understood perfectly well the difficulty of experimentally studying Time and the impossibility of investigating it directly. But the scientist was convinced that the properties of Time could be studied indirectly — "through the study of various physical systems and the processes occurring within them." At the same time, Kozyrev rated the role of practical experiments exceptionally highly.

Fortunately, Kozyrev had no problems with creative thinking. He created a whole series of simple yet remarkably ingenious instruments and developed unique methodologies that allowed him to detect unexpected properties of that substance which he called Time.


---


As sensors that made it possible to respond to changes in the flows of Time, he used gyroscopes and pendulums, torsion and vibration balances, thin-film resistors and quartz oscillators, electric thermocouples... He discovered that an unknown type of physical influence could be registered through changes in the viscosity of water and the level of mercury in a thermometer, through changes in the rotational moment of systems, through the speed of chemical reactions, through the parameters of bacterial and plant growth...

For thirty years Kozyrev identified the properties of Time. And he obtained very interesting results.

What properties of Time did N. A. Kozyrev reveal in his experiments?

The course, direction, and density of Time

All processes in nature proceed either with the release or with the absorption of Time.

N. A. Kozyrev

Through his experiments, Kozyrev proved that Time possesses physical properties characteristic specifically of a substance: a course and a direction of flow. It turned out that, like a stream of water, the flow of Time is capable of acting upon objects of our world: exerting force pressure on them, saturating them with energy, flowing around them and reflecting off them.

At the same time, the degree of activity of Time's influence on systems and processes can be greater or lesser. And since Time participates in all processes, they will either speed up or slow down. Kozyrev called this variable property he had discovered the density or intensity of Time.

It was found that in some physical processes Time is absorbed and its density increases. In others, on the contrary, Time is expended, increasing the release of energy. The density of Time thereby falls.

In his experiments, the scientist also discovered that processes in which organization (negentropy, order) is growing decrease the density of Time near the system. A growing (progressing) system, as it were, draws out, absorbs Time from the space around itself. This happens, for example, during the crystallization of a substance when water freezes, during the growth period of plants, biological objects, and so on.

And, conversely, if a system is aging, if chaos (entropy) is increasing within it — that is, a loss of organization is taking place — the density of Time inside it decreases, and around it, correspondingly, it increases. In other words, a dying system radiates more Time into space than it absorbs from it.

Experiments showed that this manifests itself especially clearly wherever entropy increases. For example, in the heating of bodies, the melting of ice, the evaporation of liquids, the dissolution of various substances in water — sugar, for instance — in the mixing of substances, in the withering of plants...

Unfortunately, the scientist did not manage to introduce a quantitative characteristic of the density of Time. But this did not stop him: "It is enough for us at least to prove the qualitative connection," Kozyrev said, and he continued his experiments.

The accumulation and release of Time

As a result of numerous experiments, the scientist came to the conclusion: the course of Time exerts a mechanical action on systems, while the density of Time changes the physical properties of a substance. In what does this manifest itself?


---


Kozyrev believed that Time possesses energy and that under its action the properties of matter can change. In particular, a substance is capable of accumulating and giving off Time. For clarity, one can imagine that all physical material objects in the Universe, immersed in the flow of Time, behave like a sponge immersed in a stream of water. They are, as it were, saturated with the ethereal "water" (Time), and under any deformation — compression, rotation, vibration, destruction, that is, with an increase in entropy — they will give off this "water" into the surrounding environment. In space, the reservoirs that collect the "life principle" are massive cosmic bodies, above all, stars.

Kozyrev's experiments confirmed: bodies are capable of "remembering" the action of Time. Thus, for example, "...A body that had remained for some time near a process and was then brought up to the torsion balance acted upon it in the same way as the process itself. The remembering of the action of processes is characteristic of various substances, with the exception of aluminum" (Kozyrev, 1977).

Kozyrev's experiments showed that objects remember what the density of Time was around them rather quickly — literally within 20-30 seconds. But "forgetting" can take considerably longer — sometimes hours — and it manifests itself in the substance slowly recovering from the "structural deformation" received under the action of a process. At the same time, it turned out that this "memory" depends not so much on the mass of the object as on the density of its substance. The denser the material and the more "springy" it is, the faster it "forgets" the action of a process in which Time was being released. Kozyrev tested these effects on various materials: copper, brass, quartz, glass, air, water, coal, graphite, table salt, and others. It was found, for example, that lead (density 11 g/cm³) remembers the action for 14 seconds, aluminum (density 2.7 g/cm³) for 28 seconds, and wood (density 0.5 g/cm³) for 70 seconds. It also turned out that porous materials, such as brick or volcanic tuff, remember actions the longest.
