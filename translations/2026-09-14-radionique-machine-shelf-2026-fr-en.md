# The 2026 French Radionics Machine Shelf — A Documentary Translation

*Full translation from fr · sources: radioniquepourtous.fr (home + "Nos appareils Radioniques" catalog, retrieved 2026-09-14), biolecher.be (Appareil de radionique BIOLECHER® — autonomie totale, product page, 2024-12-30), anneauxdevie.com (Appareil de radionique, 2025-07-04) · translated by Forge (translation-qc) 2026-09-14*

This dossier translates the current retail face of French-language radionics: the machine shelf as it is actually sold, priced, and described in 2025–2026. Three houses are covered: the Dajafée/Radionique pour tous catalog (nine devices, €290–€2,190), the Belgian BIOLECHER® autonomous emitter with Lecher-antenna tuning, and the Burgundy-made Vibrasaï box from Anneaux de Vie®. The catalog copy is translated faithfully, including its numeric range claims — which are flagged at the end as the shelf's most falsifiable layer.

**Translators' note on terms.** *Radionique* = radionics (influence at a distance via "witnesses" — *témoins*: photos, names, objects — and form-waves, *ondes de forme*). *Témoin* is kept as "witness" throughout. *Ondes de forme* = form-waves, the Chaumery–de Belizal coinage. *Émetteur* = emitter; *récepteur* = receiver.

From the twentieth century onward, the Servranx brothers laid the foundations of a practice in which forms, symbols, and thoughts can emit waves of influence at a distance. Though still on the margins of academic science, it finds an echo in quantum advances and ancestral wisdoms.

## 5. Register notes — what the shelf actually claims (translator's analysis)

1. **The falsifiable numbers.** The Double Decagon claims a range "from 200 to 1,500 km depending on weather conditions" — for a 9V-battery printed-circuit box (222 × 190 × 40 mm). The Tedir claims a "minimum range of 8,000 km" — roughly the Earth's diameter — plus diffusion "via the EDF electrical network." No radionics "field" has ever been instrumentally measured at any of these distances, and no seller publishes a measurement row. These numeric range claims are the shelf's clearest falsification targets: specific, checkable, and attached to ordinary retail objects.
2. **The autonomy paradox.** The shelf markets two mutually complementary postures: devices that *require* mains power and night-only operation to "avoid electromagnetic disturbances" (Brocéliande), and devices whose selling point is *total* electrical independence (BIOLECHER, Anneaux de Vie). Both postures are presented as advantages over the same imagined rival.
3. **The instrumentable seam.** The BIOLECHER device is the one entry on the shelf whose tuning knob could plausibly produce a number on a bench: a Lecher-antenna read before/after tuning would emit a *measurement*, where every other device emits a story. The same house's editorial boundary-statement (insects/plants out of scope, Servranx never claimed it) makes it the lane's most honest actor.
4. **Lineage stacking.** The shelf names its ancestors openly: Servranx brothers (the house doctrine page), Dr D.R.J. Winsfield + Raphaël Dajafée (Winsfield), Dom Bernadin pentacles (Hermès Shield), Teilhard de Chardin / Durville / Lakhovsky (Anneaux de Vie), Saint Hildegarde remedies (Winsfield). The commercial 2026 layer presents itself as the direct continuation of the French-Belgian radionics tradition this corpus documents from its primary sources (Servranx, Chaumery–de Belizal, Enel, Ravatin).
5. **Regulatory posture.** Every device carries a compliance formula ("complement to traditional medicine," "use remains the responsibility of each person," "gives no diagnosis") — matching the Miviludes regulatory environment in which health/well-being is the #1 surveillance category. The disclaimers are standardized; the claims (disease search, remedy transmission) are not.
