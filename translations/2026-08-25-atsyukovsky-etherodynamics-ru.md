### Introduction (Key Passages)

> "The fifth book of the five-volume series 'Foundations of Etherdynamic Natural Science' describes experiments performed by various researchers that confirmed the tenets of ether dynamics. Some experiments were motivated by the author's desire to verify theoretical propositions of ether dynamics; others were conducted independently, but their results could only be explained by invoking etherdynamic concepts."

> "It should be noted that agreement between experimental results and theoretical predictions does not mean the theory is correct — it only means the theory does not contradict those particular experiments."

> "The so-called 'null' result of Michelson and Morley's 1881–1887 experiments, in which they attempted to detect the ether wind in a basement room and obtained a 'null result' (in reality — indeterminate), served as the basis for A. Einstein's creation of the Special Theory of Relativity and led physicists to believe that the ether does not exist in nature. This deprived physical interaction fields of their material carrier of interaction energy and led all of physics into a dead end — despite the fact that Einstein himself later acknowledged the necessity of the ether (1920, 1924)."

> "Ether dynamics is the result of strict logic based not on postulates, 'principles,' and axioms, but on the analysis and generalization of widely known facts verified throughout the course of natural science. These include the fact of the materiality of all objectively existing objects and processes, the fact of the existence of space in which all objects are placed, and the fact of the existence of time encompassing all processes."

### Chapter 1 — The Ether Wind (Key Passages)

> "The history of ether wind research is one of the most tangled stories in modern natural science. The significance of ether wind studies goes far beyond the investigation of a single physical phenomenon: the results of the first works in this direction had a decisive influence on all of 20th-century natural science."

> "It turned out that the direction of this wind does not coincide with the direction of the Earth's motion, as initially assumed, but is almost perpendicular to it. It was found that the orbital component of the Earth's velocity is barely noticeable against the background of the large cosmic velocity of the ether flowing around the Solar System."

On Michelson's 1881 experiment:

> "The obtained results, however, are shown more clearly when the actual curve is plotted together with the theoretical curve. [...] These results can be interpreted (! — V.A.) as the absence of fringe displacement. The result of the stationary ether hypothesis thus turns out to be incorrect, whence it follows: this hypothesis is erroneous. [...] we will not be inclined to believe without explicit confirmation that the ether moves freely through the solid mass of the Earth."
> — A. Michelson, "On the Relative Motion of the Earth and the Luminiferous Ether," 1881

On the Michelson-Morley 1887 experiment:

> "It seems entirely fair to conclude from the figure that if there is any displacement due to the relative motion of the Earth and the luminiferous ether, it cannot be more than 1/100 of the distance between fringes."

Atsyukovsky's analysis:

> "As can be seen from the graph, the second harmonic corresponding to the ether wind is quite clearly visible. [...] In the Michelson-Morley experiment, the existence of an ether wind with a velocity of 3 to 6 km/s was demonstrated, which did not correspond to the 'theoretical' value of 30 km/s, but was by no means a 'null' result."

On Dayton Miller's work:

> "By 1933, when Miller's research was completed, the school of relativists — followers of Einstein's special theory of relativity — stood firmly on its feet and vigilantly ensured that nothing could shake its foundations."

> "Miller's results were classified as 'unrecognized.' This was also facilitated by results of some experiments in which their authors, without wishing to, made errors. [...] One such error was shielding the interferometer with a metal enclosure. Metal, as it turned out, reflects not only electromagnetic waves but also any ether streams — so measuring ether flow velocity in a closed metal box is like trying to measure the wind blowing outside by looking at an anemometer installed in a tightly sealed room."

### Chapter 6 — Energy from the Ether (Key Passages)

> "It should be noted that there are currently many devices whose output energy exceeds input energy. Most authors simply record this fact and fend off criticism from those who doubt the reliability of the results."

> "In recent years, thanks to the work of Georgian inventor Tariel Kapanadze, working prototypes of electric energy generators of 3, 5, and 100 kW have been created — in the latter case as three-phase 220V 50Hz voltage, which can already be used in industry. The device is started from a 9V 'Krona' battery, which is then disconnected. Positive feedback has been implemented, allowing the system to be self-sustaining."

> "Despite the successes achieved by many authors in creating devices with efficiency greater than one, practically none of them have an explanation for the effects they obtain. Ether dynamics puts everything in its place, offering its explanation: the additional energy is obtained from the ether surrounding the device by creating ether vortices in the surrounding space, which supply this additional energy."

> "The process of energy concentration in space is one of the most important effects in physics, since it is precisely through this process that the eternal existence of the Universe is ensured, in which physical processes never end, transitioning from one form to another: energy dissipates in space according to the Second Law of Thermodynamics, and concentrates according to the aforementioned process. An eternal circulation of energy occurs, and it will never cease."

### Appendix 3 — Critical Analysis of Relativity (Summary)

Atsyukovsky provides a critical analysis arguing that experiments commonly cited as confirmations of Einstein's Theory of Relativity are not, in fact, unambiguous confirmations. He argues that any experiment can be interpreted in countless ways that do not require accepting the Theory of Relativity, and that the Lorentz transformations — which Einstein's theory uses — were originally derived from the concept of an ether. He calls for renewed experimental investigation of the ether wind using modern methods (laser beam deflection, automated recording, satellite-mounted instruments).
