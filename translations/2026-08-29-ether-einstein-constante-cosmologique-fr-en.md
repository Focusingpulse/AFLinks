# Einstein, the Ether, and the Cosmological Constant

*By Gaetana, originally published 27 February 2019 (updated 9 July 2023) on Ma vie quantique.*

---

"We are the children of the ether, as fish are the children of water." [1]

## From the Ether to Space

The concept of a holographic universe is present in the background of the narratives of many ancient traditions. These traditions report the existence of a non-manifested substance preceding manifested creation. It can take different names depending on the tradition. It always bears the same characteristics, however: it fills space, creates or even perpetually recreates matter, permeates everything, unifies all.

Classical physics at one time considered the existence of a substance that would fulfill such a unifying function: the ether. The purpose of this article is to examine how theories of physics have evolved the concept of ether over time, and the implications this has had on our representation of the world.

My experience tells me that everything is connected (see My Story). The links that made my experience what it was, and the way these links were created, are explained in the article "Is the Universe Deterministic?" There I highlight the circulation of information, in what some would call the ether, or in what Nassim Haramein calls space. My purpose here is to see how this physicist has in a sense rehabilitated the concept of ether, which underlies his unified field theory.

## The Ether in Ancient Traditions

The foundational texts of Hinduism, the Upanishads, speak of a primordial substance called Prakriti. From this substance all physical existence emanates and returns. According to these texts there is also a second level of manifestation, the ether. It is described as an invisible substance present in all things. By densifying through material manifestations, the ether becomes visible. It produces movement, then identified with prana.

For the Native Americans, the ether would be the grandmother spider. She would weave from the origins of the world the web of the universe, and ensure its unity. A metaphor also known in Buddhist philosophy, under the name of Indra's Net. It is a multidimensional net whose each node is adorned with a jewel that reflects in the others. Thus, each jewel contains the reflection of all the jewels, infinitely... as in a holographic universe. Indra's Net illustrates for Buddhists the concept of emptiness, or the dependent arising of phenomena [2]. For them, everything has an influence on everything, even if one is not aware of it because the threads of the universal web are invisible.

## The Ether: From Classical Physics to Quantum Physics

Classical physics took up the concept of ether, but diverted it. Physicists were seeking, on one hand, a representation of forces that act at a distance, such as gravitation. And on the other hand, a representation of the supposed medium that propagates light. In doing so they moved away from the original context of the ether by giving it a highly debatable status, since they made it material... while not including it in the equations...

In fact, from Newton to Einstein and up to relativistic quantum physics in 1930, physicists had contradictory attitudes toward the ether. They declared successively that it must exist, then that it did not exist, then that it might exist but since the equations worked without it, there was no need to take it into account. Except that in the latter two cases, by retaining the original reference to the ether, they induced the idea that things are separate from one another. And this idea has fundamental implications for how we consider our relationship to ourselves, to others, and to the universe.

### Quantum Field Theory

In 1930, relativistic quantum physics develops. The quantum field theory that follows from these advances aims to introduce into quantum mechanics the concepts of Special Relativity proposed by Einstein.

The quantum world then becomes a sea of dynamic energy. It teems with ephemeral particles in perpetual creation and dissolution. The vacuum is no longer empty -- as was believed until then -- but filled with electromagnetic energy fields. These retain a residual energy when the temperature drops to absolute zero [3]. An energy called zero-point energy or vacuum energy. It represents the energy that remains when all other forms of energy have been removed.

It is, in short, a form of energy recalling the properties of the ether, even if it is not named as such. We speak of quantum vacuum fluctuations. The calculation shows that the total zero-point energy is infinite. How, then, to use it in calculations?

Physicists use a process of renormalization that makes this infinite value finite. However, if the zero-point energy is no longer infinite, it remains gigantic since its density is 10^93 g/cm3. That is, 10 followed by 93 zeros...!

In parallel with these developments, other discoveries are made at the cosmological level: in 1922, the Russian physicist Alexander Friedmann publishes the founding article demonstrating the expansion of the universe. And in 1998, two international teams of physicists [4] demonstrate that this expansion is accelerating. In order to describe the force that accounts for this phenomenon, they are led to suppose -- without it having been detected to date -- the existence of a new energy: dark energy. This behaves like a force that opposes gravitational attraction, thus explaining the acceleration of the universe's expansion.

## Dark Energy and Einstein's Cosmological Constant

"Historically, the only (hypothetical) form of energy behaving like [dark] energy was the cosmological constant, proposed in a different context by Albert Einstein in 1916 (...). Einstein's initial motivation remained, however, quite far from those that motivate current interest in [dark] energy. In 1916, a date at which the expansion of the universe was not known, Albert Einstein considered that the universe must be static, and so he needed to introduce a new force opposing gravitational attraction. The ideal candidate was found with the cosmological constant, which allowed, under very particular conditions, to exactly counterbalance the attractive effect of the gravitational force." [5]

When the expansion of the universe was discovered, Einstein went back on the addition of this parameter. Nevertheless, some physicists consider that dark energy would correspond to the average energy density of the vacuum on cosmological scales, a density modeled by the cosmological constant postulated by Einstein.

In practice, it is not so simple, because the estimated value of dark energy is 10^-29 g/cm3, a difference of 122 orders of magnitude compared to the quantum vacuum energy (10^93 g/cm3). This prediction is moreover known as the worst prediction ever made in physics: the vacuum catastrophe. And it has destroyed all hope of linking the quantum scale and the cosmological scale through a form of energy with properties close to those of the ether.

## Cosmological Constant = Quantum Vacuum Energy

In 2011, however, Nassim Haramein demonstrated that the value of the quantum vacuum energy density and that of the cosmological constant were both correct. They are simply unequal in appearance because they are expressed on two different scales. You can read the article "Quantum Gravity and the Schwarzschild Proton" to understand how they are linked, and ultimately, the fact that dark matter does not exist [6].

In Nassim Haramein's world, ultimately, everything happens as if the ether existed. Let us recall that originally, for physicists, the ether was supposed to make light waves travel. On one hand these physicists presupposed the uniform rectilinear motion of light in a vacuum. And on the other hand they needed a material ether to try to account for this motion.

## Everything Is Connected

Nassim Haramein shows that in fact, the true movement of light is not what we perceive (see the article "Movement and Perception"). In this sense, we could say that light does not travel. Then there are two possibilities: either we realize that it does not travel, and consequently the question of a material ether has no reason to be. Or we are deceived by our observations -- because we ignore the dynamics truly at work in the universe -- and believe that light travels. Since we do not detect the presence of a material ether, we arrive at the incorrect conclusion that things are not connected to each other.

But this deduction is erroneous not because things effectively have no link between them, but because the starting postulates -- light travels and the ether is material -- are incorrect.

Nassim Haramein considers that everything in the universe is connected. However, he does not speak of ether. He does not need to. He simply says that everything is connected by space, the only element common to all scales. Since he shows that light does not travel, this cuts short all speculation about the ether. This also avoids referring to the entire explanation we have just discussed.

Ultimately, he proposes a unification theory that does not call upon the material ether of certain physicists while revealing the immaterial ether of ancient traditions!
