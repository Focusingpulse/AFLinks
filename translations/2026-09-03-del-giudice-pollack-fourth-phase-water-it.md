# Emilio Del Giudice Explains Gerald Pollack's Discovery of the Fourth Phase of Water

The Italian physicist Emilio Del Giudice, in this lecture, speaks about a property of water discovered by Gerald Pollack. A discovery that clarifies one of the greatest mysteries of biology, already highlighted in the 1950s by the Nobel Prize winner for medicine Albert Szent-Györgyi.

Let us give a brief summary. In living matter, an enormous quantity of chemical reactions take place. These are possible only if some substance is capable of releasing electrons. But which of the substances present in living matter is capable of releasing them? That is the mystery. Györgyi thinks it is water. Yet it takes 12.6 electronvolts to release an electron from a water molecule. This requires a level of energy (nuclear or thermal) incompatible with life. Where do the electrons come from, then, that are needed for the chemical reactions that constantly occur in the body? As Albert Szent-Györgyi intuited, they come from water itself.

Here is Emilio Del Giudice's account. In the early 2000s, the American physicist Gerald Pollack of the University of Seattle experimentally observed that water near surfaces, to a depth of several microns, is water that has different properties from bulk water and is capable of easily yielding electrons. The water molecule, when isolated, requires 12.6 electronvolts to release an electron, but the same is not true when this same molecule is together with other water molecules and when it is near a surface.

We must remember that in living matter everything is organized through membranes and that there is no point in our organism that is more than one millionth of a centimeter from some membrane. This means that there is no water molecule (99% of all molecules present in the body are water) that is more than one millionth of a centimeter from a membrane. Pollack thus demonstrates that water near a membrane has a different nature and that it is sufficient to place the end of a pipe in the water to observe how the water that comes out is different from the water that remains inside. The water that is near surfaces is called the "fourth phase of water" or "EZ water" (Exclusion Zone water), because it excludes many substances, including micro-particles and dissolved molecules.

This discovery has enormous biological significance, because it finally explains where the electrons needed for the chemical reactions of life come from. Water near membranes, being in its fourth phase, can release electrons with very little energy. This is the mechanism that makes life possible.
