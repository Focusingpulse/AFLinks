Introduction to Free Energy, fundamentals.

"Free energy" is also known as the energy extracted from the quantum vacuum or the zero-point field; it is the "energy of space." James Maxwell, in the 19th century, believed that for light to propagate it must necessarily require a medium in which to do so, which he called the "luminiferous aether," also understood as "ether." The ether is called the fifth element, after water, fire, earth and air. The ether is also known in Sanskrit as akasha.

With the birth of quantum physics at the start of the 20th century (see chronology of the evolution of quantum physics), at the hands of physicists such as Max Planck, Niels Bohr, Werner Heisenberg, Erwin Schrödinger, Albert Einstein, among others, the world as it had been understood underwent a drastic change. The ultimate building blocks that construct the reality in which we live are no longer atoms, but in fact the quantum vacuum. This is so because atoms subdivide into particles called subatomic particles. The best known are electrons, neutrons and protons, but these in turn subdivide further, and these further still, until one reaches a point where we find ourselves with the vacuum. So the table my computer sits on, the computer itself, my body, and everything around me is in reality constructed of vacuum. What is more, the ratio between the nucleus of an atom and an electron is approximately 40 km, which means that all that vast space is "empty."

Nevertheless, such a vacuum is not empty; in fact it is full — full of energy. Einstein's famous equation E (energy) = M (matter) * C² (light) [E=m*c²] entails that all observable matter is equivalent to light and light to energy, or in other words, that all matter is energy and all energy is light. That is, in everyday terms, my table, my computer and I are matter, but at the same time also energy, or materialized light. This quantum vacuum is found at the level of the Planck constants, which define time, space, temperature and mass. Space is reduced to 10⁻³⁵ meters — that is, 0.0… with thirty-four zeros followed by a 1, 0.000000….01. And in seconds, it is equivalent to 10⁻⁴⁴. Very, very small.

As the nuclear engineer Thomas E. Bearden explains in the documentary "The Race to Zero Point," the density of the quantum vacuum is on the order of 10¹⁰⁸ Joules/cm³.

The Joule (J) is the International System unit for energy, work and heat. It is defined as the work done by a force of 1 newton over a displacement of 1 meter. Well then, the energy in Joules found in one cm³ is enormous — nothing more and nothing less than 10¹⁰⁸. That is a 10 followed by 107 zeros; an astronomical number. But to give us an idea of the energy these numbers carry, if the energy contained in 1 cm³ were materialized, it would be more matter than all of the observable universe. What is more, with the energy contained in 1 m³, according to the great physicist Richard Feynman, one could boil all the oceans of the planet, or supply the planet with energy for a day.

The presence of such energy is confirmed by the Casimir Effect. This effect is a physical force exerted between separated objects due to the resonance produced between the energy fields of the space between them. The simplest experimental evidence for the existence of zero-point energy in quantum field theory is the Casimir Effect. This effect was proposed in 1948 by the Dutch physicist Hendrik B. G. Casimir.

It is also important to add that the laws governing the quantum world are not the laws of the macroscopic level, the world we see with our eyes. Or at least, the macroscopic world operates only in part with a set of laws very different from those that emerge from the subatomic world. I say only in part, because the macroscopic world is constituted of the microscopic world and it would make no sense to separate them, since one is contained within the other. This great difference is a complete change of paradigm. The quantum paradigm leaves behind the so-called Newtonian view, in which reality is observed as objective (separate from the subject observing it); it views the universe as a gigantic clockwork machine in which, if all its pieces were known, all events could be predicted. This idea leads us to think deterministically, to think that freedom does not exist, since one day — supposedly in the Big Bang — all matter together with time began, and since then everything has been evolving; therefore, everything is already determined by causes and effects.

According to this Newtonian-Determinist-Cartesian vision, the individual's Consciousness — our consciousness, the capacity to become aware of our experience — is alien to the world around us. In other words, our consciousness does not influence at all the reality in which we are immersed. Nevertheless, this entire paradigm upon which our society, economy, social values, etc. are based is seriously called into question by the new quantum paradigm. As I said above, the laws of the subatomic level are very different from the laws of the macro level. Among them we find the following: Werner Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle, according to which the quantum world is a sea of probabilities, in which, when consciousness enters — the observer — and decides, one possibility out of many "collapses," materializing into reality. I use the word "collapses" because, according to quantum physics, the wave functions of the quantum sea become particles when the observer comes into play. This means that everything we see exists because there is someone who has imagined (thought) it and is looking at it.

This also entails that reality is not deterministic, but rather the future is a sea of possibilities (probabilities), in which the individual's consciousness plays a very important, or crucial, role in the way situations unfold. We can change, with our consciousness, the reality in which we live, because in fact it is our consciousness that creates reality. This is confirmed — and since we are at Exopolitics, let us say it — by the various testimonies of extraterrestrial contactees. Among them, Alex Collier, who in this interview speaks of the Andromedans, and of the fourth and fifth dimension (or density) of consciousness, in which thought instantly creates what is thought.

This is scientifically confirmed by experiments such as the Double Slit, in which the observer conditions the result of the experiment. The scientific discussion began when the Dutch physicist Christiaan Huygens, in 1680, sought to demonstrate that light was transmitted in waves; later, in 1704, Isaac Newton refuted this, defending the thesis that light traveled in particles and not in waves as Huygens had said. A century later, the physicist Thomas Young again postulated that light propagated in waves and demonstrated it by proposing the famous double-slit experiment (see video of the experiment, very pedagogical and graphic). An experiment with which a new question was opened: what role does the human observer play in reality?

Another principle is the Principle of Non-Locality, that is, the possibility of action at a distance; in English it is called "entangled." In 1983, Dr. Alain Aspect (of the Orsay Institute of Theoretical Optics) reported an experiment with photons that violated John Bell's inequalities. In fact it violated the principles established by Einstein — that nothing could travel faster than the speed of light, and that any particle accelerated to that speed would require great mass and great energy. However, Aspect, with his experiment, confirmed the non-locality of the universe at the subatomic particle level. These particles seem to exchange information at speeds greater than light through connections that have been called "mysterious." Although, what really happens is that two photons emitted at the same time (in his experiment) must be considered as a single quantum state, as a reality expressed by a single wave function. Einstein and his colleagues Podolsky and Rosen refused to accept the mysterious correlation between particles. They thought there must be hidden variables (not yet observed) that would distinguish these apparently identical systems (the EPR paradox). Aspect's experiment closed, forever, the old paradox in favor of quantum mechanics.

This principle, translated, means that all the matter of the universe is united, which gives rise to the possibility that from my consciousness I can affect someone or something at great distances. But it not only allows affecting the space variable but also the time variable, since consciousness can go backward or forward. This is well confirmed by all the research that has been carried out with so-called "remote viewing." It is the vision that allows one to observe, from an altered state of consciousness, places physically inaccessible — for example other planets, galaxies, or, as it has mostly been used, for espionage.

This brief introduction is only an introduction to the world of quantum physics and to the energy implicit in this quantum sea. This new paradigm opens up infinite possibilities from many perspectives. In our case, in relation to the subject of Energy, the basis of all economy. The question is: can we capture the energy of the zero-point field or of the quantum vacuum? That is the question. If so — and it is so — it means we have energy to make a civilization, ours, run in a free and infinite manner. But as we shall see later, it allows us to be independent, as individuals or as a community, from the great multinational oil, gas and electricity companies. Being able to capture the energy of the quantum vacuum will allow us, among many other things, to travel through space and open up its exploration.
