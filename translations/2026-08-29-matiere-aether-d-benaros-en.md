# Matter and Æther D, Two Regimes of the Same State of Cohesion: Cosmic Nucleus, Cosmological Constant, and the Link with Hawking (Silentium, Pillar I)

*Pierre Benaros, Cannes, June 2026. Working version v3.2 — harmonized base + additions (J_final = 0, memory, reading of D). Translated from the French by Forge.*

## Nature of this document

This document is not a finished theory. It constitutes an evolving working document, intended to formalize the first pillar of the Silentium scenario. It assembles equations, hypotheses, estimates, internal tensions, and a research program. Certain proposals already have a quantitative form; others remain interpretative or speculative. None is presented here as definitively established.

The ambition of Pillar I is simple: to propose a coherent structure linking matter, Æther D, the fundamental state E, the Cosmic Nucleus, the cosmological constant, the factor D, Hawking radiation and, at the local scale, the cohesion field C(r).

This text takes up the most solid structure of the recent version of Pillar I, while reinjecting several important conceptual elements of the previous version: the distinction between maximal cohesion and minimal volume, the explicit comparison with general relativity, the argued progression toward the diameter of the Cosmic Nucleus, the distinction between chronological age and energy cycle, as well as a first memorial reading of the factor D. The present version v3.2 further adds three stabilized elements: the condition of global zero angular momentum J_final = 0, a structured section on the memory of the Cosmic Nucleus, and a dimensional reading of the tension on D.

## Introduction

Pillar I rests on a simple but structuring hypothesis:

**Matter and Æther D are two regimes of the same energetic reality.**

One concentrates energy; the other distributes it. One corresponds to the cohesive regime visible in the form of mass; the other to the diluted regime of the cosmological continuum. The Silentium model hypothesizes that these two branches are not foreign to one another, but that they converge toward a same fundamental state, denoted E.

The objective of the present document is to explore the consequences of this symmetry:
1. define the two energy branches and their common limit state;
2. propose a non-singular reading of the Cosmic Nucleus;
3. relate this Nucleus to a finite maximal density ρ_max_E;
4. interpret the cosmological constant as residual curvature of an initial state of cohesion;
5. introduce the factor D as a measure of the dilution of the Nucleus in the continuum;
6. examine the possible link between this architecture and Hawking radiation;
7. finally relate this global structure to the local cohesion field C(r), introduced in the Research Note on emergent gravity.

This document does not aim to propose a finished theory of cosmology or gravitation. It rather seeks to bring out a coherent energetic framework, sufficiently precise to be discussed, corrected, or contradicted.

## Chapter 1 — The fundamental energy symmetry

### 1.1 The founding equation

Pillar I starts from the idea that the two great forms of cosmic energy can be written from different natural variables, while referring to a same underlying structure.

For matter:

**E_M = mc² (1.1)**

For Æther D:

**E_Æ = N·V_c (1.2)**

where:
- m denotes the mass in the matter branch;
- N denotes a density or energy quantity proper to the Æther D regime;
- V_c denotes a volume or characteristic volume associated with this regime.

The central point here is not the final definition of N or V_c, but the fact that the model authorizes two distinct energy writings corresponding to two regimes of a same reality:
- matter: concentrated energy;
- Æther D: diluted / transmitted energy in the continuum.

Pillar I does not yet affirm that these two equations are rigorously derived from a common formalism. It affirms more modestly that they describe two complementary ways for energy to exist, concentrate, and redistribute in cosmic history.

### 1.2 Natural variables of each state

| State | Natural variables | Equation |
|-------|-------------------|----------|
| Matter | m, c | E = mc² |
| Electromagnetism | λ, ν, c | E = hν |
| Æther D | N, V_c | E = N·V_c |
| Cohesion E | unknowns to specify | research program |

This table is not a definitive taxonomy of the states of energy. It serves to make visible a simple hypothesis: as one moves away from the usual descriptions of matter and radiation, there could exist a regime in which energy is no longer described primarily by mass or frequency, but by a structure of transmission in the continuum.

### 1.3 Track of transition

An open question remains: could the border between the electromagnetic state and the Æther D state correspond to a transition of the type

**(λ, ν, c) → (N, V_c)?**

If this were the case, this border could be linked to a critical threshold of the continuum, perhaps close to the critical gradient G_crit introduced later. This idea remains speculative, but it defines a research direction: understanding how a description founded on frequency and propagation could switch toward a description founded on the cohesive transmission of the continuum. It also leaves open the question of an intermediate state, or a dynamic border, between electromagnetism, matter, and the Æther D continuum.

## Chapter 2 — The fundamental energy state E

### 2.1 Definition and properties

The symbol E denotes the state of maximal energetic cohesion of the Silentium model. The two energy branches converge toward it:

**M → E + ε_M (2.1)**
**Æ → E + ε_Æ (2.2)**

where ε_M and ε_Æ denote the residual exceptions associated with each branch.

Thus, matter and Æther D are not conceived as two independent ultimate realities, but as two distinct trajectories toward a same state of cohesion.

**Properties attributed to E:**
- **Incompressibility:** E is not supposed to be compressible to infinity. The model replaces the perfect singularity by a cohesion limit.
- **Maximal cohesion:** E represents a limit state of cohesive energetic concentration.
- **Minimal exception:** in accordance with the First Law of the Exception, no state is absolutely perfect; a minimal asymmetry always subsists.

**Central conceptual point: maximal cohesion does not necessarily imply minimal volume.**

This sentence plays a fundamental role in Pillar I. The Cosmic Nucleus is not conceived as a point of zero volume, but as a state of extreme cohesion still endowed with a finite extension. It is one of the clearest divergences between Silentium and the classical reading of the singularity.

In other words, Pillar I does not replace a singularity by a simple "big black hole." It proposes another reading of the final state: not an object of minimal size, but a state of maximal cohesion compatible with an extended structure.

### 2.2 Density limit of E — calculation

If the Cosmic Nucleus constitutes the final state of cohesion of the cycle, then an estimation of the maximal density associated with E can be obtained by relating the total energy of the system to the volume of the Nucleus:

**ρ_max_E ≈ E_total / V_NC = (E_M + E_Æ) / (4/3πR³_NC) (2.3)**

**Working hypotheses:**
- diameter of the Cosmic Nucleus: D_NC = 500,000 ly ⇒ R_NC = 250,000 ly ≈ 2.365 × 10²¹ m
- observable baryonic mass: M_baryons ≈ 10⁵³ kg
- current dark energy density: ρ_Λ ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ J/m³

These hypotheses do not all have the same status. The observable baryonic mass and the order of magnitude of ρ_Λ refer to known cosmological estimates. The diameter of the Cosmic Nucleus, in contrast, remains at this stage a working hypothesis and not a derived quantity of the model.

#### 2.2.1 Matter branch
The energy of the matter branch is estimated by:

**E_M = M_baryons c² ≈ 10⁵³ × (3 × 10⁸)² ≈ 9.0 × 10⁶⁹ J (2.4)**

#### 2.2.2 Æther D branch
The volume of the observable Universe is approximated by:

**V_obs = 4/3πR³_obs ≈ 3.45 × 10⁸⁰ m³ (2.5)**
with R_obs ≈ 46 × 10⁹ light-years.

The energy associated with the Æther D branch then equals:

**E_Æ = ρ_Λ V_obs ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 3.45 × 10⁸⁰ ≈ 2.07 × 10⁷¹ J (2.6)**

**Ratio of the two branches:** E_Æ / E_M ≈ 23 (2.7)

The Æther D branch therefore dominates the matter branch today by a factor of about 23. In this framework, the final Cosmic Nucleus would not be primarily a residue of reconcentrated matter, but a state of Æther D reconverted into cohesion.

#### 2.2.3 Maximal cohesion density
The total energy equals:

**E_total = E_M + E_Æ ≈ 9.0 × 10⁶⁹ + 2.07 × 10⁷¹ ≈ 2.16 × 10⁷¹ J (2.8)**

The volume of the Nucleus is:

**V_NC = 4/3πR³_NC ≈ 4/3π(2.365 × 10²¹)³ ≈ 5.54 × 10⁶⁴ m³ (2.9)**

Hence:

**ρ_max_E ≈ 2.16 × 10⁷¹ / 5.54 × 10⁶⁴ ≈ 3.9 × 10⁶ J/m³ (2.10)**

#### 2.2.4 Comparisons

| State | Energy density | Ratio with ρ_max_E |
|-------|----------------|---------------------|
| Current dark energy ρ_Λ | 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ J/m³ | ×6.5 × 10¹⁵ |
| Cosmic Nucleus ρ_max_E | 3.9 × 10⁶ J/m³ | 1 |
| Nuclear density | ~2 × 10³⁴ J/m³ | ÷5 × 10²⁷ |
| Planck density | ~10¹¹³ J/m³ | ÷10¹⁰⁷ |

The Cosmic Nucleus is therefore about 6.5 × 10¹⁵ times denser than current dark energy, while remaining immensely less dense than an atomic nucleus or a Planck density. In Silentium, it is thus not a singularity, but a state of extended cohesion at finite maximal density.

## Chapter 3 — The Cosmic Nucleus

### 3.1 Definition and equations

The Cosmic Nucleus is the final state of maximal cohesion toward which the two energy branches of the model converge. It must not be understood as a point, but as an extended energy structure representing the limit of convergence of matter and Æther D toward E.

Its energy formulation is written:

**E_NC = E_M + E_Æ = mc² + N·V_c (3.1)**
**NC → E + ε_M + ε_Æ (3.2)**

At this stage, its volume can be approached as the sum of the final Schwarzschild volumes associated with the Black Cores of the scenario:

**V_NC = Σ_i 4/3πR³_BH,i, with R_BH,i = 2GM_i/c² (3.3)**

This writing is not worth a definitive demonstration of the volume of the Nucleus. It serves as a construction scheme: the Nucleus is treated as a global final state resulting from the convergence of the ultimate compact structures.

### 3.2 Comparison with general relativity

| Classical general relativity | Silentium |
|------------------------------|-----------|
| initial volume → 0 | initial volume → V_NC ≠ 0 |
| density → ∞ | maximal finite density ρ_max_E |
| perfect singularity | extended maximal cohesion |
| Big Bang as a point | Cosmic Nucleus as a structure |

Classical general relativity cancels the initial volume. Silentium on the contrary attributes to it a finite radius. Between the two lies perhaps the quantity that today appears as the cosmological constant.

### 3.3 Size estimates

The diameter of the Cosmic Nucleus is not yet derived from a single principle. It results at this stage from a progressive construction founded on successive bounds.

| NC diameter | Hypothesis | Status |
|-------------|------------|--------|
| 120,000 ly | visible matter alone | lower bound |
| 240,000 ly | ×2 correction for unseen matter | intermediate estimate |
| 500,000 ly | retained working hypothesis | current reference value |
| >10⁶ ly | with complete Æther D branch | possible high estimate |

The diameter of 500,000 light-years is therefore not presented as a demonstrated result, but as a high working hypothesis, coherent with the idea that the minimal calculation founded on visible matter alone necessarily underestimates the final size of the Nucleus.

### 3.4 Physical meaning of this progression

The estimate at 120,000 light-years comes from a calculation founded on the matter branch alone already accounted for. It does not take into account:
- dark matter and unobserved matter;
- galaxies beyond the horizon;
- the Æther D → E branch;
- the energetic inheritance of past radiation.

The value 500,000 ly must therefore be understood as a coherent working hypothesis with a progressive correction of the lower bound, and not as an arbitrary number.

**The Cosmic Nucleus is not conceived as the smallest possible object. It is conceived as the state of maximal possible cohesion.**

This sentence must be taken seriously: it forbids reading the Nucleus as a simple compacted residue of standard cosmology. The Nucleus is a hypothesis of global energetic architecture, not just an extrapolation of the terminal black hole.

### 3.5 Global zero angular momentum

A structural property adds to the definition of the Cosmic Nucleus. A state of maximal cohesion is a state from which the great global asymmetries have disappeared; the overall rotation is one of them. One therefore posits, as a structural condition of maximal cohesion, the cancellation of the global angular momentum of the final state:

**J_final = 0 (3.4)**

This is not an assertion about the dynamics of a particular pair of Black Cores in fusion, but a condition on the final state itself. A residual net angular momentum would define a privileged global axis; the final state would then no longer possess the symmetry properties required to serve as the point of origin for a new cosmological cycle. Several dynamic histories can satisfy this condition: the cancellation is the constraint, while a compensation of successive angular momenta,

**Σ_i J_i → 0 (3.5)**

is only one possible mechanism.

The Cosmic Nucleus thus appears not only as a maximum of energetic cohesion (ρ_max_E ≈ 3.9 × 10⁶ J/m³), but also as a minimum of residual structure. One adds in this way to the model a third terminal condition, alongside the conservation of energy and memory: the final cancellation of the global angular momentum.

### 3.6 Memory of the Cosmic Nucleus

The Cosmic Nucleus conserves, in cohesive form, a structural information on the history that produced it. Three levels are distinguished, articulated but distinct.

**Cohesion memory (interpretation):** as the energetic content of the Universe converges toward E, the accumulated energetic inheritance — radiation, Æther D continuum, formed structures, Black Cores — is conserved in the cohesive state. Since the Æther D branch dominates by a factor of 23 (§2.2), this memory is above all that of the reconverted continuum, and not that of matter.

**Matter memory (interpretation):** the history of matter is a component of the cohesion memory, and not an independent memory in the naive sense. Matter does not necessarily subsist in its ordinary form; on the other hand, its energetic, gravitational, and structural history remains inscribed in the final state. To the question "what remains of matter?", the model answers not by the survival of material bodies, but by the persistence of their history in the cohesive regime. Its relative part is fixed by the ratio E_M/E_Æ ≈ 1/23 (§2.2).

**Exception memory (speculative):** if E were a state of perfect, fixed cohesion, no new state could appear and the cosmological cycle would be interrupted. The final state would therefore conserve not only the trace of what has been, but also the architectural possibility of a new departure. This "Exception memory" naturally identifies with the minimal exception ε already introduced (§2.1, eqs 2.1–2.2): the irreducible residual dissymmetry is not a defect of the final state but a necessary condition for the reopening of the cycle,

**E → Exception → Ignition → Universe → E (3.6)**

**Flash:** if the exception ε disappeared totally in E, then E → nothing: the cycle would stop. The memory of the Exception would be precisely what prevents the state of maximal cohesion from being a definitive final point. Speculative statement, conserved as an architectural track: the Exception is conserved across cycles.

## Chapter 4 — Observable universe, visible fraction, and total energy cycle

The chronological age of the observable Universe is today approximately 13.8 billion years. But this age does not necessarily coincide with its degree of global energetic maturity. Within the framework of the Silentium scenario, three different quantities must be distinguished:
1. **the convergence of the observable part**, i.e., the degree of transformation of the content already engaged in the dynamics accessible to observation;
2. **the visible fraction of the total final content**, i.e., the part of the final energetic content already included in this observable sector;
3. **the convergence of the total cosmic cycle**, which includes the whole content destined to join the state of cohesion E.

### 4.1 Convergence of the observable part

If the characteristic time of convergence of the currently observable sector is worth τ_SCF ≈ 15–20 Ga, then the degree of convergence of the observable part can be written:

**C_obs(t₀) = 1 − e^(−t₀/τ_SCF) (4.1)**

For t₀ = 13.8 Ga, one obtains:

**C_obs(t₀) ≈ 0.50–0.60 (4.2)**

The residual non-convergence part of this observable portion then equals:

**Y_obs(t₀) = 1 − C_obs(t₀) ≈ 0.40–0.50 (4.3)**

In other words, the currently observable portion of the Universe would already be engaged to about half in its energetic convergence, without having reached its final state.

### 4.2 Visible fraction of the final content

This estimate does not necessarily mean that the total Universe, taken as a whole, has already accomplished half of its cycle.

The Silentium scenario leaves open the hypothesis that a part of the final content of the Cosmic Nucleus:
- is not yet observable;
- is not yet accounted for in the visible energy balance;
- or has not yet joined the phase of cohesive convergence described by C_obs(t).

One then introduces the factor of energetic visibility:

**f_vis = E_obs / E_final (4.4)**

where:
- E_obs denotes the energetic content currently observable or already integrated into the visible balance;
- E_final denotes the total energetic content destined to converge toward the final Cosmic Nucleus.

By construction, 0 < f_vis ≤ 1. When f_vis < 1, this means that the currently observable sector still represents only a fraction of the total final content of the cycle.

### 4.3 Convergence of the total cycle

In this writing, the convergence of the total cosmic cycle can be approximated by:

**C_tot(t₀) ≈ f_vis · C_obs(t₀) (4.5)**

This relation is not a derived fundamental law; it constitutes a working parameterization allowing the distinction between:
- the energetic maturation of the observable sector;
- and the energetic maturation of the total cosmic cycle.

The factor f_vis therefore does not correct the internal dynamics of C_obs; it corrects only the extension of the observable sector with regard to the total final content.

If, by way of illustration, one takes C_obs(t₀) ≈ 0.52 and f_vis ≈ 0.50, then:

**C_tot(t₀) ≈ 0.26.**

In this reading, the observable Universe can therefore already be advanced in its own convergence while still representing only about a quarter of the total energy cycle.

### 4.4 Conceptual formulation

**The observable Universe can already be engaged halfway in its local energetic convergence while still representing only a fraction of the total cosmic cycle.**

In other words:
- C_obs measures the convergence of the currently modeled observable sector;
- f_vis measures the fraction of the final content already included in this sector;
- C_tot measures the position of the system in the global energy cycle.

Cosmic youth is therefore measured not only by elapsed time, nor even by the sole state of the observable sector, but by the relation between this sector and the totality of the energetic content destined to join the state of cohesion E.

### 4.5 Status of the hypothesis

At this stage, the factor f_vis does not yet possess an independent derivation. It must be understood as a structural parameter allowing to relate two levels of the scenario: (1) the observable dynamics already engaged in the visible continuum; (2) the totality of the final content called to converge toward the Cosmic Nucleus.

Two physical readings remain open:
**Reading 1 – content beyond the horizon:** a part of the final content already exists, but remains beyond the horizon or beyond observational access.
**Reading 2 – content not yet converted:** a part of the final content has not yet entered the cohesive convergence phase described by C_obs(t).

In both cases, f_vis < 1 means that the currently visible sector must not be confused with the complete final system.

## Chapter 5 — The cosmological constant as residual curvature

### 5.1 The pivot equation

Pillar I proposes to interpret the cosmological constant as the diluted trace of the initial cohesive curvature of the Cosmic Nucleus. The candidate equation is:

**Λ_SCF ~ 1/(R_NC·D)² (5.1)**

where:
- R_NC is the radius of the Cosmic Nucleus;
- D is a dilution/dispersion factor to be defined physically.

This relation is not worth a demonstration yet. It plays the role of a pivot equation: it condenses the idea according to which a cosmological quantity observed today could conserve the geometric and energetic trace of an initial state of cohesion.

### 5.2 Numerical verification

From the observed cosmological constant, one obtains a characteristic scale:

**R_Λ = 1/√Λ_obs ≈ 9.5 × 10²⁵ m ≈ 10¹⁰ ly (5.2)**

Taking R_NC ≈ 2.5 × 10⁵ light-years, one obtains:

**D = R_Λ / R_NC ≈ 10¹⁰ / 2.5 × 10⁵ ≈ 4 × 10⁴ (5.3)**

which leads to:

**Λ_SCF ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻⁵² m⁻²**

in agreement in order of magnitude with observation.

### 5.3 Meaning of this relation

In this framework, Λ would no longer be an arbitrary constant added to the vacuum, but the residual curvature of an initial state of cohesion of finite radius. The factor D would then measure the way in which this cohesion has been distributed in the continuum since the Ignition.

This reading is important for Pillar I, because it displaces the question of the cosmological constant. Instead of asking "why does the vacuum possess this value?", it asks: "what initial cohesive quantity must be diluted to recover this curvature today?"

## Chapter 6 — Physical definition of D

The factor D appears in the relation:

**Λ_SCF ~ 1/(R_NC·D)² (6.1)**

and plays a central role in the interpretation of the cosmological constant. But a numerical result has no theoretical value as long as its physical significance remains indeterminate.

### 6.1 Four hypotheses

At this stage, four readings of D remain open:

| Hypothesis | Definition | Reading |
|------------|-----------|---------|
| A – geometric | D = R_Λ/R_NC | scale ratio between the Nucleus and the diluted Universe |
| B – energetic | D = E_NC/E_current | measure of energetic dispersion since the Nucleus |
| C – cohesive | D = ρ_max_E/ρ_Λ | measure of loss of cohesion between the initial and current state |
| D – memorial | D ~ M_NC/M_Universe | measure of dispersion of cosmic memory since the Ignition |

These four hypotheses are not mutually exclusive. They can designate partial readings of a same deeper quantity. The problem is therefore not only to "choose the good definition," but to determine whether D is a simple or a composed quantity.

### 6.2 An important tension

Hypotheses A and C lead to very different orders of magnitude:

**D(A) ≈ 4 × 10⁴ (6.2)**
**D(C) ≈ 6.5 × 10¹⁵ (6.3)**

This tension must not be masked: if D is a simple ratio of lengths, it is worth ~10⁴; if it is a ratio of densities, it is worth ~10¹⁵. These two numbers cannot designate exactly the same quantity, except if D possesses a more composed definition.

One possibility is then that D is neither purely geometric nor purely cohesive, but a mixed quantity, combining spatial dilution, energetic dispersion, and loss of cohesion.

### 6.3 Dimensional reading of the tension

The previous tension between D(A) ≈ 4 × 10⁴ (geometric) and D(C) ≈ 6.5 × 10¹⁵ (cohesive) admits a simple reading. The two quantities are dimensionless, but do not compare the same thing: D(A) = R_Λ/R_NC is a ratio of lengths, while D(C) = ρ_max_E/ρ_Λ is a ratio of densities.

Now the total energy is quasi-conserved between the two states (E_total ≈ 2.16 × 10⁷¹ J at the Cosmic Nucleus, against E_Æ ≈ 2.07 × 10⁷¹ J diluted today, §2.2). At constant energy, a density ratio is an inverse volume ratio, therefore the cube of a length ratio:

**ρ_max_E/ρ_Λ ≈ V_obs/V_NC = (R_obs/R_NC)³ (6.4)**

Numerically, with R_obs = 46 × 10⁹ ly and R_NC = 2.5 × 10⁵ ly:

**(R_obs/R_NC)³ = (1.84 × 10⁵)³ ≈ 6.2 × 10¹⁵ ≈ D(C) (6.5)**

The cohesive density is therefore, to very good approximation, a length ratio cubed. The "exponent" sometimes evoked is not 3.6 but:

**p = ln D(C) / ln D(A) ≈ 3.44 (6.6)**

and the deviation from the integer exponent 3 is explained entirely by the choice of the outer radius: D(A) uses the curvature radius R_Λ ≈ 10¹⁰ ly, while D(C) uses the observable radius R_obs ≈ 4.6 × 10¹⁰ ly. The residual factor is exactly their ratio cubed:

**D(C)/(D(A))³ ≈ 100 ≈ (R_obs/R_Λ)³ (6.7)**

**Conclusion.** The tension is neither a contradiction nor a mysterious power law: it is (i) a passage length → volume → density (exponent 3) and (ii) a choice of outer radius (R_obs vs R_Λ). The clean physical question becomes: why does the cohesive ratio select the observable radius, and the geometric ratio the curvature radius? — the bridge between the two being the factor (R_obs/R_Λ)³.

### 6.4 Candidate definition adopted

The most fruitful working definition at this stage is the following:

**D represents the degree to which the initial cohesive energy of the Cosmic Nucleus has been distributed in the cosmological continuum since the Ignition.**

This formulation allows the geometric reading to be conserved, while leaving open a deeper reading in terms of cohesion or memory. It has an important advantage: it does not impose deciding immediately whether D is a length, a density, an energy ratio, or a more subtle effective quantity. It defines first its physical role before fixing its dimension.

### 6.5 Memorial reading of D

The most ambitious version of the model consists of interpreting D no longer only as a scale factor, but as a ratio of memorial dilution. In this reading, Λ would not measure only a residual curvature; it would also measure how much the Universe has moved away from its initial state of cohesion. In other words, the factor D would not translate only an expansion or an energetic dilution, but also the progressive dispersion of the cosmic history initially contained in the Nucleus.

This reading remains speculative. It is not necessary to the calculation of Λ, but it could become important if Pillar I had to later integrate explicitly the notion of cosmic memory. It constitutes a natural junction point with the future developments on the memory of matter, the memory of cohesion, and the memory of the Exception.

## Chapter 7 — The link with Hawking

Pillar I is not content with associating Λ to the Cosmic Nucleus. It also explores the idea that a same architecture could relate:
- the maximal cohesion density ρ_max_E;
- the maximal gradient of the continuum;
- the local transition toward E;
- and the Hawking radiation around compact objects.

### 7.1 The logical chain

The proposed reasoning is the following:

**Λ_SCF fixes the diluted scale of Æther D ⇒ this scale fixes a maximal gradient ∇_max ⇒ ∇_max commands the local transition rate →E ⇒ Hawking could indirectly measure the weakness of this transition.**

This chain is not a demonstration. It defines a program of coherence: relating a global cosmological quantity to a local observable quantity around the Black Cores.

### 7.2 The maximal gradient of Æther D

Between the Cosmic Nucleus (ρ_max_E) and the current Universe (ρ_Λ), the maximal gradient of the continuum can be estimated by:

**∇_max ~ (ρ_max_E − ρ_Λ)/R_Λ ≈ 3.9 × 10⁶ / 9.5 × 10²⁵ ≈ 4.1 × 10⁻²⁰ J/m⁴ (7.1)**

This quantity represents the maximal energy slope between the cohesion state of the Nucleus and the current diluted state of the continuum.

### 7.3 The Hawking temperature

For a Black Core of mass M, the Hawking temperature equals:

**T_H = ħc³/(8πGMk_B) (7.2)**

For a typical mass of the order of 10¹⁵ M_⊙, one obtains:

**T_H ≈ 6.2 × 10⁻²³ K (7.3)**

an extraordinarily low temperature.

### 7.4 The Silentium connection

Pillar I explores the following idea: if the local transition rate toward E is proportional to the local gradient of the continuum, then:

**Γ→E ∝ ∇_local ∝ 1/M (7.4)**

Yet Hawking predicts precisely:

**T_H ∝ 1/M (7.5)**

The coincidence of the 1/M dependence does not constitute a proof, but it suggests a possible reading:
- the more massive a Black Core, the weaker its local gradient;
- the weaker its gradient, the slower the local transition toward E;
- the slower this transition, the lower the Hawking temperature.

In this reading, Hawking radiation would become the observational signature of a local transition of the continuum toward the state of cohesion.

### 7.5 Complete chain

The complete chain of Pillar I can then be written:

**ρ_max_E —dilution→ ρ_Λ —gradient→ ∇_max —local→ Γ→E ~ T_H (7.6)**

At this stage, this chain does not have the value of a complete derivation. It serves as an interpretative framework relating the Cosmic Nucleus, the cosmological constant, the Æther D continuum, and the compact objects.

The passage from ρ_max_E to the local field C(r) is not a change of subject, but a change of scale. Pillar I first establishes the global structure of the scenario: Cosmic Nucleus, maximal density, dilution, link to Hawking. The field C(r) then intervenes as the local version of this architecture, i.e., as the variable susceptible of relating the global cosmological cohesion to the gravity observed around compact objects.

In this sense, the Research Note on C(r) does not constitute a separate branch: it is the local formalization of the dynamics already sketched by Pillar I.

### 8.1 Motivation

We have calculated a maximal cohesion density ρ_max_E ≈ 3.9 × 10⁶ J/m³ and proposed a qualitative link with Hawking via ∇_max.

There remains however a missing link: how to pass from the global density of the Nucleus to a local gradient around a Black Core?

The answer proposed by the Research Note is the local cohesion field C(r).

### 8.2 Definition of the field C(r)

One introduces a dimensionless scalar field:

**C(r) ∈ [0, 1] (8.1)**

with the limit interpretations:
- C(r) → 0: transmissive regime, dominated by Æther D;
- C(r) → 1: cohesive regime, dominated by E.

A natural link with the maximal cohesion density is:

**C(r) ≈ ρ(r)/ρ_max_E ∈ [0, 1] (8.2)**

Thus:
- the current diluted Universe satisfies C ≪ 1;
- the Cosmic Nucleus corresponds to the regime C → 1.

One must take care not to confuse this local and spatial field C(r) with the global and temporal convergence fractions of Chapter 4 (C_obs(t) for the observable sector, C_tot(t) for the total cycle): C(r) is its local version, restricted to the neighborhood of an object, while C_obs and C_tot describe the overall state of convergence.

### 8.3 The candidate equation for gravity

The Research Note then proposes the candidate equation:

**g(r) = −α_C ∇C(r) (8.3)**

where α_C is a coupling coefficient to be determined.

The reading in Silentium language is the following:
- E provides the cohesion;
- Æther D provides the transmission;
- gravity appears as the dynamic response of the continuum to the existence of regions of different cohesion.

In other words:

**cohesion gradient → tension in Æther D → gravity**

This equation does not have the vocation to replace Newton or Einstein at this stage. It proposes a local interpretative variable from which one will attempt to recover the known limits.

### 8.4 Link with the Schwarzschild factor

Pillar I already reinterprets the Schwarzschild factor not as a time dilatation, but as a local indicator of the state of the continuum. A natural conjecture is then:

**C(r) ↔ Φ_S(r) (8.4)**

where Φ_S(r) would play the role of observable proxy of the underlying cohesion field. This relation remains to be demonstrated. It constitutes one of the central questions of the research program.

### 8.5 The critical gradient G_crit

The model supposes that Æther D possesses a domain of stability. When the local energy gradient exceeds a certain critical threshold, the continuum switches toward a cohesive regime:

**G < G_crit ⇒ stable transmissive regime (8.5)**
**G ≥ G_crit ⇒ transition toward E (8.6)**

A singularity would then not be a fundamental physical object, but the mathematical symptom of a regime transition still poorly described.

### 8.6 Complete chain Cosmic Nucleus → Hawking

The Research Note can be reinscribed in Pillar I by the following chain:

**ρ_max_E → C(r) → ∇C(r) —α_C→ g(r) —G_crit→ Γ→E ~ T_H (8.7)**

| Step | Status |
|------|--------|
| ρ_max_E ≈ 3.9 × 10⁶ J/m³ | calculated |
| C(r) ≈ ρ(r)/ρ_max_E | defined |
| g(r) = −α_C ∇C(r) | candidate hypothesis |
| C(r) ↔ Φ_S(r) | conjecture |
| G_crit | research program |
| Γ→E ~ T_H | qualitative coherence |

### 8.7 Open questions of the Research Note

The open questions associated with the field C(r) are the following:
1. Is the Newtonian limit recoverable from g = −α_C ∇C for a field C(r) sourced by a mass distribution?
2. Can the Schwarzschild factor be reread as an indicator of cadence or state of the continuum while preserving the relativistic observations?
3. Can a field C(r) be defined independently of the metric it is supposed to explain?
4. Can cohesion alone source a gravitational gradient without passing through the classical notion of mass?
5. What is the physical origin of α_C?
6. Can G_crit be derived rather than postulated?
7. Does the equation g = −α_C ∇C admit a variational formulation?

## Research program of Pillar I

At this stage, the research program can be summarized in seven structuring questions:

1. **Size of the Cosmic Nucleus:** can D_NC be derived from an internal dynamics of the model rather than introduced as a working hypothesis?
2. **Status of ρ_max_E:** does the calculated maximal density correspond to a stable property of the state E, or does it still depend too strongly on the hypotheses on the diameter of the Nucleus?
3. **Definition of D:** is the factor D geometric, energetic, cohesive, memorial, or a combination of these four readings?
4. **Local transition toward E:** can the link Γ→E ∝ ∇ be formalized in the form of a complete equation?
5. **Link with Hawking:** can the 1/M dependence between local gradient and Hawking temperature be justified otherwise than by analogy?
6. **Local field C(r):** can the cohesion field be rigorously related to observed gravity and to the Schwarzschild factor?
7. **Cosmic memory:** does the final state of cohesion conserve only an energy, or also a structural information on the history of the Universe — memory of cohesion, memory of matter, and memory of the Exception (cf. §3.6)?

## Founding phrases of Pillar I

- Maximal cohesion does not necessarily imply minimal volume.
- The Cosmic Nucleus is a state of extended cohesion, not a singularity.
- The final Cosmic Nucleus possesses a global zero angular momentum: J_final = 0.
- The Æther D branch dominates today the matter branch by a factor of about 23.
- The cosmological constant could be the residual curvature of a Cosmic Nucleus of finite radius.
- Hawking could constitute an indirect measure of the local gradient of Æther D around a Black Core.
- The local field C(r) is the possible gravitational translation of a more fundamental cohesion architecture.

## Document status

Pillar I does not claim at this stage to demonstrate a complete cosmology nor a competing theory of general relativity. It proposes a coherent energetic architecture, articulated around five ideas:
1. matter and Æther D are two regimes of the same energetic reality;
2. both converge toward a fundamental state E of maximal cohesion;
3. this state can be represented by a Cosmic Nucleus of finite radius;
4. the cosmological constant, the factor D, Hawking, and the field C(r) can be read as different manifestations of a same cohesion dynamics;
5. the model is worth only if it becomes precise enough to be contradicted.

In this sense, Pillar I is not an achievement. It is a research framework.
