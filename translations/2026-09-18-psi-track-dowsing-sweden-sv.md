# Psi-track (Psi-spåret)

Aliases: psi-spår, psi-spåret, and the psi-track phenomenon.

## Summary

Summary: Göte Andersson, an artist from Värmland, has for several years conducted investigations showing that one can follow a so-called psi-track with a dowsing rod to hidden or lost objects, and in this way find them. With support from the John Björkhem Mirmes Foundation, Jens Tellefsen, PhD, and Nils-Olof Jacobson, Med Dr, together with Göte Andersson, have carried out scientific experiments with very good results that demonstrate the existence of the psi-track. We present here the methodology and results of the investigation, and discuss some of the psi-track's properties. The report on the experiments was published in the January issue of the Journal of the Society for Psychical Research.

Have Swedish researchers discovered something significantly new in psi research — something that could be a repeatable parapsychological experiment?

A thought — what is it? An idea, or an inner image, arising in a consciousness and perhaps followed by action — yes, that is perhaps how one might answer. But that the thought could be something extended in space, something concrete and measurable, does not belong to our usual notions of how the world is constructed. Swedish parapsychological investigations show, however, that this may be the case.

## The Discovery of the Psi-Track

Text 24728, v1.

It is now nearly seven years since the so-called thought-track was discovered. The track, which came to be called the psi-track, is thus the measurable track of a thought.

Since the first experiment was made, a number of Swedish researchers have, over several years, conducted a series of experiments that have attracted international attention. Nils-Olof Jacobson and Jens A. Tellefsen have written a report that won first prize in a parapsychological competition in England and was published in one of the leading parapsychological journals, the English Journal of the Society for Psychical Research. In Sweden, the first book on the psi-track experiments is soon to appear, written by Göte Andersson, a Värmland artist, who was the one who discovered the psi-track.

One day in 1987, when Göte was visiting his father Arthur Andersson, he wanted to try to measure the human aura with the help of a dowsing rod. He had gotten the idea from a newspaper article, which claimed this was possible. He asked his father to measure his — Göte's — aura. Arthur, who is an experienced dowser, had previously searched for, and found, water veins with the dowsing rod. He thought the idea was odd but did as Göte asked, and to his surprise got a strong rod reaction around Göte.


---


Göte then wanted to try to extend his aura in a certain direction, but at first this did not work as well as he had hoped. He then tried to think with great intensity of a chair a few meters away — and since he is an artist, he has a highly developed inner visual sense. He then asked Arthur to walk around the chair with the dowsing rod. To Arthur's surprise, the rod gave a strong reaction all around the chair, and to both men's ever-growing astonishment, Arthur also found, with the rod's help, a clear track between Göte and the chair. Thus was the first psi-track discovered.

## Scientific Experiments

Text 24729, v1.

Göte, who became enormously interested in this discovery, devoted several years to exploring the psi-track together with several skilled dowsers from his home district. He understood that he had come upon the trail of something hitherto unknown, and wanted it to be investigated scientifically. To this end he contacted Jens A. Tellefsen, a physicist at the Royal Institute of Technology in Stockholm, and later Nils-Olof Jacobson, a psychiatrist with great knowledge of the psi field, was also attached to the project. Together with Kristina Anjou, Jens's wife, they made scientific studies of the psi-track.

The experiments have mostly been conducted outdoors with the help of a dowsing rod, which has proven to be an excellent instrument for finding psi-tracks. To make the experiments flow easily, they have preferably worked on large open areas. They have generally chosen fields of at least about 100 × 100 meters, which before the start of the experiments are swept with the dowsing rod to establish that there is nothing there — such as water veins and fracture zones — that the rod reacts to.

The participants in these experiments have the following tasks: One person, the hider, hides the object that is to be found with the help of the psi-track. He then leaves the experiment area. A second person, the sender, sends the thought from a sending location toward the hidden object — that is, he creates the psi-track. A third person, the dowser (who could of course just as well be a woman) searches out the psi-track with the help of a dowsing rod. Sender and dowser can be the same person. But it is naturally a fundamental requirement that the dowser must not have the slightest clue about where the hider has placed the object.

A double-blind trial can be briefly described as follows:

One preferably works outdoors in a large field, as free from disturbances as possible. The hider places an object — for example a crystal or a watch, preferably with a personal connection to the sender. It goes without saying that the other participants in the experiment have no possibility of seeing where the object is placed. When the hider has signaled that he is done, and has left the area, it is the sender's turn. The sender suitably stations himself somewhere in the middle of the field and concentrates intensely on the object that has been hidden. He has no knowledge of where the object is. The sender usually uses some kind of visualization technique. During this process the psi-track is created.


---


The sending location is well marked, and the third person, the dowser, begins to search for the track. The dowser works slowly in small circles around the sending location, and the places where the rod reacts are marked. The circles widen, and when the markings made in the circles form a line in some direction, the dowser begins to work in a zigzag across this line. Through the reactions he gets, the psi-track can be followed — usually dead straight toward the hidden object.

A question that almost immediately comes up, when one tells about these experiments, is whether the hider leaves tracks behind him so that the dowser unconsciously follows these. To this one can answer that the laying-out procedure is set up so that all possibility of such tracks arising is completely eliminated.

Of the 40 double-blind trials that the research group carried out on six different occasions over a three-year period, they succeeded in 32 cases, within about half an hour, in finding the hidden object. Given that this is about finding, for example, a small rock crystal in a field 100 × 100 meters or larger, the result is remarkable, to say the least. Is this the first repeatable psi experiment in history?

## Practical Use of the Psi-Track

Text 24730, v1.

If in real life one needs to use the technique, it is an advantage to be able both to send oneself and to handle the dowsing rod. Several of Göte's assistants usually do both of these things. When the sender has concentrated on and visualized the hidden object, he lets go of all thought of it completely and leaves the sending location. When he has released the thought of the object, the psi-track does not follow the sender — instead it seems to remain in place, with the sending location as its point of origin. It is therefore entirely possible, and in many cases also practical, for the sender to continue the work as the dowser. If the sought object is mobile, on the other hand, the track follows along with its movements. One can easily test this by hiding something in the pocket of a person who walks away.

It has been found that the psi-track emanates from the sender's body somewhere around navel height, and is about 1–1.5 meters wide. Dowsers who use a forked branch usually find the middle of the track, while those who use angle rods tend to get reactions at the outer edges and must therefore measure out the center line themselves.

Göte has all along hoped that the psi-track method could be put to practical use in several different ways — for example, to search for lost persons and in this way help the police and rescue services. He and the other participants in the experiments have, on several occasions, made practical use of their knowledge of the psi-track.

Once, Göte and some friends had gotten lost while picking mushrooms in the forests of Värmland. By sending toward the car at the parking place, and cutting a branch for a dowsing rod, they easily found the track back.


---


On another occasion, a dog went missing during a hunt. The dog's two owners sent toward the dog, and a third person searched for the track with a dowsing rod. Sendings were made from several different locations, but unfortunately the search had to be called off when darkness fell. On a map one could afterwards see that all the tracks led straight toward a small ice-covered lake about two kilometers away. The next day the search continued at the lake, where the dog was found at exactly the expected spot — sadly drowned.

In fact, people who have learned the technique often use it to find things that have gone missing: to track down the cat that needs to come in for the night, to find the boot that the fox dragged off into the woods, or the garden hoe that has been gone so long. All of these are examples of what Göte and his friends have found with the help of the psi-track method.

One phenomenon that at first caused a good deal of confusion during the experiments was that the track often sought out several different objects that looked alike. When, for example, a gym shoe was hidden on one occasion, it turned out that the track went in three different directions at once. When all three tracks were followed, it was discovered that one found not only the sought gym shoe, but also two piles of old gym shoes in two nearby houses.

## Continued Exploration of the Psi-Track

Text 24731, v1.

Despite several years of experimentation, the exploration of the psi-track is still in its beginner phase. Above all, one hopes that the international attention the discovery has received will lead to more people, independently of one another, doing experiments with the psi-track, and that a number of questions that came up during the trials will be answered.

One question is: over how long a distance can the psi-track run? It seems it can be detected with the dowsing rod at least several kilometers (miles) from the sending location.

Is it possible to send purely mental images? Göte has made several interesting attempts suggesting this is possible. For example, tracks have been discovered between two people who, unknown to each other, are thinking about the same thing. It has also been found that simple mental forms projected onto the ground by the sender can be detected without difficulty.

Better investigations are needed of the psi-track's relationship to different materials. So far it is known that plastic, metals, and impregnated material more or less block the track. Water probably weakens the track, but on the other hand it passes straight through such natural materials as trees, wood, stone, rock, and soil.

Must one really use a dowsing rod? It has turned out that the rod is a very practical aid, but the researchers are very interested in finding other ways of detecting the track. It is possible to find the track using only the feeling in the wrists, but this has not been explored in any depth. The researchers also hope to find some aura-seeing person who can see the track. In the psi-track's early history, Göte had contact with such a person, a young boy — but unfortunately this ability disappeared as the boy grew up.


---


What significance, then, does this discovery — that the thought leaves a track behind it — have? We realize at once that if thoughts produce a measurable change in the world around us, then our thoughts may be as important as our actions — something that various esoteric teachings have long claimed. This means, then, that we are constantly surrounded by a tangle of psi-lines, especially if our thoughts and feelings are intense. It has been found that if the sender has strong positive feelings for the object or the person that is the target of his sending, the tracks usually become very clear and easily detectable. The connection between them evidently becomes very strong. Could it perhaps be that such phenomena as healing and love have some connection to such a strong bond?

Perhaps knowledge of the psi-track can also lead to an explanation of several different parapsychological phenomena.
