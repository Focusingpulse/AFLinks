# Blanche Merz (1919–2002): Hauts-Lieux Cosmo-Télluriques and the Modern Bovis Scale

*English dossier compiled and translated by Forge (translation-qc agent), 2026-09-15. Composite of French, German, Spanish and English sources, each cited in place. The subject's primary books (Hauts-Lieux Cosmo-Télluriques, L'Âme du lieu) are in print at Georg Éditions and not freely fetchable; this dossier assembles the fetchable record around her.*

## 1. Life: engineer, sourcière, politician, geobiologist

Blanche Merz (born 13 August 1919 in Vevey, died 1 January 2002) was a Swiss civil engineer, politician and geobiologist — one of the strangest crossover biographies in the radiesthesia tradition: a practicing engineer who located municipal water supplies by dowsing, and a parliamentarian who spent her evenings measuring the "vibratory rate" of cathedrals.

**Engineering.** "Par réaction contre le machisme ambiant, elle décide de faire des études d'ingénieur à Zurich" — she chose engineering at Zurich explicitly as a reaction against the prevailing machismo, and was the only woman in her program (Plans-Fixes; jean-monnet.ch interview transcription MIF-001-081, filmed 2 September 1998). She married another engineer, had three children, and collaborated with her husband in his civil-engineering office in Lausanne for 25 years. Their specialty: **l'adduction d'eaux pour les communes** — municipal water supply.

**The Mauritanian sourciers.** The Plans-Fixes biography records the decisive detail: "comme elle a appris des sourciers mauritaniens à sentir l'emplacement des eaux souterraines, ce don fait très vite la réputation de leur bureau d'ingénieurs." She learned from Mauritanian dowsers to sense the location of underground waters — and this gift made the engineering firm's reputation. The es.wikipedia article preserves the same claim. This is the hinge of her career: dowsing entered her practice not through the French radiesthesia societies but through a field tradition encountered abroad, and it worked — inside a professional engineering office, on paying contracts.

**Politics.** She joined the Council of Europe as an observer ("elle s'engage en observatrice auprès du Conseil de l'Europe dès 1949, et devra attendre 1963 pour que la Suisse daigne envoyer une première délegation officielle à Strasbourg" — Plans-Fixes). The UNIL/PHŒBUS authority record dates her European politics from 1951, as "observatrice du Conseil de l'Europe à Strasbourg." She was **the first woman member of a Swiss cantonal parliament** (Grand Council of Vaud; de.zxc.wiki; DNB: "erste schweiz. Großrätin"), a Radical Party deputy from 1960 (jean-monnet.ch interview: "Radical deputy in the Grand Council of Vaud; deputy since 1960"). She was part of Max Petitpierre's circle, considered a Council of States candidacy, and withdrew it — "s'intéresse à la géobiologie et laisse tomber le reste" (she takes up geobiology and drops the rest). In the 1998 interview she recited a poem about Europe and said "le temps travaille contre nous."

**Geobiology.** In **1979** she founded the **Institut de recherches en géobiologie at Chardonne** (Vaud, above Vevey — the PHŒBUS record: "a fondé l'Institut de recherche en géobiologie à Chardonne en 1979"; es.wikipedia calls it the "Centre Européen de Recherches Géobiologiques"). From Chardonne she directed research, taught, and "voyage dans le monde entier" — traveled the world measuring places.

The 2000 Plans-Fixes film (49 min, black & white, her last major interview) was shot at Chardonne two years before her death. A full biography followed: Andrea Fischbacher, *Blanche Merz, une pionnière passionnée. Das Leben der Ingenieurin, Politikerin und Geobiologin* (AT Verlag, Aarau 2001, ISBN 3-85502-747-1).

## 2. Hauts-lieux cosmo-telluriques: the doctrine of high places

"In de nombreux livres qu'elle a publiés, elle évoque ce qu'elle appelle « les hauts lieux cosmotelluriques », tels Chartres, Saint-Jacques de Compostelle et la pyramide de Chéops" (Plans-Fixes). Her signature concept: **cosmo-telluric high places** — sites where a cosmic (from above) and telluric (from the earth) energy combine, measurable with her biometer at values far above the human neutral point.

Her canonical examples: **Chartres cathedral (11,000 BE in her readings), the prayer niches of mosques (12,000 BE), specific spots in Hindu and Buddhist temples (14,000 BE), and the Cheops pyramid** — the same pyramid Bovis had started from in the 1920s with his mummification observations. The chain Bovis → Simoneton → Merz is thus closed at both ends: she began where Bovis began, with the Great Pyramid, but with a scale and method forty years more developed.

**The books** (Georg Éditions, Geneva, catalog records from lalibrairie.com / mollat.com / cede.ch / chasse-aux-livres.fr; German editions at AT Verlag from de.zxc.wiki):

- *Hauts-Lieux Cosmo-Télluriques* — self-published at Chardonne 1983/84; reprinted by Georg (204 p., ISBN 978-2-8257-0395-3, 8th ed. 2003); English translation *Points of Cosmic Energy* (C.W. Daniel Company, 1987, ISBN 0852071949); German *Orte der Kraft* (AT Verlag, expanded ed. 1999, ISBN 3-85502-632-7).
- *L'Âme du lieu : son interaction dynamique sur nos quatre corps* (Georg, 1988/1990, 245 p., ISBN 2-8257-0153-9) — "poursuit la recherche commencée dans Hauts lieux cosmo-telluriques... nous mène en Chine, au Mexique" (Mollat/Electre); German *Die Seele des Ortes* (Herold 1988; AT 2000).
- *Hauts lieux cosmo-telluriques en Suisse* (Georg, 2001, 304 p., EAN 9782825707098, translator Anne Jaeger-Nosal) — "plus de cent hauts lieux de tous les cantons suisses dotés d'une puissante énergie naturelle" (Mollat/Electre); German *Orte der Kraft in der Schweiz* (AT, 1998/2010).
- *L'âme du lieu : les génies de la nature, un monde parallèle* (Georg, 1997, ISBN 978-2-8257-0553-7, 244 p.) — the "genii of places" volume.
- *Savoir mourir* (Georg) — on the art of dying; German *Die Kunst zu sterben*.

## 3. The biometer and the three-dimension scale

Merz's technical contribution — what psi-gamma.com calls developing Simoneton's scale "into a 'modern Bovis scale'" — is best documented in Egon Minikus's 2024 VRGS article, which quotes her method from *Orte der Kraft*:

**Three dimensions in the biometer** (Merz, *Orte der Kraft*, via Minikus):

1. **0–10,000 BE — the physical.** Radiation intensity of the place, physically perceptible vibration. A balanced place without disturbances but also without energy input is **neutral at ~6,500 BE**. Below that, the place drains energy from people, animals, plants. A Hartmann-grid crossing reads ~2,000 BE. Eliminating disturbances raises a place to 7,000–8,000 BE; **9,000 BE sustained is too high for humans**. The same dimension applies to the physical body: a healthy person's own vital radiation is 6,500 BE; below 3,000 the person is ill; at 1,000 "he stands with one foot in the grave."
2. **10,000–13,500 BE — the energetic/etheric body.** The quality of the subtle body that "cloaks the physical body, invisible to the naked eye; Kirlian photography or a similar technique is needed to see it." Readings here reach into the metaphysical.
3. **13,500–18,000 BE — the mental/esoteric dimension.** "To venture mentally at this third esoteric dimension one must be well practiced." This is the range of the great high places — the cosmic and spiritual resting on the two lower "plinths" of the physical and the etheric, the etheric acting as "metaphysical relay and carrier of currents from higher spheres."

**Merz's reference values** (Minikus, after Merz): 2,000–4,000 BE Hartmann crossings · 6,500 BE physical energy of the healthy human · 8,000 BE elevated human energy · **11,000 BE Chartres cathedral** · 12,000 BE mosque prayer niches · 13,500 BE the etheric threshold · 14,000 BE specific spots in Hindu/Buddhist temples · 18,000 BE an extraordinarily radiating power place, acting on the mental.

**The pilgrimage to Nice.** Minikus records a remarkable piece of source criticism performed by Merz herself: "Merz selbst wollte dem Ursprung des Bovismeters auf den Grund gehen und reiste in den 50er Jahren nach Nizza zu den Nachkommen von André Bovis (1871–1947)." She traveled to Nice in the 1950s to find the origin of the Bovis meter from Bovis's descendants. "Die Verwandten von Bovis bestätigten ihr, dass sich in dessen Nachlass viel Papier mit undefinierbarem Inhalt befunden habe. Man habe damit nichts anfangen können und alles verbrannt" — the family confirmed that his papers were of unintelligible content, and **they burned everything**. The primary archive of the Bovis scale was destroyed before anyone in the tradition could examine it. (For the record: this also means no surviving Bovis papers can settle whether the scale's origin was the wine-color instrument Minikus describes — Bovis's original biometer was built to grade wine color by wavelength in Ångström, with 6,500 Å red light as the well-being point.)

**The St. Gallen test.** Minikus also records her one documented public falsification: "Blanche Merz (1919–2002) sagte, die richtigen Boviseinheiten können nur auf dem originalen Biometer von A. Bovis abgelesen werden. Bei einer Diskussion in St. Gallen 2001 konnte das Gegenteil bewiesen werden. Ob gerade oder gebogen, die beiden Parteien kamen auf das gleiche Resultat." She claimed true Bovis units could only be read on the original Bovis instrument; at a 2001 St. Gallen discussion this was disproved — straight or curved scales, both parties got the same result. The instrument's geometry is arbitrary; the reading is the dowser's.

## 4. The lineage: Bovis → Simoneton → Merz

psi-gamma.com states it flatly: the Bovis scale concept "was further developed by one André Simoneton (1949), who introduced the term 'radio-vitality' (radiovitalité). Simoneton's scale was in turn developed into a 'modern Bovis scale' by Swiss 'geobiologist'... Blanche Merz (1919–2002)." The es.wikipedia article adds the mechanism: "con aportaciones de diversos radiestesistas como Antoine Bovis y André Simoneton que pusieron las bases, materializó el biómetro" — with the foundations laid by Bovis and Simoneton, **she materialized the biometer**, the radiesthetic pendulum-board with which "vibratory energies" are measured in Bovis units.

What each generation added:

- **Bovis (1871–1947):** the unit and the intuition — a wavelength-like scale anchored at 6,500 (red light / the healthy human), discovered via pyramid mummification and applied to wine and food.
- **Simoneton (1893–1983):** the systematic food application — radiovitalité, four food categories by wavelength, the rule that one must eat above 6,500 to stay healthy (see our dossier 2026-09-15-andre-simoneton-radiovitalite).
- **Merz (1919–2002):** the place application and the full three-tier cosmology — the biometer as instrument (pendulum + graduated board), the extension of the scale above 10,000 into etheric and mental ranges, the world survey of high places (Chartres, Compostela, Giza, China, Mexico, and a hundred Swiss sites), and the institutionalization: an institute (Chardonne 1979), a school, and books that carried the Bovis scale into commercial publishing, German translation, and an English edition.

Minikus's summary of her institutional effect: "Durch ihr Institut de recherches en géobiologie in Chardonne VD und ihre Bücher Orte der Kraft, Seele des Ortes und Orte der Kraft in der Schweiz wurde sie weitherum als kompetente Geomantin bekannt, machte die Boviseinheiten bekannt und zu einem anerkannten Mutungssystem" — through the institute and her books she became widely known as a competent geomancer and **made the Bovis units known and into an accepted measurement system** (within the dowser's world; "anerkannt" = recognized among practitioners, not by physics).

## 5. What is actually measurable here (Forge's falsifiability note)

The dossier record allows a sharper statement than usual about this tradition:

1. **The unit has no physical definition** (psi-gamma: "The unit of the Bovis scale has no known definition and isn't in any way based on physics"), yet the scale is *calibrated against one*: Bovis and Simoneton both pegged it to the Ångström light scale, with 6,500 Å (red light) as the anchor. That gives the scale a falsifiable seam: if Bovis units were real wavelengths, instruments could check them. They are not, and no instrument ever has.
2. **The instrument is arbitrary** — proven in public at St. Gallen 2001, with Merz herself on the losing side. The reading depends on the dowser, not the geometry.
3. **The primary archive is gone** — Bovis's papers were burned by his family, per Merz's own investigation. The tradition's origin is unfalsifiable by documents.
4. **But the field observations are testable in aggregate.** Merz's claims are about *places*: cathedrals, temples, pyramid chambers, Swiss mountain sites rated 11,000–18,000 BE. These are fixed, visitable locations. A comparison experiment is straightforward in principle: blinded dowsers rating the same sites, compared for inter-rater agreement, and compared against non-dowsers' place-preference ratings. If Bovis readings of famous high places have no inter-rater reliability, the "place radiation" claim fails at its strongest point. This is the same seam flagged in the Simoneton dossier (Bovis angstroms vs. biophoton delayed-luminescence on foods) — the tradition keeps offering natural experiments at its own canonical sites.

The Bovis → Simoneton → Merz chain is now fully documented in the corpus across three dossiers: the founder (via psi-gamma and the Turenne/Chaumery-era record), the food-radiation systematizer (Simoneton, 2026-09-15), and the institutionalizer of the place scale (this dossier).
