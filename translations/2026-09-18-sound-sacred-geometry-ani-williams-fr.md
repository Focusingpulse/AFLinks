Another masonic view

Sound and sacred geometry: a cosmic harmony at the heart of creation
By Charles-Albert Delatour — July 6, 2025 — 0 Comments — 2323 views — Facebook X Pinterest WhatsApp

In an article published in January 2004 in Spirit of Ma'at magazine (Vol. 4), Ani Williams explores, with enchanting depth, the intimate relationship between sound, sacred geometry and human consciousness. Titled The Sacred Geometry of Sound, this essay draws on ancient traditions, scientific discoveries and mystical experiences to reveal how sound, through its frequencies and vibrations, shapes the universe, matter and the human soul. This article, enriched by philosophical, scientific and spiritual perspectives, invites us to rediscover the divine song that resonates through Creation, linking the microcosm of the human being to the macrocosm of the cosmos.


---


For this reflection, we dive into a detailed and vibrant exploration of this sacred link between sound and geometry, where music, forms and consciousness intertwine to sing universal harmony.

Sound: A Vibratory Matrix of Creation — Since Antiquity, mystical and philosophical traditions have celebrated sound as a primordial force of Creation. Ani Williams evokes the legend of Orpheus, whose lyre, a gift from Apollo, united Nature in a harmony of peace and joy. This Orphic vision, in which music becomes a bridge between the human and the divine, finds an echo in the work of Pythagoras of Samos, considered one of the first physicists in history. By plucking strings of different lengths, Pythagoras discovered that sound vibrations followed a repetitive sequence of seven tones, forming the octave — a fundamental structure that reflects cosmic order.

The idea that "all of Creation is a singing matrix of frequencies" lies at the heart of the sacred geometry of sound.

Like the seven colors of the rainbow, the seven notes of an octave (from do to do) are manifestations of one and the same vibratory reality, which translates into colors, sounds, matter and states of consciousness. The physicist William Tiller, cited by Williams, reinforces this idea by demonstrating that human consciousness permeates the space and matter of the universe. Thus our intention — our capacity to direct our thoughts and emotions — plays a determining role in the quality and direction of Creation. The universal matrix, according to Williams, awaits our sounding of "the most harmonious strings" to manifest an ideal and perfect form of the cosmos.

The Platonic Solids: An Alchemical Dance of the Elements

Bust of Plato. Marble, Roman copy of a Greek original from the last quarter of the 4th century BC.

At the heart of sacred geometry, the five Platonic solids — tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron and icosahedron — embody the fundamental elements of Creation: fire, earth, air, ether and water. These three-dimensional forms, all of whose facets are identical, are not mere geometric figures; they are symbols of universal order, keys to understanding the vibratory structure of the world. Ani Williams recounts her initiation into this truth through the experience of Michael Helios, who had tuned his keyboard to frequencies corresponding to the geometric proportions of each Platonic solid. During meditations guided by these musical compositions, participants could feel and "see" the corresponding forms — striking proof of the power of sound to command Creation.


---


Among these forms, the dodecahedron, associated with ether, occupies a special place. Composed of twelve pentagonal facets, it symbolizes the perfect balance between the infinite (the sphere) and the finite (the cube). Recent research in quantum physics, mentioned by Williams, even suggests that the universe itself may have a dodecahedral structure, based on the residual cosmic waves of the Big Bang. This bold hypothesis links sacred geometry to modern cosmology, suggesting that the universe sings a pentagonal harmony whose echo resonates in the very structure of human life.

The Human Body: A Temple of Golden Proportions — Sacred geometry is not limited to the macrocosmic universe; it is also reflected in the microcosm of the human body. Williams points out that the body, with arms and legs outstretched, is inscribed in a pentagram, a five-sided figure whose proportions respect the Golden Number (Phi, approximately 1.618). This ratio, found in nature, art and architecture, is also present in the five fingers of each hand, the five toes of each foot and the five openings of the face. Our five senses — sight, hearing, touch, taste, smell — reinforce this connection with the number five, which binds humanity to cosmic harmony.

Pentagram with Saint John's candle-type lights

The pentagram, with its angles respecting the Phi ratio, is also the foundation of the musical interval of the fifth, a fundamental harmony present in sacred musics, from Gregorian chant to indigenous compositions. As Williams notes, the fifth is "the first harmony that sounds when a string is plucked," giving the note its depth and beauty. This sonority, described by Pythagoras as the "archetypal expression of harmony," links the human microcosm to the universal macrocosm, demonstrating that "what is above is like what is below."

The Circle of Fifths: A Map of the Chakras

Ani Williams's article also explores the Circle of Fifths, a musical progression that mirrors the harmonic relationships of the human energy system, particularly the chakras. Each chakra, or energy center, is associated with a tone and a color: the root chakra (C, red), the sacral chakra (D, orange), the solar plexus (E, yellow), the heart chakra (F#, green), the throat chakra (G, turquoise), the third eye (A, indigo) and the crown chakra (B, magenta). By applying the Circle of Fifths, Williams reveals subtle connections between these energy centers, which mirror deep human issues. For example, the root chakra, linked to survival and sexuality, is connected to the throat chakra, associated with the expression of truth.

Flower according to the Fibonacci sequence


---


Working on these two centers can help heal blockages related to material security or personal authenticity. This musical progression, which also follows the Fibonacci sequence — where each number is the sum of the two preceding ones (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, etc.) — illustrates the harmonic spiral of Creation. The Fibonacci sequence, found in the spiral forms of nature (seashells, flowers, galaxies), is a mathematical mirror of musical intervals, reinforcing the idea that sound and sacred geometry are expressions of one and the same universal law.

Gothic Cathedrals: Frozen Music — Sacred geometry and sound are not limited to theory; they manifest in sacred architecture.

As Goethe said, "the architecture of the sacred is frozen music."

The great Gothic cathedrals, such as Chartres, Notre-Dame de Paris or Salisbury, were designed according to the principles of sacred geometry and harmonic proportions. These edifices, built from the 12th century under the influence of the Templars, incorporate the ratios of the Golden Number and musical intervals, such as the fifth, to create spaces where human consciousness can rise toward other dimensions.

Williams evokes the Templars' discoveries in the Temple of Solomon, where manuscripts and artifacts are said to have revealed alchemical knowledge about sound, astronomy and sacred geometry. This knowledge, inherited from Hermetic traditions, inspired the architecture of the cathedrals, conceived as "temples of sound" where monastic choirs, like the "perpetual choirs" of ancient Britain, resonated in harmony with cosmic cycles. These chants, synchronized with the seasons and planetary movements, were perceived as a means of "singing the world," a practice found in many indigenous cultures.

Cymatics and Modern Science — Modern science confirms these ancient intuitions. The Swiss researcher Hans Jenny, pioneer of cymatics, demonstrated in the 1960s that sound frequencies applied to powders, liquids or pastes created complex geometric patterns, similar to those observed in nature and sacred architecture. These experiments reveal that sound is an organizing force, capable of giving form to matter. Similarly, the work of Dr. Masaru Emoto on water crystals shows that water exposed to positive thoughts or harmonious music forms balanced geometric structures, while negative emotions generate chaotic forms.

These modern scientific discoveries echo the wisdom of the Navajo healer Billy Yellow: "Our task is to sing the world, to sing beauty. The world is the mirror of our song."


---


The Song of the Cosmos: Johannes Kepler's Vision — The astronomer and mystic Johannes Kepler (1571–1630) also explored the relationship between sound, geometry and the cosmos. In his work, he established correspondences between planetary orbits and musical frequencies, suggesting that each planet "sings" a specific note, forming a celestial choir. According to Stephen Hawking, Kepler even identified planetary "voices" — soprano, tenor, contralto, bass — revealing a cosmic harmony. This vision, inspired by the Platonic solids, illustrates that the laws governing the stars are the same as those animating life on Earth.

Raphael: Plato and Aristotle debating politics?

Plato himself, in his Timaeus, described the universe as a vibratory creation, animated by musical spirals. In The Republic, he evokes eight cosmic spirals, each carried by a siren singing a specific note and color, weaving the musical fabric of Creation. These ideas, taken up by Hermetic traditions and medieval mystics, remind us that sound is a unifying force, linking humanity to the divine order. A Renaissance of Sacred Song

Ani Williams notes that sacred song experiences periods of renewal at key moments in history, particularly at the turning of millennia. At the beginning of Christianity, then during the Grail's romantic era around the year 1000, sacred music played a central role in spiritual awakening. Today, at the dawn of the third millennium, song is reclaiming its place in the creation of a new paradigm. Music therapy practices, like those taught by Williams, use specific tones to harmonize the chakras, dissolve emotional blockages and raise consciousness. These approaches, combined with meditation and choral singing, allow individuals to become "aware of sound in their lives" and to participate actively in the universal symphony.

Conclusion: Singing the Harmony of the Universe — Ani Williams's The Sacred Geometry of Sound is an invitation to rediscover the power of sound as a key to Creation. Through the Platonic solids, the golden proportions, the Circle of Fifths and modern scientific discoveries, we understand that sound is not merely a physical vibration, but a spiritual force linking humanity to the cosmos. The Gothic cathedrals, the perpetual choirs, the cymatics experiments and the intuitions of the mystics converge toward one and the same truth: the universe is a song, and we are its choristers.


---


By playing "the most harmonious strings" of our consciousness, we can participate in creating a more balanced and harmonious world. As Billy Yellow affirms, the world is the mirror of our song. By integrating sacred geometry and sound into our lives, we become architects of the soul, sculpting our own rough stone to align ourselves with divine harmony. The question remains open: are we ready to listen to the song of the cosmos and add our voice to it?
