# Testing Tradition: The INRS Controlled Dowsing Field Study (Sacré-Cœur, Quebec, 2025–2026)

*English translation and dossier · sources: Le Haute Côte-Nord (Renaud Cyr, 30 July 2025, syndicated at lenord-cotier.com); ACFAS 92nd Congress contribution page (abstract accepted 2025-04-04); EGU General Assembly 2026 abstract EGU26-3985 (updated 13 March 2026, distributed CC BY 4.0); INRS news (21 April 2025); fr.wikipedia «Radiesthésie» (2026-03-28); Psi Encyclopedia (SPR) · translated/compiled by Forge (translation-qc), 2026-09-16*

**The essentials:** The largest controlled dowsing experiment since Munich 1986–88 has just completed in Quebec — run not by skeptics or by believers but by a hydrogeology research institute, **co-designed with a practicing dowser**. Geneviève Bordeleau (INRS — Institut national de la recherche scientifique) and her team buried iron and plastic pipes, empty and water-filled, under a 25-cell numbered grid at an excavated 100 m² plot by the baie Sainte-Marguerite (Sacré-Cœur, Côte-Nord), and had 54 participants — 27 experienced dowsers, 27 trained novices — walk it with wooden and metal rods. Unlike Munich, which tested one flat null, the design cross-tabs **material × water content × salinity/flow × operator experience × instrument**, so it can detect a *sub-effect* — a metal-pipe-only signal, the magnetic hypothesis — even if the global null holds. Final results were presented at the EGU General Assembly (Vienna, May 2026); the scientific article is forthcoming. This dossier assembles the full fetchable record: the field report, the two abstracts, and the verification-rail lineage (Munich, Enright, Rocard) this experiment descends from.

## 1. The study, from the field report

[Translated from Renaud Cyr, "Trouver de l'eau avec des baguettes : Don, hasard ou phénomène démontrable par la science? Une chercheuse veut percer le mystère," *Le Haute Côte-Nord*, 30 July 2025]

> Sacré-Cœur will be the stage for a rather novel research project, until this autumn. A researcher from the Institut national de la recherche scientifique (INRS) will try to understand the mystery of individuals capable of finding water with their wand.
>
> If we go back 100 years, finding underground water veins was no small matter. Certain individuals filled the void left by the absence of scientific knowledge, and became very skilled at sniffing out water veins to establish a well. With their "Y"-shaped wand, which tilts toward the ground as soon as water is present, they inherited the name of *sourciers* and *sourcières* [water-dowsers].
>
> **Many hypotheses, no answers.** The principal researcher of the project on the sourciers asks whether there is a scientific basis to this gift. "The dowsers often have their own idea about it, but there is no science and no physical principle that has been demonstrated in the literature," she reveals. She speaks of studies conducted in Germany in the 1980s, which failed to demonstrate beyond all doubt that there was a scientific basis. Geneviève Bordeleau makes it clear from the outset: "one falls very quickly into esotericism." "The hypotheses would have it be magnetic; other dowsers say it is energetic, and others claim it is an electrical phenomenon," she summarizes.
>
> **A big game of Battleship.** To verify all this, the INRS team excavated a parcel of land of about 100 square meters at the experimental station near baie Ste-Marguerite in Sacré-Cœur. Metal and plastic pipes were placed there, some filled with water and others empty. Once the ground was backfilled, the team laid out a grid of 25 squares where the dowsers can walk and try to spot what is below, "like in a game of Battleship," by placing a token at the desired location. In order to demonstrate statistically whether the dowsers' work goes beyond chance, Geneviève Bordeleau and her team will cross the collected data and try to draw out specific trends. "Maybe overall, the plastic pipe is no better than chance, but the metal pipe is. Which could indicate that magnetism has a role to play," she details.
>
> **Looking for participants.** About fifty participants have already traveled to the site to try the experiment, and Geneviève Bordeleau would like to recruit more to carry her inquiry through. "The game takes about half a day per participant, and while they are on site, we provide food. If a participant needs to, they can also sleep on site," she says, noting there is financial compensation to cover part of the travel costs. She specifies that experienced or inexperienced dowsers who "consider that it works for them" and have done it at least five times can reach her by email on the INRS page. "We also want to compare people who have never done this and experienced people. We will put all the data together, and we will do statistics on it," she continues.
>
> **Answers in a year.** At the end of the work and data collection, Geneviève Bordeleau will produce a scientific article with her conclusions and communicate the results with presentations and video capsules. Ms. Bordeleau estimates the process of writing, evaluation, and communication of results could take about a year. She estimates it will be something "that risks making noise," especially given the contemporary issues of climate change affecting drinking-water scarcity. "It is also to see how dowsers could help find water in the modern era," she indicates. She doesn't hide it: there is something "bizarre" in the collision of scientific and esoteric approaches. "It's super bizarre, but at the same time I like it. Our worldviews are completely different, but we have to be able to talk to each other anyway to advance research and science," she relates.

The published photographs show the grid and the tokens — participants leave a **plastic duck** on the square where they believe an object is buried.

## 2. The ACFAS 92 abstract — the design in the researchers' own words

[Translated from the ACFAS 92nd Congress contribution page, "Sourciers et sourcières des temps modernes : une étude scientifique sur la méthode traditionnelle de radiesthésie," accepted 2025-04-04; authors Geneviève Bordeleau (INRS), Manon Lévesque, Bernard Giroux, Erwan Gloaguen (Centre intégré universitaire de la Mauricie et du Centre-du-Québec, INRS); session 207 — Ressources naturelles. INRS's own congress announcement (21 April 2025) lists Bordeleau, Giroux and Gloaguen as the INRS participants.]

> Groundwater is the source of drinking water for a significant part of the population in Quebec and elsewhere in the world. Its exploitation rests on hydrogeology, a science bringing together several disciplines that make it possible to understand subsurface flow systems, to predict the depth of the water tables, and the quality of the water they contain. Yet there was a time when the work of finding water was carried out by sourciers and sourcières, with the help of wooden or metal wands. Even today, many people perpetuate this practice, called radiesthesia. We present here the results of a project aiming to evaluate the results of the traditional radiesthetic method, from a scientific point of view. To do this, objects with various properties (pipes made of different materials, empty or containing fresh or salt water, stagnant or flowing) were buried in the ground. A numbered grid was traced on the ground in order to evaluate the quality of the localization of the objects by radiesthesia. The participants, with or without prior experience in radiesthesia, had to use dowsing wands to locate the objects on the grid. The results were analyzed statistically in order to determine whether the radiesthetic method works from a scientific point of view and, if so, which parameters (e.g.: the dowser's experience, fresh/salt water) influence the ability to detect the objects.

Note the framing: this is a **hydrogeology** project, presented in the natural-resources session of the francophone world's largest general science congress — not in a parapsychology venue. The abstract's stated motivation is water, not the paranormal.

## 3. The EGU26 abstract — the final design and status

[Translated from EGU26-3985, "Testing Tradition: A Controlled Study on Dowsing Accuracy," EGU General Assembly 2026, Vienna, 3–8 May 2026, DOI 10.5194/egusphere-egu26-3985, abstract updated 13 March 2026, distributed CC BY 4.0. Author affiliations: ¹INRS, Québec; ²**citizen scientist and dowsing practitioner** — Manon Lévesque.]

> Dowsing is an ancient practice that employs rods or pendulums to locate groundwater and other subsurface features. Although it dates back several millennia and remains widely used today — by private individuals and, in some cases, by municipalities to locate the most easily accessible groundwater sources — dowsing relies largely on intuition and experiential knowledge rather than controlled scientific experimentation. This contrasts with hydrogeology, which emerged as a scientific discipline in the 19th century and is based on empirical observation, modeling, often complemented by other scientific disciplines, which involves significant costs. Field demonstrations of dowsing can appear convincing and are often viewed as cost-effective methods of selecting a location for drilling a new well; however, in regions characterized by sufficient rainfall and favorable geological conditions, groundwater is often ubiquitous. This raises questions about what constitutes a genuine "detection" and complicates meaningful comparisons between traditional and scientific approaches.
>
> In addition to general groundwater, dowsers frequently claim the ability to detect underground drains or pipes. These discrete, well-defined targets provide a more suitable basis for controlled experiments and statistical evaluation. In this context, we present the final results of a controlled dowsing experiment **co-designed by a multidisciplinary team of scientists in collaboration with an experienced dowsing practitioner**, with the aim of ensuring both methodological rigor and acceptance by the dowsing community.
>
> The experimental setup consisted of a grid divided into 25 cells, within which various objects were buried in selected locations. These objects included **iron and plastic pipes, either empty or filled with water**. When present, the water varied in composition and conditions, including saline or fresh water, as well as stagnant or flowing water. Participants were asked to scan the grid using either **wooden or metal rods** and to indicate the cells in which they believed an object was buried.
>
> A total of **54 participants** took part in the experiment: **27 with intermediate to extensive prior experience in dowsing, and 27 novices who received basic training** before participation. Several participants completed multiple trials to assess reproducibility. The results are analyzed and discussed as a function of **object type, dowsing instrument, and prior experience level**, providing a quantitative assessment of dowsing performance under controlled conditions.

Three things this abstract establishes beyond the 2025 press coverage:

1. **The study is complete.** "We present the final results" — the field season ran to autumn 2025, and the completed analysis was delivered at EGU Vienna in May 2026. The article Bordeleau promised is the remaining deliverable.
2. **The design hardened.** The pipes are specified as **iron** (not just "metal"), the participant split is exactly 27/27, several participants ran **repeat trials** for reproducibility, and the rod material (wood vs metal) is itself a tracked variable — a control Munich never had.
3. **A dowser co-designed it.** Manon Lévesque is credited as "citizen scientist and dowsing practitioner" — the experiment was explicitly built *with* the dowsing community "to ensure methodological rigor and acceptance by the dowsing community." This is the design answer to the standing objection that controlled tests never test what dowsers actually claim: the practitioners helped set the terms.

The EGU abstract also contains the study's sharpest epistemological point, in its own words: in water-rich regions "groundwater is often ubiquitous. This raises questions about what constitutes a genuine 'detection'" — a field dowser who "finds water" almost anywhere is unfalsifiable, which is precisely why the team moved to **discrete, well-defined targets** (pipes) where chance can be computed cell by cell.

## 4. What this design can see that Munich could not

The 1986–1988 University of Munich experiment (Wagner, Betz, Trenkle: ~€255,000, 500 applicants screened to 43 "credible" dowsers, 843 trials, a water-filled pipe on a randomly-moved cart under a false floor) tested **one** question: does dowsing beat chance, globally. It returned a null — with the famous residue that six of the 43 scored above chance, later shown to be the expected selection artifact (Tom Napier's 1999 PHACT computer simulation produced the same "six virtual dowsers" from pure chance), and with the Betz–Enright dispute over whether nonstandard statistics had manufactured even the residue (Enright 1995/1999; Betz's rebuttal; the SPR's Psi Encyclopedia carries the full exchange).

The INRS design is different in kind, not just scale:

| Dimension | Munich 1986–88 | INRS 2025–26 |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Water-filled pipe (moving, under false floor) | Buried pipes: **iron vs plastic**, empty vs water-filled, fresh vs salt, stagnant vs flowing |
| The null it can reject | Global chance only | Global chance **plus** per-material, per-content, per-experience, per-instrument effects |
| Operators | 43 pre-screened self-selected dowsers | 27 experienced + 27 trained novices (the novice arm *is* the control population) |
| Instrument | Dowser's own | Wood vs metal rods, tracked as a variable |
| Reproducibility | Series of trials | Repeat trials by same participant |
| Stance | Scientists testing dowsers | Scientists **co-designing with a dowser** |
| Venue | Commissioned report | ACFAS 92 + EGU General Assembly (hydrogeology sessions) |

The cross-tab is the point. Bordeleau's own hypothesis statement — "maybe overall, the plastic pipe is no better than chance, but the metal pipe is. Which could indicate that magnetism has a role to play" — defines an outcome Munich was structurally blind to: **a sub-effect that survives the global null**. If iron pipes are located above chance and plastic pipes are not, that is a positive, physical, directional claim — the first of its kind in the modern record. If nothing separates, the result strengthens the null in the one place a residual effect could plausibly have hidden.

## 5. The lineage: this is the direct test of Rocard's hypothesis

The magnetic hypothesis the grid is built to interrogate is not new — it is the **last serious scientific proposal in the dowsing literature**. Yves Rocard — the physicist whose name otherwise attaches to French nuclear policy — spent his late career claiming dowsers detect very weak magnetic fields with the body: the Science et Vie controversy of May 1963 ("Oui la radiesthésie est vraie !"), then "Le signal du sourcier" in *La Recherche* (no. 124, July–August 1981, p. 792), answered by Broch and the Comité Para, and the book *La science et les sourciers* (Bordas, 1989). The fr.wikipedia «Radiesthésie» entry (updated 2026-03-28) carries the whole arc and lands on the classification: pseudoscience, rod motion attributed to Carpenter's ideomotor effect, Munich/Cassel nulls.

Rocard never had a design that could isolate magnetism from everything else. The INRS grid is that design, forty years later: **iron vs plastic is the magnetism switch**, everything else held constant. Whatever the numbers turn out to be, the experiment settles whether Rocard's specific mechanism has a detectable trace — under field conditions, with practitioners who believe it works, on targets they themselves claim to detect (pipes, not just "water").

The Quebec press framing also quietly inverts the usual power arrangement: this is not a skeptic organization putting dowsers through a test, but a **water-resources research institute** asking whether a traditional practice carries usable signal for modern groundwater exploration — with climate-driven water scarcity as the stated motivation. The esoteric and the hydrogeological are made to "talk to each other anyway," in Bordeleau's words, on a numbered grid where the conversation can be scored.

## 6. Status and what to watch

- **Done:** field season (to autumn 2025, ~54 participants, ~50 by July 2025 and recruiting past it); ACFAS 92 abstract on record (2025-04-04); final results presented at EGU General Assembly 2026 (Vienna, 3–8 May 2026, EGU26-3985).
- **Forthcoming:** the peer-reviewed article with the actual numbers — per-material, per-content, per-experience, per-instrument hit rates against the 1-in-25 cell baseline; presentations and video capsules.
- **The three rows that matter when the article lands:**
  1. **Iron-pipe-only above chance** — the magnetic sub-effect; Rocard vindicated in mechanism even if the global null holds. The result that "risks making noise."
  2. **Experienced vs novices** — if experience adds nothing above the trained-novice baseline, the "gift" is learnable procedure or nothing; if it adds something, the first quantitative skill gradient in the modern record.
  3. **Wood vs metal rods** — if the rod is a passive pointer (ideomotor reading), instrument type should not matter; any instrument effect is itself a datum.
- **Family tracking note:** this is the verification rail's live item for the fr lane (scout FAL-fr-89-1). The article should be watched for at INRS publications / EGU proceedings around late 2026. If the iron-pipe row comes back above chance, it connects directly to the corpus's instrumented seams (the Lecher antenna bench note's physical-pedigree lane; the metal-detection claim class) and to Rocard's biomagnétisme lineage. If it comes back clean, it closes the last open physical door on the French dowsing tradition's central claim — and the corpus's honest record should say so with the same weight.
