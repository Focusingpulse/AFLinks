What is a morphic field? The morphic field is a controversial concept proposed by the British biochemist Rupert Sheldrake. According to this theory, morphic fields are a kind of immaterial structure that determines the shape and behavior of both living organisms and inanimate systems. In the context of humans, the morphic field would constitute a kind of "guide" for growth, development and behavior. Sheldrake suggests that such fields may also store a kind of collective memory that is available to all members of a given species. In other words, if one person learns a new skill, other people may learn it faster thanks to the existence of this shared field. Although the idea of the morphic field is intriguing and thought-provoking, it is worth noting that it has not found widespread acceptance in the scientific community. There is a lack of solid empirical evidence to confirm it, and most scientists consider the theory inconsistent with the current understanding of physics, chemistry and biology. This theory is often associated with esoteric and alternative fields rather than mainstream science.

What is morphic resonance? Morphic resonance is a key concept in the morphic field theory proposed by Rupert Sheldrake. Morphic resonance refers to the phenomenon in which similar patterns or structures in different places and times influence each other through a shared morphic field, without the need for direct transmission of information through the space and time known to us. According to this theory, if a certain group of organisms learns a new behavior, other similar organisms, even far apart, will be able to learn that behavior more easily and quickly. It would also explain phenomena such as "collective memory" among species, where the ability to learn certain skills or behaviors becomes easier as more individuals of a given species acquire them. Many scientists consider the theory of morphic resonance unsupported by evidence and inconsistent with existing scientific models. Nevertheless, the idea of morphic resonance has found its place in some alternative and esoteric circles, where it is combined with concepts such as life energy, telepathy or collective consciousness. Some claim it may explain certain phenomena that are not fully understood within current science.

The memory of nature in the context of the morphic field The memory of nature is a central concept in the morphic field theory proposed by the British biochemist Rupert Sheldrake. In the context of this theory, the memory of nature refers to the way in which information or "memories" are stored in morphic fields and can be made available to various organisms through a phenomenon called morphic resonance. According to Sheldrake, when an organism learns a certain behavior or develops a certain trait, this information is recorded in the morphic field. Other organisms of the same species, and sometimes even similar species, can access this information through resonance with that field, which can lead to faster acquisition of similar skills or traits. The memory of nature is a controversial concept and has not been widely accepted by scientists working in biology, chemistry or physics. However, for some proponents of the theory, this concept explains phenomena that are difficult to explain within conventional science, such as the spontaneous and simultaneous appearance of the same innovations or behaviors in different parts of the world. The idea of the memory of nature also suggests that not only living organisms but also inanimate systems may possess their own morphic fields, which store information about structure and form. This approach leads to a more holistic, integrated view of the universe, in which everything is connected through a network of mutually interacting morphic fields. The fields of the human mind The fields of the mind are a special aspect of Rupert Sheldrake's morphic field theory, which relates to the function and nature of consciousness. In this context, the fields of the mind are immaterial structures that shape thoughts, memories, and the mind in general, just as morphic fields shape structures and behaviors in the physical world. Sheldrake proposes that the mind is not bound exclusively to the brain, but extends beyond it, forming a field that can influence other minds through morphic resonance. This theory suggests that thoughts and memories are not stored in the brain, but rather in an immaterial field that is accessible through the whole network of similar fields.

The fields of the mind could explain phenomena such as telepathy, intuition or collective consciousness, which are difficult to explain within conventional scientific models of consciousness. It is claimed that through resonance with the mind fields of other people, an individual can gain access to information, experiences and thoughts that are not their own.
