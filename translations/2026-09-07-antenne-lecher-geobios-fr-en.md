# The Lecher Antenna: The Essential Tool of the Professional Geobiologist

The Lecher antenna is by far the best existing tool for geobiological detection, particularly when it comes to emanations from the subsoil.

The human body, thanks to the Lecher antenna, is capable of selecting information below the electromagnetic noise floor of virtually all existing devices.

The Lecher antenna was invented by the German physicist Reinhard Schneider. He named it in homage to the renowned physicist-engineer Ernst Lecher (1856–1926), the first to have demonstrated the speed of the electromagnetic wave using parallel Lecher wires in 1890. Professor Walter Kunnen improved it and made it a useful instrument for physicians.

## Use in Geobiology

### The Lecher Antenna: The Essential Tool in Professional Geobiology

Under certain conditions of use, the Lecher antenna is by far the best existing tool for geobiological detection. It is particularly effective for emanations from the subsoil.

The Lecher antenna is the essential tool of the professional geobiologist, who must use it during expert assessments for maximum precision.

The Lecher antenna is to date the most precise, rapid, reliable, and credible tool available for detecting harmful waves. Moreover, this tool allows detection in the vertical direction as well as in panoramic rotation. It can receive a polarity rod and also provides access to bioenergy.

### A Radiesthetic Tool Also Capable of Physical Detection

However, some who have not mastered it often criticize it for being a radiesthetic tool. Certain wavelength graduations do indeed correspond to radiesthetic conventions. But there are numerous wavelengths that are very useful in geobiology and correspond to physical, reproducible phenomena:

- **5.7:** magnetic field, magnetic north
- **7.8:** electric field
- **8.6:** ionizing gamma radiation
- **3.5:** radon and natural radioactivity
- **9.1:** hyperfrequencies
- **7.4:** emission due to forms
- …

By associating a specific wavelength with each type of detection, the Lecher antenna considerably reduces the risk of induction. As a result, this detection tool is much more reliable than all traditional radiesthetic instruments such as bent rods held in parallel (dowsing rods), pendulums, or lobe antennas!

### The Lecher Antenna Works on the Principle of Lecher Wires

A well-designed Lecher antenna operates on the principle of Lecher wires taught in physics. This discovery found a major application in radio broadcasting. Indeed, everyone has noticed that by moving the tuner on a transistor radio, one changes stations and therefore frequency.

The two metal branches held by the operator are connected to a closed circuit on which a slider (shunt) moves, allowing positioning at the antenna length corresponding to the exact wavelength sought, on a graduated rule in millimeters.

Its designers have meticulously calibrated the antenna lengths.

### A Downward Movement

The whole assembly thus forms a closed circuit powered by the operator, who serves as a "battery." Upon contact with the wavelength of the phenomenon being sought, the electromagnetic energy is converted into a mechanical effect. Like a dowsing rod, the antenna then dips automatically (even against one's will, impossible to hold it back!) toward the operator. One is then clearly in the presence of the phenomenon sought and can delineate it precisely.

## Learning to Use a Lecher Antenna

Taking up the Lecher antenna is not easy for a beginner: it does not work instantly. Learning requires solid training, time, and much daily practice. But the journey is truly worth it!

The French School of Geobiology emphasizes learning to handle the Lecher antenna through numerous practical exercises. This is why this geobiology training cannot be done remotely.

Mastery allows most students to make identical observations without prior consultation.

## Not All Antennas Are Equal…

Attention: not all Lecher antennas are equal. Beware of inexpensive antennas, which very often do not allow proper functioning and reliable detection.

For example, the base of some antennas being closed, they can neither receive waves in the extension of the metal wires, nor emit them.

The French School of Geobiology advises its students for a safe choice.
