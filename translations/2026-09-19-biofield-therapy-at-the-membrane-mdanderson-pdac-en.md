**An analysis of:** Peiying Yang, Daoyan Wei, Sharmistha Chakraborty, Phuong Nguyen, Andrew Cusimano, Defeng Deng, Shafaqmuhammad Iqbal, Monica Nelson, Meng Cui, Jianliang Dai, Mihai Gagea, Richard Wagner, Yisheng Li, Lorenzo Cohen — *"The Preclinical Effects and Mechanisms of Biofield Therapy on Pancreatic Cancer Cell Growth and Metastasis."* Cancer Medicine, published 2026-04-01. ## Why this paper is a bridge

The Aetherforce corpus lives on two banks of a river. On one bank: the outer rings — radiesthesia, biofield healing, subtle energy, the French physical school. On the other: bioelectricity — Michael Levin's program of endogenous voltage potentials controlling cell behavior, pattern, and cancer. The two banks rarely speak in the same units. The outer rings describe what a practitioner's hands do; the bioelectric program measures millivolts across a membrane.

This paper is, to my knowledge, the first time a top-3 US cancer center has reported a biofield effect **in the membrane-voltage currency itself** — not as warmth, not as "energy," but as a number from a voltage-sensitive dye: a **36.7% reduction in cell voltage potential** in PANC-1 pancreatic cancer cells (p < 0.01), with similar effects in MiaPaCa-2 and mouse KPCY cells.

That is Levin's language. Whatever one thinks of biofield therapy, this is the interface where it can be tested.

## What they did

- **Cells:** multiple mouse and human pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) lines — PANC-1, L3.7, COLO357, MiaPaCa-2, and mouse KPCY cells.
- **Exposure:** human biofield therapy (BT) delivered by **three different therapists using a standardized procedure** — not one gifted individual, which is the standard failure mode of this literature.
- **Controls:** sham control (SC — a person mimicking the procedure without the intent/practice, the design matters) and incubator control (IC — cells left alone). Both, in every experiment.
- **Mechanistic arm:** FOXM1 CRISPR knockout and Tet-on FOXM1 overexpressing PDAC cells — they tested whether the observed effects run through a named transcription factor, not just "viability went down."
- **In vivo:** orthotopic mouse models with both human PANC-1 and mouse KPCY cells — tumor growing where pancreatic tumors grow, metastasis to liver as the endpoint.

## What they found

1. **Proliferation:** significantly reduced across multiple lines and multiple studies, vs both controls.
2. **Ultrastructure:** swollen mitochondria in PANC-1 cells — visible physical damage to the cell's power plant.
3. **Cell cycle:** increased G1 arrest in PANC-1, L3.7, COLO357 — cells stacking up before DNA replication.
4. **Molecular:** downregulation of key cell-cycle regulators including **FOXM1** (p < 0.05). The invasiveness reduction is FOXM1-dependent (shown by the KO/overexpression arms).
5. **The headline:** **36.7% reduction in cell voltage potential**, measured with DiBAC4(3), a bis-oxonol anionic dye whose fluorescence is inversely proportional to membrane potential — a standard tool in the Levin-lab arsenal.
6. **In vivo:** reduced primary tumor growth and liver metastasis in both orthotopic models (p < 0.05).

## The lineage this lands in

The paper's own reference list carries the whole 55-year arc of the voltage-controls-mitosis hypothesis:

- **Cone 1971** (*Journal of Theoretical Biology*) — the ancestral paper: a unified theory of normal mitotic control and oncogenesis via transmembrane potential. This is where the idea that voltage gates cell division was first stated cleanly.
- **Lobikin, Chernet, Lobo & Levin 2012** (*Physical Biology*) — resting potential, oncogene-induced tumorigenesis, and metastasis: the bioelectric basis of cancer in vivo.
- **Chernet & Levin 2014** (*Oncotarget*) — transmembrane voltage potential of somatic cells controls oncogene-mediated tumorigenesis **at long range**.
- **Levin 2014** (*Molecular Biology of the Cell*) — the program statement of molecular bioelectricity.

A biofield paper that cites Cone and Levin in both directions is not describing "energy" in the colloquial sense. It is claiming: *the practitioner's effect, whatever its nature, terminates on the membrane — and the membrane's voltage is a known, causal regulator of the cell cycle.*

For the Aetherforce thesis (bioenergy as layered communication along intelligence pathways), this is the sharpest available articulation of the seam: if there is a signal, the paper says, its measurable terminus is bioelectric.

## Controls audit — what's strong

- **Three therapists, standardized procedure.** Single-healer studies are uninterpretable; this design tests the *practice*, not a person.
- **Two control arms** (sham + incubator) distinguish "a human was present doing something" from "nothing happened."
- **A named molecular pathway** (FOXM1) with gain- and loss-of-function tests — the effect is anchored to a mechanism the field already knows drives PDAC progression.
- **In vivo confirmation** in two orthotopic models, including the metastasis endpoint, not just dish viability.
- **Institutional provenance:** MD Anderson integrative oncology group (Cohen lab, h-index 63), NCI funding, and a prior-methods paper (Cohen et al. 2024, *Scientific Reports*) that simultaneously measured electrophysiological and cellular outcomes during BT.
- The reference list even includes a **retracted** biofield trial (Trivedi et al.) — cited as such. The group polices its own literature.

## The open seams — where this still needs work

Being useful means being calibrated. What the paper does **not** establish:

1. **The transmission gap.** Nothing in the methods explains how the therapist's state reaches the dish. No field was measured between practitioner and cells (no magnetometer, no electrometer, no photon counter). The effect is real *at the membrane* and invisible *in transit*. This is exactly the gap the "handover distance" concept (Coimbra, *ISME Journal*, Sep 2026) is built to name: the effective range over which a bioelectric perturbation elicits a response in a recipient. The natural next experiment — instrument the space between the hands and the dish — has an obvious design and hasn't been run here.
2. **DiBAC4 measures relative, not absolute, potential.** A 36.7% fluorescence shift is a strong signal in this assay class, but the mapping to millivolts requires calibration curves the abstract doesn't state.
3. **Single lab, preclinical, 2 citations.** The biofield literature's replication record is poor (the field's own retraction, cited above, is evidence). Until an independent group repeats the DiBAC4 result, it stands as one well-controlled observation.
4. **Blinding of the exposure itself** is the hard problem in this genre — the paper's sham control addresses it as well as the design allows, but therapist-knowledge asymmetries remain a known residual.

## What this means for the corpus

- For the **verification rail**: this is the new primary anchor for the biofield lane — stronger than the survey/clinical-adjacent material, weaker than a replication. It slots beside the INRS dowsing ledger (protocol-verified null/verdict pending) as the "instrumented" end of the spectrum.

If it isn't, they can kill it. Both outcomes are progress.

The MD Anderson paper supplies the endpoint; Coimbra supplies the metric; the corpus already holds the controls doctrine.
