# Louis Turenne — A Biographical Sketch

*"A few glimpses of his life…"*

Monsieur Louis Turenne was born in Paris on March 21, 1872, into a family of industrialists and dealers in pumping equipment and water installations (pumps, turbines, electric motors, etc.).

He studied in Paris, graduating as an engineer from the École Centrale des Arts et Manufactures at the age of 19.

The war of 1914–1918, during which he was wounded (at Verdun) as an artillery battalion chief, earned him the Croix de guerre and the Légion d'Honneur. After his wound, in 1917, he was appointed Professor of T.S.F. (wireless telegraphy) at the Artillery School of Fontainebleau under the orders of General Ferrié. It was his relations with that scholar which developed in him the taste for scientific research, particularly in the domain of "waves," to which he little by little devoted himself entirely. His family's activities having put him in contact with water-diviners, Louis Turenne took an early interest in subtle waves, working in every possible direction, with a particular passion for geology and mining prospecting. It was he who, in Morocco, determined the phosphate deposits and the Sidi Harazem mineral water source, comparable to Perrier.

He set up his laboratories in Paris on the Rue de Chazelles, followed official scientific research very closely, and carried out special wave-control experiments which he mentions in the books he would write under the title:

*"From the Hazel Rod to the Prospector's Detectors"* (De la baguette de Coudrier aux détecteurs du Prospecteur)

Louis Turenne was the first to announce the existence of a "93rd body" in Mendeleev's classification and to give its characteristics. A few years later, the Italian physicist Enrico Fermi would isolate it, but he paid homage to Turenne only in 1940. It would be named: Neptunium. Taking refuge at Eymoutiers (Haute-Vienne) from 1943 to 1947 to escape a demand for collaboration from Germany (?), he took the opportunity to map the radium deposits in the Limousin region, but was not followed up by official research.

*"After the war, all the drilling confirmed his plans."*

In 1947 Louis Turenne settled in Nice, continuing his work with his numerous correspondents: Doctor BEBEL of Lausanne, Dr. ROUY and Dr. MACHETOUT in Paris, Abbé LAMBERT, Abbé MERMET. They verified the work of LORENZ, all the strains of the Boiron laboratories, etc.

In 1952 he was prosecuted by the Order of Physicians of Nice, but the trial, held in October–November 1952, ended with purely symbolic damages of one franc.

Madame Turenne and Colonel De Blois were precious collaborators; they trained numerous students. Monsieur Turenne died in Nice on June 30, 1954, at the age of 82, but his work would continue in the spirit he had adopted.

Books IX and X would be published after his death by "Omnium Littéraire" in Paris.

## Preface to Book IX (L. Turenne)

I wanted to reach my 80th birthday and the results of my numerous measurements before publishing this work. I would like, at the end of my life, to have created the scientific instrument that can unite in a single science (the Science of Waves) all the Methods, ancient and new, whose purpose is to relieve humanity and to make the passage on Earth less painful for the unfortunate sufferers of ill health, so numerous in every country. I thank the Physicians, the Pharmacists, and my numerous Students who kindly helped me with their competence, their advice, their encouragement in the pursuit of this scientific work of devotion to our fellow beings. May my work be understood as a creative work of rapprochement among all the methods tending toward the betterment of "humans." (Louis Turenne, 1952)

## Funeral Eulogy of Monsieur Turenne, pronounced by Paul Reboux

…After the death of Auguste Lumière, we could believe that the cruel fate that had just struck French science had been sated.

The sudden death of Louis Turenne is a new blow of fate. All those who know the importance of the work he leaves behind, all those who knew the power of work and thought he preserved to his last day, were shattered by this tragic news.

When so fine an intelligence disappears, one feels the world lose one of its riches. When a man so good, so compassionate toward all suffering, so relentless in finding the means to improve human existence, to push back pain, to make death wait, dies, one has the feeling that a shadow has spread over us.

One of the lights of the mind has just gone out abruptly. And one is beginning to understand that Louis Turenne was, in the matter of "human waves," the equivalent of what Claude Bernard was in physiology and Louis Pasteur in bacteriology. He guided the gropings of a science being reborn. He brought back to experiment, reason, and discipline a practice that minds of good will but too-rich imagination confused with magical practices. The extent of his work will appear more fully when the "science of wave capture," which has made giant progress in recent years, becomes — like homeopathy and chiropractic — an official science, for the greatest good of the physicians whose indispensable auxiliary it will have become, and of the patients who will owe it their cure.

From the beginning of his career, Louis Turenne was a scholar listened to with fervor by the young officer-students to whom he taught T.S.F. in a great official school.

His clear-sightedness, his discernment, his concern never to depart from what can be measured and proven, allowed him to compose treatises nourished by mathematical precision, on the Science of Waves, Radium, the Law of Similars, Radioactivity, heavy waters, and so many other subjects…

He created precision instruments for all those who ventured into the incoherence of the new sciences. He made fascinating initiations possible.

He lifted the veil on Mysterious Laws. He revealed them and fixed them. He penetrated an unexplored zone of discovery.

The marvels he revealed earned him a renown against which rose, as is the custom, the envious, the jealous, the backward. He was attacked, persecuted. He was made to appear, this venerated scholar, before a criminal court! He was first acquitted, then condemned, by hypocritical verdicts that gave small concessions to his adversaries while proclaiming the dignity and integrity of the accused.

Bah! These are the signs by which glory is recognized. The higher one is placed, the more dangers one runs. Louis Turenne, half a century ago, was one of the first "scientific water-diviners." Now, forty thousand disciples consider him one of the most eminent doctrinaires of the art they once practiced empirically.

He gave the waves "their science," as Branly refined the principles of wireless transmission, as the Lumière brothers perfected the cinema.

It is to these great names that the name of Louis Turenne must be compared.

He will live in the History of French Science.

He will live in the heart of all who could know what a Friend he was.
