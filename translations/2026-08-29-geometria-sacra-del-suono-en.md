## Sound and Vibration

The study of sacred geometry has been handed down for thousands of years by the ancient mystery schools. The most common geometries considered sacred are the Egyptian Flower of Life, the Hebrew Vesica Piscis, and the Italian Borromean Rings (also known as the Holy Trinity).

Naturally, there are many others, such as the Kabbalah, the Seed of Life, the Platonic Solids, the Fruit of Life, and so on.

These geometries are sacred because they are the patterns by which creation manifests itself: from stars to trees, these patterns are found throughout all of reality. One of the easiest ways to see this is simply by looking at nature.

This geometry was described as "emanations" by the Gnostics, indicating their awareness that they were patterns formed by the intersection or interference of waves.

And since music travels in waves, sacred geometry became intertwined with the *musica universalis*, the ancient theory of everything. Through musical proportions, everything could be modeled using sacred geometry.

To see how it works, consider the following musical chromatic ring divided into twelve equal sections according to the earlier harmonic color model. This is known as the cyclic ring model Z/12Z, where Z is the set of integers from 1 to 12.

The labels indicate the bilateral symmetry between each half of the ring as divided by the harmonic axis through the harmonic center, or the 9th partial harmonic. The spring symbols are used to indicate the regions in which the harmonics share energy, i.e., between the tritone and the major third tonic.

From the chromatic ring, a double-ring system can be used to geometrically represent the standard Fourier wave consisting of complementary sine and cosine wave components in phase-quadrature (out of phase by 90 degrees).

For the ancients, this interlaced ring arrangement was known as the Vesica Piscis, or the Mandorla, referring to the union section of the two rings. In the Hebrew Kabbalah, all life is described as entering the world through the interference region of the mandorla between the two rings.

This can be applied in many ways, from the human being born through a woman's vagina, to the shape of a seed that germinates and becomes a tree.

## The double torus

The double rings are a vertical section of the double torus produced by the Coriolis effect — a geometry found in the formation of galaxies and our solar system. Life itself is toroidal, like a hurricane, twisting around an axis, as seen in the whorl of hair at the crown of the head. When we are born, the birth canal is truly a mandorla.

For these and other reasons, the mandorla is used to frame religious icons and divinities in Christianity, indicating their origin from the matrix of creation.

Going beyond religious interpretations, we can better understand the physical properties of the double rings by separating the twelve tones into two groups of six, divided between the rings — one group in the upper ring to represent the sinusoidal components in phase with the harmonic center (D for the harmonic series of C), and another group in the lower ring to represent the complementary cosinic components.

The two groups of tones are known as whole-tone scales, of which only these two exist.

Interestingly, the mandorla corresponds to the so-called 'dominant' interval (e.g., {G, B} in C major), used in common practice music to create tension and resolve to the tonic.

This so-called 'dominant–tonic' resolution is also used as a central element in musical harmony to chain a series of chords in what is called the Cycle of Fifths. When represented as an interlocking Vesica Piscis lattice, dominant triads can be seen as a cascade through a twelve-tone orbit.

When this same lattice progression is then transformed into a lattice of orthogonal waves using dominant seventh chords, something truly surprising happens: the Cycle of Fifths traces the profile of a fish.

Indeed, the tense leading tone is added upon itself in such a way as to produce a prominent fin geometry, appearing very similar to a shark.

A sacred geometry!
