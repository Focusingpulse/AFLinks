Abstract. From the system of equations of compressible oscillating ether, the following have previously been derived: a generalized nonlinear Maxwell-Lorentz system of equations invariant under Galilean transformations, whose linearization yields the classical Maxwell-Lorentz system of equations; the Biot-Savart-Laplace, Ampère, and Coulomb laws; representations for the Planck and fine-structure constants; formulas for the electron, proton, and neutron; and an ether theory of the atom and atomic nucleus has been developed. In the present work, an ether theory of gravity is constructed, and the similarities and differences between gravitational and electrostatic fields are explained. It is shown that gravity does not involve attractive forces, but rather pressing (compressive) forces, and that the gravitational constant is not in fact constant, but weakly depends on the chemical composition of the interacting bodies. Gravitational interactions between bodies do not propagate from one body to another at some velocity; rather, at any moment in time, the gravitational fields of all bodies exist together with and around the bodies, for which reason neither gravitational waves nor gravitons exist in nature. Using the experimental values of the gravitational constant, the values of all ether parameters are found, including the density of its unperturbed state.

Keywords: Compressible oscillating ether, charges, electrostatic and gravitational fields, gravitational constant, ether parameters.

1. INTRODUCTION

      Gravity is one of the most mysterious physical phenomena. No modern physical theory is capable of clearly explaining the nature of this phenomenon. In Newtonian theory, gravity is identified with the gravitational interaction of material bodies, which is described by the very simple law of universal gravitation:

In formula (1.1), we are talking about two forces connecting two bodies, equal in magnitude and directed toward each other along a straight line, with which, by Newton's third law, the bodies act upon each other. However, Newtonian mechanics prefers not to answer the question of the mechanism of transmission of gravitational interaction from one body to another, relying on

It is considered that any body of mass m creates around itself a gravitational field with strength E = Gm/r², having the dimension of acceleration m·s⁻² in the SI system. But then, as follows from experimental data, the speed of transmission of gravitational interaction must exceed the speed of light by several orders of magnitude, which has no reasonable explanation. An attempt to resolve this paradox is Einstein's general theory of relativity, according to which gravity is considered not as the propagation of a force interaction in space, but is allegedly the result of masses somehow miraculously curving the four-dimensional spacetime continuum in their vicinity. This hypothesis is merely a geometric abstraction contradicting common sense, and can in no way be considered an explanation of gravity. For all experimental observational data that allegedly confirm the theory of relativity, there exist alternative and far more reasonable explanations.
