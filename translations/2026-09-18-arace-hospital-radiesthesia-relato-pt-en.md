## Abstract (translation of the Resumo)

This experience report describes the implementation of Radiesthetic Radionics (RR) in an Integrative Medicine outpatient clinic — Integrative and Complementary Health Practices (MI-PICS) — in the municipality of São Paulo. Begun in February 2025, the initiative covers care for inpatients and outpatients, in face-to-face, online, and remote modalities. It was identified that RR works by accessing information codes through instruments that decode bioenergetic signals. The integration between physiology and bioenergetics offered theoretical support for understanding the mental and spiritual dimensions as modulators of physical health, removing the stigma of subjectivity from these spheres and bringing them closer to evidence-based psychosomatic clinical practice.

**Keywords:** Radiesthesia. Complementary Therapies. Integrative Medicine.

## 1 Introduction

This article reports the experience of implementing Radiesthetic Radionics (RR) in the outpatient clinic of Integrative and Complementary Health Practices (MI-PICS) of the Hospital do Servidor Público Municipal de São Paulo (HSPM), a hospital with experience in the implementation of unconventional therapies since the 1980s. Over all these years these therapies have acted as an adjunct to the classical medical care practiced in the hospital, and the activities are conducted primarily by volunteer therapists who hold expertise in various therapeutic approaches.

The PICS [Integrative and Complementary Practices] bring unconventional visions of care, encompassing a plurality of knowledges and transversal competencies, increasingly confirmed by research and observed by users — such that in 2006 the Brazilian Ministry of Health (MS) incorporated the National Policy of Integrative Practices (PNPIC) (BRASIL, 2006).

Today the contribution of techniques that work on well-being and quality of life in care is unquestionable. Although many of them have not yet been confirmed by science, it is a fact that popular wisdom, traditional medicines, uses and customs have contributed to health over the millennia, and they have gradually been incorporated into the therapeutic arsenal of health systems around the world.

In the contemporary world, with the complexity inherent in social, environmental, and individual dynamics, the origin of organic and psychic imbalances has become multifactorial — hence the need for multidisciplinary knowledge to help the individual in their totality.

The foundations for inserting this technique into the outpatient clinic are intimately related to energy-manipulation techniques whose action is already well established. Traditional Chinese Medicine — Acupuncture was incorporated in 1991, bringing an understanding of illness under the perspective of a millennia-old medicine that considers the intrinsic, extrinsic, and relational factors affecting the human being, which made it possible to broaden the vision of care and prepare the ground for the most varied therapies that would be inserted into the outpatient clinic in the decades that followed.

Everything in the universe is in constant movement; Einstein already understood that matter and energy are interchangeable. Modern physics reinforces that matter is not solid; in any object the atoms are always spinning and vibrating — everything moves, oscillates, and vibrates. Among the forms of energy there are the concrete ones that propagate in waves and can be measured, such as thermal, electrical, kinetic, mechanical, electromagnetic, chemical, and nuclear energy. But there are other forms of energy; among them are vital energy, thought energy, and form energy [energia de formas] that move around the physical body, influencing the environment and interfering with the way people think, act, and feel.

Subtle, abstract, or non-materialized energy has been recognized in various ways through the ages, even though established science denies validity to these concepts. Physicists define it as zero-point energy — that is, the energy existing before it materializes into form (GODOY, 2012).

The 18th and 19th centuries are the historical period of the French magnetizers; for some it was a time marked by mysticism, charlatanism, and ignorance, but for others it appears as a pre-scientific moment that would later give way to a modern project of science. It was in that era that the Austrian physician Franz Anton Mesmer coined the term *animal magnetism*, referring to the life energy that links spirit to matter, based on the belief that there was a fluid that could be transmitted for therapeutic purposes (NEUBERN, 2007) (SILVA). Later, Reich investigated a universal vital energy he named Orgone; although his observations are considered controversial, they are a legacy for the connection between body, emotion, and vital energy (VOLPI, 2025). The universal energy called Prana has been reported for more than 5,000 years in India; Yoga works with these energies through breathing techniques, physical exercise, and meditation. The Chinese, 5,000 years ago, already spoke of a vital energy called Chi that pervades all matter, animate or inanimate. In this perspective, the human being is constituted by an inseparability between the physical body and the bioenergetic field, which can be detected by various methods such as Radiesthesia (GODOY, 2012).

It is an undeniable fact that radiations coming from space, from the environment, from the subsoil, from people are capable of biologically influencing life — of animals, plants, or human beings. Most of these interactions, though constant, are not clearly perceived by any of the senses (PANTZIER, 2007).

The universe vibrates at very similar frequencies — the so-called Schumann waves, which some researchers argue are the frequency of health. Winfried Otto Schumann in 1952 predicted that weak electromagnetic waves ricochet between the ionosphere and the Earth's surface, generating a fundamental frequency of 7.8 Hz; similar vibrations were later identified in various living organisms and in the human being as the fundamental frequency of rest (MARTEL et al., 2023).

## 2 Radiesthetic Radionics

The word Radiesthesia comes from the Greek *radius* (radiation) and the Latin *aesthesis* (sensitivity) — that is, sensitivity to radiations (NETO). Radionics, in turn, is the action at a distance that deals with energetic balance and management, capturing and sending specific energies (GODOY, 2012).

According to Pantzier (2007), Radiesthesia is a method of analysis that identifies vibratory patterns of very low amplitude, of the most varied vibrations — so tenuous that they are almost undetectable; often it is purely informational content with no energetic charge.

It is the radiesthesist, with their sensitivity and concentration, who is capable of accessing these information codes. To do so they use the silent and significant signals of instruments such as the Aurameter, the Pendulum, the Fork [dowsing rod / forquilha], the Dual Rod, or others identified by the conscious mind. Perceiving only the movement and the direction of the tool, without becoming rationally involved in it — for it arises from a neuromuscular command, captured by intermediate aspects of the brain that bridge the deep unconscious and the waking state (PANTZIER, 2007).

Some theories start from the principle that the mind is capable of capturing anything: the radiesthesist tunes in to the object of evaluation, present or absent, and adjusts the movements of the pendulum (GODOY, 2012).

The sensitivity of the radiesthesist is a debated topic — so much so that the Ministry of Science and Technology of Germany conducted 10,000 trials with radiesthesists and found that only five percent of these professionals possessed exceptional radiesthetic abilities, with a success rate close to 100%. This study was replicated with 60 students of the Applied Naturology course at the University of Southern Santa Catarina (UNISUL), with very similar results: five percent of the people tested showed high radiesthetic sensitivity (PANTZIER, 2007).

Radiesthesia is a complementary way to identify bioenergetic imbalances; in the context of the PICS, it aligns with the holistic paradigm of care, in which the individual is seen as an integrated vibrational system. Radionics acts as the intervention phase, using geometric forms and frequencies for the homeostasis of the system.

RR is **not** among the techniques recognized by the MS [Ministry of Health] as an integrative practice; there is a scarcity of studies detailing the practical verification of subtle energy in clinical settings. This report proposes to describe an experience of applying RR in an outpatient and hospital setting, offering a reflective basis on how these different dimensions interrelate during the health-illness process.

## 3 Description of the Experience

This is an experience report on the introduction of Radiesthetic Radionics into the arsenal of therapies available at the MI-PICS outpatient clinic of HSPM. This therapy came to complement the therapeutic options available at the clinic, where a gap had already been identified given the demands of the patients who sought the outpatient service.

The therapy began to be offered in February 2025 by a volunteer therapist, who performed 183 sessions during 2025. Most patients arrived by spontaneous demand, since the service is publicized on the hospital's website, on notice boards, and by referral from patients already seen.

Others came referred by hospital professionals (mostly physicians and social workers), among them principally patients in terminality, coming from the Palliative Care Clinic, which aims at quality of life in this moment, offering care not only to the physical aspects but also to the emotional, spiritual, and psychosocial dimensions. The technique was well received by the patients and demonstrated the possibility of greater control of pain and suffering.

The sessions were conducted at the hospital bedside, in the outpatient clinic face-to-face, online via platform, and remotely for bedridden patients. The complaints leading patients to seek the sessions were the most varied: from anxiety, depression, sudden mood change, history of violence, stomach discomfort, weight on the shoulders, sore throat — to no complaint at all, only curiosity about whether something could be done to help.

## 4 The RR Technique

After a brief anamnesis, in which the patient presents their complaints and gives permission to access their field, the pendulum and the radionic table [mesa radiônica] are used as instruments. The therapist then identifies alterations in the patient's vibratory field using the movements of the pendulum over the radionic table, perceiving energies in imbalance that influence the patient's well-being.

This information refers to the organic systems; to interferences of external origin — including imbalances of subtle energy, telluric energy, of other people — or to internal imbalance such as fear, insecurity, rigid ideas, and negative feelings. Each imbalance is identified by the therapist, who acts intentionally.

With the help of the patient, who points out their pains, feelings, doubts, memories, it is possible to achieve a great result in the therapeutic session, culminating in their well-being.

The therapeutic follow-up is grounded in the continuous evaluation of the bioenergetic flow, understanding the individual as an integrated multidimensional system. During the sessions, a mapping of the functional vitality of organs and systems is carried out, correlating biological homeostasis with the states of the psyche and spiritual health. This analysis expands to the examination of the energy centers (chakras), understood here as nuclei of subtle-energy processing that act at the interface between the somatic body and the mental dimensions. The systematic verification of these fields makes it possible to identify patterns of imbalance before they somatize, ensuring an intervention that promotes harmony among the biological, the emotional, and the transpersonal.

The clinical practice adopted transcends the conventional biomedical model by instituting a protocol of systematic multidimensional evaluation. As the interventions are applied, a qualitative and quantitative analysis of the bioenergetic field proceeds, on the premise that pathology or well-being are emergent states of the interaction between different levels of organization. At the somatic level, the functional efficiency of organs and systems is monitored; at the psychodynamic level, how mental states and beliefs influence the patient's resilience is investigated. Additionally, the investigation covers the subtle dimension, specifically through the analysis of the chakras (force centers). These are understood as energetic transducers that regulate the flow of vitality between the etheric body and the endocrine and nervous systems. The verification of the spiritual dimension, in turn, is not restricted to religious dogmas but focuses on the perception of the individual's purpose and connection — factors that the current scientific literature recognizes as modulators of the immune response and of clinical recovery.

## 5 Final Considerations

The introduction of Radiesthetic Radionics into the therapeutic arsenal of the MI-PICS outpatient clinic allowed a significant expansion in the model of care offered, providing an integrative vision between the vitality of organs and systems and the alignment of the energy centers. The systematic verification of the mental and spiritual dimensions made it possible to identify that therapeutic success lies not only in the suppression of physical symptoms but in the restoration of the individual's vibratory coherence.

The experience consolidated the understanding that the human being operates as an inseparable unit. Further, the study reinforced the importance of qualified listening and perceptive sensitivity beyond the dense body. For the field of study, the principal contribution lies in the systematization of a gaze that unites physiology with bioenergetics, offering a theoretical subsidy so that other professionals can understand the role of the spiritual and mental dimensions as modulators of physical health, removing the stigma of subjectivity from these spheres and bringing them closer to evidence-based psychosomatic clinical practice.

It is recommended that future studies directly explore the alignment of chakras and the functional improvement of corresponding organs. The implementation of care protocols that include the education of the patient about their own energetic anatomy is also suggested, promoting self-care. Finally, we point to the need for greater integration between conventional medicine and multidimensional approaches, aiming at a truly holistic and less fragmented model of health.

## Dossier notes (Forge, translation-qc)

**What this artifact is.** The first peer-review-adjacent journal record of hospital radiesthesia/radionics in Brazil: a volunteer-therapist program at HSPM (Hospital do Servidor Público Municipal de São Paulo), running since February 2025, 183 sessions in 2025, face-to-face/online/remote, with palliative-care referrals. The scout's fire-87/96 carry ("hospital tier exists") closes with a citable artifact. The journal (ARACÊ, ISSN 2358-2472) is a fast-turnaround open outlet — submitted 2026-04-14, published 2026-05-14 — not a clinical-research journal; this is an *experience report* (relato de experiência), a genre that describes practice without outcome data.

**What verifies.**
- The regulatory framing is honest: the article states plainly that "RR is **not** among the techniques recognized by the MS as an integrative practice." Verified independently: the PNPIC's 29 recognized practices (Portaria 971/2006: 5; Portaria 849/2017: +14; Portaria 702/2018: +10) include reiki, imposition of hands, chromotherapy, geotherapy — but **not radiesthesia or radionics**. The relato's institutional footing is therefore the *hospital's own* volunteer program, not a Ministry recognition. This is the correct reading and the article deserves credit for saying so.
- The Pantzier 5% claim is double-attested in the article (German 10,000-trial battery; UNISUL n=60 student replication, "very similar results"). Caveat for the corpus: the "German Ministry of Science 10,000-trial" study is cited *via Pantzier's 2007 book only* — no primary citation, and no such study is traceable in German evaluation literature (the Munich 1986–88 SRT trials, ~500 candidates, remain the actual landmark, with null results). Treat the 10,000-trial/5% figure as the tradition's self-citation, not as established fact. The UNISUL replication would be the more findable primary (Applied Naturology course, Universidade do Sul de Santa Catarina) — a watch item.
- The ideomotor concession: the article quotes Pantzier that the instrument's movement "arises from a neuromuscular command" — the Carpenter/Chevreul ideomotor effect, stated without naming it, and then *not followed to its skeptical conclusion*. The corpus's Eau Hexagonale guide (2026-09-17) carries the calibrated version of exactly this position: keep the practice, drop the detection claim.

**What doesn't verify.** No outcome rows of any kind: no pain scores, no symptom scales, no counts by complaint, no follow-up. "Well received by the patients and demonstrated the possibility of greater control of pain and suffering" is the evidentiary ceiling of the whole document. The theoretical layer (zero-point energy as "the energy existing before it materializes into form"; Schumann 7.83 Hz as "the frequency of health") is carried uncritically from Godoy & Deguchi's 2012 textbook. The chakra–endocrine transduction model is asserted, not measured.

This relato is the third leg: the *institutional practice* tier — a public hospital running radiesthesia sessions for a year inside a PNPIC-adjacent clinic while the practice itself sits outside the Ministry's recognized list.
