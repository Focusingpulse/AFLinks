# Radiesthesia: History, Scientific Evidence, and Applications

Here are some words from Abbé Bouly, creator of the word *radiesthesia*:

> "We live in an ocean of radiations that we do not perceive: invisible effluvia emanating from all things. It is merely a matter of detecting their existence by constituting oneself a living detector. A fragile antenna allows one to more easily capture the hidden radiations: it is the famous dowser's rod. I am but a seeker of vibrations, and that is all..."

## What is radiesthesia?

Radiesthesia is an art that relies on the use of radiations or magnetic waves. For some it is a science, and its practice is based on observations. The word radiesthesia comes from the Latin prefix *radio-* (radiation, from *radius*, ray) and the Greek *aisthesis* (sensation). It therefore means "aptitude to perceive radiations."

The applications of radiesthesia are many and varied throughout history. Radiesthesia is practiced primarily with a divinatory pendulum or a dowser's rod.

## The History of Radiesthesia

Radiesthesia has been used for centuries for various purposes.

### Radiesthesia in Egypt

In Egypt, excavations carried out in tombs in the Valley of the Kings have uncovered pierced wooden balls, through which a thread passes, bearing a striking resemblance to radiesthesia pendulums. There is no proof that the Egyptians used this object to practice radiesthesia.

However, it is known to have been used in ancient Egypt in magic rituals or esoteric practices. It has since served as inspiration for the famous Egyptian pendulums — the Ouadj amulet or Thoth pendulum — highly prized by certain radiesthesists today.

### The Mention of Radiesthesia in the Bible

According to biblical tradition, Moses had the reputation of being an excellent dowser, making water spring from the rock (Numbers 20:11). He is in fact its patron saint.

### Radiesthesia in the Time of the Greeks and Romans

The Greeks and Romans used rods for divinatory purposes and to detect underground water sources. Julius Caesar's troops regularly used rods to find water sources, very useful for choosing the encampment sites of the legions. In the Middle Ages, the search for water sources was vital. Dowsers used rods to locate them more easily. It was during this period that the confusion between *sourcier* (dowser) and *sorcier* (sorcerer) was born.

### Development of Magnetic Medicine in the 17th Century

In the 17th century, during the spread of "magnetic medicine" in the lineage of Mesmer, rods began to be used in the search for mineral ores.

The famous Beausoleil couple successfully carried out numerous ore prospecting expeditions across Europe before being brought back to France by the Superintendent of Mines of the kingdom. He wished to have them find as many deposits as possible in French soil to replenish the state's coffers.

The Beausoleil couple had developed a technique based on the creation of 7 different rods. Each was used to detect a specific metal:

- The *lumineuse* (luminous) for gold
- The *sautante* (leaping) for copper
- The *éblouissante* (dazzling) for silver
- The *battante* (beating) for tin
- The *trépidante* (trembling) for lead
- The *tombante* (falling) for iron
- The *relevante* (rising) for mercury

The Beausoleil couple discovered more than 150 mines and numerous springs! Despite this, they ended their days in prison, prosecuted for witchcraft at Richelieu's request.

It was also in the 17th century that the first pendulums appeared in their most basic form (a ring suspended from a thread).

### The Term "Radiesthesia" Appears in the 20th Century

It was much later, in 1929, that the term "radiesthesia" appeared, coined by Abbé Bouly. We also owe him numerous discoveries: deep springs, Merovingian cemeteries, underground passages in medieval castles...

A surprising fact can be observed throughout the 20th century: most of the great radiesthesists belonged to the clergy. Besides Abbé Bouly, one can cite Abbé Mermet (inventor of distance radiesthesia, or *télé-radiesthésie*), Abbé Bayard, and Reverend Father Bourdoux. Why such enthusiasm for radiesthesia among the clergy? Prayer, self-mastery, and meditation — practices to which men of the church devote themselves — are certainly not unrelated to this.

The word radiesthesia became so popular that it nearly caused the disappearance of the term *rhabdomancy* (the art of divination by rod), which had been used until then.

### Radiesthesia Today

With the rise of modern medicine, radiesthesia has unfortunately become less and less recognized. Nevertheless, it continues to be used for various purposes such as the search for water and metals. Access to water was not once as easy as it is today, particularly in certain regions of the world, and a dowser was of great help. Using rods, pendulums, or other tools, the magnetizers of old also spotted the places where they could find metals, just as today's electronic detectors do.

## Radiesthesia According to Science

### The Work of Professor Yves Rocard

The practice of radiesthesia is based on the idea that the dowser's organism reacts to variations in the Earth's magnetic field caused by the presence of water or metals in the ground. This theory is defended by Professor Yves Rocard, who discovered the existence of magnetic receptor centers in human beings.

The American physicist R.N. Baker, for his part, discovered the physical agent that makes dowsers sensitive to the presence of running water — the presence of magnetite in the human body. Human sensitivity to water is the most common. According to Dr. Rocard, 50% of individuals who try it can feel the rod turn in their hand in the presence of underground water. Try it for yourself!

Professor Yves Rocard was a recognized French physicist who practiced radiesthesia. In the second half of his career, he published studies on radiesthesia, including *La science et les sourciers* (Science and Dowsers), which earned him sharp criticism.

While radiesthesia is incessantly called a pseudo-science, he never ceased to defend it. He refers to two American researchers, Kirschvink and Gould, who assert that all living beings are magnetic. These two biologists proved that human cells contain magnetite crystals. The same is true for whales, pigeons, dolphins, bees, and many other animals. Kirschvink and Gould found that these magnetite crystals are not present in equal quantity from one being to another. According to Yves Rocard, this is the reason why dowsers and magnetizers can better capture waves than an ordinary person.

What appears to be a gift would therefore have a biological basis.

### Radiesthesia and Magnetism

Magnetism is inseparable from radiesthesia. Indeed, radiesthesia specialists cannot speak of radiesthesia without speaking of magnetism and waves.

Magnetism can be understood in two main senses:

- In its scientific sense, it designates the various forces exerted between two magnets, as well as the Earth's magnetism and all phenomena affecting bodies subjected to electric induction fields (electromagnetism). Science therefore studies the actions of magnetic fields on living organisms.
- In its older sense, the word magnetism also refers to Mesmer's theory and the magnetic fluid of persons. These concepts, belonging to esotericism and parapsychology, are generally not accepted by science.

Mesmer was a physician and a scientist, but his work was widely criticized. This surely contributes to discrediting the very notion of magnetism in the scientific community. For Mesmer, "animal magnetism" could heal and could be transmitted from one person to another through "passes." This magnetism is nevertheless different from the Earth's magnetism, which is clearly recognized by the natural sciences.

### Electromagnetic Waves and Telluric Currents

Radiesthesia detects electromagnetic waves that can characterize currents and telluric networks. The telluric current is linked to the Earth's magnetism: it is the electric current of the Earth. Electromagnetic networks are affected by various elements, particularly water — whether underground or surface — and metals.

The Hartmann network, for example, is an electric network linked to nickel. It takes the form of a grid. According to Hartmann, it is harmful to be at one of its intersections, or nodes. Geobiology is a discipline that works with different networks, linked to different metals (the Wissman grid for aluminum, Peyré for gold, Curry for iron, etc.).

## Applications of Radiesthesia

### Medical Radiesthesia

As its name suggests, medical radiesthesia helps detect certain diseases using the pendulum (this naturally requires that the radiesthesist possess the requisite medical skills). The radiesthesist's role in this context is not to heal but rather to detect and note organic deficiencies. They will notably use the Bovis scale to measure the patient's energetic vitality rate.

Radiesthesia can also be used in a medical context to help choose a treatment and monitor its effectiveness.

Various therapists integrate the pendulum into their practice, such as applied kinesiology. This is based on a response provided by the patient's muscle tone instead of the pendulum. One questions the body as one would question a pendulum, with closed questions answered by yes or no.

### Geobiology

#### Radiesthesia Can Inform About the Energetic Structure of a Place

Radiesthesia can inform you about the energetic structure of a place, such as your home, for example.

It sometimes happens that we feel uncomfortable in certain places without knowing why. One can use the pendulum or radiesthesia rods by asking questions about the place, to see whether the discomfort is related to the energy of the location.

For example, a woman moves into a new apartment and finds that she sleeps very poorly after a few weeks. She calls in a radiesthesist who determines that a water source passes beneath the apartment, exactly beneath where she has placed her bed. The solution? Move the bed and neutralize the space above the source.

#### Difference Between Scientific Geobiology and Radiesthetic Geobiology

Etymologically, geobiology means the study of the relationship between the Earth and living beings (geo and biology). However, it is important to carefully distinguish scientific geobiology from radiesthetic geobiology.

Scientific geobiology relates to the natural history of living beings and the Earth; it is a branch of paleontology. Radiesthetic geobiology, on the other hand, observes and studies the impact of waves emitted by the Earth on living beings. Some of these waves can be beneficial to human beings while others can be pathogenic. Thus, our place of habitation can make our happiness or, conversely, make us ill.

For example, if your living room is partially situated above a grid point, you will notice that your guests all prefer one seat over another. This spot, avoided by our fellow humans, will on the contrary be greatly appreciated by cats, who are an excellent energetic indicator.

You have surely also noticed that in certain houses you feel good and want to stay, while in others you are tense and uncomfortable. This can be due to the electromagnetic grid of the place.

#### Measuring the Vibratory Rate of Your Environment Through Radiesthesia

Thanks to radiesthesia (and the pendulum), it is possible to measure the vibratory quality of your environment. This can serve to arrange it so that you feel as comfortable as possible. You can use radiesthesia charts (cadrans) that will help you determine the vibratory rate of a place and any harmful elements.

It is a good tool for organizing space, choosing the location of a house, furniture, or materials... The search for water (dowsing) and the search for metals also continue to be practiced with the pendulum by geobiology practitioners, despite the existence of modern tools.

### Divination, or the Search for Lost Objects and Missing Persons

Radiesthesia also allows the search for missing persons or lost objects. It is a technique that requires the use of a pendulum.

This practice consists of "interrogating" the pendulum in order to obtain a precise answer. It requires first establishing a convention with the pendulum, and then the practice is carried out with much concentration, absolute calm, and training. The magnetic waves will cause the pendulum to rotate in one direction or another, shortly after the question is asked.

#### Asking Simple and Precise Questions

As with any divinatory art, it is important to ask simple, clear, and precise questions. Thus, one should ask a series of simple questions rather than one long, difficult-to-understand question.

Suppose, for example, that you are looking for a missing person. Do not formulate a complex question such as: "Did Sonia leave of her own free will, or is she lost or being held against her will somewhere? Is she all right?"

Rather, ask the questions one by one, like this:

– Is Sonia all right? => Wait for the answer

– Is Sonia being held against her will? => Wait for the answer

– Is Sonia in [CITY]? => Wait for the answer

– Is Sonia in [NEIGHBORHOOD]? => Wait for the answer

– And so on.

As you will have noticed, all the questions are closed — that is, the expected answers are either "YES," "NO," or "I don't know." As the session progresses, the questions become increasingly precise and yield usable elements for the search.

#### Taking Precautions for Yourself and the Pendulum

However, too many questions only lengthen the duration of the session. Since this is a practice that requires much concentration, one should only ask relevant questions so as not to tire oneself unnecessarily and lose focus.

Note that the pendulum will not give good answers if you dowse for yourself or for someone (or something) you care deeply about. Indeed, subjectivity will blur the magnetic waves and thus alter the results. In such cases, ask someone else to dowse for you, in your absence.

A trained radiesthesist can also determine the depth of a well, locate an object or a missing person, determine whether the child a pregnant woman is carrying is a boy or a girl, and so on.

## The Radiesthesist's Tools

### Pendulum Radiesthesia

The pendulum is a relatively modern tool. It was born at the beginning of the 19th century. It was the Strasbourg physicist Gerboin who discovered it, following experiments conducted with a ring suspended from a thread. This object could react like the dowser's rod. Little by little, the idea took hold that an object suspended from a thread was capable of reacting to certain vibrations coming from the ground, metals, water...

The pendulum is an extension of your body and your mind; it amplifies your perception of waves, without you even realizing it.

The use of the divinatory pendulum is very varied, and that is in fact its great advantage. It can be used either by yes-no convention or on a radiesthesia board. Graded boards allow for more detailed questioning.

It is therefore possible to obtain answers for a very wide range of problems using the pendulum.

### Dowsing Rods

Dowsing rods were formerly used to find water points. The rod is relatively easy to make from a forked branch of willow or hazel, having first removed the buds and leaves. There are websites that offer tutorials for making your own forked rod.

Once the rod is the right size, all that remains is to learn how to hold it properly. With the palms of your hands turned upward, close your palms so as to grip the branches of the rod between your hands. Then spread your hands apart so that the rod is under a certain tension. Be careful not to spread the forks too far apart, or the rod may break.

There you are — all that remains is to walk over the terrain where you are to find your water point. Generally, the rod tilts upward or downward as you approach the target. It is the waves that act. At first, the reaction of the rod may be difficult to perceive. But with practice, you will see that the rod will become more responsive than ever!

It is also possible to replace the traditional dowsing rod with parallel dowsing rods. Parallel dowsing rods are held with closed fists, arms at the sides and forearms raised, practically at a right angle. Depending on the convention established, an inward or outward movement of the rods is the sign that a wave has been detected. They have the same uses as traditional dowsing rods (searching for water, identifying all kinds of waves, springs, or objects in the ground).

Great precision is possible with the rod, but its disadvantage is that it does not allow for multiple searches, as is the case with the pendulum.

### The Lecher Antenna

The Lecher antenna is a radiesthesia tool that pays homage to the great German physicist Ernst Lecher, to whom we owe the measurement of vibratory phenomena and the speed of the electromagnetic wave.

However, the Lecher antenna was invented by Reinhard Schneider, another German physicist. Subsequently, the antenna was improved by Professor Walter Kunnen. It was the latter who decided to use the Lecher antenna in the field of medicine.

In total, five researchers of different specialties contributed to the optimization of the Lecher antenna.

The definitive form of the Lecher antenna is as follows: two Lecher wires arranged in parallel, between which is a system of graduations and a slider. A ground mass is also connected to the antenna via a ground wire. As the waves vary, the slider moves. For information, the unit of measurement used by the Lecher antenna is the gigahertz (GHz).

The antenna is held by its two lateral branches, which easily recall the forked part of the dowser's rod. Indeed, the Lecher antenna is held in the same manner as the dowsing rod.

This tool makes it possible to detect problems in many fields, such as diseases and telluric faults. It is also possible to use it as a navigation aid — it can identify the four cardinal points purely through measuring magnetic waves.

### Radiesthesia Boards

Let us recall that the word radiesthesia means etymologically "sensitivity to radiation." While some of the tools described above have made it possible to detect and interpret waves, the radiesthesia board will allow one to obtain more precise answers across a greater number of choices. Radiesthesia boards most often use a dial system with the various available options. It is a support used with the pendulum.

For example, the dial may be composed of the letters of the alphabet to obtain answers in the form of words or sentences, percentages, potential causes of disturbances...

The best-known and most widely used board is the Bovis board. It was invented in 1900 by Alfred Bovis, hence its name. It is a board for measuring the energetic vitality of a place or a body.

On a classic Bovis dial, the waves range from 0 to 18,000 UB (Bovis Units). Moreover, the vibratory power is divided into three sectors:

- The first sector belongs to the physical world; values range from 0 to 10,000 UB
- The second refers to the energetic world; values range from 10,000 to 14,000 UB
- The third sector is in the spiritual domain; values are above 14,000 UB

You can very well make your own radiesthesia boards depending on the problem you are working on. It is of course also possible to purchase them commercially.

## How to Find a Good Radiesthesist?

Finding a radiesthesist can be very tricky. You will find some on the internet or in classified ads. However, it will be important to distinguish true magnetizers from the many charlatans. Generally, it is preferable to trust those who have been recommended by friends or relatives, but always rely on your common sense and judgment.
