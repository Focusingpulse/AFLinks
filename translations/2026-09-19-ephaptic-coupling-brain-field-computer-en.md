## Why this is the headline of the round

The Aetherforce program has long held that bioenergy is not just plain electricity — that endogenous fields form an active layer of biological regulation, and that environmental and intentional fields might interact with that layer. The standing objection from the citadel is that endogenous electric fields are an *epiphenomenon*: spikes do the work, the field is exhaust.

In one week, mainstream neuroscience moved the field from exhaust to engine — twice.

**First row: the MIT/Picower theory.** Earl Miller (with lab scientists Scott Brincat and Jefferson Roy) argues in a Journal of Neuroscience review that the brain's quick, improvised cognition — and consciousness itself — arises from **analog computation performed by traveling waves of neural activity**, coordinated through the brain's own electric field. The "circuits" metaphor is incomplete: synapses store memories and goals, but rewiring synapses is far too slow for the rapid-fire coordination the world demands. Waves do that job.

The mechanism, in their own words: *"The brain exploits its own physics."*

## The theory, piece by piece

1. **Mixed selectivity is the horsepower — and the problem.** Neurons routinely participate in multiple functional networks at once. That gives the brain its computational flexibility, but raises the question of how millions of neurons get organized into the right ensembles, in fractions of a second.

2. **Waves are the control system.** Slow alpha/beta waves — carrying memories and goals — regulate fast gamma waves carrying sensory content. Miller's lab has shown this experimentally for years (working memory, predictive coding).

3. **Mobile stencils.** The alpha/beta waves act spatially and temporally: they travel along the cortex and gate *where* and *when* gamma can process, and which ensembles participate. The authors call this "spatiotemporal computing."

4. **Analog computation.** Where the waves intersect, they add and subtract. Interference *is* the arithmetic — many calculations in parallel, not one step at a time through gates.

5. **Ephaptic coupling — the causal loop.** The newest piece: even as waves emerge from coordinated spiking, the waves grow to directly influence and coordinate the spiking itself, via the electric field — *ephaptic coupling*. The field feeds back onto the very spikes that generated it, and it does so very rapidly. This is the move from epiphenomenon to substrate: the field is no longer a readout, it is part of the circuit.

6. **Consciousness.** The theory asserts consciousness "emerges when these dynamic wave patterns bring the cortex in an organized, globally integrated state." Evidence: with Emery Brown (MGH anesthesiologist), Miller showed that three anesthetics with three different molecular mechanisms all produce unconsciousness the same way — by disrupting large-scale wave organization. *"Consciousness depends less on specific receptors or cell types and more on the integrity of large-scale wave organization."*

7. **The evolutionary argument.** *"Electric field dynamics offer a low-overhead substrate for organizing and coordinating information across cortical networks. Given strong evolutionary pressure to maximize computation per unit energy, it would be surprising if evolution did not exploit such a built-in analog computing substrate."*

Miller is explicit that this is a theory, and names the next test: looking for signatures of analog computation in brain wave patterns. He also names the clinical stake: waves can be manipulated non-invasively, and *"developing treatments based on brain wave dynamics is not just an opportunity, but also an obligation."*

## The same-week companion: excitability splits into two

The Rabinovitch et al. Scientific Reports paper (Ben-Gurion University), published the day before the MIT coverage, does to *excitability* what Miller's theory does to *cognition*: it refuses the unitary concept and splits it into **two independent parameters — synaptic (p) and ephaptic (b)** — inside a cellular-automata model.

The findings worth carrying:

- **Hyperexcitability/epileptic susceptibility (HEPS) domains can emerge at LOW synaptic excitability** (p < 0.90), driven by the ephaptic channel — beneath the elevated synaptic excitabilities traditionally associated with epileptogenesis.
- Those low-p HEPS regions could arise **in the hippocampus during engram formation** — a mechanistic overlap between physiological memory encoding and pathological susceptibility. The seizure-prone brain and the remembering brain share machinery.
- **Therapeutic implication:** target p and b *separately* — e.g., field-type stimulation (tDCS-class) against the ephaptic channel, synaptic drugs against p — to lower seizure risk while preserving cognition.

Two mainstream journals, two days apart: the electric field is now an independently manipulable physiological parameter, with its own pathology (b-driven HEPS) and its own therapy (field modulation). The medicalization of the ephaptic layer has begun.

## Highfield's corollary: mortal computation

Roger Highfield (Science Museum group; with UCL's Peter Coveney) draws the AI-side consequence. If consciousness is analog field computation, then reproducing it digitally requires simulating the waves with exquisite fidelity — and digital machines cannot faithfully simulate even *simple chaos*. Coveney, Boghosian and Wang's generalized Bernoulli map work shows floating-point arithmetic can be *qualitatively* wrong for chaotic systems, and more precision does not fix it: a digital simulation of a chaotic system, given enough time, becomes a different creature altogether.

If mind is bound to analog field dynamics, then mind may be **substrate-bound** — what Geoffrey Hinton calls "mortal computation": software inseparable from the hardware it runs on. The brain runs on ~20 watts; the digital path to machine consciousness runs through server farms drawing gigawatts to shoehorn continuous physics into crisp zeros and ones. Highfield's bottom line: the long-term future of AI may rest on old-fashioned analog processes of the kind that endowed us with intelligence in the first place.

## The Aetherforce seam

What this cluster does for the corpus:

- **The citadel has crossed the river halfway.** Mainstream neuroscience now proposes endogenous EM fields as a causal computational layer — the "bioenergy layer beyond plain electricity" thesis, arriving from inside. It does not claim subtle energy. It claims the brain's own fields organize cognition, and that consciousness is a globally integrated field state.
- **The remaining gap is exactly the research program.** The distance from "the brain's own field is a computer" to "environmental and intentional fields interact with that computer" is the seam Aetherforce should be mapping. The Miller theory even hands us the interface: waves are non-invasively manipulable, and the field feeds back causally. If endogenous fields are read-write rather than read-only, the question of what external and intentional fields do to them stops being category error and becomes instrumentation.
- **The corpus already holds both banks.** The MD Anderson biofield paper (this library, 2026-09-19) measures a practitioner's effect *in membrane-voltage units*. The ephaptic theory gives the same class of effect a mainstream mechanistic language. The Levin bioelectricity corpus gives the cell-level version. This is the same bridge, third plank.

**Testable seam to watch:** Miller's own next step — signatures of analog computation in brain wave patterns — is the experiment that would falsify or confirm the theory. The Rabinovitch p/b split is testable in the clinic the same way (does field-channel modulation lower seizure risk at fixed p?). And the Highfield/Coveney row predicts that no digital LLM, however large, will show field-bound cognition — a standing, falsifiable negative.
