# Radiesthesia and Geobiology in the Forest of Brocéliande

**A Historical Approach to a Social Phenomenon**

*English translation from French · source: Encyclopédie de Brocéliande (broceliande.brecilien.org), article "Radiesthésie et géobiologie en forêt de Brocéliande" · translated 2026-09-10 by Forge (translation-qc agent) for the Aetherforce library · original page © Encyclopédie de Brocéliande / Station Biologique de Paimpont — research translation with full attribution*

---

Conceptualized between 1920 and 1960, radiesthesia and geobiology are disciplines without scientific foundation that postulate the existence of cosmo-telluric networks on the surface of our planet. The first works applying their principles to the forest of Paimpont only appear in the early 1980s. Forty years later, interest in energetic approaches — a genuine social phenomenon — has become one of the commonplaces of discourse about the forest of Brocéliande.

This article does not aim to deny the existence of the "energetic" sensory perceptions claimed by dowsers, radiesthésistes, magnetizers and geobiologists. It proposes to show:

- How, over the course of the 20th century, neo-druidism appropriated the discourse on "cosmotellurism" and "geobiology" associated with megaliths,
- How this current produced a discourse on the forest of Brocéliande,
- What place this discourse currently occupies on the Paimpont forest massif.

## The Invention of Radiesthesia

"Radiesthesia" is a term invented by Abbé Bouly (1865–1958) [1] and first used in 1926. — BOTROT, Jean, "La radiesthésie, ou l'art peu commun de découvrir les filons," *Le Journal*, October 30, 1926, p. 1. —

Composed from the Latin *radius* meaning "rod" and the Greek suffix *-esthesia* meaning "sensation," "perception," radiesthesia is the faculty of perceiving the radiations that certain bodies are said to emit, as well as the detection procedure founded on this faculty. — CNRTL, "Radiesthésie," 2012. —

Presented by its inventor as a continuation of the dowser's art, radiesthesia was at its creation considered an object of study by important members of the scientific community [2]. — ARCHIVES DU PAS DE CALAIS, "Naissance de l'abbé Bouly, radiesthésiste, à Condette," 2023. —

## Precursors of Cosmotellurism

In 1933, Louis Merle (1890–1973), a dowser passionate about archaeology, published a work in which he sought to establish the foundations of a correspondence between the existence of underground currents and the location of megaliths.

> I believe I am the first to have had this revelation of the relationships that exist between megalithic monuments and underground influences. This fact constitutes a discovery whose importance no one will deny from the prehistoric point of view. But since these are unpublished data, I have few or no technical terms — or only terms consecrated by usage — at my disposal. I will therefore try to explain as best I can, and in my own way, the result of my observations. By telluric current or underground influence I mean not only water, but any matter giving off radiations; and by telluric line, any line of influence that an underground current projects. [...]
>
> MERLE, Louis, *Radiesthésie et préhistoire*, self-published, 1933.

Louis Merle's theories on the links between underground currents and megalith archaeology met with an immediate echo in the world of neo-druidism, then from the end of the 1950s in that of geobiology. — BONVIN, Jacques, *Mégalithes : lieux d'énergie*, Mosaïque éditions, 1995, 284 p. [pages 133–149] —

## Atlanteans, Druids and Cosmotellurism

In 1942, Paul Bouchet (1897–1979) [3] created the Collège des Druides, Eubages, Bardes et Ovates des Gaules, of which he instituted himself "Grand Druid" under the name Bod Koad. The author of theories marked by various esoteric currents, he became an important popularizer of the associations between druidism, megalithism and cosmotellurism in the 1960s.

> The Grand Druid Paul Bouchet wrote several works popularizing the knowledge of the "Druidic College of Gaul," and it is undoubtedly in doing this that he was most anti-traditional, since knowledge was only supposed to be transmitted orally. He insisted heavily on the notion of telluric currents which, according to him, mark out the dolmens and the various ancient religious edifices.
>
> RAOULT, Michel, *Les druides : les sociétés initiatiques contemporaines*, Éditions du Rocher, 1992, 494 p.

In *Les derniers atlantes* — a novel published in 1959 — as well as in *Science et philosophie des druides* — an essay published in 1968 — he developed a theory centered on the continuity between the civilization of Atlantis and Celtic civilization. There he claimed in particular that the druids, heirs to the psychic powers of the Atlanteans, had erected the megaliths on telluric currents. — BOUCHET, Paul and DRUIDE BOD KOAD, *Science et philosophie des druides*, Blainville-sur-Mer (Manche), L'amitié par le livre, 1968, 190 p. —

Paul Bouchet's theories, long left in relative anonymity, met with success during the 1970s. His most important work, *Science et philosophie des druides*, republished by Laffont in 1976 — sold 100,000 copies — exerted its influence on numerous authors of the 1980s–1990s [4]. — BOUCHET, Paul and BOUCHET, René, *Science et philosophie des druides*, 1968, Paris, Laffont, 1976, 281 p. —

## 1937–1961 — The Birth of Geobiology

Geobiology [5], a discipline born of radiesthesia, can be defined as the study of all the influences of the environment on living beings, and in particular of waves linked to magnetic and electric fields, underground water currents, metallic networks, geological faults, etc.

This discipline without scientific foundation postulates the existence of telluric currents covering the entire surface of the terrestrial globe.

In 1947, one of its precursors, Doctor Peyré of Bagnoles-de-l'Orne, evoked the existence of a magnetic network intersecting and distributing itself symmetrically over the surface of the planet. [6] — PEYRÉ, Doctor, *Radiations Cosmotelluriques. (Rayons Peyré) : Leur topographie sur toute la planète, leur rapport possible avec la pathologie humaine, animale, végétale et notamment avec le cancer*, R. Martinet, 1947, 112 p. —

In the 1950s, Doctor Ernst Hartmann (1915–1992) — founder of geobiology — postulated the existence of a network made up of intersections of telluric radiations, present on all continents and bearing the name of the Hartmann network or H-network.

From 1951 onwards, he spread his theses at colloquia and seminars, and founded in 1961 the "Cercle de recherche en géobiologie" [Geobiology Research Circle], which he presided until his death in 1992. — BONVIN, Jacques, *Mégalithes : lieux d'énergie*, Mosaïque éditions, 1995, 284 p. [pages 121–131] —

Doctor Hartmann's theses won over numerous adherents, making geobiology a particularly influential New Age current.

> Not only do geobiologists unanimously recognize that our world is traversed by telluric or cosmic energy waves whose influence is positive or negative; they also expound theories on the distribution of telluric currents. They consider that there exists a particular grid of these currents (the Hartmann network and the Curry network). The crossing points of this mesh can provoke problems for living beings who stay too long on these places.
>
> AURAY, Christophe, *Pierres magiques et guérisseuses de Bretagne*, Ouest-France, 2018. [page 56]

## 1981–1985 — First Writings

The first writings evoking the existence of telluric currents in the forest of Paimpont date from the beginning of the 1980s. They are the work of authors with marginal audiences. Linked to the current of thought initiated by Paul Bouchet, these writings attribute to the druids of antiquity, and even to the Atlanteans, the faculty of perceiving and using the energies of telluric currents.

### 1981–1983 — Alan-Morvan Chesneau

In 1981, in an article in the review *Armor* devoted to the storyteller Patrick Lebrun, Alan-Morvan Chesneau was the first author to evoke the ambient tellurism of the Celtic Sylve of Brec'hallean [Brocéliande].

> If you know how to hear, and to keep preciously what must be heard, within the tale as messenger and transmitter, then with Patrick, or any other "necessary intermediary," you will be able to partake of this "ambient tellurism," coming straight from the nocturnal mounds of the forest.
>
> CHESNEAU, Alan Morvan, "Patrick Lebrun : conteur gallo," *Armor magazine*, 1981.

In 1983 he took part in a collective work devoted to the abbeys of Brittany published by Fayard. In charge of the chapter devoted to the abbey of Paimpont, Alan-Morvan Chesneau put forward references to druidism, esotericism, the New Age and tellurism to narrate the origin he supposed for the abbey.

> The abbey of Notre-Dame de Paimpont, situated on a site that was once druidic, assuredly enjoys the magical protections of the place. The four elements plus the mysterious "Flour of the air"! And here is the osmosis so dear to our Bards, thus realized in venerable Celtia. Drawing its energies from the womb-earth, warmed ceaselessly by the underlying tellurism of its promontory, at its cultic mound, reassured by the massive foliage of the nearby high forest, crowned by a clement sky, the abbey awaits its transumatory hour. [...] The abbey is built at the crossing of numerous underground passages and, at the same time, at the exact intersection of numerous telluric currents. Assuredly, the aestheticism, the beauty of the place where it rests, is not enough to untie the "Gordian Knot" crystallized by the mystery of its installation. The answer can only come from Brocéliande... One may nevertheless affirm that the monastery is fixed upon a "passage" leading to the Celtic Gnosis once taught by our fathers... At Paimpont, around the abbey as at the very depths of its rocky support, resides a door toward an "Elsewhere," toward "Something Else."
>
> CHESNEAU, Alan Morvan, "Notre Dame de Paimpont," in *Les abbayes bretonnes*, Fayard, 1983, p. 139–149.

### 1985 — Jacques Pegeaud

Jacques Pegeaud is the author of a work on Brocéliande published in 1985. He took up again in this publication the theme of the Atlantean origin of the druids' powers and of their use through the megaliths.

> One may think that the Atlanteans had not, like us, lost the memory of the original knowledge, and that they fully used the possibilities of their brains. [...] In short, the megaliths are there and they did not fall from the sky! [...] The site of Paimpont and the forest of Brocéliande would hold the record both in number and in quality, were it not for the multiple acts of vandalism [...] The Atlanteans peopled the forest with a predilection for its geographical and cultural center: Paimpont. The menhirs raised toward the sky like monstrous megalithic question marks, the dolmens offered as altars to the mother goddess, the covered alleys necessary for the ritual of death and the passage to the kingdom of Uranus, the barrows stripped in winter and flowered in summer, were as many markers placed at the limits of their knowledge. They knew geometry, trigonometry, units of length, the measurement of angles, the four cardinal points and a good number of constellations of the sky. As at ease in time as in space, they kept a perpetual calendar, knew the beneficial effects of telluric currents, channeled them and perhaps used them...
>
> PEGEAUD, Jacques, *Brocéliande d'hier et d'aujourd'hui !*, Saint-Méen-le-Grand, Éditions de Communications et de rencontres, 1985, 135 p. [pages 22–23]

## 1987–1999 — The First Discovery Circuits

From the mid-1980s, theories on cosmotellurism left their relative confidentiality. Works in large print runs, carried by publishing houses specializing in mass-market esoteric popularization — Robert Laffont or Guy Trédaniel — as well as by authors with national audiences such as Jean Markale (1928–2008), offered to grant access to the hidden science of the ancients. Integrating geobiology into neo-druidic theories, these works "revealed" to the general public discovery circuits of the forest of Brocéliande, one of the most fascinating cosmo-telluric high places.

### 1986 — The Opera of the Waters at the Val sans Retour

The Opera of the Waters is a project by Jean-Charles Fabre marrying architecture and geobiology at the Val sans Retour. This project — the subject of a thesis submitted in 1982 — is conceived as a regeneration center where the sun, the earth and the water come together into forms of life.

> The sensitive architectural project is underlain by the discovery of a network of close interconnections of the place with its global environment: human environment, bioclimatic, cosmo-telluric... The creator crystallizes upon this backdrop a habitation issuing from a fine and complex archi-texture. The names as well as the coats of arms maintain the strength of the spirit of the place, its energetic climate in a manner of speaking.
>
> FABRE, Jean-Charles, "L'opéra des eaux au Val sans Retour," in *Maison entre Terre et Ciel : Les vertus thérapeutiques de l'habitat*, 1986, Éditions Véga, 2004, p. 164–198. [page 197]

This architectural project inspired by geobiology prefigures the New Age fusions — marriages of shamanism, geobiology and neo-druidism — that would develop in the Paimpont forest from the 2010s onward.

> I followed the "user's manual" that the legend offered me. In search of a global vision of the place and of the adventurous quest, I plunged beneath the waters of the Mirror, guided by Viviane and Lancelot. An architectural project was born of the regenerative virtues of the place [...]. The work consisted in letting emerge forms pre-inscribed... This taking into account of architecture made it possible to "liberate" the living and life-giving forms for a given time. The object, evolving and not immutable, must follow the cycles of transformation of nature, embrace the flowing of the days.
>
> FABRE, Jean-Charles, "L'opéra des eaux au Val sans Retour," in *Maison entre Terre et Ciel : Les vertus thérapeutiques de l'habitat*, 1986, Éditions Véga, 2004, p. 164–198. [page 167]

### 1987 — Gilbert Altenbach and Boune Legrais

The first publication proposing the discovery of cosmo-telluric high places located in the Paimpont forest appeared in 1987 in the collection "Les énigmes de l'univers" at Robert Laffont. — ALTENBACH, Gilbert and LEGRAIS, Boune, *Lieux magiques et sacrés de la France*, Robert Laffont, 1987, 301 p. —

The authors — Gilbert Altenbach [7] and Boune Legrais — set out to draw up a cartography of the magical and sacred places of France: these centers of healing of body, soul and spirit, where sky and Earth come together; these high places whose knowledge has until now remained secret and reserved solely for initiates.

This work, which lays claim to the science called geobiology and the biology of habitat, takes up the main lines of Paul Bouchet's theory, according to which religious edifices were built on ancient megalithic and druidic sites initially raised on telluric high points.

> The Ancients, builders of megaliths and pyramids, the builders of cathedrals, had knowledge of cosmo-telluric subtleties and used them to reach higher states of consciousness.
>
> ALTENBACH, Gilbert and LEGRAIS, Boune, *Lieux magiques et sacrés de la France*, Robert Laffont, 1987, 301 p. [page 21]

The cosmo-telluric sites of the forest of Brocéliande are addressed in the chapter entitled "La Bretagne des Druides" [The Brittany of the Druids].

> High place of healing, high magical place, high sacred place, Brocéliande remains one of the most fascinating cosmo-telluric high places.
>
> Altenbach, Gilbert and Legrais, Boune (1987), op. cit., p. 171

The authors begin by locating the two principal telluric currents that cross the forest and cut each other perpendicularly at the abbey of Paimpont.

> The ridge of the telluric current that crosses Brocéliande from east to west is cut perpendicularly by a north-south current. This intersection occurs precisely at the edge of the pond of Paimpont, at the site of the abbey. The latitudinal current links Tréhorenteuc, the Val sans Retour, the chapels of Beauvais and Saint-Jean to those of Saint-Péran and Coganne, crossing the abbey of Paimpont. The longitudinal axis passes close to Comper — which would have been the residence of Viviane, later that of Lancelot du Lac — and the village of Beignon.
>
> Altenbach, Gilbert and Legrais, Boune (1987), op. cit., p. 168–169

The cosmo-telluric high places of Brocéliande are presented as an initiatory circuit beginning at the church of Tréhorenteuc and ending in the Chapel of the Blessed Sacrament of the abbey of Paimpont.

> The church of Tréhorenteuc seems to us to be the entry door, the key giving access to this enchanted forest. The geodynamometer [8] attests a life-wave of 25 positive units inside this high spiritual place.
>
> Altenbach, Gilbert and Legrais, Boune (1987), op. cit., p. 169

The Arthurian sites of the Val sans Retour and the Fountain of Barenton are mentioned for the first time in their telluric dimension.

> If the Val sans Retour possesses incontestably an interesting energetic potential by its situation on the telluric current, the Fountain of Barenton held many surprises for us. [...] The life-wave of the place is very weak and hardly exceeds the neutral point (zero). The vital energy of this "frozen water that boils" is limited to five positive units. However — and this is perhaps the explanation of the enigma — a heavy atmosphere hangs over the place. An energy similar to that encountered in places of occult practices. So, prudence: the sorcerer's apprentices are not far.
>
> Altenbach, Gilbert and Legrais, Boune (1987), op. cit., p. 170

The energetic visit of Brocéliande ends at the abbey church of Paimpont, curiously planted at the intersection of two telluric currents.

> Two powerful cosmo-telluric points: — The Chapel of the Blessed Sacrament, a small, intimate and severe sanctuary recalling the austerity of the monks of Saint Colomban. The life-wave there exceeds 32 positive units; — A zone in the choir that we will leave for the pilgrim-seeker to discover.
>
> Altenbach, Gilbert and Legrais, Boune (1987), op. cit., p. 170

### 1992 — Le Crapouillot

A 1992 article in *Le Crapouillot* shows the influence of Gilbert Altenbach and Boune Legrais's book on the tourist appeal of the Paimpont forest.

> It is near the hamlet of Folle-Pensée that one meets very singular visitors, drawn by the therapeutic reputation of the fountain. A handful of radiesthésistes, pendulum in hand, pace this sacred clearing that one discovers with some difficulty, just like these megaliths, veritable energetic centers scattered through the woods.
>
> PONSE, Nicolas, "Mystères et légendes païennes en forêt de Brocéliande," *Le crapouillot*, Vol. HS 9, 1992, p. 60–62.

### 1996 — Jean Markale

In 1996, Jean Markale evoked four megalithic sites — the Buttes aux Tombes, the Jardin aux Moines, the Hôtié de Viviane and the Tomb of Merlin — in a work devoted to a spiritual approach to the Paimpont forest. One finds in this publication Jean Markale's first mentions of the existence of telluric currents in the forest of Brocéliande. — MARKALE, Jean, *Guide spirituel de la forêt de Brocéliande*, Monaco, Éditions du Rocher, 1996. —

The Buttes aux Tombes are presented there — in spite of all archaeological plausibility — as Celtic burial mounds of the Iron Age intuitively erected on the location of telluric currents by the druids. — Markale, Jean (1996), op. cit., p. 21–23 —

> [...] This is a meeting point with the cosmic energies coming from elsewhere, that is to say from the exterior universe, and the telluric energies springing from the center of the earth [...]. In those ancient times, the dead were not buried just anywhere: it had to be a place chosen — by intuition or by knowledge — according to certain virtues, in particular at the meeting of telluric currents and cosmic currents.
>
> Markale, Jean (1996), op. cit., p. 21–23

### 1999 — Adolphe Landspurg and Norbert L'Hostis

In 1999, a second discovery circuit of the cosmo-telluric sites of the Paimpont forest was proposed to the readers of *Bretagne magique et vibratoire* by Adolphe Landspurg [9] and Norbert L'Hostis. The two authors begin their energetic visit at the Chapel of the Blessed Sacrament of the abbey church of Paimpont.

> The chapel is traversed by a fault and an underground watercourse oriented at azimuth 77; the choir of the chapel is placed on a second, perpendicular underground watercourse and on a geological fault, which proves that the site was chosen by builders who mastered the dowser's art. [...] The choir of the church vibrates at 375 H.L.V. units on plane III (spiritual 38). We measure the "magnetic" green color vibration, a cosmo-telluric ratio of 3/3 and a magnetism of 100%.
>
> LANDSPURG, Adolphe and L'HOSTIS, Norbert, *Bretagne magique et vibratoire : Circuits insolites et mesures sacrées*, Guy Trédaniel éditeur, 1999, 270 p. [page 149]

Notwithstanding the abundance of "scientific" vocabulary, the measurement of cosmo-telluric waves is carried out with the pendulum and the geodynamometer.

The circuit continues through the traditional sites of the Paimpont forest, blending legend and megalithism. Walking in Jean Markale's footsteps, Adolphe Landspurg and Norbert L'Hostis first go to the Jardin aux Moines.

> We measure at the center of the quadrilateral a vibration of 250 H.L.V. units on plane II (mental 25), a magnetism of 100% and a cosmo-telluric ratio of 3/3. The "electric black" color vibration — corresponding to the radiesthetic dimension of death — indicates that this monument must have served as a funerary site. We mark the first notable cult of the place at −2524.
>
> LANDSPURG, Adolphe and L'HOSTIS, Norbert, *Bretagne magique et vibratoire*, Guy Trédaniel éditeur, 1999, 270 p. [page 151]

At the Hôtié de Viviane as at the Jardin aux Moines, the authors give the dates of the first cult without offering any explanation of the origin of their assertion.

> We climb to the top of the ridge that dominates the Hôtié de Viviane. On this small east-west-oriented ridge, we measure a vibration of 2500 H.L.V. on plane III (divine 49). The cosmo-telluric ratio is 3/3 and the magnetism 100%. The "electric green" color vibration indicates that we are on an ancient place of sacrifice. Given the orientation of the ridge, I think we are here on an ancient solar cult site dedicated to Belenos. We date the first notable cult of the place at −3140.
>
> LANDSPURG, Adolphe and L'HOSTIS, Norbert, *Bretagne magique et vibratoire*, Guy Trédaniel éditeur, 1999, 270 p. [page 152]

The cosmo-telluric circuit of the Paimpont forest concludes at the Tomb of Merlin, where the authors, to their great astonishment, feel a kind of heat rising from the ground and crossing their body from feet to head.

> No doubt about it, and our radiesthetic measurements confirm it: this privileged spot of the forest of Brocéliande is a cosmo-telluric high vibratory place that must have been known and used by the druids, great connoisseurs of cosmo-telluric forces and of high places of energy.
>
> LANDSPURG, Adolphe and L'HOSTIS, Norbert, *Bretagne magique et vibratoire*, Guy Trédaniel éditeur, 1999, 270 p. [page 157]

Buoyed by its commercial success, the book was republished in 2015. — LANDSPURG, Adolphe and L'HOSTIS, Norbert, *Bretagne magique et vibratoire : Circuits insolites et mesures sacrées*, 1999, Véga Éditions, 2015, 345 p. —

## Cosmotellurism in the 21st Century in the Paimpont Forest

Confined to specialized publishers in the 1980s and 90s, cosmotellurism and energetic approaches became in the 2000s commonplaces taken up by major publishing houses and sold in the shops and tourist offices of the forest massif.

In the works published from 2010 onward [10], the vocabulary of radiesthesia and geobiology merges with numerous esoteric references, traditional or New Age beliefs, among them the tarot of Marseille, astrology, the existence of the little people (lutins, korrigans), neo-shamanism, etc.

### 2011 — Brocéliande secrète

*Brocéliande secrète* is a publication devoted to the Paimpont forest massif in which "New Age" references strongly associate with the vocabulary of radiesthesia.

The author uses the Mermet pendulum, the Lecher antenna or the Bovis scale to account for the characteristics of the high vibratory places of the forest. According to him, the forest of Brocéliande is riddled with high places that are either places of contact with our divinities, or places of passage from one world to the other. — BOCHER, Alain and ROPARZ, Klaod, *Brocéliande secrète*, Saint-Malo, Pascal Galodé éditeurs, 2011, 84 p. [page 38] —

The book surveys numerous sacred sites with extraordinary vibratory characteristics, among them the "traditional" Jardin aux Moines, Fountain of Barenton and Hôtié de Viviane.

> [The Jardin aux Moines is] truly a sacred place [...] All the more sacred since its vibration rate on the Bovis scale is very high. We are in the presence of an authentic vortex. Let us be certain of it. A vortex, as are many churches whose priests have forgotten the key (when they have not forgotten the function); that is to say, a passage from one world to the other — in this case, to help us pass into that of the fairies and the korrigans.
>
> Bocher, Alain and Roparz, Klaod (2011), op. cit., p. 24

The Hôtié de Viviane is described as "a temporal vortex such as there are few, even in Brocéliande." — Bocher, Alain and Roparz, Klaod (2011), op. cit., p. 36 — As for the Fountain of Barenton, its marvelous characteristics — mentioned since the 12th century — find their explanation in cosmotellurism.

> It would be an energetic canal between the subsoil, very charged with telluric energy, and the sky, itself charged with cosmic energy. An enormous difference of potential which, when one adds water (making, in all likelihood, a good conductor), discharges violently.
>
> Bocher, Alain and Roparz, Klaod (2011), op. cit., p. 31

Two new sites enrich the energetic itineraries of the forest of Brocéliande: the old road leading to the Château de Trécesson [11] and the Hêtre de la Gelée [Beech of the Frost].

> It was a very strange tree, since for a millennium it had been half dead and half alive, and of an astonishing vibratory power for the living half (more than 60,000 Bovis Units, which is exceptional).
>
> BOCHER, Alain and ROPARZ, Klaod, *Brocéliande secrète*, Saint-Malo, Pascal Galodé éditeurs, 2011, 84 p. [page 66]

### 2022 — Aurélie Aimé

The legendary forest of Brocéliande is one of the 50 energetic high places in France offered for discovery by Aurélie Aimé [12] in *Lieux sacrés et mystérieux en France*. This book, inscribed in the personal-development current, proposes to link the energetic approach with the discovery of spirituality. — AIMÉ, Aurélie, *Lieux sacrés et mystérieux en France : Connectez-vous à l'énergie de 50 sites d'exception (Spiritualité & intuition)*, Le lotus et l'éléphant, 2022, 280 p. —

## Therapists and Cosmotellurism

From the 2010s onward, the interest of a widened public in cosmotellurism allowed new economic actors of the forest massif — therapists, magnetizers, shamans, etc. — to offer workshops, trainings or stays in the Paimpont forest based on approaches integrating the perception of telluric currents.

Particularly present on the web, these commercial therapeutic offerings show connections to numerous New Age currents, the principal ones anchored in shamanism, geobiology or neo-druidism.

All these beliefs find their variations and marry with one another on occasion.

A report by France 3's 19/20 news dated 2015, for example, proposes to follow a shamanism initiation group in search of "energetic" contacts with the little people of the megalithic site of the Tomb of the Giant. — FRANCE 3 – 19/20 NATIONAL, "Il heurte une colonne de puissance à Brocéliande," Dailymotion, 2015. —

A druid-storyteller offers a circuit in the Paimpont forest integrating a verification of form-waves by the spectrum of colors and individual therapeutic use of the cosmo-telluric energies that flow from them, all of this according to the Tradition of the druids. — DWRDAN, "Dwrdan, le Druide accompagnateur, géobiologue et conteur à la fois," Bro Kelt Lan – association culturelle celtique, 2018. —

Interest in cosmotellurism in the Paimpont forest is not limited to its commercial exploitation. Many inhabitants of the forest massif also integrate radiesthesia and the existence of telluric currents into their personal beliefs, like this inhabitant of Concoret, interviewed in an article of *Bretagne Magazine* from September 2023.

> Here, he explains to me, the forest is rocky, seated on purple schist very rich in iron. The region receives much water. This water and the underlying metal create a magnetic field. That could partly explain the attraction of men for Brocéliande, for we are sensitive to magnetic fields, even if we are not necessarily aware of them.
>
> BOITIER, Emmanuel, "Brocéliande côté nature," *Bretagne Magazine*, 2023, p. 58–69. [page 62]

Forty years after the first writings evoking the ambient tellurism of the Celtic Sylve of Brec'hallean, it must be recognized that belief in the existence of cosmo-telluric currents in the Paimpont forest has spread among numerous tourists and inhabitants of the forest massif [13].

**2.** Among the first members of the Association française et internationale des amis de la radiesthésie, created December 29, 1929 in Lille, one counts:

**3.** Wounded in action and evacuated to the hospital at Albi during the First World War, Paul Bouchet used his convalescence to research druidism and begin writing *Science et philosophie des druides*. Rejected by the Gorsedd of Brittany for his ignorance of the Breton language, he established himself as a bookseller in Paris, Faubourg Poissonnière. Paul Bouchet met Philéas Lebesgue, Grand Druid of Gaul, who invited him to visit his farm at Neuville Vault near Beauvais. There he was initiated as a druid in 1942 and constituted a clandestine druidic college, the Collège des Druides, Eubages, Bardes et Ovates des Gaules, of which he instituted himself "Grand Druid" under the name Bod Koad.

> Whatever the criticisms formulated against Paul Bouchet, he worked with force and conviction for the renaissance of druidism; he oriented the Collège Druidique des Gaules in a paganizing direction, without false concessions to the established Church. [...] The Collège Druidique des Gaules strove to maintain fraternal links with the other druidic organizations [...] The Grand Druid Paul Bouchet wrote several works popularizing the knowledge of the Collège Druidique des Gaules, and it is undoubtedly in doing this that he was most anti-traditional, since knowledge was only supposed to be transmitted orally. He insisted heavily on the notion of telluric currents which, according to him, mark out the dolmens and the various ancient religious edifices.
