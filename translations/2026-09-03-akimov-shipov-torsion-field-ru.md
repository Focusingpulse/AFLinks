1 Introduction

The adequacy of our understanding of Nature is proportional to our knowledge of the laws acting within it. The history of the development of Natural Science over at least the past hundred years demonstrates that the appearance of experimental results that cannot be explained within the framework of accepted scientific concepts is a direct indication of the incompleteness of our knowledge about Nature.

Over recent decades, it has been constantly asserted that all known phenomena of Nature and experimental results are exhaustively explained by the four known interactions: electromagnetism, gravity, strong and weak interactions. However, over the past fifty years, approximately twenty experimental results have accumulated that have found no explanation within the framework of these interactions [1].

Quite independently of this situation, which is dramatic for the current stage of development of Natural Science, the search for new long-range interactions has continued since the 1930s. One need only point to the work of H. Tetrode [2] and A.D. Fokker [3], and later J. Wheeler and R. Feynman [4,5] and other authors. However, this work did not receive proper development. The exception was the concept of torsion fields.

The theory of torsion fields (fields of rotation) is a traditional direction in theoretical physics, going back to work from the second half of the last century. However, in its modern form, the theory of torsion fields was formulated thanks to the ideas of Élie Cartan, who was the first to clearly and definitively point to the existence in Nature of fields generated by the density of angular momentum of rotation. To date, the bibliography of world periodicals on torsion fields amounts to up to 10,000 articles by approximately one hundred authors. More than half of these theorists work in Russia.

Despite a well-developed theoretical apparatus, torsion fields continued to remain only a theoretical object until the beginning of the 1970s. This is why they did not become as universal a factor as electrodynamics and gravitation. Moreover, there existed a theoretical conclusion that, since the constant of spin-torsion interactions is proportional to the product G × ℏ (G being the gravitational constant, ℏ being Planck's constant), i.e., it is nearly 30 orders of magnitude weaker than gravitational interactions, then even if torsion effects do exist in Nature, they cannot make a noticeable contribution to observable phenomena.

However, in the early 1970s, as a result of the work of F. Hehl [6-8], T. Kibble [9], D. Sciama [10] and others, it was shown that this conclusion is valid not for torsion fields in general, but only for static torsion fields generated by spinning sources without radiation.

Over the following 20 years, a large number of works on the theory of dynamic torsion (spinning source with radiation) appeared. In these works it was shown that the Lagrangian of a spinning source with radiation contains up to a dozen terms with constants that depend neither on G nor on ℏ, and with respect to which the theory imposes no requirement of their necessary smallness. This fact is well known to specialists in torsion field theory. Nevertheless, the old viewpoint about the smallness of the constants of spin-torsion interactions continued for the following 15 years to psychologically hinder serious and comprehensive engagement with the search for experimental manifestations of torsion effects. Only in the early 1980s in Russia was attention drawn to the global significance of the conclusions of the dynamic theory of torsion fields. It was then that attention was drawn to the existence in physics of an extensive experimental phenomenology containing many experimental results that had found no explanation from the standpoint of the four known interactions, and which constitute the experimental manifestation of torsion effects. With the creation, in the 1980s, of the world's first torsion field generators in Russia, targeted research was deployed and carried out in many directions to search for manifestations of torsion fields, which yielded a large volume of practical results.

Torsion fields can theoretically be introduced in many different ways [11,12]. However, at a fundamental level they are naturally introduced within the framework of the concept of the Physical Vacuum [13]. For this, Einstein's equations, the Yang-Mills equations, and the Heisenberg equations are written in spinor form and fully geometrized:

• Geometrized Einstein equations

The indicated system of equations is solved in a space of absolute parallelism, supplemented by rotational coordinates.

One can construct solutions satisfying this system of equations and describing electromagnetic, gravitational, and torsion fields.

For a number of situations, it is useful to interpret fields as polarization states of the physical vacuum in a certain sense.

Let us make a number of preliminary remarks. We shall consider the Physical Vacuum as a material medium isotropically filling all space (both free space and matter), having a quantum structure and being unobservable (on average) in the unperturbed state. Such a Vacuum is described by the operator â₀ [52]. Different vacuum states arise upon violation of the symmetry and invariance of the Vacuum [14]. In particular cases, when considering different physical processes and phenomena, the observer usually creates models of the Physical Vacuum adequate to these processes and phenomena. The use of different models of the Physical Vacuum is characteristic of modern astrophysics, in which, for example, Q-vacuum, Urnu vacuum, Boulevard vacuum, Hartle-Hawking vacuum, Rindler vacuum, etc., are used as constructive models.

In the modern interpretation, the Physical Vacuum is conceived as a complex quantum dynamical object that manifests itself through fluctuations. The theoretical approach is built on the concepts of S. Weinberg, A. Salam, and S. Glashow.

However, as will become clear from the subsequent analysis, it was deemed expedient to return to P. Dirac's electron-positron model of the Physical Vacuum in a somewhat modified interpretation of this model. The return to Dirac's models, despite the known shortcomings and contradictions of this model, can be considered justified, and the models themselves as not having exhausted their constructive potential, if they help formulate conclusions that do not directly follow from modern models.

At the same time, considering that the Vacuum is defined as a state without particles, and proceeding from the model of classical spin as a ring-shaped wave packet [15] (following Belinfante's terminology [16] — a circulating energy flux), we shall consider the Vacuum as a system of ring-shaped wave packets of electrons and positrons, rather than electron-positron pairs proper.

Under the assumptions made, it is easy to see that the condition of true electroneutrality of the electron-positron Vacuum will be met when the ring-shaped wave packets of electrons and positrons are nested within one another. If, in this case, the spins of these nested ring packets are opposite, then such a system will be self-compensensated not only in charges but also in classical spin and magnetic moment. Such a system of nested ring-shaped wave packets we shall call a phyton (Fig. 1A).

A dense packing of phytons [17] will be considered as a simplified model of the Physical Vacuum (Fig. 1B).

It is useful to note that in the experiments of A. Krish [18], the observed effects are equivalent to a demonstration of the possibility of realizing, albeit dynamic, nested states in systems with opposite spins, as in the assumed phyton model. Let us also point to another important circumstance confirming, at the very least, the admissibility of the phyton model. In accordance with the model of D. Bjorken [19-21], one can construct electrodynamics without resorting to the concept of photons, based only on the interacting electron-positron field. The connection between electromagnetism and Vacuum fluctuations was noted by L.A. Rivlin [24]. Earlier, similar ideas, but for the gravitational field, were formulated by A.D. Sakharov [25].

Formally, given the spin compensation of phytons, their mutual orientation in the ensemble, in the Physical Vacuum, could seemingly be arbitrary. Let us consider the most practically important cases of perturbation of the Physical Vacuum by various external sources. This may help assess the realism of the developed approach.

If the Vacuum has a phyton structure, then the action of the charge will be expressed in charge polarization of the Physical Vacuum, as conventionally depicted in Fig. 1C. This case is well known in quantum electrodynamics [26]. In particular, the Lamb shift is traditionally explained through charge polarization of the electron-positron Physical Vacuum [27].

Taking into account the aforementioned model of D. Bjorken, the ideas of Ya.B. Zel'dovich [23], as well as [19], the state of charge polarization of the Physical Vacuum can be interpreted as the electromagnetic field (E-field).

Polarization states of gravitation were discussed in [28].

Dynamic longitudinal polarization corresponds to the property of non-shieldability of the gravitational field. V.A. Bunin [29], and later V.A. In physics, theories involving superluminal velocities are not usually considered. This is because in such cases many thought experiments lead to violations of causal connections. However, it is possible that at a higher level of knowledge, the "superluminal catastrophe" will be overcome just as the "ultraviolet catastrophe" was overcome in its time.

The proposed approach to interpreting the mechanism of gravitation is not something exotic. In theories of induced gravity [31], the gravitational field is considered as a consequence of the decompensation of the Vacuum that arises during its polarization [23, 25, 32].

In the works of Butorin [33, 34], as well as Bershadsky and Mekhedkin [35, 36], estimates of the oscillation frequency characteristic of gravitation were obtained. However, the spread of these estimates is very large, ranging from 10⁹ to 10⁴⁰ Hz. There is reason to assume that the frequency range 10²⁰–10⁴⁰ Hz is more realistic.

If the mechanism of gravitation is indeed connected with longitudinal spin polarization of the Physical Vacuum, then in this case we must recognize that the nature of gravitation is such that antigravity does not exist.

3. Let the source of perturbation be classical spin — s. We shall assume that the action of classical spin on the Physical Vacuum will consist in the following. This polarization state can be interpreted as a spin field (S-field), that is, a field generated by classical spin. The formulated approach is consonant with the ideas about torsion fields as a condensate of fermion pairs [37].

The polarization spin states S_R and S_L contradict the Pauli exclusion principle. However, according to the concept of M.A. Markov [38], at densities on the order of the Planck density [28,39], fundamental physical laws may have a form different from the known ones. The abandonment of the Pauli exclusion principle for such a specific material medium as the Physical Vacuum is admissible, probably to no lesser a degree than in the concept of quarks.

In accordance with the described approach, one can say that the single medium — the Physical Vacuum — can exist in different phase (more precisely, polarization) states, EGS-states. This medium in the state of charge polarization manifests itself as the electromagnetic field (E). This same medium in the state of spin longitudinal polarization manifests itself as the gravitational field (G). Finally, this same medium (the Physical Vacuum) in the state of spin transverse polarization manifests itself as the spin (torsion) field (S). Thus, EGS-polarization states of the Physical Vacuum correspond to EGS-fields.

All three fields, generated by independent kinematic parameters, are universal, or first-class fields in the terminology of R. Utiyama: these fields manifest themselves both at the micro- and macroscopic levels. Here it is appropriate to recall the words of Ya.I. Pomeranchuk: "All of physics is the physics of the Vacuum." The developed ideas allow us to approach the problem of, at least, universal fields from certain general positions. In the proposed model, the role of the unified field is played by the Physical Vacuum, whose polarization (phase) states manifest themselves as EGS-fields. Modern Nature does not need "unifications." In Nature there is only the Vacuum and its polarization states. And "unifications" merely reflect the degree of our understanding of the interrelationship of fields.

The concept of the phase state of the Physical Vacuum and the polarization states of the Physical Vacuum in general form has been used in many works (see, for example, [40]). In the past, it has been repeatedly noted that a classical field can be considered as a state of the Vacuum [23,25]. However, the polarization states of the Physical Vacuum were not given the fundamental role that they actually play. As a rule, it was not discussed which polarizations of the Vacuum were meant. In the described approach, the polarization of the Vacuum according to Ya.B. Zel'dovich [23] is interpreted as charge polarization (electromagnetic field). The polarization of the Vacuum according to A.D. Sakharov [25] is interpreted as spin longitudinal polarization (gravitational field). The polarization for torsion fields is interpreted as spin transverse polarization.

The described views correspond to the concept of "informational A-fields" of R. Unlike second-class fields associated with symmetries of space, first-class fields (gauge fields), as R. The EGS concept provides the idea of polarization states of the Physical Vacuum as such a general principle.

Since one cannot assert that polarization states other than the three considered above are impossible, there are no fundamental reasons to a priori deny the possibility of other long-range interactions. The possibility is not excluded that the concept of A-fields and polarization states of the Physical Vacuum (phase states of the Physical Vacuum) will mark the beginning of a breakthrough into the domain of new long-range interactions.

Torsion fields possess properties that differ substantially from the known properties of electromagnetism and gravitation.

The most important properties of torsion fields (radiations) are:

1. Unlike electromagnetism, where like charges repel and opposite charges attract, in torsion fields like charges attract and opposite charges repel.

2. Since torsion fields are generated by classical spin, the effect of a torsion field on some object will change only the spin state of that object.

3. Passage through physical media without interaction with these media, i.e., without losses. It is useful to note that, independently of torsion fields, Soviet physicists showed more than ten years ago that spin signals propagate in such a way that they cannot be shielded.

4. The group velocity of torsion waves is no less than 10⁹ × c. A large review analyzing astrophysical objects moving at speeds greater than the speed of light was published in the journal Physics-Uspekhi (UFN) [53].

The absence of losses during the propagation of torsion waves makes possible communication over long distances using low transmission power. The possibility arises of creating underwater and underground communication. The high group velocity of torsion waves removes the problem of signal delay even within the Galaxy.

5. Since all known substances possess a nonzero collective spin, all substances possess their own torsion field. The spatial-frequency structure of the intrinsic torsion field of any substance is determined by the chemical composition and the spatial structure of the molecules or the crystal lattice of that substance.

6. Torsion fields possess memory. A torsion source with a certain spatial-frequency structure of its torsion field polarizes the Physical Vacuum by classical spin in some surrounding space. In this case, the arising spatial spin structure is preserved after the indicated torsion source is moved to another region of space.

The paradigm of torsion fields has made it possible to obtain fundamentally new results in practically all scientific and technical directions.

2 Torsion Energy Sources

Over the past nearly 20 years, many authors have pointed to the potential possibility of obtaining energy from the Physical Vacuum.

The usual objections against the practical possibility of obtaining polarization effects in the Physical Vacuum are references to the necessity of creating anomalously high electric potentials on the order of 10^16 V/cm. These objections would undoubtedly be valid if we were talking about charge polarization states. But we are discussing spin polarization of the Physical Vacuum, which is not of an electromagnetic nature at all. Spatially stable torsion polarization states have been experimentally observed. The possibility of effective interaction of spinning (rotating) objects with the Physical Vacuum allows us to consider from new positions the possibility of creating torsion energy sources.

The traditional viewpoint reduces to the assertion that, since the Physical Vacuum is a system with minimum energy, no energy can be extracted from such a system. However, this fails to account for the fact that the Physical Vacuum is a dynamic system possessing intense fluctuations, which can be a source of energy. It is useful to note the considerations of Ya.B. Zel'dovich, A.D. Dolgov, and M.V. Sazhin [28], who, writing the conditions for the vacuum as a reflection of the state without particles, obtained the vacuum energy value equal to ℏω_k/2. As the authors noted, this infinite energy was simply forgotten, declared unobservable, and the energy of particles was measured from this infinitely high level.

Considering the vacuum as a collection of non-interacting oscillators with frequencies ω_k, the Hamiltonian can be written in the standard form with creation and annihilation operators. Then the vacuum, as the lowest energy state, has a nonzero energy density.

However, in reality one can quite simply construct a numerical estimate of this density. According to J. Wheeler [39], this estimate is given by the Planck energy density, on the order of 10^93 g/cm³.

In comparison with the density of nuclear matter — 10^14 g/cm³ — the energy density associated with vacuum fluctuations is a very impressive quantity. Other estimates of vacuum fluctuation energy are known, but they are all substantially larger than Wheeler's estimate.

Let us emphasize two conclusions:

1. The energy of vacuum fluctuations is very large in comparison with any other type of energy;

2. The smallness of the torsion energy required for spin polarization of the Physical Vacuum gives hope that through torsion perturbations it will be possible to release the energy of vacuum fluctuations. From these positions, the experimental results obtained in recent decades by Murom, King, Nieper, and others, in which efficiency up to 300-500% was observed [50,51], do not look inadmissibly odious. Their systems with rotation (typically torsion installations), as open systems, received from the vacuum a negligible fraction of energy through weak interaction with the vacuum. Obviously, the indicated theoretical considerations, as well as the indicated experimental results, represent only a weak crack in the door into the energy technology of the next century — ecologically clean and not requiring the consumption of not only combustible materials, but the consumption of any substance.

3 Torsion Propulsion

New ideas about fields and forces of inertia, presented in [13], made it possible to see their connection with torsion fields and to predict the existence in nature of a new class of reference frames, which were called [13] accelerated locally Lorentzian reference frames of the second kind. Unlike the accelerated locally Lorentzian frames of the first kind introduced by A. Einstein, the new frames are formed when compensated forces of inertia act on the center of mass of an isolated system.

A simple example of an accelerated locally Lorentzian reference frame is the frame associated with the center of mass of a rotating gyroscope. Indeed, compensated centrifugal forces of inertia act on the center of mass of a freely rotating gyroscope. Therefore, the center of mass of such a gyroscope is at rest or moves in a straight line and uniformly relative to an inertial observation frame. If the equilibrium of inertial forces in the gyroscope is disrupted in some way, the center of mass of the gyroscope will move with acceleration under the action of internal uncompensated forces.

This conclusion does not contradict the known theorem on the conservation of the momentum of the center of mass of an isolated mechanical system. According to this theorem, the internal forces of an isolated system cannot change the momentum of its center of mass, and the following conditions are used in the proof:

1) internal forces satisfy Newton's third law;
2) internal forces are all those forces that act within the internal volume bounded by the walls of the isolated system.

Most forces of classical mechanics satisfy the first condition and can be divided into external and internal according to the second. However, in mechanics there exist forces that do not satisfy Newton's third law. These are the forces of inertia, since one cannot say from which bodies these forces are applied. Moreover, the forces of inertia do not fall under the second condition, since they are simultaneously both internal and external for an isolated mechanical system.

Consequently, the motion of mechanical systems under the action of internal uncompensated forces of inertia does not contradict the theorem on the conservation of the momentum of the center of mass of an isolated system of Newtonian mechanics, since the forces of inertia do not satisfy the conditions under which this theorem is proved.

As an example of a mechanical system whose center of mass moves under the action of uncompensated forces of inertia, a device is proposed that demonstrates the connection between translational and rotational forces of inertia, which can be called a four-dimensional gyroscope. It consists of a central mass M and two masses m rotating synchronously toward each other about an axis fixed on the central mass M (see Fig. 2).

If at some moment this system is given mechanical energy, it will begin to move, and we have the equations of motion (1) and (2) from [13].

The considered mechanical system is called a four-dimensional gyroscope because in the equation of motion (1) rotation occurs along the spatial angle φ and the space-time angle θ, related to the translational acceleration of the system by the relation θ̇ = v/c, where c is the speed of light.

From Fig. 2 it can be seen that the reference frame associated with the center of mass of the four-dimensional gyroscope turns out to be an accelerated locally Lorentzian reference frame of the second kind. In this frame, the equilibrium of inertial forces can be disrupted in two ways:

a) by acting on it with an external force F_e (the interaction problem);
b) by acting on the rotation axis of the small weights with an internal moment M₀ (the self-action problem).

A four-dimensional gyroscope with self-action was first realized in practice, apparently, by the Russian engineer Vladimir Nikolaevich Tolchin [48] and was called by him an "inertial." Working as the chief designer of the Perm Machine-Building Plant, V.N. Tolchin built inertials of various types, a number of whose characteristics are given in his book [48]. Structurally, Tolchin's inertial is designed so that to control the speed of its center of mass there is a device called a motor-brake. The purpose of this device is to carry out the self-action of the inertial in the sectors 330°-360° and 160°-180°, with the speed of the center of mass increasing from 0 to approximately 10 cm/s in the sector 330°-360°, and decreasing from 10 cm/s to 0 in the sector 160°-180°.

The experiments performed by V.N. Tolchin indicate the reality of the existence of a new class of accelerated reference frames — accelerated locally Lorentzian frames of the second kind. They are of an encouraging nature and will make it possible in the future to create a fundamentally new type of propulsion.

4 Torsion Technologies for Material Production

It is well known that during the cooling of a melt, the formation of the solid phase of a substance (for example, a metal) is realized through two processes. The ions in the melt must take positions in the potential wells corresponding to the positions of the nodes of the crystal lattice of the solid body, and the spins of the ions (atoms) must be oriented along the edges of the lattice as prescribed by the type of crystal lattice. The latter circumstance is usually used to explain dia-, para-, and ferromagnetism. Failure to fulfill either of these two conditions leads to the structure of the solid substance being different from the natural one prescribed by the traditional laws of solid-state physics.

As a result of the action of an external torsion field (radiation) on a melt, for example from a torsion generator, only the spin state of the system of free atoms in the melt will change. If isotropic torsion radiation acts on the melt of a substance, then with sufficient exposure time and correctly set melt parameters, all atoms of the melt will transition to a state of unidirectional spin orientation. In this state, through spin-torsion interactions, the atoms will experience mutual attraction. Due to this mutual torsion attraction, the melt, as a spin system, will be internally stable. As a result, the strong mutual torsion attraction, even with slow cooling, will not allow the atoms to orient their spins along the edges of the crystal lattice, and the lattice will not be realized. The consequence of this will be an amorphous structure of the substance (metal), a quasi-glass structure.

When the above conditions are met and the melt is acted upon by torsion radiation with a non-isotropic spatial-frequency structure, either crystallization will occur but with a crystal lattice "induced" in the substance by the established structure of the external torsion field, or torsion-induced defects of the crystal lattice will arise.

All the theoretically predicted variants of the results of torsion field action on metal melts were experimentally confirmed at the Institute of Materials Science Problems of the Academy of Sciences of Ukraine in joint work with the ISTTs VENT during the period 1989-1993.

Fig. 3 shows a photomicrograph of a tin specimen after a control melt (Fig. 3A) and after a melt with torsion radiation at a frequency of 8 Hz (Fig. 3B). It is easy to see that the metal processed in the melt has larger grains of nearly identical sizes. The structure of the metal is isotropic in volume. Investigations showed that the grains lack a conventional integral crystal lattice, forming a highly dispersed state [41], close to absolute amorphization.

In other series of experiments with copper [42], changes in grain structure were observed (Fig. 4A,B), as well as the appearance of twins as a result of torsion action on the copper melt (Fig. 5A,B).

During the period from 1994 to 1995, changes in the structure and physicochemical properties of metals were demonstrated on factory smelting furnaces.

The theoretical prediction that ordinary materials cannot shield torsion fields was demonstrated using the example of torsion effects on metal melts in solid metal grounded Tamman furnaces. The predicted informational, rather than energetic, character of torsion effects was confirmed in works where structural restructuring of up to 200 kg of steel was achieved by a torsion generator consuming 10 mW of electrical energy.

5 Torsion Means of Communication and Information Transmission

In traditional radio communication, the large required powers are necessary to compensate for the weakening of signals during their passage through free space due to their attenuation according to the inverse-square law, as well as to compensate for losses during the passage of signals through absorbing media.

In this case, the compensation must be carried out to such an extent that the transmitted signal at the receiver input has an intensity exceeding the sensitivity of the receiver.

In addition, given the speed of radio signal propagation, signal delay already creates certain difficulties in satellite communication systems. These difficulties grow into serious problems for communication with deep-space probes.

Difficulties with over-the-horizon communication lead to the necessity of building complex global communication networks with repeaters.

In some cases, radio communication can be implemented not only in the very-long-wavelength range but, for example, for underground communication as well; however, this loses the speed of information transmission, not to mention the obvious technical difficulties.

A number of radio communication problems are in principle unsolvable, such as communication with spacecraft re-entering from orbit, since they are shielded by the plasma that forms around these vehicles upon entering the dense layers of the atmosphere.

Some radio communication problems cannot be solved because existing systems are close to the physically limiting capabilities. Systems with a throughput close to Shannon limits are known.

All the indicated problems are overcome when using torsion communication [43]. It is sufficient to point to the three properties of torsion radiation noted above: torsion radiation is not attenuated with distance and is not absorbed by natural media, and has a group velocity no lower than 10⁹ × c.

Since torsion signals are not attenuated with distance and are not absorbed, there is no need for high transmitter power even over long paths. Due to the absence of absorption by natural media, torsion signals make it possible to provide both underground and underwater communication, and communication through plasma. With such a high group velocity, it is possible to solve problems of communication, control, and navigation in real time even within the Galaxy, not only the Solar System.

The first experiments on the transmission of binary signals over a torsion communication channel were conducted in April 1986 in Moscow. The torsion transmitter was installed on the first floor of a building and had no devices such as a radio antenna that could be brought to the roof. The torsion receiver was placed on the second floor of a building at a distance of about 22 km (Fig. 6). Under these conditions, the torsion signal could propagate only in a straight line from transmitter to receiver.

This meant that, in addition to terrain features, given the density of construction in Moscow, the torsion signal had to overcome a screen equivalent to a reinforced concrete wall more than 50 m thick. For radio communication without repeaters, this is a practically unsolvable problem.

In the conducted communication sessions, the binary torsion signal of the start-stop telegraph code M2 was received without error while the torsion transmitter consumed 30 mW of energy. In additional experiments, the torsion transmitter was brought to the receiver (zero-length path). In this case, the intensity of the registered signal did not change. This demonstrated that for torsion communication, as predicted by theory, the torsion signal is not absorbed and is not attenuated with distance.

6 Torsion Geophysics

At the fundamental level, as already noted, the nature of torsion fields is connected with classical spin. Two important consequences follow from this.

First, since atoms in all molecules and in all crystals have not only a definite spatial position but also a strictly defined mutual orientation of spins, all molecules and all crystals have their own torsion field with a characteristic spatial-frequency distribution of intensity (spatial-frequency spectrum). A large quantity of homogeneous substance will create a collective characteristic (for the given substance) torsion field. Given that torsion fields are not absorbed by natural media and their intensity does not decrease with distance, a locally concentrated homogeneous substance located at any depth within the Planet will create outside the planet the same characteristic torsion field as if this substance were on the planet's surface. Therefore, by registering the spatial-frequency structure of the torsion fields of planets, one can obtain important information about their internal structure.

Second, the development of directions regarding fields as polarization (phase) states of the Physical Vacuum [1] made it possible to define the torsion field as a state of spin transverse polarization of the Physical Vacuum. This served as the basis, alongside other theoretical factors, to assume that by registering the spatial-frequency structure of the torsion field of a Planet or part of its surface, i.e., the spatial structure of spin polarization states, one can obtain important information about the internal macroscopic structure of the Planet.

It was possible to experimentally confirm the correctness of the assumption that when photographing any objects, the intrinsic torsion fields of these objects arriving at the photographic emulsion together with the electromagnetic (light) flux alter the orientation of the spins of the emulsion atoms in such a way that the emulsion spins reproduce the spatial structure of this external torsion field. As a result, on any photograph, in addition to the visible image, there always exists an invisible torsion image.

Understanding this fact made it possible, by analogy with optical image processing, to construct a procedure for extracting torsion images from photographs and processing them [44]. As indicated in Fig. 7, first a slide or photograph (3) is illuminated by a generator of isotropic broadband torsion radiation (1). In this case, the spin structure of the emulsion atoms can be considered as a two-dimensional spin matrix, which serves as a two-dimensional spin modulator (3).

After the passage of isotropic torsion radiation (2) through the original photograph (3), the modulated torsion radiation (4) will reproduce the spin structure of the spatial torsion field that was perceived by the emulsion during photography. However, this original torsion field is a superposition of torsion fields from all sources within the body of the Planet. These sources can be geological formations or deposits of useful minerals. Since these structural formations have characteristic spatial-frequency spectra, if the task is to isolate, for example, a zone of concentration of a particular substance (mineral), then the modulated torsion radiation (4) must be subjected to appropriate filtration. For this purpose, two-dimensional spin matrices — spin filters — were developed. 7 Torsion Astrophysics

The methods for extracting and processing torsion information presented in the previous section allow a new look at the content and capabilities of astrophysics.

All modern observational astrophysics and astronomy have had the opportunity to work only with visible objects ("visible" in a broad sense, including, for example, radio observations). Given that light from distant sources travels sometimes thousands of light years, and during this time stars undergo significant displacements in space, it is obvious that modern astronomy is in fact not contemporary in the proper sense, but merely paleoastronomy (we study what has long since ceased to be). Let us take into account the superluminal velocity of torsion waves and note that all stars rotate, i.e., are torsion sources. By registering their torsion radiation, one can obtain the true distribution of stars in the sky, their positions in real time of the Universe. The first experimental results on fixing stars in their true positions were performed by N.A. Kozyrev [45], and later by M.M. Lavrentiev, I.A. Eganova [46], and A.F. Pugach [47].

The second important problem of astrophysics is the following contradiction. If we proceed from the existence of only two long-range interactions — electromagnetism and gravitation, in which the wave velocity cannot exceed c, then the interaction time between the edges of the observable Universe would be comparable to the lifetime of the Universe. Then it must be admitted that most of the distant objects of the Universe practically do not interact, i.e., the Universe cannot be considered as an integral system of internally interconnected objects (A.A. Silin was the first to point to this circumstance).

At the same time, it has been known for many decades that stars form a cellular structure, i.e., there is a physical interaction that holds the Universe in the form of such an integral and stable structure. It is possible that, having a velocity on the order of 10⁹ × c, it is precisely the torsion fields of stars that ensure the emergence and existence of the cellular structure in the distribution of stars in the Universe. The possibility is not excluded that the "hidden mass" problem is in fact a consequence of the failure to account for torsion interactions.

In this preliminary analysis, let us point to one more important circumstance. Just as torsion processing of cosmic images can produce images of the internal structure of our planet, by performing such processing on images of stars, for example the Sun, one can obtain information about their internal structure and internal dynamics. This approach opens a fundamentally new possibility in the observation of astrophysical objects. Finally, one more fundamentally new possibility: in standard observations, with the exception of nebula regions, cosmic space appears "isotropically empty." However, as predicted by theory, through the spin states of the Physical Vacuum, i.e., through the torsion fields of free space, one can obtain information about the large-scale structure of cosmic space, the large-scale structure of the Physical Vacuum.

Not only theoretical, but numerous experimental results testify to the fact that torsion fields are an important reality of Nature. The presented information reflects only a small part of the large-scale research carried out, in which more than half a hundred scientific organizations participated. What has already been achieved allows us to conclude that the technologies of the 21st century will be torsion technologies.
