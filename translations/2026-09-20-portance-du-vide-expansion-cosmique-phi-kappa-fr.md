2     Physical foundation: the vacuum as an inertial medium

2.1    Hypothesis of the inertial density of the vacuum
    We posit here a central hypothesis, both conceptual and physical:

   The vacuum possesses an effective inertial density, variable according to the local
conditions of velocity and baryonic density.
   Contrary to the classical view of the vacuum as an "absence of matter," we adopt
here an active conception: the vacuum is a reactive medium, capable of responding
dynamically to the acceleration of matter within its volume.
   This inertial density of the vacuum, denoted ρeff_vacuum, is not constant but emerges from a
kinetic coupling with the local baryonic matter, of the form:

                                       ρeff_vacuum ∝ ρ · v

where:
   — ρ is the local baryonic density,
   — v is the local orbital velocity (galaxy),
   — the proportionality constant α will be introduced formally in the following
      section.

2.2    Experimental results – The vacuum has weight
   The precise cosmological measurements from the Planck, DES, and BOSS
collaborations (2021) give for the energy density of the cosmological vacuum:

ρΛ = (60.3 ± 1.3) × 10−31 g/cm3

This value, associated with dark energy, represents approximately 68% of the total energy
content of the universe.

Conceptual distinction: The dark energy density ρΛ observed
      cosmologically differs from the hypothesis of a "local inertial density of the vacuum" proposed
      in the Φκ model. The former is a global property of spacetime,
      while the latter would be a local reaction to moving matter.

Another well-established experimental datum: the Casimir effect, observed since 1948,
demonstrates that the vacuum exerts a measurable force between two neutral
metallic plates, solely by virtue of the local reorganization of the permitted quantum modes.
   These results suggest that the vacuum can be treated as a compressible physical
medium, endowed with a dynamic topology, sensitive to:
   — the geometric configuration,
   — the local energy density,
   — and the motion of matter.

2.3    Dynamic interpretation: compressible fluid analogy
    The behavior of the vacuum is here modeled as that of a weakly compressible anisotropic
fluid, whose inertial response to a moving baryonic
source gives birth to a scalar field Φκ, analogous to a distributed lift.
    A galaxy rotating in the vacuum acts like a moving wing in a fluid:
it creates an inertial gradient in its environment, perceptible as an additional
force.
    This force is the macroscopic manifestation of the Φκ field, defined as:

Φk ∝ ρ · v 2

This field modifies orbital trajectories as if an invisible force (of the dark matter
type) were present, whereas it is in fact a delayed inertial reaction of the vacuum.

2.4    Position within the framework of general relativity
    The definition of a Φκ field does not contravene the foundations of general
relativity:
    — It is a classical scalar field, not tensorial, that does not modify the Einstein

    — Its coupling is external to the geometry, introduced via an additive Lagrangian, and

    — It can therefore be superposed on a ΛCDM model, or partially or totally

    This field is invariant under a change of local inertial reference frame (covariant
formalism), and its effects are null in zones of low velocity or negligible density —
which makes it compatible with precise observations of the solar system.
