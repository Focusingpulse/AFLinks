No — everything you have read above is not an excerpt from a science fiction novel, but a fragment from a "closed" documentary film about an unprecedented experiment conducted ten years ago at the Odesa Institute of Clinical Experimental Medicine (IKEMA) under the direction of Academician Vlail Kaznacheev. The results of the experiment were not made public. It is known only that one of the participants in the experiment died under strange circumstances (his voice sounds in the documentary footage), others ended their lives by suicide. In some measure the experiment is a continuation of the research of the late astrophysicist Kozyrev, who studied the interconnection of space and time.

In the opinion of Academician Kaznacheev, "Kozyrev was the first to approach experimentally the understanding that in the Universe, besides Einstein's space (- time and space), as a form of interaction of universal matter, there exists another principle of interconnection, carried out by means of certain energy flows radiated by stellar bodies. And these flows can be registered by earthly instruments. In the course of his research, Kozyrev discovered that these stellar signals reflect similarly to light off aluminum plates. And 'if one creates spheres of aluminum and places a person inside, then that person will find themselves in the space of a world unknown to us. By means of these mirrors, a person can for the first time find themselves simultaneously in two spaces with a geometry unknown to us.'"

The first experiments with the "Kozyrev mirrors" began in 1989. The "mirrors" were simply wooden shields with aluminum plates. But already after the first sessions, one of the experimenters received information (from the mirror space?) about how the real mirrors should look. On IKEMA's order, aluminum spheres were manufactured at the Chkalov factory — enormous cylinders. Eight cylinders, 2.5 meters tall, in all. Then four people were selected — two men and as many women, employees of the institute. The experiment lasted three months. Sessions were held three times a week, 40 minutes each.

The mirrors — a channel in time and space

"The mirrors by themselves are just pieces of iron; their unique properties manifest only through people. Personally, in all that time I saw almost nothing. But I felt a great deal. For example, that the mirrors take away energy. It is precisely on this basis that many of those who had been there before us developed suicidal moods. The mirrors also open a space-time channel, but not for everyone. For Dmytro and me (his voice sounds in the footage), it opened and held in our reality for two months. As we learned later, it was a channel of transition into 49 worlds. The exit into these worlds went through Shambhala, which was a kind of checkpoint into the unknown. Once we were 'sitting' in the mirrors, and Dima says to me: look, there is Shambhala, the pyramids. But I saw nothing. We did not enter a trance, but there was a special feeling upon entering the mirror-world. As if you cross some boundary, beyond which the real world behind the mirrors seems illusory. Over 35 sessions we visited all 49 worlds. Not physically, of course. Dmytro described in great detail where we had been. But for some reason I did not want to record his stories on tape. The very presence in that space already changes you somehow, and that is enough. These changes are not external, but internal, deep ones that are impossible to describe.

I never asked to go into the mirrors, but that is how it turned out. Dima told me: if you think you ended up here by accident, you are greatly mistaken."

Two months after the start of the experiment, the military became seriously interested in its results. Vadim and his comrade were not at all eager to play the role of test rabbits for army needs, and they quietly, "English-style," left the experiment. Dmytro became a collaborator of the famous "white" lama Ole Nydahl, began building a Buddhist temple in the Altai, but soon died under strange circumstances. It happened at 8 in the morning. The car Dmytro was driving suddenly flipped over on absolutely flat ground. (As it later turned out, the local traffic police generally consider it a strange place.) It suddenly just began to flip, and then froze upside down. Dmytro died two hours later. He could still have been saved, but neither his companions as they "hitchhiked" (they, for some reason, survived) nor anyone else stopped. It was as if they were not seen. Then cars stopped every five minutes, but it was already too late. Telling of his friend's death, Vadim seems slightly bewildered. Dima was full of energy. Ole Nydahl told him: if I had a dozen guys like you, I would turn the world upside down. The impression is that Dima was stopped at full speed. By whom? Vadim shrugs. Will we ever find out? In all likelihood, it was somehow connected with the experiment. Soon after leaving the mirror-world, Vadim's own wife died. He knows little about the other participants in the experiment. — "They tried to lure me back into the mirrors, but I refused. They continue to experiment with the mirrors, but now this program is financed by the military. As far as I know, the goal of the project is to send information tentacles into the future, in order to somehow influence it."
