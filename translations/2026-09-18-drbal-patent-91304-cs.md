KAREL DRBAL, PRAGUE

Method of maintaining razor blades and razors sharp

Filed 4 November 1949 (P 2399-49)
                             Patent validity from 1 April 1952

The invention relates to a method of maintaining razor blades and razors sharp without using any auxiliary source of energy for this purpose. In sharpening razor blades according to the invention, therefore, no mechanical, thermal, or chemical means are used, nor electrical means from an artificial electrical source. For grinding worn razor blades, various mechanical grinding devices have hitherto been used, in which the dulled edge is sharpened by rough intervention of an abrasive mass, which always entails a certain new wear of the edge material caused by the grinding process. It is further known to improve the sharpness of razor blades and razors by using an artificial magnetic field, into which these objects are placed so that their cutting edge lies substantially in the direction of the lines of force.

According to the invention, the razor blade is placed in the Earth's magnetic field under a hollow cone of dielectric material, e.g. of stiff paper, paraffined paper, hard cardboard, or some synthetic material. The cone has an opening at the bottom of square, round, oval, etc. shape, through which the blade is inserted. A four-sided cone with a square base is best suited, wherein the side of the square advantageously equals the height of the cone multiplied by half of Ludolph's number (π/2). Thus, for example, for a height of 10 cm a base of 15.7 cm is chosen. The blade or razor is laid on a support likewise of dielectric material, the same as the cone or also another, e.g. cork,

wood, stoneware, paraffined paper, etc., whose height is chosen between 1/5 and 1/3 of the height of the cone. This support likewise rests on a surface of dielectric material. The dimensions of the support are chosen such that the cutting edge of the blade or razor is free, and its height may also differ from the stated range. Although this is not an essential requirement, it is recommended to place the blade on the support so that its cutting edge points west or east respectively, and thus its lateral sides, as well as its longitudinal axis, point north and south. This means that, to increase the effectiveness of the device, it is recommended that the cutting edge of the blade or razor lie substantially in the direction of the magnetic lines of force of the horizontal component of terrestrial magnetism. This position improves the effect of the device, but is not necessary for exploiting the principle according to the invention. After correct placement of the blade, it is covered with the cone so that its side walls advantageously point in the direction of the compass points, and its edges thus point northwest, southwest, southeast, and northeast.


---


It is advantageous to leave a new blade in the cone for one to two weeks before beginning its use. However, it is necessary to place the blade in the cone immediately after the first shave, and not when it is already old and dulled. Nevertheless, an old blade may also be used if it has been correctly re-sharpened. The blade, placed in the cone in the manner described above, is left at rest until the next shave. After each subsequent shave, the blade is always placed again as described, taking care that the same cutting edge always points toward the same compass direction, e.g. toward the west, as in the previous placement, which promotes the sharpening effect.

Example

By using the device according to the invention, 1778 shaves were achieved with 16 blades of the Czechoslovak brand "Dukát Zlato" (Dukát Gold), which makes an average of 111 shaves per blade. The smallest number was 51 and the largest 200. With medium beard strength, an average of 50 shaves can be considered easily attainable.

From the standpoint of the national economy, the advantages of the present invention, both as regards the saving of valuable raw material and as regards monetary gain, appear as follows:

One blade of the stated brand weighs 0.51 g. Let us consider as an average 50 shaves when stored in the cone, as opposed to 5 shaves without storage. It is self-evident that the number of shaves, i.e. the degree of dulling of the blade and the capacity for regeneration of its dulled edge, depends on the quality of the material, the quality of the grinding, and the hardness of the beard. The stated values are therefore chosen as medium ones, but they can be far more favorable still.

Thus, in one year, without the cone, 73 blades are consumed with daily shaving; with the cone only 8 blades, giving an annual saving per person of 65 blades, i.e. 33.15 g of steel.

For the present invention, the cone shape was tested above all, but the invention is not limited to this shape only; it may also relate to another geometric body of dielectric material, used in the manner according to the invention and defined by the fact that in it, too, regeneration of the razor blade's edge takes place, i.e. a reduction of tension in the lattices of crystal bundles and a decrease in the number of defects in them — in other words, a recovery that renews the mechanical and physical properties of the edge.

Subject matter of the patent


---


1. A method of maintaining razor blades and razors sharp by placing them in a magnetic field, in such a way that the cutting edge lies substantially in the direction of the magnetic lines of force, characterized in that the blade or razor is placed in the Earth's magnetic field and covered with a hollow cone of dielectric material, such as stiff paper, optionally impregnated or paraffined, hard cardboard, or synthetic material, preferably four-sided with a square base whose side is most advantageously equal to the height of the cone multiplied by half of Ludolph's number (π/2), wherein the cone is provided at the bottom with a square, round, oval, or elongated opening and is positioned in space with its side edges toward the northwest, southwest, southeast, and northeast, and wherein the blade or razor is advantageously placed horizontally on a support likewise of dielectric material, the same as the cone or also another, such as cork, wood, paraffined paper, or stoneware, whose height is advantageously between 1/5 and 1/3 of the height of the cone and which likewise rests on a base surface of dielectric material, and wherein the position of the blade or razor is chosen so that the cutting edge points from north to south.
   2. A method according to claim 1, characterized in that the blade is placed, after each use, always with the same side facing the same direction.
