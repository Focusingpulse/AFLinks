# Form Waves and Sacred Geometry

October 16, 2025 — Esotericism, Paranormal, Frontier Science

## The hidden energy behind perfect shapes

Perhaps you have never heard of form waves, or you have come across them by chance without ever digging into their meaning. Yet behind this term lies a fascinating concept, one capable of uniting science, spirituality, and natural philosophy. It may represent a key of access to subtle realities, where matter, vibration, and consciousness merge into a single universal language.

Matter, as is well known, emits radiations of various kinds. Some of these are thoroughly familiar — such as electromagnetic or sound waves — while others remain mysterious, difficult to measure yet perceptible through their effects. Among the latter are the so-called form waves, created by the geometric configuration of matter. As the name suggests, it is not the substance itself that generates the phenomenon, but the shape it takes in space. The wavelength of these emissions is infinitesimal, and yet, according to various researchers, extremely influential on the surrounding environment.

Everything that exists vibrates; everything is frequency. Every object, every living being, every thought is a manifestation of energy organized according to a particular harmonic structure. At the atomic level, everything possesses a specific shape determined by the molecular arrangement of its elements. And since form and energy are inseparable, the vibration emitted by a body also varies according to its geometry.

### From French radiesthesia to subtle physics

Modern interest in form waves was born in France in the first half of the twentieth century thanks to the researchers Léon Chaumery and André de Belizal, authors of the work Physique micro-vibratoire et forces invisibles (1959). The two researchers, experts in radiesthesia, theorized that every geometric object was capable of absorbing, transforming, and re-emitting subtle energies coming from the Earth's magnetic field. Their research led to a threefold classification of energies: electric, magnetic, and neutral. The latter, they held, were closely bound to human thought and capable of amplifying its effects. In other words, a geometric shape can become a true mental catalyst, able to interact with the will and consciousness of the observer.

Subsequently, scholars such as P.A. Morel and Jean de La Foye attempted to place the phenomenon within a broader scientific context, linking it to the theory of morphogenetic fields proposed by the Cambridge biologist Rupert Sheldrake. According to Sheldrake, nature possesses a kind of collective memory capable of guiding the shape and behavior of living structures. In this light, form waves could represent the physical expression of these invisible informational fields.

### Sacred geometry: the language of the gods

The idea that the universe is governed by harmonic proportions is ancient. Pythagoras and his disciples spoke of a "harmony of the spheres," a cosmic music produced by the movement of celestial bodies according to perfect mathematical ratios. In the Tetraktys, sacred to the Pythagoreans, the sum of the first four numbers (1+2+3+4=10) represented the totality of the universe. Plato too, in the Timaeus, associated the five Platonic solids with the fundamental elements of matter: earth, air, fire, water, and aether. Centuries later, Johannes Kepler, in the Mysterium Cosmographicum (1596), took up this geometric vision of the cosmos, seeking to explain planetary orbits as the expression of a divine order.

Sacred geometry is born precisely from this awareness: the universe has a harmonic structure, and perfect shapes are its visible manifestation. It is no accident that Egyptian temples, pyramids, Gothic cathedrals, and Islamic mosques share proportions based on the Golden Number (1.618), the symbol of the ideal equilibrium among parts.

### The Flower of Life and primordial knowledge

Among the best-known symbols of sacred geometry we find the Flower of Life, present in very ancient cultures and engraved even on the granite of the Temple of Osiris at Abydos, in Egypt. The symbol is composed of perfectly interwoven circles, from which fundamental figures originate, such as the Vesica Piscis, the Cube of Metatron, and the kabbalistic Tree of Life. In early Christianity, the Flower of Life was interpreted as a symbol of the divine Sun, a representation of God the Father and of the resurrection of Christ. In Celtic traditions, by contrast, it was considered a solar chariot, the emblem of the vital force that renews creation.

The center of the Flower, formed by the perfect intersection of the circumferences, represents the point of absolute equilibrium: the source from which everything comes and to which everything returns. It is the synthesis of the divine, the supreme knowledge that leads to enlightenment. In Hebrew esotericism, the structure of the Flower of Life is connected to the Sephirotic Tree, since the ten Sephiroth — symbols of the divine emanations — can be derived from its geometry.

### Vesica Piscis, the Golden Number, and the shape of divinity

Another figure of great importance is the Vesica Piscis ("fish bladder"), obtained from the intersection of two circles of equal radius, whose centers lie on each other's circumference. The resulting shape, called a mandorla, recurs throughout sacred art: many Byzantine icons show Christ inscribed in a luminous mandorla, a symbol of his twofold human and divine nature. Beyond its symbolic value, the Vesica Piscis also has mathematical significance: from its construction one derives the golden ratio, the proportion that confers harmony and beauty upon forms. This number — found in the spirals of seashells, in the arrangement of flower petals, and even in galaxies — was studied in 1509 by the mathematician Luca Pacioli in his De Divina Proportione, illustrated by Leonardo da Vinci, who made use of it in his most celebrated works.

### The echo of perfection: energy, architecture, and spirit

Looking at the great Gothic cathedrals, one cannot help but perceive a force that transcends matter. Medieval architects and master builders knew and applied sacred geometry as a spiritual language: proportions, angles, arches, and rose windows were designed to channel subtle energies, amplified by the thought and faith of the faithful. Authors such as René Guénon and Fulcanelli have suggested that these architectures functioned as spiritual machines, capable of converting cosmic energy into inner vibration. Today, some scholars find analogies between these ancient intuitions and the modern physics of informational fields, such as that proposed by David Bohm or Ervin Laszlo, according to whom the universe is an immense field of unified consciousness in which information (form) precedes matter.

### Sacred geometry is the bridge between science and the divine

In light of all this, we might say that sacred geometry is not merely a set of aesthetic shapes, but a symbolic language connecting man to the cosmos. Every shape is a vibration, every vibration is a piece of information, and every piece of information is a fragment of divine thought. Perfection belongs to God, but man, imperfect though he is, may aspire to it through knowledge, contemplation, and inner harmony.

Perhaps, as the ancient hermetic principle taught: "That which is above is like that which is below, and that which is below is like that which is above, to accomplish the miracles of the one thing." And so, from the invisible waves of matter to the perfection of sacred geometries, a single truth manifests: the universe is form that vibrates, and vibration that prays. In the end, every temple, every symbol, every proportion is a way through which man attempts to draw near to the divine mind. Form waves are nothing but the reflection of a greater principle: the idea that the whole of creation, in every detail, is a perfect symphony.

I do not pretend to convince anyone, but I invite you to observe reality with new eyes. Perhaps, behind the simplest shapes, there truly hides the secret of the connection between God, matter, and consciousness.

MARIO CONTINO
