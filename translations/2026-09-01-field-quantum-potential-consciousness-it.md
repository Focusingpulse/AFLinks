# Field and Quantum Potential of Consciousness

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.48274/IBI16

**Author:** Antonio Manzalini (ORCID: 0000-0003-1633-3099)

Abstract
Living organisms, from a thermodynamic point of view, are similar to open systems that operate far from equilibrium. In fact, the metabolic processes of life are based on continuous internal flows and exchanges of energy, matter, and information between organisms and the surrounding environment.
At a deeper physical level, however, these exchanges are mediated by wave-like quantum oscillations that include, for example: mechanical, electromagnetic, nuclear, and thermal oscillations. Even the vacuum, according to Quantum Field Theory (QFT), is the seat of unceasing quantum fluctuations. In light of this, it is reasonable to argue that living organisms are open systems far not only from thermodynamic equilibrium but also from quantum equilibrium. This determines and influences the different levels of consciousness of living organisms.
The article proposes a model of the consciousness of living organisms, based on a broader approach than that of Quantum Mechanics and QFT: this new approach in fact proposes to consider the processes of consciousness beyond quantum equilibrium, a fundamental characteristic of life. The proposed consciousness model leverages the de Broglie-Bohm theory that led to the definition of the quantum field and quantum potential: in particular, consciousness is defined as a quantum field, whose associated potential is processed by the living organism as active information. In analogy with the quantum field of the de Broglie-Bohm theory for inanimate matter, the quantum field of consciousness of living matter also has no sources, and its shape or curvature has more physical importance than its amplitude: in particular, it is proposed that this curvature is associated with condensations of Nambu-Goldstone bosons (as predicted by QFT) following symmetry breaking of this field.

Introduction
Living organisms, from a physical point of view, can be modeled as open systems that operate far from thermodynamic equilibrium. Life, in fact, is based on exchanges of energy, matter, and information with the environment. This high-level model conforms to the definition of dissipative structures proposed by Nobel laureate Ilya Prigogine.

Descending to a deeper descriptive physical level, these exchanges are mediated by wave-like quantum vibrations such as, for example, mechanical, electromagnetic, nuclear, and thermal vibrations. Even the vacuum, according to Quantum Field Theory (QFT), is the seat of quantum fluctuations that constitute a fundamental ground state. All these quantum vibrations and oscillations create, through complex interference patterns, a continuum of quantum fields capable of shaping the structure of matter, coherence levels, and, consequently, various biological functionalities (Umezawa, 1993; Blasone et al. 2011; Lloyd, 2011) and consciousness.


---


The idea of finding a model for the dynamics of life with Quantum Mechanics (QM) has a long history and encompasses a very rich literature. One of the first examples in this regard dates to 1944, when Nobel laureate E. Schrödinger wrote his book "What is Life?". Another example is the pioneering research of H. Fröhlich aimed at demonstrating that the concept of quantum coherence is an intrinsic property of living cells (for example, used for long-range interactions such as the synchronization of cell division processes). This research direction has been carried forward by numerous theoretical and experimental activities and publications (Fröhlich, 1968; Del Giudice et al., 1983, 1985, 1986, 1988). Recently, new confirmations are found in the rapid progress of quantum biology (Ball, 2011; Salari et al., 2011; Al-Khalili and McFadden, 2015).

In light of these developments, it seems reasonable to argue that living organisms should be considered open systems not only far from thermodynamic equilibrium but also far from quantum equilibrium. In that case, life and, consequently, consciousness, should be modeled through a broader approach than Quantum Mechanics and QFT.

The main purpose of this work is to analyze and propose this new approach, in line with what Antony Valentini envisioned in (Valentini, 2009), where he argues that quantum theory could be a special case of a much broader physics, in which the systems considered are far from quantum equilibrium. In particular, the reference physical theory is that of the de Broglie pilot wave.

De Broglie's theory states, for example, that a quantum particle is always accompanied by a field and that together they form a single quantum system. The quantum field that accompanies matter governs its dynamics and evolves in time according to the Schrödinger equation. This theory was taken up again in 1952 by D. Bohm, who noted that the theory is non-local, a consideration that led to the development of the concept of quantum potential and active information.

In summary, this article extends this vision of de Broglie-Bohm by proposing a general model of consciousness as a quantum field that accompanies living matter. The potential of consciousness associated with the field is active information that is "processed and exchanged" by the living organism through symmetry breaking processes far from equilibrium.


---


What is proposed is also in line with (Hiley, Pylkkänen, 2005) where the Authors reinterpreted Eccles' proposal on the existence of a mental field in light of the de Broglie-Bohm theory. In fact, Eccles in (Eccles, 1986) proposed considering the importance of quantum processes in understanding brain activity, and thus, following a suggestion by Margenau (Margenau, 1984), proposed the idea of a mental field associated with brain activity. In (Hiley, Pylkkänen, 2005) this mental field is defined as a container of active information that contributes to the quantum potential.

In analogy with the quantum field of the de Broglie-Bohm theory for inanimate matter, the quantum field of consciousness of living matter also has no sources and its shape or curvature has more physical importance than amplitude: in particular, it is proposed that this curvature is associated with condensations of Nambu-Goldstone bosons (as predicted by QFT) following symmetry breaking of this field.

The quantum potential (in the de Broglie-Bohm theory) should therefore be considered as a special subset of the potential of consciousness, in the same way that, as argued by Antony Valentini, quantum theory constitutes a special subset of a broader physics that deals with systems far from quantum equilibrium (namely, living organisms).

In summary, the main contributions of this work propose that:
   •   Theoretical foundations of a broader approach (than that of current Quantum Mechanics) in the study of consciousness processes, based on the fact that living organisms are far from quantum equilibrium.
   •   A model of consciousness as a quantum field, based on the extension of the concept of quantum field from the de Broglie-Bohm theory. The potential of consciousness associated with the field is active information that is "processed and exchanged" by the living organism through symmetry breaking processes far from equilibrium.

A new model: field and quantum potential of consciousness
Recall that according to de Broglie's theory, the motion of a particle is determined by the wave function of the quantum field, called the pilot wave, which can be described by the Schrödinger equation. In 1952, D. Bohm took up de Broglie's pilot wave theory and applied it to methods of measuring quantum quantities: in this research, D. Bohm demonstrated that the outcome of a measurement on a particle can depend on macroscopic operations (the so-called hidden variables) performed instantaneously on a distant particle (a phenomenon also called non-locality).

J. Bell also contributed to this line of research, arguing that the pilot wave should be understood as a real field in the proper sense, and not simply a mathematical object or a probability wave, as previously hypothesized. As well explained in (Valentini, 2009), the pilot wave theory provides the same observable results as conventional quantum theory, but in the case where the initial positions of the particles have a standard distribution according to the Born rule (which is precisely an equilibrium condition). Recall that the Born rule (formulated by Max Born in 1926) was considered a key postulate of quantum mechanics: in its simplest form, the Born rule states that the probability distribution of finding a particle at a given point is proportional to the square of the amplitude of the wave function.

However, in de Broglie's pilot wave theory, the quantum equilibrium distribution — according to the Born rule — should be considered only a special case. The pilot wave theory would also allow other non-equilibrium quantum distributions. D. Bohm, as well as other physicists after him, sought to provide proof of this fact: the Born rule should not represent a postulate, but rather a quantum equilibrium hypothesis that applies when equilibrium is reached during the time evolution of the Schrödinger equation. On this basis, as mentioned earlier, Antony Valentini (Valentini, 2009) argued that quantum theory could be a special case of a much broader physics, in which the quantum equilibrium distribution — according to the Born rule — is only a special case.


---


As mentioned, living organisms are examples of systems far from quantum equilibrium (not only thermodynamic). Ensembles of quantum particles, composing for example membranes, organelles, cells, tissues, organs..., could be able to perform actions at a distance and non-local information exchanges (based on entanglement), thus creating conditions of quantum coherence and absolute simultaneity. These characteristics, in effect, are considered an essential prerogative of life and consciousness, a distinguishing element of living matter (far from equilibrium) from inanimate matter (mainly in equilibrium).
Following the reasoning above, this article proposes a general model of consciousness as a quantum field, based on the extension of the de Broglie-Bohm theory to living matter. Bohm's quantum field (which influences and guides inanimate matter) can be seen as a special subset of the quantum field of consciousness (which influences and guides living matter). Similarly, the quantum potential can be seen as a special subset of the potential of consciousness: in effect, as stated, quantum theory is to be considered (ontologically) as a special subset of a broader physics that deals with systems far from quantum equilibrium.

                        Electromagnetic Potentials and Quantum Potential
It has been demonstrated that one of the main characteristics of living organisms is coherence (Fröhlich, 1968; Del Giudice et al., 1983, 1985, 1986, 1988). In order to analyze the impact of quantum potentials on coherent systems, this section provides a relevant example, albeit limited to the electromagnetic vector potential.

Recall that the Aharonov-Bohm effect (Aharonov, Böhm, 1959) demonstrates that the electromagnetic vector potential can be present even in spatial regions where the corresponding electromagnetic fields are absent. This means that, in principle, the electromagnetic vector potential has no energy content: it can be considered as a pure rhythm of oscillation that can propagate at the phase velocity (unconstrained by the limits of the speed of light).

In particular, electromagnetic potentials have two components: the magnetic vector potential A and the electric potential A₀. As described in (Brizhik et al., 2009), for a coherent system, where the phase φ assumes definite values, A and A₀ become observables, through changes in phase. A coherent system is influenced by potentials even in spatial regions where the corresponding electromagnetic fields are absent. On the other hand, a non-coherent system is influenced by electromagnetic fields, and not by potentials, in a manner similar to classical physics. In other words, the characteristic of coherence is to allow a system to perceive the impact of electromagnetic potentials on its phase: this impact can be conveyed at the phase velocity (which is not limited by the speed of light) even in spatial regions where the corresponding electromagnetic fields are absent.

In (Trukhan, Anosov, 2007) the electromagnetic vector potential is in fact described as an information channel that influences living organisms. If the electromagnetic vector potential can change the phase of coherent systems, the latter become like a record of events occurring in the environment, even without the intervention of energy.


---


In summary, in the above analysis of electromagnetic and quantum potentials we can note that:
   •   classical fields, such as gravity or electromagnetism, are generated by sources (e.g., masses or electric charges); on the other hand, the quantum field of the de Broglie-Bohm theory does not appear to be generated by any source;
   •   electromagnetic fields can express an influence on a generic system with the intensity of a wave function, while electromagnetic potentials can guide the evolution of the phase of a coherent system even in the absence of electromagnetic fields (Aharonov-Bohm effect);
   •   the effect of the quantum potential is independent of the intensity of the quantum field but depends only on the shape or curvature of the wave function. This is different from classical waves, which transfer energy and momentum, and therefore produce effects proportional to the intensity of the wave.

Active Information of the Quantum Field of Consciousness
Bohm and Hiley interpreted the quantum potential Q as active information capable of encoding the entire boundary condition of a given surrounding environment. Bohm and Hiley stated that this active information (carried by the wave field and represented by Q) guides the path and velocity of the quantum particle to which it is associated, using the particle's own energy.

This information was defined as active because it is processed, in a sense, by the quantum particle: the working hypothesis was, in fact, that active information was transferred with little or no energy to the particle, but, despite this, it could direct the movement of the particle, which exploited its own internal energy (Bohm and Hiley, 1993). This suggested to them that an elementary particle must have a complex internal structure to process this active information.

Extending this same hypothesis, this article proposes that a living organism can be guided in its dynamics by the potential of consciousness C, which is likewise active information capable of encoding the conditions of the surrounding environment. Obviously, the complexity of the living organism, furthermore far from equilibrium, exceeds that of inanimate matter in processing this type of active information of consciousness.

In analogy with the de Broglie-Bohm theory, the field of consciousness acts on the living organism (through the potential of consciousness) but the living organism does not react back upon it, at least the action-reaction principle is not enacted in classical terms. In fact, just as the quantum field and a particle are not separate entities, the same holds for the field of consciousness and the living organism.


---


The quantum potential Q is not a classical potential, in the sense that it does not propagate, and is not proportional to 1/r (where r is distance): for example, in a many-particle quantum system it produces a non-local effect that does not decrease with increasing distance. This also holds for the potential of consciousness.

As mentioned earlier, in the definition of quantum information, the shape or curvature of the quantum field has more physical importance than the amplitude. This is clearly observed from equations (3) and (4) where Q and C are proportional to the curvature or shape of the field described by the Schrödinger equation.
As well explained in (Riggs, 2008), the shape of a field (such as, for example, the quantum field) can be described with reference to its wave fronts. A wave front is defined as a surface on which the phase of the wave is constant: in the case under examination, the wave fronts depend on what is happening (for example, events) in the environment and on what the quantum field encounters.

The active information of the potential of consciousness C is correlated to the curvature or shape of the quantum field of consciousness: in particular, the article proposes that this curvature is determined by condensations of Nambu-Goldstone bosons (as predicted by QFT) following symmetry breaking of this field. The next section is dedicated to exploring this aspect in depth.

Active Information as Condensation of Nambu-Goldstone Bosons
Quantum Field Theory (QFT) is recognized as the fundamental theory of quantum physics, underlying the Standard Model. QFT provides a theoretical reinterpretation of quantum reality that has been confirmed by several experimental validations (Peskin, 2018).

According to QFT, quantum particles are considered excited energy states of the underlying fields (of force and matter) associated with them. Examples of force fields include: gravitational, electromagnetic, strong nuclear, and weak nuclear fields; examples of matter fields are those that determine the emergence of the electron, neutrino, and up and down quarks.

These fields are subject to continuous oscillations, whose ground state energy level is called the quantum vacuum. The quantum vacuum, therefore, is very different from the classical vacuum, as it is the seat of energy oscillations that couple everything to everything. In consideration of this, any quantum system is considered in QFT as an open system, that is, one that interacts with other systems and the environment through these background fluctuations of the quantum vacuum. This has notable consequences, such as, for example, the fact that the Hamiltonian (which describes the total energy) of any quantum system (including living organisms) always comprises components of the quantum system and of the environment in which it is found (Umezawa, 1993; Vitiello, 1995). In other words, no system is isolated in Nature, and the quantum vacuum contributes to its coupling with the environment.


---


Symmetry breaking (SB) is another physical phenomenon well described by QFT (Umezawa, 1993). SB concerns the transition of a quantum system from a symmetric state to another state of lower symmetry. A lower level of symmetry corresponds to a higher level of order and, therefore, to a lowering of entropy. There are two types of SB: explicit symmetry breaking and spontaneous symmetry breaking. Explicit symmetry breaking implies the presence of a perturbation that determines a change in the basic dynamics of a system. In spontaneous symmetry breaking, the perturbation is weak and acts only to trigger the SB; it does not influence the basic dynamics of the system, which is indeed spontaneous. In this case, it is not the strength or intensity of the perturbation that is important, but its phase (Blasone et al., 2011).

A symmetry breaking phenomenon is accompanied by the presence of Nambu-Goldstone bosons, which are bosons devoid of (or nearly devoid of) mass and energy (in the absence of edge effects): they constitute collective vibrational modes that encode ordering information following symmetry breaking (Ricciardi, Umezawa, 1967; Vitiello, 1995). The accumulation, or condensation, of NG bosons in the quantum vacuum state maintains the minimum energy condition (the bosons are without mass and energy), called the condensed state (Del Giudice et al., 1983, 1988). This condensed state is therefore stable (minimum energy state). NG bosons therefore do not mediate force interactions; rather, they represent ordered modes of such interactions. The evolution of the phase of a coherent system is therefore linked to SB phenomena and as such to condensations of NG bosons.
NG bosons should not be confused with Gauge field bosons. A Gauge boson is an elementary bosonic particle that acts as a force mediator: in Gauge theory, elementary particles interact with each other through the exchange of Gauge bosons, usually as virtual particles. Photons, W and Z bosons, and gluons are examples of Gauge bosons.

Recall that Gauge theory describes the interactions and energy exchanges between quantum particles and their associated wave fields (O'Raifeartaigh, Straumann, 2000). In Gauge theory, the Lagrangian (the functional that expresses the difference of kinetic energy and potential energy) of a system is maintained invariant under continuous local symmetry transformations (called local Gauge transformations), precisely thanks to the presence of Gauge fields. These fields, in other words, are a kind of compensating field capable of maintaining this invariance in the symmetry of the Lagrangian.


---


In every symmetry breaking phenomenon, a kind of balanced competition occurs between long-range ordering correlations from both NG and Gauge bosons. Equilibrium is reached as a function of boundary conditions and the relative strengths of the fields. According to QFT, therefore, the interaction between Gauge and NG bosons plays a key role, also in living matter, in the encoding, storage, and processing of information responsible for the characteristics and biological functions (Del Giudice, 1986) and therefore the different levels of consciousness.

For example, (Freeman, Vitiello, 2008) provides an interesting dissipative quantum model of the brain, based on these QFT considerations. The model is an extension of the many-body system model of the brain, as proposed in 1967 by Ricciardi and Umezawa (Ricciardi, Umezawa, 2004). The model leverages the fact that, in the physics of matter, any information is memorized and transferred through ordered structures maintained by long-range correlations mediated by NG bosons. In brain dynamics, according to this model, NG bosons are called dipole wave quanta (DWQ) because they are generated by the breaking of rotational symmetry of the vibrational field of electric dipoles of water. The density of the NG boson condensation provides a measure of the degree of ordering or coherence of the system: in fact, the condensation of NG bosons is described by a condensation function f(x) that acts as a "form factor" (Freeman, Vitiello, 2010).

Therefore, in line with what was reported in the previous sections, this article proposes that the active information (or potential of consciousness C) of the extended de Broglie-Bohm theory (to living systems) can be expressed in terms of NG boson condensation functions (which are form factors of the quantum field of consciousness).

Conclusions
The article starts from the consideration that living organisms, from a thermodynamic point of view, are similar to open systems, operating far from equilibrium, thanks to continuous flows and exchanges of energy, matter, and information with the surrounding environment.


---


Shifting the analysis, however, to a deeper physical level, these flows and exchanges in living systems are mediated by quantum mechanical, electromagnetic, nuclear, and thermal oscillations. Even the vacuum, according to QFT, is an arena of quantum fluctuations. Living systems are therefore to be considered systems far from both thermodynamic and quantum equilibrium. On the basis of this reasoning, the article proposes a more extended approach than the current state of the art of quantum physics, for the study of living systems and their corresponding levels of consciousness.

In particular, the article proposes a general model of consciousness as a quantum field, based on the extension of the de Broglie-Bohm theory to living systems. The potential of consciousness associated with this field is active information that is processed and exchanged by the living organism through symmetry breaking processes (as predicted by QFT). In other words, the quantum potential (in the de Broglie-Bohm theory) should be considered as a special subset of the potential of consciousness. This is in ontological analogy with what Antony Valentini argued: quantum theory must be considered a special subset of a broader physics that deals with systems far from quantum equilibrium (such as, for example, living organisms).

The article concludes by observing that, in analogy with the quantum field of the de Broglie-Bohm theory for inanimate matter, the quantum field of consciousness of living matter also has no sources and its shape or curvature has more physical importance than amplitude: in particular, it is proposed that this curvature is associated with condensations of Nambu-Goldstone bosons (as predicted by QFT) following symmetry breaking of this field.
