Article #4.1 Geometric qualities of matter and energy

Qualities of Geometry, Matter and Energy

Sustainable Geometry (formerly called Sacred Geometry) explains the steps that exist between free energy and the consolidation of this same energy into matter. Energy can be understood as particles in chaotic motion, while matter is that same energy only trapped gravitationally. Free energy is associated with rectilinear movements and the zero point has the powerful characteristic of causing those free particles to be organized around a point of gravity to create matter. Energy and matter are only differences in geometric arrangement. Matter can exist because of the geometric matrices that support and promote the suction of energy in a constructive interface of waves, which means that given the organization of certain geometries, an ordered implosion of chaotic particles is created. This same principle is applicable to healing, the arts, and architecture.

Let us think of a universe made of a single substance. What in science is known as unified field theory, or what in religions is known as a single God. No matter how it is defined, we live in a universe made of the same substance but with different geometric arrangements and different functions. It has been said: one God, many His Names. One Science, many its angles. In the unified field, the first movement of consciousness that is made to create all matter, literally to create the atoms that constitute the universe, is to fix a zero point, a focus of attention.

Fixing the point of attention is fixing a void, a fractal space around which everything will revolve. Accepting that void is accepting the process of passing from energy to matter. From fixing attention, fixing "no tension," the particle or event is subjected to the three mathematical constants that govern the geometric matrix that supports matter: phi, pi, and euler.


---


Phi (pronounced fi) is the harmonic relationship of the smaller to the larger and the larger to the whole. It defines the spiral movement around which everything rotates at the zero point, that spiral unfolds infinitely. The first movement to generate life is spiral movement. Everything in nature, the swirling of water, tornadoes, the sexual encounter between two animals is not a linear confrontation but occurs in a spiral dance. However, the phi spiral or golden spiral does not extend indefinitely but there comes a moment when it begins to curve to create matter. In scientific terms, phi, the golden number, creates heterodynamic conjunction of wave phase.

Phi is an irrational number (whose decimal is infinite and non-periodic) whose numerical value is 1.618033... and we can obtain it in three ways: by the Fibonacci numerical sequence, through a mathematical formula, or by a geometric construction.

Phi has the shape of a "0" crossed by an "I" symbolizing the crossing of the void swept by linear masculine energy, to begin the toroidal process of implosion/explosion. The number phi has been known in history by different names: in Greece and Rome it was known as the golden number, in the Renaissance as the divine number or number of gold. This number has impressive characteristics, but the greatest of them is that it is the number that structures the torsion movement that interrelates different scales, dimensions, or planes of Reality. It is the first constant of ordering of life. Phi besides being a number is a proportion. If we place a straight line and seek to divide it in a way that is sustainable, that is, that the smaller is found in the larger, just as the larger in the whole, we would find that it is only possible to make a cut in one place: at 0.618033... of the total of that line. This is the beginning of fractality, what in most philosophies and religions we find symbolized as the Trinity. In Christianity, it is said that the Father is to the Son, as the Son is to the Holy Spirit; in the Egyptian tradition, Osiris, the creator father; Isis, the mother, and Horus the son; in North American Indians, Safari, father; Ani, son, and Fayo, the spirit. Even in the Hindu Brahmanic tradition there is talk of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva as perfectly interrelated divine forces.

The size of the spiral is determined by pi, which allows phi (fi) to know the dimension of the curvature, that is, the size of matter. Our arm that unfolds in phi patterns does not grow indefinitely but has a limited size. The unlimited nature of the universe is not in size, but in forms. It is as if we took a spiral and it were wrapped in a sphere.


---


Pi is an irrational number with a value of 3.141592... and defines the constant value of the radius of a circle with respect to the circumference. In psychogeometric terms it defines the size of the toroid and is used to circumscribe the energy trapped gravitationally by the spiral and the point of attention or zero point.

The sphere will take consistency through the mediation of euler. It is the most important number in mathematical analysis, determines the constant of the area under the curve, and allows giving consistency to the design created by phi in the spiral and pi in the circumference. For example, euler fits almost all the properties required to describe the strong interactions of elementary particles.

Euler, the last constant, represented by the letter "e," has a value of 2.718281... and is the base of natural logarithms. This means it is the only number with the property that the area of the region bounded by the hyperbola of the "x" axis and the "y" axis is equal to 1. Euler has the property of solidifying the toroid. In biology, one of its main applications is exponential growth. This type of growth arises when there are no factors limiting growth, as occurs in certain bacterial populations, or in the recovery of a forested surface after a fire. This constant is also linked to the golden ratio and the logarithmic spiral. When a chain or cable is hung by its ends, it tends to adopt a shape related to the number "e." Even in something as mundane as calculating bank interest, it is necessary to resort to the euler constant.

Let us see it graphically in the following way. In the chaos of the universe (in our own sexual, emotional, intellectual universe, etc.), the first geometric movement that is made is the spiral movement like a tornado shape, the spiral will extend until it seeks to cover the largest possible area, emerging from the chaos of the void and moving toward order, toward the toroid. In this process of creation, the spiral extends to the size dictated by a sphere; if it continues growing, the spiral begins to surround the sphere and goes around it to start rotating in the opposite direction, moving from the outermost toward the center. Once it reaches the center, it radiates the energy it captured from the outermost and solidifies its own structure, giving it rigidity or flexibility.

In Psychogeometry, this entire process is known as the Pentaflor process. Depending on the level at which this process is viewed, whether in 2D, 3D, or 4D, it can be seen as a point at the center (the void), a spiral (the golden spiral), and a circle that delimits the spiral (a sphere). In most indigenous rituals we find a center of power surrounded by a circle of fire that "protects" from the outside, from the "spirits" or chaotic energies of the order contained in that cosmos. Cosmos means 'Order'; in the cosmic process we emerge from the void and return to it, not without first experiencing the journey through the entire toroid. The experience of our life is summarized in this metaphor. It is the maxim of "know thyself," it is when the spirit travels its creation to the farthest from itself to experience and, finally, to return to itself.

Psychologically, this happens when the Soul lives at the periphery of the Self, in the personality, to understand culture, society, family, and integrate the interaction with others within itself. From the Pentaflor and the toroidal creation it entails, the pattern of the Flower of Life begins to be created, always after the spirit has traveled through itself. The Flower of Life is a symbol used in many religions and beliefs of the world; it is a geometric figure composed of properly separated or spaced circles, superimposed in a coincident manner, and ordered in such a way that they reflect, in a polyptych symmetrical design, a flower with a shape similar to a hexagon. The center of each circle is on the circumference of six more circles that surround themselves, all of the same diameter (we will see them in detail later) in Article 4.2.
