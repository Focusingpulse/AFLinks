In the midst of the new Italian spring, turbulent on all fronts, a sunny day gave its blessing to the long afternoon of science at the Casa dell'Aviatore in Rome, which hosted — thanks to the President of the Anua, General Mario Majorani — one of the most heated Coherence meetings, dedicated to Electromagnetic Fields and the Memory of Water.

After the opening remarks — anything but ceremonial — from General Mario Murace, Gianni Mattioli, and the President of the Italian medical press association, Mario Bernardini, who highlighted the relevance of interdisciplinarity and innovation, the journalist Raffaella Rosa also took the floor, putting her finger on the wound of a press that risks losing the sense of the ethical mission of information.

The proceedings then began with the talk by Cesare Salvaggio, one of the protagonists of the challenge of innovation against the crisis of the Eighties and Nineties, who showed how in companies and military corps the creation of a team spirit and of capacity building is essential to optimize the potential of organized groups, just as also happens in elementary coherent systems.

On the techniques for measuring biological coherence in living systems spoke Massimo Sperini who, with Massimo Scalia, has worked for over 20 years on the development of an equivalent electrical model of the skin and of fine technologies capable of measuring the electrical state along the meridians, seen as an Ohmic system which somehow (still far from clear) plays a role in the regulation of human physiopathology.

The group of Gaetano Gigante of Tor Vergata discussed classical biotechnologies — but not only — analyzing the experimental data from a device that makes it possible to study the evolution of the sad and disabling disease of Parkinson's.

Ubaldo Mastromatteo of STMicroelectronics had instead presented the most advanced biotechnologies applied to classical genetic diagnostics. One may ask when the integration will come between biotechnologies of genetic and molecular derivation and those emerging from the studies of Benveniste and Montagnier — which had been preceded by the German genius of Reinold Voll — which in recent years have developed, particularly in Eastern Europe, into increasingly sophisticated technologies from Kiev to St. Petersburg to far-off Siberia.

And yet Europe — beginning with Voll and Morell, and in Italy too, from Miclavez and then Botarelli to Sperini and many others — has continued in technological and theoretical development, as emerged from the lecture of the physicist Odoardo Calamai, who presented a theoretical development of the cyclotron resonance of ions that could explain the effects of water activation which, in a test during the conference, proved to be anything but trivial.

Studies on cells, again with activated water, were carried out at the CNR by Settimio Grimaldi in collaboration with Umberto Grieco (justifiably absent), shedding light here too on how Benveniste's experiments find confirmation at various levels, both clinical and experimental.

While wrestling with the whims of the computers, in anticipation of hearing about the Memory of Water as seen by a group of analytical chemists such as Pasquale Avino and Mario Russo of the University of Molise, Jerome Benveniste took the floor — a research director at ESA in Frascati — who gave a brief remembrance of his father and of the future of research in this field, which continues particularly in the East: from Russia, where a Benveniste Award is conferred every two years, to China, where a department has been created with about a hundred researchers under the direction of Luc Montagnier, who continues to work on these themes.

Piero Quercia and Giuseppe Quartieri — a biologist and a physicist of long standing — had examined an enigmatic and emerging aspect of coherence in bacteria and in photosynthesis, which would tend to account for the role of quantum forces in biological processes that until today were considered of little interest, almost irrelevant.

Signals of the relevance of electromagnetic fields also come from the work of the Secretary General of CIFA, Gualtiero Valeri, who showed how the pioneering work of Giorgio Piccardi finds concrete application in the protection of waters, both in the industrial sphere and in other areas of biological interest.

Splendid, and once again sacrificed to the tyranny of the schedule, was the lecture by Rosanna Cerbo on quantum influences on stress and chronic pain.

A tyranny that also struck the talk by Vincenzo Valenzi on coherent and incoherent interactions in therapy. But time was pressing, and after the talk by Eng. Vincenzo Caprioli, who raised anew the alarm about atmospheric pollution from nanoparticulates, it was the turn, at 9 p.m., of the theoretical physicist Yogendra Srivastava, who gave us a few flashes on the enigma of the magnetic potential and the story of the great physicist David Bohm, who had published with [Aharonov–Bohm effect] a couple of articles on the magnetic potential whose greatness is still difficult to appreciate today, with the role of magnetic force remaining shrouded in a halo of mystery that it would be worth unveiling, also in order to better understand biological functioning and the great emerging power of the magnetic field, which is about to be exploited in various ways.

The proceedings were concluded by General Stefano Murace, who congratulated all the speakers and the large audience that followed the numerous talks with passion — talks that continued at the table of the Casa dell'Aviatore over good wines and excellent dishes until late at night, reinforcing that team spirit which Cesare Salvaggio had spoken of at length in his introductory lecture on how to win the challenges of innovation.

Vincenzo Valenzi, www.cifafondation.org
