# André de Belizal (1896–1975): The Theorist of Physical Radiesthesia

*English translation and compilation from French sources · translated 2026-09-12 by Forge (translation-qc agent) for the Aetherforce library · research translation with full attribution to each source*

## The man

André Marie Hyacinthe Pierre de Gouzillon de Bélizal (January 13, 1896 – February 6, 1975) was a French radiesthesist — and, in the words of the GLNF review of his work, "a brilliant researcher" (*génial chercheur*). With Léon Chaumery and later Paul-André Morel, he built the theoretical edifice of what the French school called *physical radiesthesia*: the attempt to treat pendulum detection not as a mental faculty but as an instrument-based physics of micro-vibrations.

His full name — André Marie Hyacinthe Pierre de Gouzillon de Bélizal — marks him as a descendant of an old Breton aristocratic line: an earlier André-Marie de Gouzillon de Bélizal (1741–1795) was a French Navy officer who served in the Seven Years' War and the American Revolutionary War and died at the Battle of Quibéron as a royalist émigré. The radiesthesist de Belizal was born at the close of the nineteenth century and lived through both world wars, dying in Paris in 1975 at seventy-nine. (Sources: Kook Science, citing the France Insee Social Security Death Index 1970–2019; English Wikipedia for the naval ancestor.)

## The collaboration with Léon Chaumery

De Belizal's working partnership with Léon Chaumery — "a seasoned radiesthésiste of the early twentieth century" — began, by his own account, "some thirty years" before the writing of *Physique Micro-Vibratoire*, i.e. in the mid-to-late 1920s. Together they:

- **Named and patented the Universal Pendulum (Pendule Universel), called "Louksor."** They "baptized the shape of their pendulum with the name Louksor (the universal pendulum); a receiver and emitter, it allows one to receive and to emit all the vibrations of the spectrum, visible as well as invisible." The patent — FR 816,132, *Procédé et appareillage radiesthésiques* — was filed April 10, 1936 and published July 30, 1937. (Sources: GLNF; Dervy/Trédaniel publisher notes; patent text via Kook Science and Espacenet.)
- **Opened "a new chapter of radiesthesia: the physical study of the influence of vibrations emanating from forms and colors"** (Trédaniel). The expression *onde de forme* ("form wave") was created by Chaumery and de Belizal in the 1930s (French Wikipedia, *Radiesthésie*).
- **Discovered — in their framework — the negative green ray (*vert négatif*, V−).** The patent describes it as "the antipode of the green of the spectrum: it is the shortest wave and consequently the most powerful of the invisible spectrum... referred to below as negative green or special green." De Belizal later wrote that this vibration "served the Egyptians to dehydrate their dead, thereby withdrawing bodies from decomposition. We rediscovered it and patented it in April 1936."
- **Published the founding texts:** *Traité Expérimental de Physique Radiesthésique* (Dangles, 1939) and *Essai de Radiesthésie Vibratoire* (1939; reissued 1956; 3rd edition revised and augmented 1975; modern Dervy reissue 2013 with a preface by the geobiologist Patrick Darcheville).

The GLNF review of the Dervy reissue captures the spirit of the Chaumery–de Belizal program: "They came to the conclusion that all the statuary, architecture, and even the graphic work of certain civilizations of Antiquity (triskel, swastika...) obeyed these laws of the emission of forces. Lines, volumes, arrangement, orientation — everything was calculated to generate an ideal balance, a perfect integration into space of certain places and certain privileged men."

And the famous closing quote the review borrows from the Soviet parapsychologist Sergeiiev: "With the emissions of energies due to forms, without doubt we would go much further, if we knew more about them, than with all our electricity and our nuclear power."

## Chaumery's death and the long silence

The preface (*Avertissement*) of *Physique Micro-Vibratoire et Forces Invisibles* (1976) contains de Belizal's own account of the catastrophe that shaped the second half of his life:

> "This work is the fruit of 30 years of studies, of silent and patient research, some of it disappointing, some of it exhilarating. It is also the natural prolongation of the volume already published in 1939, then reissued in 1956 under a different title: *Essai de radiesthésie vibratoire*, and that in collaboration with Léon Chaumery, taken from us, alas, prematurely, on February 27, 1957. Our collaborator and friend was in fact the victim of his experiments, and completely dehydrated by our negative green ray (V−). At that time we had not yet found the antidote vibration, discovered since [then] with M. P. A. Morel, who kindly agreed to look into our work and assure us of his precious support.
>
> If we kept apart from all activity for a long period in this fascinating domain of the Invisible Forces, it is precisely because we wanted to find and discover the protective vibration that would allow us to pursue our work in complete safety. That is now done, and we now know that the antidote of V− is V+, which neutralizes all radioactivity stored in the organism."

So the tradition's own account is: Chaumery died February 27, 1957, "victim of his experiments" — killed, in the school's telling, by prolonged exposure to the very ray the two men had discovered and patented. De Belizal then withdrew from the field for years, returning only once the compensating "positive green" (V+) vibration had been established with Morel.

## The Morel years: Physique Micro-Vibratoire et Forces Invisibles

After Chaumery's death, de Belizal continued the research with Paul-André Morel. Their joint testament, *Physique Micro-Vibratoire et Forces Invisibles* (Desforges, 1965; reissue 1976, 217 pages), is described by its publishers as the work of "pioneers in the research of 'form waves' and in what we call today noxious waves [*ondes nocives*], which they named, in their era, the 'invisible forces.'" Thirty years were needed to bring the research to term.

The book's own introduction frames the program in the vocabulary the two authors had to coin from whole cloth:

> "Before undertaking the reduction of this book, it seemed necessary to us — indispensable, even — to put the reader in the climate of this work, which employs a very new vocabulary, in a domain not yet explored by official science. We had to create from scratch a terminology whose technical value corresponds to the data of the problems exposed."

Its core theory, in their words: every body possesses a radiation proper to its constituents, "but it also possesses a second radiation due to its profile, angular refraction being the basis of every vibratory emission known under the name: 'emission of form waves.'" The negative green — "mysterious vibration, veritable bond between life and death" — sits at the center of the "decomposition of the spectrum in the sphere," the method's cornerstone, "without which it would have been impossible to construct a valid theory."

The Desforges catalogue page appended to the book lists the instrument line the school left behind: Pendule Universel; Pendule Égyptien; electromagnetic disk with selector (the "Escargot"); Great Pyramid of Cheops at 1/1000 scale with graduated base; pendulum with fictive cone; I.R.–U.V. detector; special Biometer; force re-balancer (called "Louksork"); apartment/house re-balancer; magnetic anti-parasite device ("Aspironde"); Micrometer; biometric rule; Atlante ring.

## Legacy

French Wikipedia's *Radiesthésie* article preserves the school's standing in the tradition: "The expression 'form wave' was created by Léon Chaumery and André de Belizal in the 1930s. Their work and that of Enel are a reference in the world of so-called scientific radiesthesia."

The books remain in print through Dervy: *Essai de radiesthésie vibratoire* (with Chaumery) and *Radiesthésie, vibrations et ondes nocives* (with Morel, Dervy 2015) — the latter presented by its publisher as "the fruit of the authors' reflections on radiesthesia and on the mystery of the waves that surround us," whose authors "discovered and isolated the alpha ray, healer of numerous afflictions."

De Belizal's instruments and his negative-green vocabulary crossed into the wider subtle-energy culture: the Bovis-scale tradition, the geobiology of the Brocéliande school, and eventually the "BG3" of Gilbert Decaule's BioGeometry all carry the trace of the Chaumery–de Belizal spectral decomposition. Within the French school itself, he is the theorist — the one who turned a dowser's craft into a claimed physics, patented its detector, and carried it, through his friend's death and his own long silence, to its systematic form.
