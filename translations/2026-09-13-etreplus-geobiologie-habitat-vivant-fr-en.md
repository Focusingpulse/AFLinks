# Geobiology: When the Home Comes Alive

*English translation of "La Géobiologie : quand l'habitat devient vivant" — Être Plus magazine (Belgium), Laurent Jouvet, September 7, 2026. Original: https://magazine.etreplus.be/habitat/la-geobiologie-quand-lhabitat-devient-vivant/*

Geobiology explores how our homes influence health and proposes harmonizing living spaces for better balance.

### Summary for readers in a hurry

Geobiology studies the interactions between the environment and the living. From electromagnetic fields to telluric networks, and including the memory of places, it explores how a space influences our physical, emotional, and energetic balance. At the frontier of ecology, architecture, and consciousness, geobiology reconciles the house and the body, the human and the Earth. Its practitioners do not claim to "purify" a place, but to restore a harmonious dialogue between the home and those who live in it.

### Mini-questionnaire

- Do you sleep badly for no apparent reason?
- Do you feel unease in certain rooms of your house?
- Do your plants wither without explanation?
- Do you often feel tired at home but better elsewhere?

👉 If these questions speak to you, geobiology may shed some light. It explores what science measures and what intuition perceives: the subtle interactions between the place, the Earth, and the living.

## From cathedral builders to modern sensors

Long before the word "geobiology" appeared, builders knew how to read the Earth. Greek temples, dolmens, and Gothic cathedrals were often sited on crossings of telluric currents, springs, or cosmo-telluric lines¹. These places, chosen for their resonance, were reputed to support vitality and spiritual elevation.

In the twentieth century, researchers such as Ernst Hartmann (a German physician) and Manfred Curry mapped the first geomagnetic networks². Their work, extended by modern bioelectromagnetics, opened the way to studying the impact of natural and artificial radiation on the living³.

## Science of place or art of listening?

Contemporary geobiology combines scientific rigor with an intuitive approach. The geobiologist measures electromagnetic fields, radioactivity, underground water veins, and geological faults, but also observes the ambiance, the sensations, the "breathing" of a place.

> "A place is not neutral; it interacts with the living… Felt perception is an instrument, just like a measuring device, provided it is exercised with rigor."

Recent studies confirm that electromagnetic fields can alter melatonin production and disturb sleep⁴, or influence heart rate⁵. For practitioners, these physical effects are only one part of a larger whole: the vibrational quality of a place also includes its emotional, historical, and symbolic dimension.

## When the place influences health

Many geobiology consultations begin with diffuse symptoms: insomnia, chronic fatigue, headaches, agitation in children. The geobiologist then proceeds to a global analysis:

- orientation of the building,
- underground water circulation,
- telluric networks,
- electric and magnetic fields,
- emotional memory of the walls.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), domestic electromagnetic pollution is now recognized as an aggravating factor of stress and sleep disorders⁶. In geobiology, this data adds to field observations: "energy-void" zones, withering plants, damp or cold walls, places where one never feels at ease.

> "When a place breathes better, so do its inhabitants," sums up a geobiology instructor and former interior architect.

## The art of reharmonizing: between technique and presence

A geobiologist's intervention can be very concrete: moving a bed located over a geological fault, reducing Wi-Fi exposure, improving grounding, encouraging natural light. But some also include a more subtle dimension: energetic harmonization, symbolic cleansing, rituals of intention.

The goal is not to impose a belief, but to restore coherence between the visible and invisible elements.

> "It is a work of listening: we repair a place less than we accompany it toward balance."

The effects are often perceptible within the first days: deeper sleep, a feeling of calm, better concentration, a lighter atmosphere.

## Training in geobiology: a discipline in expansion

Geobiology schools are developing in Europe and Belgium. They teach environmental physics, radiesthesia, telluric cartography, and the sensitive reading of places. The curricula are addressed as much to therapists as to architects, engineers, or people in career transition.

Many discover there a new language of the living: listening to the Earth, perceiving the resonance of a space, learning to harmonize rather than to control. This approach can become a complementary activity or enrich professions already centered on care, construction, or human accompaniment.

## Where to find a geobiologist?

You will find some directly alongside this article.

Many practitioners work today in Belgium and France, often in connection with eco-construction or holistic health. It is advisable to choose a trained professional, transparent about their method, and open to interdisciplinary dialogue.

## To inhabit is to cohabit with the living

Geobiology does not set science against the sensible: it connects them. It reminds us that our well-being also depends on the quality of the place that carries us. In a world saturated with waves, objects, and solicitations, it proposes an ecology of presence: relearning to listen, observe, feel.

> "The house is a living organism. Caring for it is also caring for oneself."
