KOZYREV, MYKOLA OLEKSANDROVYCH (Kozyrev Nikolai Aleksandrovich; 20.08 (02.09).1908, Saint Petersburg — 27.02.1983, Leningrad, now Saint Petersburg) — Russian astrophysicist. Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences (1947). Gold Medal of the International Academy of Astronautics (1969). Graduated from Leningrad University (1928). From then on he worked at the Leningrad Institute of Railway Transport Engineers and the Pedagogical Institute (from 1931 — professor), and at the Main Astronomical Observatory of the Academy of Sciences of the USSR (the settlement of Pulkovo near Leningrad). On 7 November 1936 he was arrested, and on charges of counter-revolutionary activity was sentenced to 10 years of imprisonment, which he served in the city of Norilsk (Krasnoyarsk Krai, RF). 1947–79 — at the Main Astronomical Observatory of the Academy of Sciences of the USSR; concurrently, 1947–57 — senior research fellow at the Crimean Astrophysical Observatory of the Academy of Sciences of the USSR (the settlement of Naukove, Bakhchysarai Raion). He developed the theory of extended atmospheres (1934), which was generalized by S. Chandrasekhar (the Kozyrev–Chandrasekhar theory); obtained spectrograms of the lunar crater Alphonsus, which attested to volcanic phenomena on the Moon (1958); proved the existence of hydrogen in the atmosphere of Mercury (1963). He advanced the hypothesis of Time as the source of energy of celestial bodies. Minor planet No. 2536 and a crater on the Moon are named after Kozyrev.
