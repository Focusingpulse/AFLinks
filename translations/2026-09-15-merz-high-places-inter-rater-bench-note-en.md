# Merz's High Places: A Bench Note on Testing the Bovis Place-Scale at Its Own Canonical Sites

*Authored by Forge (Translation QC & Assembly agent), 2026-09-15. Second application of the bench-note template introduced in "Simoneton's Angstroms vs Biophotonics" (2026-09-15-simoneton-vs-biophotonics-bench-note-en.md). ## 1. The claim under the lens

Blanche Merz (1919–2002) rated places on the Bovis scale with a pendulum and graduated biometer. Her published values are specific and repeatable by anyone who visits: **Chartres cathedral 11,000 BE**, **mosque prayer niches 12,000 BE**, **specific spots in Hindu and Buddhist temples 14,000 BE**, an extraordinary power place 18,000 BE; neutral ground 6,500 BE; Hartmann-grid crossings ~2,000 BE (Minikus 2024, after Merz, *Orte der Kraft*). Her Swiss volume lists **over a hundred named high places across the cantons** with their values — a pre-registered target list, published in 2001 before anyone thought of it as one.

The food-scale bench note asked whether Simoneton's *ordering* of foods carries signal against delayed-luminescence instruments. The place-scale question is different, and in one way cleaner: **there is no instrument counterpart**. No physical detector has ever confirmed a Bovis unit, and after the St. Gallen 2001 demonstration (straight or curved scale, same result — the geometry is arbitrary) plus the burning of Bovis's own papers (verified by Merz's 1950s pilgrimage to his descendants in Nice), there is no calibration chain and no archive. What remains testable is the *social* claim the tradition actually rests on: **that trained dowsers, independently and blind, converge on the same readings at the same places.** If they do, something is being transmitted — sensory, cultural, or environmental. If they do not, the "measurement system" Minikus says Merz made "accepted" is a shared vocabulary, not a measurement.

This is the experiment the tradition has never run on itself. Merz institutionalized a school, an institute, a hundred-site survey — and never once published an inter-rater reliability figure. The St. Gallen event was the closest approach, and it was a debate demonstration, not a study.

## 2. What would count as signal

Three outcomes, all informative:

1. **High inter-rater reliability, matching Merz's values.** Dowsers blind to her published figures independently converge near 11,000 at Chartres, near 6,500 at neutral sites. This would be genuinely surprising — it would mean the scale transmits *something* shared across practitioners who never compared notes. The next question becomes environmental: what physical covariate (CO₂, geomagnetic field, air ionization, acoustic resonance, even crowd density and expectation) tracks the readings?
2. **High reliability, NOT matching Merz.** Dowsers agree with each other but not with her numbers. The scale is a real social instrument with drift — a tradition, not a standard. Her values are then one school's calibration, not a property of Chartres.
3. **Low reliability.** Independent dowsers scatter widely at the same spots. The Bovis place-scale fails at its strongest point — its canonical, famous, high-contrast sites — and the "accepted measurement system" dissolves into private judgment. This is the outcome skeptics expect; the point of running it is that the tradition has never forced the test.

Note what is NOT testable and should not be pretended otherwise: whether 11,000 BE "exists" at Chartres the way 650 nm red light exists. The unit has no physical definition (psi-gamma: "no known definition... not in any way based on physics"). Only the *agreement structure* is on the table.

## 3. The bench protocol

The falsifiable core: **do independent, blinded dowsers converge on Bovis values at Merz's canonical sites, and does their convergence match her published figures?**

1. **Site panel.** 8–12 sites spanning her range, all from her published lists so the comparison is pre-registered: 2–3 canonical high places (a cathedral crossing or crypt she rated ≥11,000; if accessible, a known mosque prayer niche — otherwise substitute from her Swiss volume), 2–3 of her Swiss high places (the 2001 book names 100+), 2 neutral indoor sites (~6,500 target), 2 degraded targets (a site she would class low — e.g. a parking structure, a Hartmann-crossing claim indoors). Same day or adjacent days per site to hold weather constant.
2. **Dowser arm.** 6–10 experienced practitioners, recruited from geobiology associations (the VRGS/pendeln.org world Minikus writes for is the natural pool). Each visits sites **separately, on different schedules, blind to the others' readings and to Merz's published values** (recruiters screen for prior exposure to her books; her values are famous in that world, so a "values-blind" arm may be impossible — record self-reported familiarity and treat it as a covariate). Each dowser: one reading per site with their own instrument (per St. Gallen, geometry doesn't matter — which conveniently removes the "original Bovis biometer" objection Merz herself once raised and lost).
3. **Control arms.** (a) *Sense-of-place arm:* 10–15 non-dowsers rate each site on ordinary scales (awe, comfort, "would you linger?") — this is what the environmental-psychology literature calls sense of place, and it is the mundane signal dowsers might be encoding. (b) *Environmental logging:* CO₂, temperature, humidity, geomagnetic field (fluxgate or phone-magnetometer log), RF/EMF survey, sound level and reverberation time at each site — cathedral acoustics alone produce measurable physiological effects, and are the kind of covariate a "high place" reading could be tracking.
4. **Analysis.** Intraclass correlation (ICC) across dowsers within sites (two-way random effects; Koo & Li 2016 conventions — ICC < 0.5 poor, 0.5–0.75 moderate, > 0.75 good). Then: mean reading vs. Merz's published value per site (does the tradition reproduce its own canon?). Then: dowser means vs. sense-of-place means vs. each environmental covariate (is the reading tracking anything physical or just shared aesthetic response?). Report dowser-experience and Merz-familiarity as moderators.

**Pre-registration note:** the whole study is fieldwork — travel, access permissions, and dowser recruitment are the costs; instruments are a CO₂/EMF/acoustic kit under $1,000. Main failure modes: site access (cathedrals and mosques need coordination and the mosque-niche arm needs cultural care), schedule coupling between dowsers (they must not compare notes — run them on staggered weeks), and the familiarity problem (her Chartres figure is folklore; the Swiss sites are less famous and are the better blind targets). The Swiss volume is the experiment's best asset: **a hundred named sites with published values that only specialists know.**

## 4. Why this matters beyond the dowser's world

- The **Bovis → Simoneton → Merz chain** is the corpus's best-documented subtle-energy lineage, and each generation made the same move: encode judgment in physical-looking units (Å, then BE), anchor it at a plausible number (6,500), and never test transmission between practitioners. The food-scale note found the instrumented counterpart (DL decay); the place-scale has none — so its honest test is *reliability*, and reliability is cheap.
- If outcome 1 or 2 obtains, the follow-up is the interesting one: **what are dowsers actually reading?** The environmental-psychology and acoustic literature gives real candidates (reverberation, proportion, light, expectation). A dowser converging with non-dowsers' sense-of-place ratings would suggest the pendulum is a *sensory amplifier with a shared scale*, not a detector of a new field — which is exactly the "bioenergy has layers beyond plain electricity" question in testable form: the layer may be perceptual, not physical.
- If outcome 3 obtains, the corpus should say so plainly. The French school's food ordering survived its first contact with instruments (Simoneton's classes track DL structure); the place scale deserves the same contact with its own best evidence.
