The Absent Master — Present At Last

Nikola Tesla

the magnifying transmitter, telluric waves, radiant energy — the real machine lineage

Tesla's published patents are famous. What this vault holds is the other Tesla: the magnifying transmitter work (beyond the coil — whole-earth telluric transmission), the radiant energy receivers, and the lineage of engineers who tried to rebuild what he actually did. That lineage runs through Eric Dollard — the only living engineer to successfully reconstruct the magnifying transmitter's operating theory (longitudinal magneto-dielectric waves, the "LMD" mode), through Nathan Stubblefield's earth batteries, and into the modern longitudinal-wave experiment community.

The honest status: Tesla's magnifying transmitter demonstrably worked as a machine — Wardenclyffe was built, Colorado Springs produced real discharges and measurable earth currents. What is unverified is the claim layer: worldwide wireless power transmission, the "Tunguska was Tesla" story, the confiscated-classified narrative. The physics fight (can longitudinal waves carry power faster-than-light or not) is unresolved and is exactly what the archive's Dollard collection documents. The technology is absent from the mainstream only in the sense that no institution has funded a serious replication — the blueprints are public, in this vault, and the Replication Yard exists for this.

The Phantom Genome

Peter Gariaev — Wave Genetics

the DNA phantom effect, the linguistic-wave genome, the quantum biocomputer

Peter Gariaev (1942–2020), Moscow — the founder of wave genetics. The core claims: DNA emits a phantom wave imprint that persists in equipment after the sample is removed (registered 1984, correlation laser spectroscopy); the "junk" 98% of the genome is a linguistic structure — fractal holographic text; and genetic information can be transmitted electromagnetically between organisms (the pancreas-regenerated-rats experiments, the DNA "teleportation" into water, confirmed — per the group — by PCR).

The honest status — the most extraordinary claims in this entire archive: none of it has replicated independently. The phantom-DNA effect was reported by Gariaev's own lab; the therapeutic claims (cystic fibrosis cured via "spinor spectra of childhood photos", tooth regeneration, stroke recovery in 4–5 days) appear on the group's promotional sites with no peer-reviewed trials. Gariaev died in 2020. What survives as a replicable thread is the neighboring work: Luc Montagnier — Nobel laureate, not a fringe figure — reported related DNA electromagnetic signals in water (EMS), published in a peer-reviewed journal, and that experiment is specific enough to test. If any thread of the wave-genetics cluster is true, it's the Montagnier one — and it is testable at home-lab scale.
