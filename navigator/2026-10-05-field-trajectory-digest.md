## 2. Worth Teaching

Three curriculum-ready items. Each carries the sources a teacher needs.

**Item 1 — "Grade a null by who ran it."**
The week's single most portable lesson, and it is teachable in one sitting because the corpus now holds four worked specimens across two directions. Take the archive's nulls and sort them: **insider nulls** (Veynik's rigid-mount margin note, in the founder's own book; the KPI authors' "unfounded", in their own paper; Waseda's synchrony closure, run by the group whose claim it closed) versus **outsider nulls** (the Observatoire Zétistique's 10-of-30, run on a skeptic's initiative and filmed on TF1). The lesson is not "insiders are right" — it is that **an insider null and an outsider null are different channels that see different things, and neither substitutes for the other.** An insider null is harder to explain away (*"they'd have every reason not to publish it"*); an outsider null is harder to dismiss as motivated. Ask of any contested claim: *has anyone run its cheapest control, and did the person who ran it have a stake in the answer?* Report 27 §6.2 supplies the framing; the diversity argument it rests on is Yang et al. 2026 (arXiv:2602.03794).
*Sources: `paradigm/2026-10-05-the-recantation-in-the-margins.md` §3, §6.2; `paradigm/2026-10-02-self-correction-from-inside.md`; `paradigm/2026-10-03-institutions-are-not-evidence.md` (Report 25's register rule: *"institutions certify, censor, sue, and fund; only instruments measure"*).*

## 3. Worth Building / Testing

Three candidate projects on **three different channels** — shared-infrastructure, textual, and household bench. The spread is the point: Yang et al. 2026 (arXiv:2602.03794) found **two diverse channels beat sixteen identical ones**. Each below was chosen after competing options were set side by side — Maryanskyy 2026 (arXiv:2603.20324): **select the standout rather than blend**, and note its **weak-model paradox**: the modest cheap experiment teaches more per dollar than the grand one.

**Project 1 — Run Dossier 065: the At-Risk Archive Mirror Test. Make one household a node.**
*The discriminating first test:* **pre-register before fetching anything** — pick **ONE named at-risk site**, count its pages from its `sitemap.xml` or the Wayback CDX API, and write down the site, the page count and its source, the mirror method, the completeness threshold, the self-containment test, and the criteria; **photograph the sheet**. Then mirror with a queue-based tool (**MERLib Mirror**, or `wget --mirror` / `httrack`), respecting `robots.txt` and rate limits, and record the copy's file count, total size, and a `sha256sum` manifest. Then verify **two separate things**: **completeness** (fraction of pre-counted pages present, with the missing list) and **self-containment** (block the original via `hosts` file, network off, or a different port; serve the local copy; check that pages render with their assets and their internal links resolve locally). **What would prove/disprove:** **≥95% of the source's pages present AND the copy serves with the original blocked, no missing assets = the mirror preserved the site**; **<50% present, or home-page-only = the tool does not preserve this site at home scale — the expected result for dynamic sites, and a complete one (Skeptic's Star)**; a blocked crawl is **VOID, not a null**; and the **ARTIFACT** case — complete by page count but **not self-contained** (assets still pointing at the live domain) — is the failure that *looks* like success and is why the self-containment arm exists.
*Effort envelope:* **~$0** — a computer, free software, an evening. **Dependencies:** a named at-risk site (the archive already seeds candidates: **`buch-der-synergie.de`** — 60 live PDFs, no mirror, next non-wayback slot; **`elib.biblioatom.ru`** — viewer-only; **`coldfusioncommunity.net`** — 297 live-verified ICCF PDFs on a single volunteer host with no mirror); `robots.txt` compliance. **Safety:** none. **Note the discipline required:** the card's own honesty about its tool (**MERLib: 1 star, 1 open issue, single maintainer, MIT, created 2026-03-04**) is what makes the result meaningful — a mirrored copy that is not self-contained has preserved nothing, and the test is designed to catch exactly that.
