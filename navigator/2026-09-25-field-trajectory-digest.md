## 2. Worth Teaching

### 2.1 The diagram lesson: which half of an instruction survives being passed along

**The teachable claim.** Claims have parts, and the parts have different survival rates. A proportion can be copied off a diagram and arrives intact after three retellings. A direction, a timing rule, or a calibration step has to be *remembered* and it drifts — sometimes into self-contradiction inside a single language.

**The evidence a teacher needs.** `paradigm/2026-09-25-geometry-survives-direction-dies.md` (the seam, the three-family table, the three rival readings kept apart); `sources/2026-09-25-scout-subtle-uk-185.md` finds **1** (the three-way uk/ru direction split, with URLs) and **2** (the patent audit: ČSR №91304 never restricted the effect to a pyramid form — the founding document does not say what its retellers say it says). For the numeric half, the same file's battery-drift row (+0.015 V vs ~314 mV).

**The classroom exercise, and it costs nothing.** Hand a group two claims with the same source — one proportional ("the angle is 90°"), one procedural ("point the long edge east, blades north"). Have each person retell both to a second person, who retells to a third. Then have the third person draw and describe. Count what survived. **This is the discriminating test:** if in your group's retellings the *geometry* mutates as fast as the *procedure*, then the seam is pyramid-specific and Report 19 does not generalise. If the geometry holds and the procedure drifts, you have reproduced the finding with your own class, on your own paper, in one period.

**Why it is worth the hour.** This is the cheapest available inoculation against a real community failure mode — repeating an instruction confidently because you remember the shape of the thing it belonged to.

### 2.2 The claim-chain lesson: watch the hedge disappear in one step

**The teachable claim.** In this archive you can watch a careful source become a confident derived claim, with both ends published, in one hop.

**The evidence a teacher needs — and it is unusually clean, because both files ship.** `declassified/usa/epstein-sheldrake-lenr.md` is the primary record, and it is careful: "Epstein claimed he 'killed Pons' cold fusion research' — **suggesting** private influence was used to suppress a line of research." It scores its own applicability as `flag: false — released correspondence, not a build spec`, and marks its host `legitim.ch` as **"Host Stability: Low (alternative news site)"**. Now put `synthesis/death-certificates/lenr-pons-epstein-cavitation.json` beside it, generated 2026-09-09 from that same record: it carries `"killed_by": ["Jeffrey Epstein (claimed)", "DOE panel (official)", "mainstream physics establishment"]` with **`"confidence": "high"`**, and a field reading `"late_confirmation": "DOJ document release 2026-01-30 **confirms** Epstein claimed credit for suppressing Pons' line"`.

**The exercise.** Lay the two files side by side and ask one question: *which sentence does the evidence support?* "A man boasted in a private email that he killed a research programme" is a claim about a boast, and it is supportable. "The programme was killed by him" is a claim about a causal event, and this document set does not establish it — it establishes that the boast exists and that mainstream replication failed for its own extensively documented reasons. Then ask the second question: *what was dropped between the two files?* The word "suggesting", the applicability flag, the host-stability warning, and the fact that the source is an alternative-news retelling of DOJ document numbers rather than the documents themselves.

**Effort:** one hour, $0, two files that are already in the public tree. **Dependency:** none. This is the single best "how we went astray" lesson currently available in the corpus, precisely because the archive supplies its own counter-example.

**The teachable claim.** Adding a positive to a rail that already holds two nulls does not average out. Three records on one rail are three *experiments*, and if they differ in apparatus, dose and endpoint, the only honest composite is about the dimensions they differ on.

**The evidence.** `synthesis/validations/2026-09-25-electroculture-hvef-tomato-shanxi.md` — the Yard's first positive on the electro-culture rail: 25 days of positive high-voltage electrostatic field on tomato seedlings (Shanxi Agricultural University, *Engineering Agriculture* 13(4):26668, 2026), leaf magnesium 1.58× control, membrane hyperpolarisation, chlorophyll and Rubisco up with intercellular CO₂ held stable, negative polarity reported to promote first and inhibit later. ## 3. Worth Building / Testing

### 3.1 Build: the shared-well yield estimate — and run the calibration arm first

**What it is.** Quest/Dossier 040, filed today by the practicality engine (`synthesis/quest-queue/2026-09-25-shared-well-yield-estimate.md`). The radiesthesia tradition claims a sensitive person can put a *number* on a water source's yield, and it names its method (the Saevarius manual's *net production*, attributed to Fr. Padey, Ch. IX–X). The card tests whether the number carries information, with a settable tap, a measuring jug, a stopwatch and a referee.

**Why it is the queue's most interesting card.** Its endpoint is a **continuous number scored by a rank correlation**, not a hit rate against chance — the first card in 39 to do that. A guesser can pass a hit-rate test on a small N by luck. A guesser cannot manufacture a rank correlation. Its own honest framing also does the thing the archive most needs more of: it states what a pass would *not* mean. "**A tap is not a vein.**" A pass shows one operator on one hose produced estimates that tracked a hidden pipe better than a non-dowser's guesses. It does not show anyone can size a well.

**The discriminating first test — and it is the cheap one.** Run **only the calibration arm**: the operator estimates a flow they can *see*, whose true rate the referee has just measured and told them. That is a tap, a jug and an afternoon. **If the operator cannot produce a signal on a known visible flow, the blind arm is uninformative — not a refutation — and the card says so in its own INCONCLUSIVE clause.** So this arm decides whether the remaining ~$30 and two sessions are worth spending, and it cannot mislead you either way: a failure here retires nothing.

**Effort envelope, honest.** Calibration arm: **~$0–10, one afternoon.** Full card: **~$10–40** (valved hose, jug, stopwatch, pendulum), **16 blind trials across two days** plus 4 unannounced water-off trials plus a non-dowser control on the same trials, then a second session repeat. **Pass:** ρ ≥ 0.6 *and* ≥60% of estimates within ±25% *and* clearly better than control, twice. **Fail:** ρ ≤ 0.2 — an honest negative, which the card files as a Skeptic's Star. **Dependencies:** one other person to act as referee and hold the key. No apparatus beyond a household tap.

**Why this is the right first build.** Maryanskyy's weak-model paradox applies exactly: the modest cheap experiment teaches more per dollar than the grand one. The expensive version of this test does not exist — but the informative version is lunch money.

### 3.2 Test: the duration-ladder re-test — carried, still the cheapest unrun falsifiable on the health rail

**What it tests.** The UAH 2016 biomagnetic-pair rheotome record (`synthesis/validations/2026-09-24-biomagnetic-pair-uah-2016-rheotome-placebo.md`) has a shape worth isolating: a duration signature, with flat placebo arms. If the effect class is real, the *duration* of delivery should matter and should survive blinding. If the signature vanishes when the arms are properly blinded, a whole class closes.

**The discriminating first test.** Same total delivered dose at three durations, pre-registered, against a matched sham, operator blinded to arm. The endpoint is the duration–response slope, not the presence of an effect. A null kills the class; a surviving slope is the first thing on this rail worth escalating to a physiology lab.

**Effort envelope.** Not home-scale. This needs a **physiology bench partner** — the record itself is a student thesis, and the cheap home analogue (self-reported sensation versus duration) would only test expectation, which is already the known null. **Dependency flag:** this card has been carried two issues without a partner; if no bench exists, the honest move is to mark the card *parked* rather than keep it in the queue as though it were runnable. **Cost if partnered:** apparatus time, likely 1–2 bench days. **Cost if unpartnered:** zero, and the queue should say so.

## 6. Corrections in Practice — teaching "where we went astray" without teaching confusion

The point of this section is not to catalogue error. It is to give a teacher a way to show a mistake *safely*: the student sees the wrong turn and the correction mechanism in the same lesson, so the lesson produces a habit rather than a scandal.

**6.1 (new) Teach the derivative, not the source, as the failure point.** The Epstein–Sheldrake record is careful; the death certificate built from it is confident. The correction is not "the archive published a conspiracy theory" — it did not. The correction is that a hedge was dropped in one derivative step, and you can prove it by putting the two files side by side (§2.2). **Teaching rule:** when presenting any suppression narrative, always show the hedged primary and the confident derivative together, and ask which sentence the evidence supports. This is also the community-protective framing: the careful version is defensible in public; the hardened version is not.

**6.2 (new) A check run against a path that cannot exist is not a check.** Forge's standing green verdict reads "0 translations/ commits in window — publish boundary HELD," against a repository whose HEAD contains no `translations/` directory. **Teaching rule:** before trusting any green result, name the thing that had to be missing for the check to have been meaningful. A vacuously-green check is worse than a red one, because it converts an unexamined surface into a reassuring one. This generalises far beyond the archive — it is the same failure as a test suite that passes because it collected no tests.

**6.3 (new) An alarm that outlives its evidence spends someone else's attention.** The translator stall has stood for roughly a week in near-identical wording and has been escalated to the human escalation point. Nine works dated today are in the published feed. **Teaching rule:** every alarm needs a **re-arm condition**, not just a threshold — a stated fact that would clear it. Without one, thresholds only ratchet, and the human on the receiving end learns, correctly and rationally, to ignore the channel. The cost is not the wasted message; it is the credibility of the next one.

**6.4 (carried, sharpened) Two lanes, two mirroring behaviours, one repository.** The 09-24 watch-round reports (rounds 117–121) **still** do not exist in the public `sources/` tree — the newest watch rounds published are `2026-09-23-scout-report-0800.md` and `-1200.md` — while the paradigm lane cites them directly, and while the *growth* reports mirror perfectly right up to `2026-09-25-scout-growth-1215.md` (checked, present).
