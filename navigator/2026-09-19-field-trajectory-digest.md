## 2. Worth Teaching

**1. "One word, three claims, three verdicts" — electro-culture as an evidentiary triage exercise.**

The Vault treats "electro-culture" as one shelf. The Yard now holds **three tested claims under that word**, and they do not agree:

| Claim under the word | What it actually is | Verdict in the Yard |
|---|---|---|
| **Active electro-culture** | Hundreds of volts through soil/wires (Blackman, USDA tree electrodes) | Not yet tested at home |
| **Passive conductor** (copper dowels, "electroculture" as sold) | ~2 mV from a rod in a pot | **Clean independent null** (Chier et al., *PLOS ONE* 20(8):e0329615, 2025; NSF-funded, n=10 × 4 species) |
| **Magnetopriming** (pre-sowing magnetic seed treatment) | Magnetism applied *before* planting | **Positive in mainstream ag literature**, small, mechanism unclear — and now the subject of Dossier 026 |
| **Shaped conductor** (Lakhovsky one-turn coil, 1924) | A ring of copper, no power source | Untested; bundled into Dossier 026 as arm B |

The teaching move is to hand a class the word first, then the four physical claims it hides, then the three different verdicts. A single null does not close a word this broad — and the null that closed copper dowels says nothing about magnetopriming. *Sources:* `synthesis/validations/2026-09-16-electroculture-constant-field-null-wheat-mustard.md`, `...-2026-09-18-electroculture-copper-dowel-container-null.md`, `...-2026-09-18-magnetized-water-caraway-yield.md`, `synthesis/replication/2026-09-19-dossier-026-electroculture-growth-stimulation.md`.

**2. "The instrument must be able to lose" — the Munich 1986–88 null and the six dowsers who were chance.**

Now in the Yard as a validation, and it is the best single teaching case in the corpus for selection artifacts. A German federal ministry commission (~DM 400,000) screened ~500 applicants down to **43 credible dowsers** and ran **843 trials** with a water-carrying pipe on a **randomly moved cart under a false floor**: no effect above chance. The famous residue — *six of 43 scoring above chance* — was later reproduced from a random-number generator alone by Napier's 1999 PHACT simulation. The published design was also structurally blind to sub-effects (it never crossed iron vs. plastic pipes).

*Classroom use:* ask which of the 43 dowsers should be believed. Then show that "the best six" is what chance produces from 43 draws. Then close with Dossier 026's design decision — **a pre-registered trial count, so the run is scored and not the best performer in it** — which is the Munich lesson turned into an instruction on a card. *Sources:* `synthesis/validations/2026-09-19-dowsing-munich-1986-null-campaign.md`; `sources/scout-report-2026-09-19-0000.md` (fire 116); Dossier 026.

**3. "A tradition can print its own null — or seal it."**

Costérisant 1937 reviewed ~200 water-supply projects in Puy-de-Dôme and Haute-Loire for the *Revue des sciences naturelles d'Auvergne* and wrote, in his own tradition's journal, that "the number of observed successes is effectively zero," refusing to count indications given very near a spring. Jarricot 1959 ran 129 trials inside the French medical establishment (55.2% mean error, below chance) and published the refutation.
