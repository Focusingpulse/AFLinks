## 2. Worth Teaching

Three items, each curriculum-ready, each traced to a record in the archive. None of these promises a result; each is a lesson about **how to hold a claim**, which is teachable regardless of how the claim lands.

### 2.1 The sign-reversal lesson — an effect that changes sign is a different kind of claim

**The teaching point, in one sentence:** *an effect with a changing sign is not a weak effect — it is a different kind of claim, and it can only be tested at more than one operating point.*

**The case.** A 2026 growth-chamber trial on spring wheat tested Steiner's eight biodynamic preparations against compost and mineral nitrogen on two soils, at a held-equal 60 kg/ha available N. The preparations **enhanced growth and yield where nitrogen was not limiting and were slightly inhibitory where it was.** That is not what a nutrient effect looks like — a nutrient effect does not reverse sign. It is also not what a straight "life force" claim predicts.

**Why it is teachable rather than just interesting:** the reversal has now been reached **twice, by independent groups, against two different variables.**

| Year | Who | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| 1996 | Raupp & König, *Biol. Agric. Hortic.* 13(2):175–188 — 28 field-plot/pot experiments near Marburg + a 9-year spring wheat trial at Darmstadt | Preparations **500** and **501** *increased* yields at low yield levels, had little effect at medium, and **lowered** yields at high levels (Y = 28.930 − 0.87X, r = −0.767 in the spring wheat trial). Their reading: **normalisation of yields**, not stimulation |
| 2000 | Carpenter-Boggs, Reganold & Kennedy | Short-term trial, lentil and spring wheat: **few consistent short-term differences** from the preparations |
| 2024 | Dietrich, Dahn, Fritz, Berg, Köpke & Döring — the **27-year DOK** rotation, Therwil, Switzerland | Preparations had **no effect on yield or yield stability in any crop, including spring wheat**, in 27 years — *even though* they measurably changed the soil biological community |
| 2026 | Spoerk Gabriel & Goldstein (this record) | Help where N is not limiting; slightly inhibitory where it is; strongest biodynamic-compost effect on the biodynamic soil |

Read together these four do not say "preparations work" or "preparations don't." They say the preparations **do not raise yields on average**, and when they do show up they show up as a **modifier whose sign depends on how well the plant is already supplied**. Raupp & König found it against *yield level*; the 2026 preprint finds it against *nitrogen availability*. Two independent groups, two different variables, the same reversal.

**The warning that makes it curriculum:** a trial that reports only its mean will see nothing, and a trial run at a single fertility level will report whatever sign that level happened to select — and will have learned nothing either way.

**Sources a teacher needs:** `synthesis/validations/2026-09-23-biodynamic-preparations-spring-wheat-nitrogen.md`; Spoerk Gabriel & Goldstein preprint doi **10.21203/rs.3.rs-9059525/v1** (*not peer reviewed, "Under Revision", abstract-level read*); Raupp & König doi **10.1080/01448765.1996.9754776**; Dietrich et al. doi **10.1017/S1742170524000218**; Carpenter-Boggs et al. doi **10.1017/S0889189300008614**; Goldstein, Koepf & Koopmans 2019 doi **10.1515/opag-2019-0018**. Curriculum home: **Section V (Goethean & Anthroposophical Method)** — where `PHASES.md` already places it and where, until today, the Yard held no experimental record at all.

### 2.2 The two-observable lesson — a heat signal is one observable, not two

**The teaching point:** *an anomalous thermal claim needs either checklist-gated calorimetry or a nuclear product signal. Elemental composition is not a nuclear product signal.*

**The case.** A single-author paper from **ZHAW Zurich University of Applied Sciences** (Winzeler), published in *JCMNS* **41**:153–161, reports **COP 1.4–2.4** in a compact quartz-glass reactor under inductive high-frequency excitation, with palladium-coated and pure nickel foils, a repeatable pressure-ramp **activation** and a short-hydrogen-pulse **deactivation**, and surface change measured by SEM and **EDX**.

**Why the claim earns attention:** a named university affiliation, a peer-reviewed venue with a DOI and page range, a **specific, gradeable, falsifiable** thermal claim rather than a qualitative "we saw something," and — the word the Yard cares about — the author reports the activation as **repeatable** and describes a **deactivation control**. A device that can be turned off is a better claim than one that merely runs hot.

**Why the second observable sinks it — and this is the whole lesson.** The reported new elements are **carbon, oxygen, aluminium and silicon.** Those are the four elements a **quartz-glass** chamber, its fittings, ordinary handling and laboratory atmosphere are most likely to deposit. EDX gives **elemental composition, not isotopes**, and cannot separate a transmutation product from contamination or from the chamber itself. **No SIMS, no ICP-MS, no isotopic ratios** appear in the abstract. That does not make the result false; it makes the EDX evidence **uninterpretable for the claim being made**, and "transmutations" in the title a stronger word than the data supports.

**The archive already wrote the protocol for exactly this shape of paper.** **Dossier 021 — The Anomalous Heat Effect (LENR): A Double-Observable Replication Protocol** exists precisely to gate a thermal claim on one of two routes. Teaching this paper beside Dossier 021 is the lesson; teaching the paper alone is the mistake.

**Scope limit, stated honestly:** the full text (pp. 153–161) was not accessible this run — **this record rests on the abstract.** Numbers were not verified against figures and the calorimetric method was not inspected. Flagged **abstract-only**.

**Sources a teacher needs:** `synthesis/validations/2026-09-23-lenr-winzeler-high-frequency-induction.md`; `synthesis/replication/2026-09-17-dossier-021-anomalous-heat-effect-double-observable.md`; and for contrast, the two LENR cards that *did* carry a second observable — `synthesis/validations/2026-09-16-lenr-he3-nanocomposite-nuclear-signature.md` and `synthesis/validations/2026-09-21-lenr-nicu-ni-radiant-spectrum-revision.md`.

### 2.3 The preregistration anatomy — the design *is* the finding, including the part that failed

**The teaching point:** *a positive result is not a clean sweep. Publish the preregistered analysis that came back null.*

**The case, and it is the most instructive single record the Yard has produced.** Dean Radin and Erik Brinsmead (Institute of Noetic Sciences, Novato CA; funded by the Bial Foundation) preregistered and ran a replication of the claim that two **physically isolated** water volumes, exposed to the *same* weak pulsed magnetic-field sequence, develop an anomalous correlation. The lineage was **one laboratory** — Rouleau, Carniello & Persinger at Laurentian — and Radin's own paper states the problem plainly: these "entanglement-like" effects "have been reported only by researchers from a single laboratory."

**What makes it a curriculum item is the design, which is worth stating in full:**

- **Preregistered before data collection** (OSF `https://osf.io/nt76h/overview`), with prediction, endpoint, windows and analysis plan fixed in advance.
- **Run at a different site, by personnel not previously engaged in this line of research** — the authors' explicit attempt to remove the experimenter.
- **100 runs pre-planned, 50 experimental interleaved with 50 sham.** The sham used identical equipment, protocol and statistics but dropped the acid into a separate beaker — so electromagnetic, thermal and vibrational factors were matched and **only the acid differed**.
- **Three 40 mL beakers**: Local (centre of halo A; receives acetic acid), Remote (centre of halo B; nothing added, ever), Control (between them; no magnetic exposure, nothing added).
- Two Arduino boards; halos of 225 loops of 16-gauge copper on 25.4 cm hoops generating ~20 nT fields; Atlas Scientific **Gen3 ISFET pH probes at 0.001 pH resolution**, sampled at 0.5 Hz.
- **Built-in adversarial tests:** two negative controls (anchor moved 430 s early; windows slid before the event), an **81-window sensitivity grid with max-statistic family-wise error-rate correction**, a run-level mixed-effects model, and a run-order correlation.

**The result, honestly split:**
- Experimental mean ΔD = **+0.0034** pH units; sham **−0.0007**; between-group difference **+0.0041**, one-tailed permutation **p = 0.005**. Sham runs showed no effect (p = 0.53). Both negative controls null (p = 0.26, p = 0.27).
- Sensitivity grid: global **FWER-corrected p = 0.018**; **15 of 81** window pairs survived correction, **all** in the predicted direction.
- **The preregistered secondary analysis was NULL** — the linear mixed-effects model gave an Epoch × Condition interaction of **p ≈ 0.48**. The shift was *not* sustained across the 12-minute effector phase. It is a narrow transient excursion of about two minutes.
- Exclusions disclosed, including **three sham sessions in which acid was accidentally dropped into Local** — extra sessions were run so the final analysis held exactly 50/50.

**Why this teaches better than a clean positive would:** the effect is **+0.0041 pH units on a sensor with 0.001 resolution** — two orders of magnitude above the floor but small enough that the honest label is "near its own sensor floor." And the preregistered secondary analysis **failed** while the primary passed. That is what a real record looks like, and it is the single best artefact in the archive for teaching students what preregistration buys them: it does not manufacture a positive, it makes the *shape* of a positive legible.

**Sources a teacher needs:** `synthesis/validations/2026-09-23-water-radin-excess-correlation-preregistered.md`; Radin & Brinsmead, *J. Biophys. Chem.* **17**(2), doi **10.4236/jbpc.2026.172002**; preregistration **https://osf.io/nt76h/overview**; the pilot, Radin 2025, *J. Biophys. Chem.* **16**:15–29; and the originating single-lab reports — Rouleau, Carniello & Persinger 2016, *J. Signal Inf. Process.* **7**:136–147, and 2014, *J. Biophys. Chem.* **5**:44–53.

## 3. Worth Building / Testing

Three candidate projects. Each carries a **discriminating first test** — the cheap experiment that separates the competing readings rather than splitting the difference — and an honest effort envelope. Two are home-scale; one is season-length. Nothing here promises a result.

Both load-bearing papers are applied deliberately: **Yang et al. 2026** (diverse channels beat homogeneous scaling — two diverse agents match sixteen identical ones) is why the three candidates below span **unrelated domains and different methods** rather than deepening any one line; **Maryanskyy et al. 2026** (select after competing options; its weak-model paradox — the modest cheap experiment can teach more per dollar than the grand one) is why each first test is deliberately the *smallest* thing that could settle its question.

### 3.1 Build: the ruler test — Dossier 032, the Bovis dial-swap

**The question it answers:** *does the number stay put or double with the ruler?*

**Why it is the best-value experiment on the board.** The radiesthesia line's readout is the **Bovis scale** — a numeric unit that practitioners read off a pendulum. The line's own record already shows part (c) of its claim *is false*: the human baseline was quietly rescaled **6,500 → 12,500 UB in 2014 with no instrument change** (+92%), the dials ran 18,000 → 60,000 → 120,000, Earth is now quoted at **2.39 million UB and climbing 2–3,000/day**, and ACMOS — *inside the tradition* — calls a 400,000-UB goji-berry reading "totalement loufoque." Meanwhile parts (a) and (b) of the readout claim have **never been tested**. This experiment tests the instrument the whole line depends on, and it costs the price of two printouts.

**The discriminating first test.** Arm C2 dial-swap ratio: **R = UB on a silently ×2-rescaled dial / UB on the standard dial.**
- **R ≈ 1** → the number is a stable physical readout and the tradition's rescale history needs another explanation. This is the *defensible* outcome and it would materially strengthen the claim.
- **R ≈ 2** → the corpus's own stated mechanism — *"re-label the ruler and the pendulum will adapt itself"* — caught in the act at a kitchen table.

Either outcome is a result. Neither requires ground truth on the physical side.

**Effort envelope:** **$0–30, two printouts, one afternoon.** Dependencies: none beyond a printer and one operator. This is the Maryanskyy weak-model case almost literally — the smallest possible experiment on the load-bearing question.

**Sources:** `synthesis/replication/2026-09-22-dossier-032-bovis-scale-calibration.md`; `navigator/trajectory/2026-09-23-form-waves-radiesthesia.md` (§ the readout); `synthesis/validations/2026-09-22-radiesthesia-argenton-2007-observatoire-zetetique.md`.

**Scope guard:** this is not a dowsing test and does not reopen the blind-discrimination question, which is settled at six records across 92 years. It tests **the ruler only**.

### 3.2 Test: the ground-truth rung — Dossier 035, blind water-line location

**The question it answers:** *does a dowser locate a buried water-filled line better than chance when the operator cannot know the answer?*

**Why it is the live rung.** The buried-water-line claim is the one part of the line that has **not** been refuted — and it is the part with the newest evidence. The **INRS / Bordeleau** study ran **54 participants (27 experienced / 27 novices)** on a **25-cell grid** over buried iron and plastic pipes, empty or water-filled, saline or fresh, stagnant or flowing, with rod type tracked, **co-designed with a practising dowser**, and reported at **EGU26 Vienna, 3–8 May 2026** (doi 10.5194/egusphere-egu26-3985). The abstract says "we present the final results" and is publicly retrievable — but **no numeric table is published anywhere in the public record as of 2026-09-23.** Verified externally this run: the study is funded by an **FRQNT Engagement** grant 2022–2025 ("Sourciers et sourcières des temps modernes") — i.e. *engagement* research, not a debunk programme. That nuance cuts in the tradition's favour and changes no number.

**Dossier 035 is the queue's first card with all three things the line has always lacked:** (a) **ground truth**, (b) a **non-dowser control arm**, and (c) a **pre-registered key-holder**.

**The discriminating first test.** **20 blind direction trials against a 50 % baseline**, plus **≥5 marked points** scored as perpendicular distance from the true hose path, plus **10 static sham trials**. The card states in its own body that *"the hose is not a water vein"* — an honest scope limit that keeps the test from over-claiming.

- **At chance** → the buried-line claim joins the six refuted records, and the line's last live rung closes.
- **Above chance, direction and distance both** → the line's **first** positive under controls in 92 years, and the strongest possible reason to fund the INRS table request below.

**Effort envelope:** **$20–40, one afternoon.** Dependencies: a hose, a layout that can be blinded without digging, and **a key-holder who is not the operator** — the third item is the one that historically has not existed, and it is the whole difference between this and Dossier 026.

**Sources:** `synthesis/replication/2026-09-23-dossier-035-blind-water-line-location.md`; `synthesis/validations/2026-09-19-dowsing-inrs-2026-controlled-field-study.md`; `synthesis/replication/2026-09-19-dossier-026-water-dowsing-buried-targets.md`; `synthesis/validations/2026-09-19-dowsing-munich-1986-null-campaign.md` (for the loose-first-stage trap: 457 of 500 eliminated at a *loose* screening, and the "six good dowsers" residue reproduced from a **random-number generator**).

**Design warning carried forward from the line's own history:** do not screen loose and then re-test the survivors. The Munich residue is the archive's cleanest demonstration of what that produces.

### 3.3 Build: the multi-operating-point trial — biodynamic preparations at three fertility levels

**The question it answers:** *at how many fertility levels does the sign flip?*

**Why this is the right first test rather than "do preparations work."** Per §2.1, two independent groups have now found the **same reversal** against two different variables, and a 27-year DOK trial found **no average yield effect at all**. A single-level home trial therefore has roughly a coin-flip chance of reporting whichever sign its fertility happened to select — and will have learned nothing either way. The transferable lesson the trail keeps teaching is that **an effect with a changing sign can only be tested at more than one operating point.**

**The discriminating first test.** Run **three fertility levels** — low / medium / high available N, held per level across arms — and compare **preparations + compost** against **compost without preparations**. The discriminating outcome is a **sign flip across levels** with the no-preparation compost arm flat.
- **Sign flip present, control arm flat** → the modifier reading stands and the domain has its first home-scale confirmation of a specific structure.
- **Sign flip absent, or the control arm moves with it** → the effect is not preparation-specific, and the 27-year DOK null is the better account.

**The control is compost *without* preparations, not nothing.** Replicating the standard mistake — testing against an unfertilised control — would reproduce the error the 1996 trial was designed to avoid.

**Effort envelope:** **$20–60, one growing season.** This is the honest envelope: the test is cheap in materials and *expensive in time*, and it needs at least three fertility levels managed simultaneously. Flagged as **season-length** — the only candidate here that cannot be finished in an afternoon.

**Sources:** `synthesis/validations/2026-09-23-biodynamic-preparations-spring-wheat-nitrogen.md`; `synthesis/replication/2026-09-22-dossier-032-element-day-sowing.md` (an adjacent but *different* design — note the number collision, see §6); `synthesis/quest-queue/2026-09-12-kolisko-group-steigbild.md` (the Kolisko lineage, a different endpoint on the same tradition).

**Note on the diversity principle (Yang et al. 2026):** the three projects above deliberately share no method — a pendulum-and-printout instrument test, a blinded field test with ground truth, and a multi-factor agronomic trial. The Yang result is that **two diverse agents match sixteen identical ones**; the same logic says a community that runs one kind of test sixteen times learns less than a community running three kinds once each.

## 6. Corrections in Practice

*How to teach "where we went astray" without producing confusion.* Six rules drawn from this issue's material and from the archive's existing record. The governing principle throughout: **teach the test, not the verdict.**

### 6.1 A wrong turn is usually narrower than the story told about it

The 1989 dossier shows the **rejection was narrower than its legend**: the ERAB panel **adjudicated electrolytic palladium and never cavitation.** For thirty-seven years the community's account has been that "cold fusion was rejected" — and the paper trail says a specific claim, in a specific configuration, was reviewed and not confirmed.

**How to teach it:** draw the boundary of what was actually adjudicated *before* discussing whether the adjudication was right. Most disputes about this episode are disputes about scope disguised as disputes about merit.

### 6.2 Keep the ledger, and name its holes

The archive holds exactly **one death certificate** — `synthesis/death-certificates/lenr-pons-epstein-cavitation.json`. The death-certificate layer **does not cover the radiesthesia line**, despite that line holding **six validation records**, two published null campaigns, an engineering-scale field null, and a tradition-recorded self-miss. A reader therefore meets the most-tested family in the archive as a set of **open questions** rather than a documented history.

**How to teach it:** teach the *ledger and its holes together.* "Six nulls are on file and the line is still filed as live" is a more honest and more useful sentence than either "the line is open" or "the line is closed." The backlog is part of the lesson — and `PHASES.md` already carries the Wrong-Turn Death Certificates lane as **running**, so this is a known gap with a named owner.

### 6.3 Watch for mirror errors — the correction can have the same shape as the error

The 1989 paradigm report names one exactly: **treating Epstein's private boast as mechanism is the same untested-instrument mistake the Bovis dial-swap dossier now catches in radiesthesia.** A suppression legend built on a private claim nobody tested is structurally identical to a readout nobody calibrated.

**How to teach it:** when you introduce a correction, ask whether the correction itself rests on an untested instrument. The mirror error is the hardest one to see because it arrives wearing the costume of scepticism.

### 6.4 A null from a friendly funder is stronger than a null from a hostile test

The **Munich 1986–88** campaign: **843 trials, 43 dowsers, chance** — and the "six good dowsers" residue was reproduced from a **random-number generator** alone. Two facts make it the best null in the archive to teach with: the **rig was inspected by a professional magician**, and **the German government funded it hoping to validate dowsing.** A null that survives a design review by a hostile-to-nothing party and a funder with a favourable prior is worth more than a null produced by someone with a position to defend.

**How to teach it:** state the funder's prior *with* the result. It is the single most persuasive fact in the record and it is usually omitted.

### 6.5 Let the claimant set the conditions, then blind them

**Argenton 2007**: a >20,000-Bovis claim tested against ten coded boxes, with a **pre-registered 1 % criterion** — and the **practitioners set their own separation distance, their own instrument, and in one series their own sample.** Result: chance three times (1/7, 2/10, 4/32).

**How to teach it:** this design removes the objection that would otherwise be available — *"you tested it wrong."* It does not survive that design. When teaching a contested null, the first question should be *"who set the conditions?"* Argenton's answer is why the result holds.

### 6.6 Teach the part of a positive result that failed

The Radin & Brinsmead record (§2.3) is the archive's best teaching artefact precisely because the **preregistered secondary analysis came back null (p ≈ 0.48)** while the primary passed. If only the primary were reported, a student would learn that preregistration produces positives. What actually happened is that preregistration made the *shape* of the positive legible: a **~two-minute transient excursion**, not a sustained effect.

**How to teach it:** give students both analyses and ask which sentence they would write. Then note the effect size — **+0.0041 pH units against a 0.001-resolution sensor** — and ask what "replicated" should mean at that scale.

### 6.7 Two housekeeping corrections, for the record

Not corrections in the field — corrections in *our own notes*, and worth teaching as a habit:

1. **The JCMNS volume number.** JCMNS **41 (2026)** is the **ICCF-26** proceedings volume (Morioka, May 2025), **not** ICCF-27. Our own notes had it wrong; the validation lane caught it. The practical consequence is a possible misdating of the frontier — a paper can look newer than it is.
2. **The dossier number collision.** Dossiers **021-grid** and **022-grid** are the same 7,909-byte file under two numbers, and **both render in the feed**; **Dossier 032 — The Biodynamic Element-Day Sowing Test** and **Dossier 032 — Bovis Scale Calibration** are two different documents sharing a number, and one of them is the project in §3.1. Basename dedupe cannot catch either. The update worth recording: **Dossier 035 *does* have a matching quest card, filed the same day**, so the card/dossier gap is closing from the newest end.

**How to teach the habit:** when the archive cites a source, check the *file* rather than the *citation*. Both of these were found by doing that, and both would have propagated silently otherwise.

## 7. Community Digest

*Five bullets and one conversation starter — written to be shared as-is.*

**1. The archive just gained 7,228 documents in about 36 hours — and the fleet's own report says that is the problem, not the win.** The field's last editorial institution, *Infinite Energy* (founded by Eugene Mallove in 1994), published its final issue this month and the archive banked it the same week. The selection problem has moved downstream into the archive itself. Honest numbers: **78,620 of 88,547 documents readable, ~2,933 awaiting OCR, and 144 translations against a heavily Russian / German / French / Spanish corpus.**

**2. Two positive results landed in one day, and the archive is being careful about both.** A **preregistered** replication run at a *different site with different personnel* found a **+0.0041 pH-unit** correlation between magnetically paired water beakers (p = 0.005, sham at zero) — and its own preregistered secondary analysis **came back null**, so the effect is a two-minute transient near the sensor floor, not a sustained signal. Separately, an **anthroposophical-method trial got its first entry in the archive's replication record**: biodynamic preparations **helped where nitrogen was not limiting and slightly inhibited where it was.**

**3. The biodynamic result has a shape that has now been found twice, by different groups, against different variables.** In 1996, Raupp & König found the preparations *raised* yields at low yield levels and *lowered* them at high ones; a 27-year Swiss trial found **no average yield effect at all**. The lesson is precise and it is teachable: **an effect whose sign changes is a different kind of claim — and it can only be tested at more than one operating point.** A single-level home trial has roughly a coin-flip chance of reporting whichever sign its fertility happened to select.

**4. The archive's most-tested line is its least-documented one.** Radiesthesia and radionics hold **6 of the archive's 21 validation records** — the highest validation density of any family — while the whole subject sits in **one bucket of 57 documents**, the smallest real science category on file. Its evidence is *procedural*, not bibliographic: six blind-discrimination nulls across 92 years and four countries, plus a live rung (buried water lines) and an uncalibrated ruler (the Bovis scale was quietly rescaled 6,500 → 12,500 in 2014 with no instrument change).

**5. The ladder is built and the bottom rung is empty.** **34 quest cards, 39 dossiers, 21 validations — and zero submitted community results.** Every quest is `proposed`; every dossier is `draft`, `protocol`, or `protocol_ready`. Two of the proposed first tests cost under $40 and can be finished in an afternoon: **a two-printout dial-swap that tests the Bovis ruler** (either the number stays put, or it doubles with the ruler) and **a blinded buried-hose test with a pre-registered key-holder** — the first card in the queue with ground truth *and* a non-dowser control arm.

**Conversation starter for this week:**

> **"If an effect changes sign depending on how well-fed the plant already is — what is the claim actually claiming?"**

It is a real question with a real answer in the record, it needs no laboratory to discuss, and it opens onto the archive's whole methodology: the difference between an effect that is *weak* and an effect that is *conditional*. Ask it at a PMA meeting or a curriculum session and the follow-up writes itself — *how many operating points would you need to see the flip?*
