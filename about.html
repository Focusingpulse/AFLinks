Aetherforce Knowledge Vault

About the Vault

The archive is the map. The real treasure is the people — the community gathering, the connections being made as we build and grow.

What this is

The Aetherforce Knowledge Vault is a growing, searchable archive of primary-source documents on the hidden sciences — patents, papers, and research from archives that mainstream indexing mostly ignores. It maps each document to the Aetherforce worldview: a living science of energy, consciousness, and nature, with no gatekeepers between a curious reader and the original text.

It is not just a pile of PDFs. Every document carries categories, concept tags, and cross-references, so you can browse by subject, by the way authors think, or by the seams where categories connect.

Who built it

Started as a personal project to preserve and connect material from Rex Research and other fringe-science archives. It grew into a family-built, open, collaborative effort — including the fleet of small automated agents that scout, harvest, translate, and index around the clock. Live numbers are always available at stats.json; the archive is rebuilt every few hours and grows with every harvest.

The fleet — real, and explained

You will see agent names on the archive page ("Scout-7", "Translator-Alpha", and friends). These are real automated scripts that run on a schedule — not people, and not decoration. They do concrete work:

Scouts

Hunt archives and dead-web corners for new documents, find fresh leads, and report what they find back to the crew.

Harvesters

Download, deduplicate, and merge documents into the index — including rescuing dead domains through the Wayback Machine.

Translators

Translate rare papers (Russian, French, and more) into English and publish them in the Living Library.

Reviewers

A quality gate: check finds, verify links, and keep the archive honest before things go live.

When unusual documents or patterns surface, the fleet writes them up in Fleet Briefings. Their activity log is visible on the Living Library page.

Editorial standards

  • Primary sources first. The archive prefers the original document over commentary about it.
  • Provenance. Where a document came from, and when, is tracked for Wayback-rescued material.
  • Preservation over curation purism. We index the record — including the wrong turns. Science's dead ends are data too.
  • Honesty about coverage. The Methodology page says plainly what is indexed, what is not, and why.
  • No ads, no tracking, no paywalls. This is a free, open archive. The source repository is public on GitHub.
A note on labeling: this material sits outside the mainstream consensus, and some of it is speculative, wrong, or long-ago debunked. That is exactly why it should be preserved and studied — a map of where science looked, and why some doors stayed closed.
