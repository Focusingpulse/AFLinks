What this page is

BioGeometry is a "qualitative science" — it claims that shape, colour, motion, and sound carry measurable energy qualities that affect living systems. Its founder, Dr. Ibrahim Karim of Cairo, calls the resulting framework a Physics of Quality. This page documents its founding elements, its instruments, its lineage, and the honest status of each claim.

Honest framing: BioGeometry is not recognised by mainstream physics. But it is not an isolated claim either — it sits at the end of a hundred-year chain of European instrument research, and it makes claims specific enough to test. Both facts belong here. Nothing is endorsed; nothing is dismissed.

The Method

Physical Radiesthesia vs. Mental Dowsing

The load-bearing distinction of the entire system — and Karim states it explicitly. Physical radiesthesia uses calibrated instruments and measurement scales: a perceived quality is read off a scale on a level of perception that precedes subjective meaning. Mental radiesthesia (what the English-speaking world calls dowsing) uses the pendulum as a channel to the subconscious — which Karim himself calls "very subjective and prone to auto-suggestion."

BioGeometry claims the first and rejects the second. That is why it presents itself as instrument science rather than divination — and it is also exactly where the validation question lives. A calibrated scale is objective only if the operator's perception is stable and unaffected by knowing what the answer should be. That is a testable proposition: blind protocols, control readings, multiple operators. It has not been independently done.

What would settle it: a blinded detection trial — operators reading an unknown sample through the instrument, versus an identical control. BioGeometry's claims are specific enough to meet that bar, which most subtle-energy systems are not. That testability is why it has a page here rather than a footnote.

The Founder

Dr. Ibrahim Karim — Cairo

Architect and scientist (D.Sc./Dipl. ETH Zurich). Karim spent four decades synthesising Pythagorean harmonics, subtle-energy research, radiesthesia, geobiology, building biology, sacred architecture, and modern wave theory into a single framework from which BioGeometry emerged in the early 1970s. His family's architecture firm, Alemara, was founded by his father, Dr. Sayed Karim, in 1939. He served as advisor to Egypt's Ministers of Health, Culture, Tourism, and Scientific Research, and headed a research unit at Egypt's National Research Centre studying the effects of geometric shapes on micro-organisms.

The claimed results (each checkable in principle): the Egyptian National Research Centre geometric-shapes study; the Egyptian National Hepatitis C Research Project application; Swiss electrosmog projects in collaboration with the Swiss Mediation Authority for Telecommunication & Environment and Swisscom — for which a Swiss magazine named him Man of the Year in 2005.

Status: these are claimed institutional engagements, documented in his own book and Vesica's materials. No independent replication of the outcomes has been published in the peer-reviewed literature. That is the gap — and it is a specific, nameable gap, not an unknown.

The Transmission

Dr. Robert J. Gilbert (1961–2025) — Vesica Institute

The man who carried BioGeometry out of Egypt. A former U.S. Marine Corps instructor in nuclear-biological-chemical warfare defence who left the service in 1985 and turned to independent research into the geometric basis of science. In 2009 he founded the Vesica Institute for Holistic Studies, and became the first person outside Egypt granted permission by Karim to teach BioGeometry — training more people in the system than anyone but Karim himself.

Gilbert taught both the French radiesthesia methods and BioGeometry, produced the Gaia TV series Sacred Geometry: Spiritual Science, and authored Egyptian and European Energy Work. He held one of the world's largest collections of rare European vibrational research texts — the primary-source base of the whole tradition. Where that collection now resides is an open question, and an important one.

Died 2025-02-10 (obituary: International Journal of Healing and Caring, July 2025). The Vesica Institute continues his mission, and the teaching line passes to the next generation of instructors, including Doreya Karim and Kris Attard.

The honest bottom line

BioGeometry is presented by its practitioners as a physics — a science of energy quality with instruments, scales, and protocols. This page has laid out that claim and the lineage behind it in full, because the lineage is real: a hundred years of European instrument research, transmitted through a documented teaching line.

What is missing is equally specific: a blinded, independent test of whether the instruments read anything beyond the operator's expectation. That single experiment would move BioGeometry from "unverified claim" to "verified method" or "refuted method." Until it runs, everything on this page stays in the ledger as a claim — presented at its strongest, with its evidence named, and with the missing piece named too.

A claim is a claim until tested. The archive preserves BioGeometry's claims and its lineage; it does not certify them — and it does not dismiss them.
