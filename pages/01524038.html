The precise and detailed bending of star light grazing the sun can be found for light rays, just by applying the Maxwell Analogy (which has firstly been suggested by Heavyside at the end of the 19th century) and by using the sun's orbital motion and its rotation. I find that the bending on the sun' poles complies with the measured values, and I find different bending at the left and the right side of the sun. Also observation confirms an asymmetric deviation of light, depending on the latitude where the star's light grazes the sun. Overview The Calculation of the Bending of Star Light Grazing the Sun is a four-page paper by Thierry De Mees , written in 2004 and updated 15 October 2010, published in the General Science Journal . It applies the same "Maxwell Analogy for gravitation" — mass for charge, a "gyrotation" field Ω for the magnetic field — that underlies his On the Orbital Velocities Nearby Rotary Stars and Black Holes . The deflection is assembled from three contributions. The main term comes from the light's own speed. De Mees argues that "since the rest mass of light rays is zero we must not consider the gravitation force of Newton" — only the gyrotational term survives.
