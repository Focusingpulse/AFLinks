GC2026-SGG041 Comparison of Signals Spectra and Amplitudes from Vertical Pulsed Seismic Sources Konstantin V. Kislov1 , Alexey Y. Marchenkov2 1 Institute of Earthquake Prediction Theory and Mathematical Geophysics, Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow, Russia kvkislov@yandex.ru Since 2024, we have been conducting experiments to compare low-power artificial pulsed seismic sources. The ultimate goal of this series of experiments is to compare the characteristics of handy, low-cost artificial seismic sources, to determine their metrological and operational parameters, and to develop recommendations for their application [1]. The selection of an appropriate source should not be considered a purely technical task. Each survey area and target requires specialized equipment and the optimization of parameters: pulse-frequency content, source power, and operational characteristics [2]. During this time, the methodology of experiment has been developed; various sources have been designed and constructed, enabling vertical (e.g., sledgehammer blow on a plate), horizontal (e.g., pendulum impact of a weight on the wall of a trial pit), and volumetric impact (e.g., a blank shot from a seismic gun in a shallow borehole); and the suitability of the test site has been determined. Testing has confirmed the stability and reliability of the equipment [3]. At this stage, a comparison of seismic signals generated by various types of vertical impacts has been carried out.
