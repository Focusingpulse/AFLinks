07/31/14 - Lawsuit challenges TA-65 pills on anti-ageing claims The drug TA-65 is claimed to be able to remodel ageing immune systems by lengthening the telomere caps on chromosomes. The lawsuit threatens to put the science of telomeres � repetitive nucleotide sequences that protect the ends of chromosomes during DNA replication � on trial. But Noel Patton, president of TA Sciences, denies all of the allegations. �We stand by what we say,� he adds. The connection between cellular ageing and telomere length is rooted in solid research. Telomeres become shorter every time a cell divides, and when they are lost cells can no longer reproduce. The enzyme telomerase can lengthen telomeres, possibly slowing or reversing degenerative diseases. In one study1, mice genetically engineered to lack functional telomerase showed brain degeneration and shrunken testes, but those effects were reversed when the enzyme was reactivated. Such findings have sparked a lot of hype and encouraged a cottage industry of companies that assess a person�s �biological age� on the basis of their telomere length. But TA Sciences has taken the buzz further: it sells a pill called TA-65, which it says can lengthen short telomeres. The pill brings in an annual revenue of US$6 million in the United States alone. The active ingredient of TA-65 was isolated from the herb Astragalus membranaceus and patented by Geron, a biopharmaceutical firm in Menlo Park, California.
