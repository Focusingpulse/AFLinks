kimo2026-main136 Export of the Indian Digital Public Infrastructure model to Southeast Asian countries: political and institutional conditions and constraints Olga Kharina1,2 1 Federal State Autonomous Institution of Science "Institute of China and Modern Asia of the Russian Academy of Sciences". 2 National Research University Higher School of Economics olaishra@mail.ru Abstract. The Indian Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) model, encompassing platforms such as Aadhaar, UPI, and DigiLocker, has transformed digital governance, financial inclusion, and service delivery within India. Its potential export to Southeast Asian countries presents both opportunities and challenges. This paper examines the political and institutional conditions that facilitate or hinder the adoption of the Indian DPI model in the region. Drawing on comparative case studies of Indonesia, Thailand, and Vietnam, it analyzes regulatory frameworks, state capacity, digital literacy, and governance structures that shape DPI implementation. The study identifies key constraints, including privacy concerns, political resistance, inter-agency coordination, and reliance on domestic technology ecosystems. By highlighting both enabling and limiting factors, the paper provides policy insights for governments and international organizations aiming to foster digital public infrastructure in Southeast Asia. The findings contribute to the broader debate on digital sovereignty, transnational digital governance, and South–South cooperation in technology policy. Keywords.
