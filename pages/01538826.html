Correa Aurora Biophysics Research Institute, Concord, Ontario, Canada Abstract We provide experimental evidence that the Schumann frequency is not a characteristic of the ionospheric cavity per se, nor an electromagnetic function. Instead, using a three-band analyzer spanning 1 to 13 cps, we show that it can be picked up by an isolated ground connection inside a Faraday Cage room, and that, accordingly, it is a geo￾acoustic electric signature. We speculate on its novel causation, one that accounts for its presence in the crust, oceans and atmosphere. We suggest that the main Schumann frequency strikes a C0 tone at 8.0054 cps, rather than at 7.83 cps, and that the intercalation frequency of the damped wave responsible for the spectrum of the six Schumann resonances is A0 = 6.6711 cps. In line with our published model of a 1,512 eV terrestrial geoplasma originating at the Earth's core, we propose that the last (or 6th) Schumann resonance strikes at 41.36 cps = E1, corresponding to the acoustic signature of the terrestrial ambipolar radiation that drives the geoplasma.
