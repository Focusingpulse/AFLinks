Title The Meaning of "Psi Function" of Quantum Theory Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Yuri N Keilman Keywords Quantum Theory , Frequency Published 2010 Journal General Science Journal No. of pages 4 Abstract The "Psi" Function of Quantum Theory is actually a complex amplitude of some standing wave that has different frequencies of oscillations in different locations. If we assume that De-Broglie frequency of moving electron has to be equal to the local frequency of this standing wave then we obtain the Schroedinger Equation. Overview The Meaning of "Psi Function" of Quantum Theory is a four-page paper by Yuri N Keilman , published in the General Science Journal in 2010. Its longer internal title is 1913 Niels Bohr's Approach to Quantum Theory Yields Relativistic Schroedinger Equation and suggests that the Physical Meaning of a Wave Function is Different from Probability . The paper begins by rejecting gauge invariance. Its stated premise is that "everything in the realm of E-M Potential is physically important and can be represented uniquely by E-M Potential", and it notes that the Lorenz condition ∂ k A k = 0 "looks like exact copy of the law of conservation of electric charge", from which Keilman concludes that the condition "is the mandatory condition and represent the conservation of ‘vacuum polarization’ or ‘vacuum charge’." Three consequences are set out explicitly: the potential is physical though unmeasurable; parts of it carry no energy or momentum; and "obviously we deny ‘gauge invariance’. Different ‘gauges’ represent different physical situations." If the potential is a pure gradient, A k = ∂ k G, the Lorenz condition forces □G = 0. Keilman names A k the "Dummy Potential" and G the "Dummy Generator", describes them as longitudinal potential waves carrying no field and no energy, and locates their sources on "the surfaces of elementary particles (which are disruption surfaces)".
