Integral Reduction Formulas Enhanced With a TI84 Timothy W. Jones May 5, 2026 Abstract The TI84 has both a definite integral function and a recursive list gen￾erator. We explore whether the combination can be used to solve single and double integral problems that reference recursive formulas for integral evaluations. Introduction Figure 1: The cardioid r = 1 − cos(θ). Thomas’s Calculus text has an example problem that references a re￾duction formula for cosn integrands: Find the moment of inertia, about the 1 y-axis, about the area enclosed by the cardioid r = a(1 − cos(θ) [1]. The TI84-CE can graph functions expressed in polar coordinates: Figure 1. With a few manipulations and references to formulas, the solution distills to solving the integral Z 2π 0 a 4 4 cos2 (1 − cos(θ))4 dθ. (1) There are several instances of powers of cos in the integrand: cos2 , cos3 , cos4 , cos5 , and cos6 . Each has a coefficient. These coefficients can be stored in a TI84 list. The evaluation of the indefinite integral of cosn can proceed with an integration by parts. A reduction formula pops out; in simplified form it is Z cosn = n − 1 n cosn−1 sin + Z cosn−2 . If the definite integrals have upper and lower bounds that make the first term on the right 0 (typical as in this problem), this further reduces to Z cosn = n − 1 n Z cosn−2 . Although this is termed a reduction formula, it really works through re￾cursion. If the limits of integration make either sin or cos zero, we can proceed; a simple test on a TI84 using an IF with intFn – a definite integral evaluation. Let F(n) give the n case in the formula. Then once F(0) = Z b a cos0 = Z b a 1 = b−a and F(1) = Z b a cos = | b a sin = sin(b)−sin(a) are determined we can crunch others: for example, F(2, 3, 4, 5, 6) = 1 2 F(0), 2 3 F(1), 3 4 F(2), 4 5 F(3), 5 6 F(4), all the integrals in (1).
