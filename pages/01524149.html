The Living Universe Book Author James Carter Language English Subject Circlon theory; an evolutionary alternative to the Big Bang Published 2010 Publisher Self-published Pages 148 The Living Universe is a 2010 book by James Carter , the self-taught physicist and inventor from Enumclaw, Washington, who has spent more than half a century building a mechanical alternative to modern theoretical physics. It is the cosmological part of that programme: an account of the universe as something that evolved rather than something that began in an explosion. Carter published it himself, as he has published all his work, and set out the same material on his website living-universe.com. The argument The book rests on what Carter calls the Principle of Circlon Synchronicity . Its founding assumption, in his words, is "that protons and electrons exist permanently in the universe and that they are exactly what we measure them to be" — that is, that the fundamental particles are stable mechanical objects with real shapes, and that matter is produced by their orderly combination rather than by any act of creation. From this he offers a cosmology built on the same observations as the standard model but read the other way round. His own summary is direct: The Living Universe is a story about the evolution of the reality that we call the universe. The Living Universe uses the same evidence and phenomena as the Big Bang theory but it is interpreted in opposite ways to produce a picture of the universe without the many paradoxes and violations of physical law inherent in the Big Bang theory. The mechanism that makes the reinterpretation possible is Electron Transformation , the proposal that the mass and size of the electron change slowly over cosmological time.
