The gist of these articles, plus a little food for thought, has been gathered together in the following article by staffer Bill Nelson, a Flying Saucer buff. Early in 1967 astronomers in Cambridge, England, detected rhythmic radio pulses from a source deep in space. Detected by radio telescope these signals, covering every radio band, were at first believed to be signals from a distant galaxy. For years astronomers have been listening to star systems, from which erratic radio signals emanate, in the hopes of detecting a signal with a regular pattern to it which signify an intelligent sender on the other end. The discovery of the first of what have become known as pulsars had scientists in a tizzy. Could this be an attempt to contact earth? Then someone calculated the strength of the signal and fed all the available information to a computer. The signal was of such a magnitude as to require about 100 trillion watts, or about 100 times the combined electrical output of all the generators in operation on earth today.
