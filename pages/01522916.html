Continuation of Faster than light (FTL) signals from 29 June; dealing with some issues raised. Summary A continuation of the session a fortnight earlier, Roger J Anderton taking up four points that had been raised against it. He hosted himself; the talk had been moved to an unfamiliar time and the green room was almost empty. The mathematical charge The centrepiece is an argument he had passed over quickly the first time, taken from a paper of Ralph Baierlein's on two myths about special relativity: that the Lorentz transformations do not reduce to the Galilean ones as v / c tends to zero, contrary to what students are taught. The demonstration is short. Setting γ to 1, the time transformation becomes t ′ = t − vx / c 2 . If x were a constant, vx would go to zero with v — but x is a variable, and can be made large as v is made small, so the product need not vanish. The limit is therefore t ′ = t − vx / c 2 , not t ′ = t . Anderton put up a set of published lecture notes stating the false claim in terms, and drew the conclusion he wanted: a great deal of the mathematics taught to students of relativity is wrong, and the physicists then interpret experiments through it. Faster-than-light signals Answering the objection that only Harold W Milnes claims to send information faster than c , Anderton showed a segment of the documentary The Time Lords on Günter Nimtz, who splits a microwave signal, sends half through air and half at a barrier, and finds the tunnelled portion arriving first — at 4.7 times c over about 14 centimetres, since reported at some 300 times.
