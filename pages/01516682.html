The device has been created by a British scientist who said it could also save the NHS huge sums by getting patients out of hospital quickly. Working in a similar way to dialysis, the blood is removed – but in this case it is cleaned of dangerous microbes using magnets. The machine is to undergo the first human clinical trials next year and is also being tested for use against blood-borne diseases such as malaria and leukaemia. Dr George Frodsham, who came up with the idea while studying for a PhD at University College London, said: 'This is early-stage technology we are doing, we have got a prototype but at the moment this is in-lab concept work. 'But it could save the NHS a lot of money. Sepsis patients are very expensive if they hang around in intensive care.'
