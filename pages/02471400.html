E. Engineer Is Honored at Dinner at M. I. T. on 80th Birthday CAMBRIDGE, Mass., March 29 (Special). — E. W. Rice jr., a past president of the General Electric Company and now honorary chairman of the board of directors, was the principal speaker at the testimonial dinner given tonight at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology to Professor Elihu Thomson in honor of his 80th birthday. Professor Thomson, one of the premier scientists of the G. E. Company, has been a professional associate of Mr. Rice for more than 50 years, and before they entered business together Thomson was Rice's teacher In the Central High School in Philadelphia. Mr. Rice recalled Professor Thomson's technical soundness in predicting the trend of development in various inventions which other men had made. He told Rice that Bell's telephone receiver would eventually make use of permanent magnets in place of the electro-magnets which Bell at first thought necessary. He prophested that Edison would discard using tinfoil for his phonograph and would shorten the excessive length of the field magnets in his dynamo. These changes all came to pass soon afterward.
