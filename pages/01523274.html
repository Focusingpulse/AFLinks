Joseph F. Cuny Born ( 1929-03-30 ) March 30, 1929 New Jersey, United States Residence Lebanon, OR, United States Nationality USA Known for An aether model of the photon built from Faraday's lines of force Scientific career Fields Civil and sanitary engineering; the aether and the structure of the photon Joseph F. "Joe" Cuny (born 30 March 1929) is an American engineer who has argued that Faraday 's lines of force , taken seriously, both locate the aether and give the photon a definite structure — and that light speed is therefore not the absolute limit special relativity makes of it. He is recorded in the World Science Database as member 319, and presented at three Natural Philosophy Alliance conferences between 2002 and 2009. Education B.S. in physics — University of Nevada, Reno No further qualification is recorded. His working career was in engineering rather than physics, and the World Science Database lists his title as "Civil Engineer". Career Cuny was born in New Jersey and worked as a professional civil and sanitary engineer , in aerospace and for the City of Los Angeles, before retiring to Lebanon, Oregon. By his own account the physics began in 1959, while he was still at school, and began as an objection to a habit of speech rather than to a theory: he was told that relativity was so complex that nobody could really understand it, and did not accept that this was how physics ought to work. That led him to cosmology and to the study he came to call "Paraphysics" — his own term for examining the cosmos through the logic of physics. The argument Cuny's work is a single line of reasoning pursued over some twenty years, and its starting point is historical. Maxwell built his equations on Faraday's field lines. Faraday had shown that lines of force are never open-ended: they run from positive charge to negative, or close on themselves.
