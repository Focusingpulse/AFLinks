Rochus Boerner Residence Tempe, AZ, United States Nationality USA Known for Two essays on longitudinal electromagnetic waves and on the suppression of dissent in physics Scientific career Fields Mathematics (wavelets); the aether and longitudinal waves; the sociology of scientific dissent Institutions Arizona State University Rochus Boerner is a mathematician at Arizona State University , where he took his doctorate and has taught in the School of Mathematical and Statistical Sciences. He appears on this wiki for two short pieces written outside that field: one summarising the case for longitudinal electromagnetic waves in vacuum, the other arguing that inconvenient evidence in physics is systematically suppressed. Education Master of Natural Science — Arizona State University, 1999 Ph.D. in Mathematics — Arizona State University, 2004 ; dissertation Wavelets with Integer Dilation Factors Larger Than Two , supervised by Anne Gelb The doctorate is recorded in the Mathematics Genealogy Project. His mathematical field — wavelet theory — has nothing to do with the subjects for which he is catalogued here, and none of the writing described below is mathematical. Writings Non-Hertzian Waves May Involve Subquantum Particles A short expository piece on whether longitudinal electromagnetic waves can exist in vacuum. Boerner sets out the nineteenth-century expectation that an aether should carry both transverse and longitudinal waves, notes correctly that Maxwell's equations permit only the transverse kind in vacuum while longitudinal waves in a plasma are orthodox, recalls Tesla 's claim to have produced the other sort, and summarises three modern proposals to extend Maxwell's theory — by Terence W Barrett , Myron W Evans and Bo Lehnert — which share the feature of treating the electromagnetic potentials as physically real.
