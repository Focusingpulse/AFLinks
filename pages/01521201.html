Relationship Between Gravity and Evolution: The Theory of the Increasing of Gravity Book Author Ramin Amirmardfar Subject Palaeogravity; comparative physiology of the circulatory system; Earth expansion Published 2000 Publisher Zeinabe Tabriz (Tabriz, Iran) Media type Print Pages 124 Relationship Between Gravity and Evolution: The Theory of the Increasing of Gravity is a book by the Iranian entomologist and independent researcher Ramin Amirmardfar (Ramin Amir Mardfar, born 1971), first published at Tabriz in 2000. It is the founding statement of what its author calls the Increasing Gravity Theory : the claim that the Earth's surface gravity has risen steadily through geological time, that this rising gravity is the main force driving the evolution of the blood and fluid circulatory systems of animals and plants, and that the fossil record of body size is therefore a record of past gravity. The book's argument runs in the opposite direction to most palaeogravity work. Where Stephen Hurrell reasons from the mechanical strength of fossil skeletons, Amirmardfar reasons from plumbing — from the work a heart must do to lift blood against gravity, repeated hundreds of millions of times in a lifetime. His conclusion, that gravity was lower in the past and is still increasing, is the same, and he presents it as biological support for Earth expansion with increasing mass. The book was followed in 2001 by a companion volume, The ABC of Evolution . Amirmardfar presented a condensed English version of the argument at the 37th course of the International School of Geophysics at Erice in October 2011, and an English-language edition appeared from a print-on-demand academic publisher in 2016; see Publication history .
