Roger Penrose

· archived # 1,461,845
Roger Penrose, a famous physicist, believes the Big Bang wasn’t the true beginning of the universe. Instead, he suggests it was just the start of a new chapter in an endless cycle of cosmic events. Here’s the gist: Penrose’s theory, called *Conformal Cyclic Cosmology (CCC)*, proposes that the universe goes through repeated cycles. Each cycle, or “aeon,” ends when the universe has expanded so much that everything is spread out into pure energy. At that point, the universe resets itself and starts over, looking a lot like what we think of as the Big Bang. Penrose even believes we might see traces of the universe’s previous cycle in the form of patterns in cosmic microwave background radiation, the faint afterglow of the Big Bang. He claims certain circular patterns in this radiation could be leftover "scars" from black hole collisions in the last cycle. What’s fascinating about this idea is that it suggests the universe didn’t have a true beginning or end, just an eternal series of rebirths. It’s like the universe is a cosmic phoenix, constantly dying and being reborn. This theory is still controversial and hasn’t been proven, but it challenges the way we think about time and existence. If Penrose is right, the universe is far older and more connected than we ever imagined - and we might be living in just one chapter of an infinite story.
