Shaw Born ( 1932-11-21 ) November 21, 1932 Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada Died March 24, 2025 (2025-03-24) (aged 92) Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada Residence Vancouver, BC, Canada Nationality Canadian Known for The "flowing aether" account of gravity ; a mechanical basis for electromagnetism , inertia and atomic structure Scientific career Fields Law; independent research in physics Duncan Weld Shaw (November 21, 1932 – March 24, 2025) was a Canadian lawyer, retired justice of the Supreme Court of British Columbia, and independent researcher who, after retiring from the bench, devoted himself to theoretical physics. Based in Vancouver, he developed an account of gravity and related phenomena founded on a revival of the aether of James Clerk Maxwell . Biography Shaw was born and raised in Vancouver. He earned a B.A. in 1955 and an LL.B. in 1956 from the University of British Columbia. From 1958 to 1987 he practised as a litigation lawyer with the firm Davis & Company, and was a Fellow of the American College of Trial Lawyers. He served as a Justice of the Supreme Court of British Columbia from 1987 to 2007, and from 2008 to 2015 acted as counsel to Davis LLP in an advisory capacity. Shaw was married to his wife Pat for 64 years and had two children. He died on March 24, 2025, in Vancouver at the age of 92. Approach Shaw came to physics late and from outside it, and was candid about both. "I am a retired Judge of the British Columbia Supreme Court," his first paper begins; he read the literature, corresponded with physicists, and concluded that "the physical cause of gravity is not known and that this subject is open to the development of new concepts." He described his own results as "strictly conceptual" — an unusually plain statement of scope, and one worth bearing in mind when reading them.
