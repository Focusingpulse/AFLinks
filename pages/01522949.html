Frank M. Meno Born ( 1934-01-07 ) January 7, 1934 Died September 7, 2010 (2010-09-07) (aged 76) Residence Pittsburgh, PA, United States Nationality Slovenian / USA Known for Gyron aether theory; gravitation as a kinetic effect of an aether Scientific career Fields Physicist , Electrical Engineer Frank M. Meno (7 January 1934 – 7 September 2010) was a Slovenian-born American physicist and biomedical engineer who spent his career in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. He taught for some twenty-five years at the University of Pittsburgh and Carnegie Mellon University, and worked professionally on medical imaging instrumentation, holding eight patents. He is listed here for the gyron aether theory , a purely mechanical model of physics in which space is filled with a hot gas of Planck -length particles he called gyrons . On this account matter consists of vortices in that gas, and gravitation — together with the other forces — is a kinetic effect of gyron motion rather than a property of space or a field. He set it out in a 1991 paper in Physics Essays and in two self-published books. Biography Meno was born in Prapretno, in what was then Yugoslavia and is now Slovenia. He received his higher education in Germany and the United States, taking degrees at Carnegie Mellon University, and joined the faculty of the medical school at the University of Pittsburgh. His professional work was in the development of medical instrumentation: nuclear magnetic resonance and X-ray imaging, electroencephalography and other diagnostic methods. Among the implementations he was involved in were digital subtraction angiography and a nuclear gauge for online, non-chemical measurement of sulphur content in crude oil. He held eight patents and wrote some forty publications. He died in Pittsburgh on 7 September 2010, of pneumonia, aged 76.
