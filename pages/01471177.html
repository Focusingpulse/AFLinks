firmament

· archived # 1,471,177
noun: (usually uncountable, literary, poetic, also figuratively) The vault of the heavens, where the clouds, sun, moon, and stars can be seen; the heavens, the sky. noun: (countable) The field or sphere of an activity or interest. noun: (uncountable, astronomy, historical) In the geocentric Ptolemaic system, the eighth celestial sphere which carried the fixed stars; (countable, by extension) any celestial sphere. noun: (uncountable, obsolete except biblical) The abode of God and the angels; heaven. noun: (countable, obsolete) A piece of jewellery worn in a headdress with numerous gems resembling stars in the sky. noun: (countable, obsolete, also figuratively) A basis or foundation; a support. noun: (countable, obsolete) The act or process of making firm or strengthening. "All machinery is derived from nature , and is founded on the teaching and instruction of the revolution of the firmament ." [ Marcus Vitruvius Pollio ] The concept of the firmament as a veil or barrier between the physical and spiritual realms has been found in a number of philosophical and mystical traditions. In Gnosticism, for example, the firmament was thought to be a realm of spiritual ignorance or deception that separated the material world from the realm of spiritual truth. In some forms of Kabbalah, the firmament is seen as a barrier between the world of creation and the infinite light of God.
