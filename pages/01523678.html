Title Planetary spin (According to 'Hypothesis on MATTER') Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Nainan K Varghese Keywords Terrestrial spin, planetary spin, planetary orbit, solar system, terrestrial-solar day, celestial mechanism, Hypothesis on MATTER Published August 2010 Journal viXra No. of pages 17 Planetary spin is a 17-page paper of August 2010 by Nainan K Varghese , one of some twenty-five articles applying his book Hypothesis on MATTER to particular physical problems. It argues that the rotation of planets is not left over from their formation but is continuously produced , as a by-product of the same central force that holds them in orbit. Abstract A part of central force between planetary and central bodies cause their spin motions. All bodies in planetary systems develop mean accelerating spin motion. Depending on their orbital parameters, planets and central bodies may spin in forward direction, rearward direction or, in rare cases, may have no spin motion at all. All bodies in a planetary system tend to spin in their common orbital plane. Angular accelerations of orbiting bodies continue indefinitely until their high spin speeds cause disintegration of planets and planetary system. In a planetary system, consistency of body-matter and radial size of a body determine relative spin speeds at different parts of its body. Equatorial region of all very large bodies spin faster than their polar region or regions towards their spin-axes. Lengthening of (terrestrial) solar days, presently misinterpreted as slowing down of earth's spin motion, is the result of insufficient compensation to earth's apparent spin motion about the sun. The framework The paper assumes the machinery of Hypothesis on MATTER rather than arguing for it. On that account all space is filled by a universal medium — two-dimensional latticework structures Varghese calls "2D energy fields," built from one-dimensional quanta of matter.
