Science Chat Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Date 5 September 2020 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Science Chat was a live session on 5 September 2020 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Summary Franklin Hu hosting alone, with technical trouble at the start — the usual live link was broken and his camera failed partway through — and with a session built around a single discipline: what a dissident should do when confronted with experimental evidence against their own hypothesis. He opened by putting a question to the audience, which nobody took up: if you met a civilisation a billion years ahead of us who knew all the physics there is, what one question would you ask? A falsifiable experiment Hu first reported an exchange with the Draft Science channel, whose author holds that light and radio arrive in clumps rather than as waves. Hu had suggested he would not accept a disproving experiment, was told that was nonsense, and welcomed that — because it means the two can agree in advance on what would settle it. The test he proposes is simple: transmit a raw square-wave carrier, put a bare receiving antenna next to it and look at an oscilloscope. His own hypothesis predicts something recognisably square; the clump hypothesis predicts a sine wave whatever is transmitted. Searching for the experiment, he found only people asserting what one would expect — that a perfect square is impossible because of the bandwidth required, or that nobody would broadcast that way because a sine wave is more efficient — and no record of anyone having actually done it. His complaint was that this is crowd expectation rather than a physical experiment, and that the thing would not be hard to do. Fractional charge The bulk of the session was Hu doing to himself what he asks of others.
