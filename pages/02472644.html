Durham Herald-Sun September 30th, 2009 Page number(s): 16 Everyday in the United States electricity is used in homes and businesses to turn on the lights. If you think about this process, you may recall the name of the light bulb, Thomas Edison. But what about the inventor who made it possible to have the AC power running in our homes? That man's name was Nikola Tesla. Nikola Tesla was born on July 10, 1856, in Smiljan, Croatia. Nikola Tesla was known for being a very intelligent student with a photographic memory. He attended the University of Prague in the early 1880's, focusing on the field of electrical engineering. His most widely known invention, the alterating-current (or AC power) was created in 1882, after his departure from University. He came to the United States in 1884 and worked briefly for Thomas Edison. By 1887, Nikola Tesla had patented his invention and sold those rights to a man named George Westinghouse. Westinghouse implemented the AC power system into all American homes and is still a major electric corporation today. This implementation was made possible by another invention of Nikola Tesla's, the Tesla coil. This coil made the transmission of the AC power possible on a large scale.
