Gerasimenko, 2010, published in Fizikokhimiya Poverkhnosti i Zashchita Materialov, 2010, Vol. 46, No. 6, pp. 640–644. 710 INTRODUCTION Coatings based on Al2O3–ZrO2, TiO2–ZrO2, MgO–ZrO2, Nb2O5–ZrO2, and K2ZrF6, Na2ZrF6 oxide systems attract attention as protective, decora tive and heatresistant coatings, as well as catalytically active compounds. These structures can be produced on the surfaces of Al, Mg, Ti, Nb, and other valve met als by plasma–electrolytic oxidation (PEO) in aque ous electrolytes containing K2ZrF6 or Na2ZrF6 potas sium or sodium hexafluoride complexes [1–3]. PEO is the electrochemical anodic oxidation of the surfaces of valve metals under the effect of migrating electric arc or spark discharges. The presence of zirconium oxide in the layers formed in aqueous K2ZrF6 or Na2ZrF6 solutions is supposedly caused by the high temperature hydrolysis of complex salts and annealing of the hydrolysis products in the vicinity of electric discharge channels [2]. Coatings with a thickness from several to hundreds microns can be formed depending on the nature of the metal treated, the electric parameters and duration of the process, and the additives introduced in the elec trolyte containing sodium or potassium hexafluoride complexes.
