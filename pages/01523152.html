Infinite Universe Theory (2017) Scientific Theory Name Infinite Universe Theory Type Cosmology Author(s) Glenn Borchardt Keywords Big Bang Theory , infinity , Neomechanics , Aether , redshift, cosmogony Year 2007 (paper); 2017 (book) Infinite Universe Theory ( IUT ) is the cosmology developed by Glenn Borchardt as the alternative to the Big Bang. It is the cosmological application of neomechanics and of Borchardt's Ten Assumptions of Science , and it holds that the universe is infinite and eternal in both the microscopic and macroscopic directions, has no origin, no edge and no age, and is therefore not a proper subject for cosmogony at all. The name belongs to both a theory and a book: Borchardt first set the position out in a 2007 paper for the Proceedings of the Natural Philosophy Alliance , and expanded it into the book Infinite Universe Theory in 2017. The central argument Borchardt's case does not begin with data but with an assumption, and with the claim that the opposing assumption is equally unprovable and usually unnoticed. Big Bang cosmology presumes a finite universe with an origin; IUT assumes infinity. Neither can be proven. His contention is that physics adopted finity without ever registering that a choice had been made, and that "fundamental assumptions exist as unconscious presuppositions before they are brought into the light of day." Once infinity is assumed, an origin becomes incoherent — in his phrasing, "it is not possible for the universe not to exist everywhere for all time" — and the assumptions of inseparability and conservation forbid matter or motion coming into being from nothing. Cosmogony, the study of the origin of the universe, is thus not a difficult science but an illegitimate one, having no object. Predictions and claims The 2007 paper set out an explicit list of what IUT predicts, several of which are shared with neomechanics generally: Time is motion — not a dimension and not a substance.
