The molecular formula is CH6N2O3, or CH4N2O.H2O2. It is a white crystalline solid that releases oxygen in contact with water. The chemical is a skin, eye and respiratory irritant. It is also corrosive and causes burns. It doesn't hurt at 10% concentration (3% hydrogen peroxide equivalent) but it might hurt at 35% (12% equivalent), causing white chemical burns on skin and gums alike. Pure carbamide peroxide has the form of white crystals or crystal powder, is slightly soluble in water (0.05 g/mL)[1], and contains approximately 35% hydrogen peroxide. Uses Carbamide peroxide is used to * whiten teeth [2] [3]; * relieve minor inflammation of gums, oral mucosal surfaces and lips including canker sores and dental irritation[4]; and to * emulsify and disperse ear wax.[4] Carbamide peroxide is commonly encountered in cosmetic dentistry, where it is used to "bleach" teeth. The active ingredient is hydrogen peroxide, which acts to oxidise interprismatic extrinsic staining within tooth enamel.
