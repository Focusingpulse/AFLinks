Spark gap correction factors Content on this page supplied from the web site of Jim Lux Spark Gap Correction Factors Effects of atmospheric conditions V = k* V0 where k is a function of the air density d d = p/760 * (293 / (273+T)) where p in Torr and T in degrees C d 0.70 0.75 0.80 0.85 0.90 0.95 1.0 1.05 1.10 1.15 k 0.72 0.77 0.82 0.86 0.91 0.95 1.0 1.05 1.09 1.12 The voltage increases with humidity varying some 2-3% over a normal atmospheric range of 8-15 g/m3. Irradiation If the gap is illuminated with UV or X-rays, it is more easily ionized. The effect is more pronounced with smaller gaps, a 20% reduction for short gaps of a few cm when irradiated. For this reason, UV irradiation is specified for accurate breakdown voltage tests, particularly with smaller gaps.
