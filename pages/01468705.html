Tyndall Effect

· archived # 1,468,705
The Tyndall effect , also known as Tyndall scattering , is the scattering of light by colloidal particles or particles in suspension. It is named after the 19th century scientist John Tyndall . It is similar to Rayleigh scattering , in that the intensity of the scattered light depends on the fourth power of the frequency , so blue light is scattered more strongly than red light . An example in everyday life is the blue color sometimes seen in the smoke emitted by motorcycles, particularly two stroke machines where the burnt engine oil provides the particles.
