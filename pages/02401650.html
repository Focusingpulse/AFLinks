GC2026-STP088 Position of the electron isotropy boundary on the night side of the magnetosphere Tatyana Popova1 , Tatiana Yahnina1 , Andrey Demekhov1 1 Polar Geophysical Institute, Russia tarkada@yandex.ru This paper examines relativistic electron precipitation (REP) caused by particle scattering in the region of stretched magnetic field lines. The pitch-angle scattering responsible for this precipitation occurs near the equatorial plane, mainly on the nightside of the magnetosphere, where the radius of curvature of the field lines becomes comparable to the gyroradius of the particles. In data from the low-orbiting NOAA/POES and MetOp satellites, REP events are observed during crossings of high latitudes in the Northern and Southern hemispheres. The identical instrumentation of these satellites allows one to compare charged particle fluxes and magnetic field strengths at different points at a satellite orbital altitude of ~850 km. It was observed that when such REP events are recorded in pairs of satellite passes close in time and magnetic local time, a shift in geomagnetic latitude is observed in conjugate regions of both hemispheres. Moreover, the difference in latitudes varies depending on the position of the precipitation region relative to the South Atlantic Magnetic Anomaly. The paper investigates the possible position of the isotropy boundary, which is determined by the adiabaticity parameter calculated using different models magnetospheric magnetic field.
