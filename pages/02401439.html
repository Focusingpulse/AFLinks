GC2026-STP021 An alternative view of the mechanism of generation of geomagnetic Pc3-4 pulsations Vladimir B. Belakhovsky1 1 Polar Geophysical Institute, Russia belakhov@mail.ru The mechanism for generating geomagnetic Pc3-4 pulsations is examined using modern ground-based and satellite observations. Data from magnetometers in the CARISMA network (Canada), data from geostationary satellites such as the GOES satellite, are used to record Pc3-4 pulsations, as well as data from the MMS and Cluster satellites to record plasma and electromagnetic disturbances in the foreshock and magnetosheath regions. It is traditionally believed that the source of Pc3-4 geomagnetic pulsations in the magnetosphere and on the Earth’s surface is ULF waves in the foreshock region, which are generated by ion-cyclotron instability in a quasi￾parallel shock wave. These ULF waves then penetrate the magnetosheath into the Earth's magnetosphere, causing resonant effects there. However, there are no clear satellite observations of the propagation of ULF waves through the magnetosheath into the magnetosphere in the world literature. For all the cases considered, according to the data from the meridional chain of stations of the CARISMA network, an increase in the pulsation period is observed with increasing geomagnetic latitude, which is a manifestation of field-line resonance (FLR).
