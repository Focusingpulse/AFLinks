GTM2026-main019 Deconstructing AI Washing: A Signaling Game Analysis of Truthful Disclosure and Substantive Innovation in Emerging Technology Markets Si Zhang1 , Yunwen Huang1 , Shouyang Wang1 1 University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, China Ponencia invitada sywang@amss.ac.cn Abstract The rapid proliferation of artificial intelligence has introduced a critical strategic friction: AI washing, wherein firms opportunistically overstate their AI capabilities to capture market valuation and policy support. Such misrepresentation creates a market-for-lemons dynamic that hinders healthy competition and obscures genuine radical innovation. While regulatory bodies, such as the SEC’s Cybersecurity and Emerging Technologies Unit, have begun investigating these misleading claims, the theoretical mechanisms governing a firm’s choice between strategic washing and substantive disclosure remain underdeveloped. This study employs Signaling Game Theory to construct a dynamic, incomplete-information model between heterogeneous firms and a social planner (government). We deconstruct the incentive structures that lead to a Pooling (Confusion) Equilibrium, where both high- and low-capability firms engage in AI washing due to insufficient regulatory friction. Our analysis identifies the critical threshold of Incentive Compatibility, a combination of dynamic penalties and long-term rewards, required to trigger a Separating Equilibrium.
