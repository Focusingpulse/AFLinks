GC2026-SGG049 Geomechanical effects of cyclic impact on the bottomhole formation zone in laboratory conditions Zakhar Ivanov1 1 Perm National Research Polytechnic University zakhar.ivanov.2013@gmail.com The bottomhole formation zone (BFZ) is a key element of the "reservoir – well" system, determining the productivity of producing wells. During operation, the filtration characteristics of the BFZ deteriorate due to colmatation and mechanical deformations. Traditional treatment methods are often expensive and not always effective, especially in poorly consolidated reservoirs. Cyclic impact is considered a promising method for controlling filtration processes, based on creating alternating pressure gradients by periodically opening and closing the well. The geomechanical essence of the method lies in the fact that a sharp drawdown creates high pressure gradients, causing elastic deformations of the porous matrix and the destruction of colmatating deposits. Subsequent well shut-in leads to stress relaxation and redistribution of solid particles. Repeated cycles result in the accumulation of irreversible deformations, changes in grain packing, and transformation of the BFZ filtration structure. To experimentally study the geomechanical effects, a laboratory setup was developed featuring a granular reservoir model and a controlled outlet valve that automatically opens and closes at a specified frequency. During the experiments, pressure drops across sections of the model and fluid flow rate were recorded to calculate permeability changes as an indicator of deformation processes.
