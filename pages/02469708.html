TRANSMITTING PICTURES BY WIRE Test Fails Because of Induction, but Success Hoped for Today — Edison and John D. Rockefeller Present. Of more than usual interest to the general public is the conversazione held yesterday in Columbia University by the American Institute of Electrical Engineers. Many new and important inventions were shown and original and interesting experiments were made. The exhibits fill twelve rooms and represent the work of fifty eminent scientists. Among them is Nikola Tesla. Interest last night centred in an effort to transmit pictures to distant cities by wire. The receiving point was the University of Chicago. The test failed, so it was reported from Chicago. The instrument, or electrograph, had worked successfully across distances of several hundred miles, but last night, owing to induction of the wires in New York, would not record the thousand of fine dots of which the pictures are composed. It was proposed to transmit between the two cities pictures of President William R. Harper of the University of Chicago, President Seth Low of Columbia and John D. Rockefeller.
