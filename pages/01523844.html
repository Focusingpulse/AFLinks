Saturday Science Chat sponsored by the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Ian Cohen, James Keene and Bill Lucas — after a brief exchange of non-scientific news, given over to a single item from the week's physics headlines and where it led. The neutron lifetime measured from orbit The paper reported the first space-based measurement: the MESSENGER spacecraft carries a neutron detector, and neutrons knocked out of Venus and Mercury by cosmic rays and counted at altitude give an estimate of how long they survive. Hu set out why the item matters. The two laboratory methods — the bottle and the beam — do not agree: the beam gives about nine seconds longer than the bottle, against a lifetime near fourteen and a half minutes. If both are equally valid and give different answers, what is being measured? He returned to this as his standing test case, alongside the gravitational constant. He also noted how remarkable the number itself is: fourteen minutes is an age for a subatomic particle, closer to the decay of an atomic nucleus than to a muon. And he explained why the measurement is hard at all: a free neutron is nearly undetectable, and a detector is essentially a counter tube wrapped in a large block of wax, registering what the neutrons produce rather than the neutrons. The verdict on the new result was that its uncertainty — something like ten to seventeen minutes — is far too wide to choose between the two laboratory values, though the group hopes to build a more precise instrument.
