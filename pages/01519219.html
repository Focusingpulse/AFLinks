Hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary The second in Dennis P Allen Jr and David M Bower 's series, moving from the previous session's neo-Newtonian mechanics to the Bergman–Allen electron model. Host Ian J Cowan noted the announcement had said neo-Newtonian mechanics where it should have said classical mechanics; David de Hilster administered the stream after technical difficulties. It was billed as part one. Bower described the collaboration's origin: he had sent Allen a copy of Kulwick's 1955 paper, and Allen invited him to help correct its electron model. Why not a sphere Kulwick modelled the electron as a spinning charged sphere while conceding it cannot be stable. Nothing prevents the charge, which repels itself, from being extruded at the poles — Bower's analogy being the Earth's magnetic field, which cannot shield the poles from the solar wind because the flux lines run nearly perpendicular there, giving the aurora. Making the mass–energy relation come out then requires a binding energy postulated for the purpose, the Poincaré stresses. Bower allowed that a sphere is still an improvement on a point charge, whose energy is infinite at the origin.
