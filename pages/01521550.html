Title The Deflection of Light in the Dynamic Theory of Gravity Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Ioannis Iraklis Haranas Keywords gravity, light , General Relativity Published 2004 Journal Romanian Astronomical Journal Volume 14 Number 1 No. of pages 8 Pages 3-9 Abstract In a new theory gravity called the dynamic theory, which is derived from thermodymical principles in a five dimensional space, the deflection of a light signal is calculated and compared to that of general relativity. This is achieved by using the dynamic gravity line element which is the usual 4 D space-time element of Newtonian gravity modified by a negative inverse radial exponential term. The dynamic theory of gravity predicts this modification of the original Newtonian potential by this exponential term. Overview The Deflection of Light in the Dynamic Theory of Gravity is an eight-page paper by Ioannis Iraklis Haranas of the Department of Physics and Astronomy at York University, Toronto, published in 2004 in the Romanian Astronomical Journal volume 14, number 1, pages 3–9. It applies a standard test to a non-standard theory. The Dynamic Theory of Gravity, developed by Pharis Williams in papers in Modern Physics Letters A , Apeiron and Entropy between 1997 and 2001, is derived from classical thermodynamics in a five-dimensional space whose coordinates are space, time and mass. It retains Einstein's postulate of the constancy of the speed of light, so special relativity follows unchanged, and by conservation of the fifth dimension it yields equations Haranas states are identical to Einstein's field equations. The theory's distinguishing feature is a non-singular potential. Through Weyl's quantum principle it gives V ( r ) = −( K / r )e^(−λ/ r ), where the exponential factor suppresses the divergence at the origin, and λ = GM / c ² is fixed by the theory.
