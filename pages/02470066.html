The career of Nikola Tesla, one of one of the great scientific minds of all history, is given only 20 lines in the Encyclopedia Britannica. But two Colorado Springs authors, Inez Hunt and Wanetta W. Draper , have made up for the slight by writing the best biography by far on this strange genius who nearly blew the top off Pikes Peak in 1899 with his experiments on Knob Hill. They call their book “ Lightning in His Hand ” and it goes on sale this week at Springs book stores. The price is $5 and the publisher is Alan Swallow of Sage Books, Denver. The illustrations are excellent and many of them are rare. Tesla was born in Serbia in 1856, came to the United States in 1884, and died in New York in 1943. Every one knows that Faraday invented modern chemistry, Thomas A. Edison the incandescent lamp and Alexander Graham Bell the telephone.
