The comments on the B(3) component of this field tensor by Hunter in this Issue address B(3) as if it were a U(1) symmetry field, governed by the ordinary Stokes Theorem, and are therefore sequentially erroneous, because there is a basic misunderstanding of the nature of O(3) electrodynamics inherent in the article. Overview O(3) Electrodynamics: A Second Reply to Hunter is a four-page reply by Myron W Evans of the Alpha Foundation, Budapest, published in Apeiron 7(1–2) in 2000. It answers a criticism by Geoffrey Hunter appearing in the same issue, and the answer turns on a single point of mathematics. The charge is stated in the abstract: "The comments on the B (3) component of this field tensor by Hunter in this Issue address B (3) as if it were a U(1) symmetry field, governed by the ordinary Stokes Theorem, and are therefore sequentially erroneous." Section 2 sets out the distinction. In O(3) electrodynamics the field tensor obeys a non-Abelian Stokes theorem relating a loop integral of the covariant derivative to a hypersurface integral of the commutator [D μ , D ν ]. Reducing the covariant derivative to the U(1) form D μ = ∂ μ + igA μ recovers the ordinary theorem, whose space part is the familiar ∮ A ·d r = ∫∫ B ·d A , and from that Faraday's law in integral form. For B (3) , Evans writes, the non-Abelian version instead gives a phase factor exp(iκ∫d r ) = exp(ig∫∫ B (3) ·d A ), and the corresponding induction equations in the complex circular basis leave ∂ B (3) /∂t = 0, so that "the B (3) component does not give rise to Faraday induction.
