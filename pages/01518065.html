Contents lists available at ScienceDirect Field Crops Research journal homepage: www.elsevier.com/locate/fcr Duckweed (Spirodela polyrhiza) as green manure for increasing yield and reducing nitrogen loss in rice production Yuanlin Yaoa,b , Min Zhanga,b , Yuhua Tiana , Miao Zhaoc , Bowen Zhanga,b , Meng Zhaoa,b , Ke Zenga , Bin Yina,⁎ a State Key Laboratory of Soil and Sustainable Agriculture, Institute of Soil Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Nanjing 210008, China b University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, China c College of Resources and Environment, Chengdu University of Information Technology, Chengdu 610225, China ARTICLE INFO Keywords: Green manure Duckweed N use efficiency Ammonia volatilization 15N recovery ABSTRACT Increasing rice production to feed the world’s growing population while protecting the environment requires more optimal use of fertilizers. In China, the current high input, high output and high reliance on synthetic nitrogen (N) fertilizer in agriculture has resulted in high N losses, especially ammonia (NH3) emission. Urea combined with green manure (GM) might be a promising approach to improve N fertilizer management. However, few studies have evaluated duckweed in this manner. Duckweed does not require arable land for cultivation and thus avoids competition with food crops. Therefore, a field experiment was conducted for three years with five treatments (CK, no N-fertilizer; CT, conventional practice, urea alone at 300 kg N ha−1 ; CTD, urea combined with duckweed at 300 kg N ha−1 ; RN, urea alone at 225 kg N ha−1 ; and RND, urea combined with duckweed at 225 kg N ha−1 ) in an intensive rice cropping system in the Taihu Region of China. The results for two years showed that urea combined with duckweed cover reduced NH3 loss by 36–52% over CT.
