Title Black hole (According to 'Hypothesis on MATTER') Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Nainan K Varghese Keywords gravitational attraction, universal medium, galaxy, black hole , photon , biton, radiation of light, galactic spin, pulsar, quasar , binary system, entropy, cosmology, Hypothesis on MATTER Published 2012 Journal viXra No. of pages 16 "Black hole (According to 'Hypothesis on MATTER')" is a 2012 paper by Nainan K Varghese , one of a long series applying his single-substance mechanical philosophy — set out in Hypothesis on MATTER — to particular phenomena. It offers an account of black holes in which they are neither singular nor bounded by an event horizon: they are simply very large, very dense, but entirely finite bodies of ordinary matter, dark because light cannot get out of the region they occupy. The contrast with the standard picture is the point of the paper. In general relativity a black hole is a singularity — a point of infinite density where the known laws fail — hidden behind a horizon fixed by escape velocity. Varghese's system admits neither infinities nor singularities anywhere, so his black hole is a body like any other, only more so: a "very large single macro body of very high matter-density" whose darkness is a consequence of its density, not of a boundary in spacetime. Abstract Physical parameters of stable galaxies produce sufficient repulsion to overcome gravitational attraction between them. However, those very large gatherings of 3D matter bodies and debris in space, which do not develop into stable galaxies (or which constitute central parts of stable galaxies, with lower centrifugal actions) succumb to gravitational attraction between constituent matter bodies and form very large single macro bodies of very high matter-density.
