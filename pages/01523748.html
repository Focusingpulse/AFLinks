Cahill Residence Adelaide, South Australia, Australia Nationality Australian Known for Process Physics; dynamical 3-space; the claim that light-speed anisotropy has been repeatedly measured since 1887 Scientific career Fields Physics , quantum field theory, foundations of physics Institutions School of Chemical and Physical Sciences, Flinders University, Adelaide Reginald T. Cahill is an Australian theoretical physicist at Flinders University in Adelaide, and the author of two connected and far-reaching proposals: Process Physics , in which space and quantum phenomena are emergent from self-organising information rather than fundamental; and the dynamical 3-space , a structured, flowing space whose motion he claims has been detected experimentally many times over — beginning with Michelson and Morley in 1887. He is unusual among the researchers catalogued here in two respects. He came to this work from a conventional career in theoretical particle physics , with published work on the Global Colour Model of QCD in mainstream journals. And his central claim is not interpretive but empirical : that a specific, measurable quantity — the anisotropy of the speed of light — has been sitting in the experimental record for more than a century, misread because the instruments were calibrated with the wrong theory. Career Cahill has spent his career in the School of Chemical and Physical Sciences at Flinders University , Adelaide. His earlier research was in hadron physics — the strong interaction, quark–gluon dynamics and the Global Colour Model of quantum chromodynamics. That work appeared in ordinary venues of the field, including Modern Physics Letters A ( A New Mass Formula for NG Bosons in QCD , 1995), together with papers on quark–gluon coupling and low-energy quark–gluon processes.
