Title The Mysterious Ether Read in full Link to paper Author(s) John-Erik Persson Published 2008 No. of pages 4 Abstract Evidence for the ether's existence and state of motion are explained. Methods for finding more knowledge about the ether are explained. Overview Presented in absentia at the 15th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in 2008, this four-page paper by John-Erik Persson does three things: it classifies the available models for the speed of light, it argues that the GPS already provides evidence for an ether wind, and it specifies a table-top experiment that would settle the matter. Four models and three mistakes Persson lists the options for light's speed as (I) c , relativity with no ether; (II) c + v , an absolute ether with a preferred frame, "most dissident's model"; and (III) c + v ( r ), an entrained ether with a preferred field , in two variants — one translated and rotated by the Earth, and one translated but not rotated, which he calls the generated ether. His thesis is that the standard model was adopted through three misreadings: an imagined conflict between the Bradley aberration effect and model III, an imagined conflict between the Michelson result and model II, and the interpretation of the Sagnac effect as an effect of rotation rather than translation, which concealed a real conflict with model I. "SRT is therefore a result of three important mistakes." GPS as evidence Because separated clocks cannot be perfectly synchronised, Persson argues, an absolute velocity must be measured by a method insensitive to a constant clock offset. GPS achieves this by using more than two satellite clocks, so that a constant receiver-clock error becomes irrelevant, making one-way light measurement possible. He then points to the Sagnac compensation applied in GPS: east–west coordinate differences are based on c + v and c − v with v the ether wind, about 10 −6 of c .
