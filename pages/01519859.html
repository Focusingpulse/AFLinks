Title Electromechanical Perpetual Motion Author(s) Leslee A Kulba Keywords Perpetual motion, permanent magnet motors, over-unity, back-EMF Published 2010 Journal Electric Spacecraft Journal Volume 48 Pages 8-10 Electromechanical Perpetual Motion is a three-page article by Leslee A Kulba , published in 2010 in issue 48 of the Electric Spacecraft Journal , the journal's final double issue. It is the second part of the ten-part handbook on perpetual motion and its testing that Kulba, the journal's technical editor, contributed to that issue, and it carries the survey begun in " Mechanical Perpetual Motion " into magnets, coils and circuits. The text of the article has not been recoverable, and this page describes its subject and its place in the series rather than its argument. The subject Why the electrical version is the durable one A wheel of weights can be watched. Its failure is visible in a minute and arguable on paper, which is why the mechanical claims of the last three centuries have mostly died quietly. An electrical machine cannot be watched in the same way. Its input and output are quantities that have to be measured , usually in circuits carrying pulsed, switched or reactive waveforms, and the measurement is genuinely difficult — difficult enough that an honest experimenter with ordinary instruments can obtain a reading greater than unity and have no idea why. That is the reason the series is shaped as it is. Having described the machines in this article, Kulba spends the remaining seven on efficiency, experimental design, instruments, low-level signals, microwave power, power calculation and error analysis. The electrical perpetual motion claim is not refuted by argument in the way the overbalanced wheel is; it is refuted by measuring properly, and most of the handbook is about how.
