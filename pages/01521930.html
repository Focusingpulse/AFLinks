Title The Structure of Energy and Motion Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Don Briddell Keywords Motion, space, plenum, electodynamics, structure, condensed matter Published 2013 No. of pages 9 Abstract Motion of all kinds is understood in structural analytics as deploying and condensing electromagnetic plenum waves. The structural relationship between boson and fermion motion is explained. Overview This is the twelfth paper in Don Briddell's Field Structure Theory series, presented at the 20th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference in 2013. Its subject is motion, and its claim is that motion is not something objects do in a passive background but something the background does to them: "The impulse to move is coming from the plenum matrix and not from the object moving in the matrix." The framework behind it is geometric rather than algebraic. Space is filled with a plenum , an "absolute action matrix" whose fundamental unit is a loop. Loops come in left- and right-handed pairs; when two twist together they acquire frequency and become an electromagnetic wave set; when one side of the set condenses and the other stays deployed, the condensed side is a fermion and the deployed side its charge field. Three such wave sets, knotted three-dimensionally in what Briddell calls a Structor, hold the condensation in place, and that is a particle. From this he derives a set of correspondences: mass is condensed field, kinetic energy is a condensed field in the act of deploying, potential energy is a fully deployed field, gravity is the effect on the plenum of condensing energy to a locality, and charge is what appears when one handedness condenses and the other does not. The paper is illustrated throughout with stereo-pair photographs of physical models — the reader is invited to cross their eyes until the third image resolves into three dimensions — several built by the geodesic geometer Joseph Clinton.
