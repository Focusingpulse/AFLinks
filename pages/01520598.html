Title Mathematical Methods to Refute Einstein's Electrodynamics Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Velimir Abramovic Keywords electrodynamics, Einstein , Twin Paradox Published 2011 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 8 No. of pages 4 Pages 1-4 Abstract This paper explores contradictions in Einstein's definition of simultaneity, and their contribution to the famous Twin Paradox. In the Galilean case, t = t', the Cosmonaut and Earth twins obviously age the same, but in the other cases we obtain ontological contradictions. The problem lies in Einstein's synthesis of non-motion and motion of one and the same light ray. Gauss's Modular or Time Arithmetic helps to identify and sort out the fundamental problems. Despite Einstein, only one time has ever existed: the present. The inequality of entities within the electromagnetic continuum creates the non-synchronicity we call motion. Overview Velimir Abramović argues that Einstein's definition of simultaneity is self-contradictory, that the contradiction is the source of the twin paradox, and that Gauss's modular arithmetic exposes it. The paper was given at the 18th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference in 2011. He begins by quoting the 1905 paper in full on the point at issue: a common time for A and B cannot be defined "unless we establish by definition that the 'time' required by light to travel from A to B equals the 'time' it requires to travel from B to A," so that with a pulse leaving A at t A , reflecting at B at t B , and returning at t ′ A , the clocks are synchronised when t B − t A = t ′ A − t B . (1) Against this he sets "the constant present time of human experience where t A = t B ," and then works through the algebra. From (1) he obtains t = t B − t A and t ′ A − t A = 2 t , then forms differences that reduce to t A − t A = t ′ A − t ′ A = t − t , hence 0 = 0.
