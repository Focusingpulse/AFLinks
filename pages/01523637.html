Title Permittivity Creates Dark Matter and an Older Universe With Accelerating Expansion Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Morton F Spears Keywords dark matter , permittivity, universe, Big Bang , stars Published 1998 Journal Unpublished No. of pages 7 Abstract The purpose of this report is to provide simple answers to the following three questions by considering the effects of what has been happening over time to one single property of open space; namely, the permittivity of that space: # What is dark matter and why does it exist? Why do so many distant stars appear to be older than the expanding "Big Bang" Universe of which those same stars are a part? Why is our Universe expanding at a greater velocity now than it did many millions of years ago? Overview Morton F. Spears, writing in July 1998, proposes that a single change — a decrease in the permittivity of open space over cosmic time — answers three questions at once: what dark matter is, why distant stars appear older than the universe containing them, and why the expansion is faster now than in the past. His starting point is a rewriting of mass–energy equivalence. Since c 0 ² = 1/ε 0 μ 0 , the relation E = Mc 0 ² can be expressed in terms of the two constants of free space directly, and he takes the permeability μ 0 as fixed while allowing ε 0 to vary. He notes in passing that early space "may have contained enough disbursed magnetic materials to increase the space effective permeability value," but sets that aside. Two arguments are offered for a permittivity that falls with time. The first is a laboratory analogy: a parallel-plate capacitor in a tuned oscillator circuit shifts to lower frequency when solid particles, vapours or gases are introduced between the plates, because the effective permittivity of the gap has risen.
