Henry P. Dart Born ( 1917-05-22 ) May 22, 1917 Died October 23, 2001 (2001-10-23) (aged 84) Residence New Orleans, Louisiana, and Tucson, Arizona, United States Nationality USA Known for A tired-light alternative to the Big Bang ; repulsive gravity in galactic cores; scepticism of black holes Scientific career Fields Law; cosmology and gravitation as an independent researcher Henry Plauché Dart III (22 May 1917 – 23 October 2001) was a New Orleans attorney who, after moving to Tucson in 1981, spent his later years on cosmology and gravitation . He proposed a tired-light alternative to the Big Bang in which photons decay into neutrinos that are recycled into matter at galactic centres, argued that gravity turns repulsive in the cores of the most massive stars and so makes black holes unnecessary, and took part in the first two conferences of the Natural Philosophy Alliance . Biography Dart was born in New Orleans on 22 May 1917, the third of that name in a line of Louisiana lawyers: his grandfather Henry Plauché Dart (1858–1934) was the first president of the Louisiana Bar Association and is commemorated by an endowed professorship at LSU Law, and his father was Henry P. Dart, Jr. He practised law in New Orleans, moved to Tucson, Arizona, in 1981, and returned to New Orleans in 2000. He died at the Ochsner Foundation Hospital on 23 October 2001, aged 84, survived by four children — among them Henry T. Dart, an attorney in turn — and ten grandchildren. It was in Tucson that the scientific work was done. The Times-Picayune records that he "worked on astronomy, geology and other research papers that were presented to the American Association for the Advancement of Science", and his own writing places him at the Southwest and Rocky Mountain Division meeting of the AAAS in Norman, Oklahoma, in May 1995.
