GC2026-PERM052 Magnetic properties change as indicator of terrestrial weathering on ordinary chondrites Dilyara Kuzina1 , Jérôme Gattacceca2 , Hugo Gouilloux2 , Hugo Mertens2 , Romain Lacube2 , Francois Demory2 , Cyril Lorenz3 1 Kazan (Volga Region) Federal University 2 CNRS, Aix-Marseille University, IRD, INRAE, CEREGE 3 Vernadsky Institute of geochemistry and analytical chemistry di-gilmanova@yandex.ru Ordinary chondrites comprise mostly silicate minerals (olivine, pyroxene, plagioclase), Fe,Ni metallic minerals (kamacite, taenite, tetrataenite) and iron sulfides (troilite). Silicate minerals are relatively stable in the terrestrial environment, the Fe,Ni minerals and troilite contained in ordinary chondrites will oxidize at the surface of the Earth. This leads to the formation of iron oxyhydroxides such as lepidocrocite, akageneite, goethite, and/or iron oxides such as magnetite and maghemite [1]. Meteorites are collected in hot deserts are exposed to rain and moisture, and are often strongly weathered [e.g., 2, 3]. The Atacama Desert, in Chile, stands out among hot deserts by its long-lived and extreme hyperaridity [4]. As such, it hosts the oldest meteorite collection in the world [5, 6]. In this work we study the effect of weathering on the magnetic properties of ordinary chondrites. We measured the magnetic properties (magnetic susceptibility, hysteresis parameters) of 117 ordinary chondrites from the H and L groups, collected in the Atacama Desert (Chile).
