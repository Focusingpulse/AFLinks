GC2026-STP002 Power transmission lines and the near-Earth space as mutually interdependent systems Vyacheslav Pilipenko1,2 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Geophysical Center of the Russian Academy of Sciences pilipenko_va@mail.ru This review considers the interactions between large-scale power transmission lines (PTLs) and electromagnetic disturbances in near-Earth space. On one hand, PTLs are sensitive detectors of geomagnetic disturbances and pulsations, which lead to the excitation of geomagnetically induced currents (GICs) in power systems. GIC surges, which disrupt the normal operation of industrial high-voltage transformers, are a response to "ionospheric tornadoes"—localized field-aligned currents. On the other hand, a power line itself can be a source of disturbances in near-Earth space. Due to distortions from nonlinear loads, an unbalanced power line emits not only the fundamental 50/60 Hz frequency, but also its low harmonics (power line emission, or PLE). The third harmonic is emitted most intensely due to the characteristic properties of a three-phase line. At high harmonics, the radiation intensity experiences additional amplification (power line harmonic radiation, or PLHR). This amplification is caused by the fact that at frequencies for which the electromagnetic wavelength is comparable to the length of a straight section of the power line, the line begins to operate as a horizontal Beverege traveling wave antenna.
