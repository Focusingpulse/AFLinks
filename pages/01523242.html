Kizer Born 1947 (age 78–79) Residence Portsmouth, OH, United States Nationality USA Known for Le Sage gravitation; Association for Pushing Gravity Research essay prizes Scientific career Fields Gravitation, biology, economics, psychology John B. Kizer (born 1947) is an American independent researcher who has written on gravitation, relativity, biological regulation and economics. He is best known on this wiki for a series of prize-winning essays defending Le Sagian — that is, pushing — theories of gravitation, and he assisted John Chappell in the years before the founding of the Natural Philosophy Alliance . Biography Kizer appeared on the General Electric College Bowl in 1967 while an undergraduate at Ohio State University, which he described as his most singular honour there. He held a National Science Foundation fellowship in 1970. He has since worked as a consultant in two fields: psychological consulting, specialising in individuals of high intellectual ability, and economic consulting. He has also been an invited speaker on energy topics. Education Undergraduate study, Ohio State University National Science Foundation fellow, 1970 Work on gravitation Kizer's gravitational writing belongs to the revival of Le Sage's theory of gravitation — the proposal, originating with Georges-Louis Le Sage in the eighteenth century, that gravity is a push exerted by a flux of particles or radiation streaming through space from all directions, rather than an attraction exerted by masses. Between 1975 and 1977 he took a prize in the annual essay competition of the Association for Pushing Gravity Research (APGR) three years running.
