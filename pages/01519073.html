This explains not only the force of gravity, but also the effects of gravitational potential. Rigorous proofs of the bending of light and the effects observed in the orbit of Mercury were included. Overview Bruce Harvey proposes that gravity arises from the electric fields of the elementary charged particles making up matter, and that the energy released when a mass falls comes from the energy stored in those fields. He opens with a checklist of what any theory of gravity must explain, and it is a fair one: the force itself, its proportionality to both the source mass and the attracted mass, the inverse-square law, the slowing of clocks by gravitational potential, the delay of radio signals passing near the Sun, the bending of light, the gravitational redshift, and Mercury's missing 43 arcseconds per century. His starting assertion is that the total energy E = mc ² of a body is electromagnetic in composition: three-quarters stored in the electric fields of the elementary charged particles, a quarter as potential energy from the separation of positive and negative charges, and the kinetic component stored in the magnetic fields generated by their motion. When these coexisting fields exert a small influence on one another, reducing their capacity to store energy, the released energy is what appears as gravitational potential energy.
