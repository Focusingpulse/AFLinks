Abstract It is nowadays taught that centrifugal force is a fictitious force that can only be observed from a rotating frame of reference. This teaching is based on the argument that when no inward centripetal force is acting, a particle will proceed in its straight line inertial path. In situations where the physical effects of centrifugal force are detected, this is dismissed as being merely the effects of inertia. Polar coordinates relative to a point origin expose a centrifugal force acting on a particle that is moving in a straight line, but this centrifugal force tends to be masked from view by virtue of the fact that the radial position vector is rotating. This article will examine ways to expose the physical reality of the centrifugal force. Overview Centrifugal Force is a four-page paper by David Tombe of Belfast, dated 10 November 2011 and published through the General Science Journal . Its thesis is that centrifugal force is a real physical force rather than a fictitious one visible only from a rotating frame. The argument opens with Newton's rotating bucket. When a pail of water is rotated relative to the fixed stars the surface rises at the sides, "yet no officially recognized upward force is deemed to be acting on the system." Tombe holds that the standard dismissal — that this is the tendency of particles to follow straight inertial paths — misdescribes what happens: "It is taught wrongly, that when the inward acting centripetal force yields, only tangential motion ensues, and that no outward radial motion is involved. But outward radial motion clearly does occur as well, albeit that the radial vector is rotating."
