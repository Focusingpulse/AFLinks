PRECAUTIONS TO BE TAKEN. Tesla, with Hands Burned Through Carelessness, Says There Is No Danger. HAIR WILL GROW AGAIN. The publicity given to the case of Henry Cohen, who lost his hair after his head had been subjected to X rays for the purpose of locating a bullet, has resulted in a new interest in the mysterious light and force that penetrate and make transparent some bodies that are opaque in all other lights. Persona who heretofore submitted unhesitatingly to an inspection of internal organs have become timid since they heard of the fate of Cohen, and they want to be reassured before they consent to sit within the radius of the cathode stream. Mr. Martin, who operated the Crookes tube that threw the X rays through Cohen's head, said, last night: "It is well known that X rays will blister and dry up the skin if the tube is held close to the patient, and there is nothing between the tube and the skin to protect the latter, provided the rays are continued long enough. The drying up of the skin would cause the hair to fall out but no serious harm would be done as it would soon grow in again. SKULL MOST DIFFICULT.
