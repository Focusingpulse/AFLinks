They had $100,000, a garage, and a dating website nobody wanted. Eighteen months later, Google wrote them a check for $1.65 billion. Three PayPal employees couldn’t share a video from a dinner party. So they built a platform that now reaches over 2.5 billion people every month. Chad Hurley, Steve Chen, and Jawed Karim were nobodies. Just regular workers at PayPal. Hurley designed the company logo. Chen was an engineer. Karim was writing code while finishing his degree at night. Then eBay bought PayPal in 2002 for $1.5 billion. The corporate culture crushed them. Most of the original employees walked out within a few years. Including these three. No big exit payouts. No master plan. Just three guys sitting in a deli in Palo Alto brainstorming ideas over sandwiches. Steve Chen had about $100,000 in savings from his PayPal days. He gave himself two years to make something work before going back to a regular career. That was his entire runway. Their first idea was a video dating website. The slogan was “Tune In, Hook Up.” It flopped. Nobody wanted to upload dating videos of themselves. But here’s what they noticed that everyone else missed. People couldn’t find or share video online. When the Indian Ocean tsunami hit in December 2004, Karim went looking for footage. Couldn’t find it anywhere.
