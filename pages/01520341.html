Title How Electrodynamics with Statistical Mechanics Can Imply Gravitation Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Cynthia Kolb Whitney Keywords electrodynamics, gravitation, Gravitational attraction Published 2013 No. of pages 4 Abstract This paper shows how the phenomenon of gravitational attraction can arise from known Electrodynamics when it is combined with ideas from the discipline of Statistical Mechanics. The key input from Electrodynamics is the classical understanding about magnetic interactions between tiny current elements. The key input from Statistical Mechanics is the classical idea of distribution among possible energy states based on maximum entropy. Overview Cynthia Kolb Whitney, editor of Galilean Electrodynamics , proposes that gravitational attraction is a statistical residue of magnetic forces between atoms. The ingredients are deliberately old: Ampère's pre-Maxwellian force law between current elements, and the Boltzmann principle that lower-energy configurations are more heavily populated than higher-energy ones. The mechanism is easy to state. A neutral atom is very like an Ampère current element — charge neutral, with electrons circulating while the nucleus barely moves. Two such atoms exert a magnetic force on one another that may be attractive or repulsive depending on their relative orientation. Since attractive orientations are the negative-energy ones, and negative-energy states are the more populated, the ensemble average over all orientations is net attractive. That residue, Whitney suggests, is gravity. The paper is offered as a sketch rather than a result — "How much smaller presently remains to be worked out" — and it closes with speculative extensions to antigravity, the matter–antimatter asymmetry, dark matter and dark energy.
