S-K Dynamic The S-K Dynamic describes how organized potential becomes observable activity when its conditions change. S-K stands for Scalar–Kinetic. In this SVP interpretation, scalar refers to the organized or balanced potential condition, and kinetic refers to its expression through motion and other physical changes. Something may appear quiet while holding considerable potential. A disturbance changes the relationships maintaining that condition, allowing activity to develop. The system then settles into another condition of relative balance. Organized Potential → Disturbance → Change of Relationship → Manifest Activity → New Relative Balance Balance need not mean complete inactivity. Opposing activities can maintain a balance until one gains predominance. Everyday Examples A stretched rubber band. Stretching stores energy in the band’s changed structure. Your fingers hold it in place. Releasing one end changes the constraint, and the band contracts. The release permits the motion; the stretching supplied the stored energy. Water behind a gate. Elevated water has gravitational potential energy. Opening the gate permits flow, which can turn a wheel. The gate controls access to the potential. A match. The match head and surrounding oxygen possess chemical potential. Striking supplies the conditions needed to begin a reaction. The resulting heat, light, and chemical changes draw upon that chemical potential. A shaken soda bottle. Opening the cap lowers the pressure above the liquid. Dissolved gas comes out of solution, producing bubbles and sometimes foam. The changed pressure allows a new arrangement to develop. A solar cell. Incoming sunlight supplies energy to charge carriers in a semiconductor. The cell’s organized structure separates and collects them, permitting useful current through a connected circuit.
