Title The Fully Quantized Electromagnetic Field Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Richard Oldani Keywords electromagnetic field, electromagnetic radiation, quantum mechanics , Frequency, photon Published 2003 Volume 3 Number 1 No. of pages 5 Pages 81-85 Abstract Electromagnetic Phenomena , V3, N1 (9), pp. 81-85. Einstein derived his coefficients of induced and spontaneous emission by assuming that electromagnetic radiation is directional, having the form of "needle radiation". That idea is extended here and shown to suggest a model of the atom in which excited atomic states are treated as three-body problems; nucleus, electron, and photon. The fully quantized electromagnetic field, or photon, is conceived of therefore as having a central core surrounded by a continuous sinusoidal field; and stimulated emission is the result of recoil momentum. "Real" frequency doubling oscillators replace fictitious harmonic oscillators as the mechanism that connects excited atomic states with radiation fields. This allows the mathematical formalism of quantum mechanics to be assigned a more precise physical interpretation. Evidence of the fully quantized electromagnetic field and frequency doubling oscillators is briely described at the macroscopic level as well, by citing experiments from chaos theory and high speed photographs of spark discharges. The totality of the experimental evidence indicates that quantum phenomena occur when field sources are of balanced intensity while classical phenomena are the result of imbalanced intensities, i.e. when elds are describable by test charges. Overview Richard Oldani argues that quantum mechanics took a wrong turn in 1925 when Heisenberg built matrix mechanics on a picture of the radiating atom as emitting a spherically symmetric wave with no net momentum transfer, rather than on Einstein's picture of directed "needle radiation." Einstein's 1917 derivation of the emission and absorption coefficients assumed localised quanta of energy h
