Kelly (BSc, H.Dip.Ed., born 1947) is an independent researcher and retired science teacher who argues that the aether exists and that Einstein was mistaken to abandon it. He is the author of Did Einstein Get it Wrong? (2006), a short book setting out an aether-based "theory of everything" in which space is pervaded by antimatter and in which mass, gravitation and the wave-particle duality of light and matter are all consequences of that medium. He worked on the theory outside any academic institution, after taking early retirement from teaching. Not to be confused with Michael J. Kelly, the Cambridge semiconductor physicist and Fellow of the Royal Society, who is a different person. Biography Kelly was born in 1947 and educated at John Hamilton High School in Liverpool. He took a B.Sc. in electronics at the Royal College of Advanced Technology, Salford, and then worked as a systems analyst for Hewlett Packard near Windsor, Berkshire. He subsequently moved into education, undertaking postgraduate study in philosophy, psychology and education at Trinity College, Dublin — the source of the H.Dip.Ed. that appears after his name on his book — and went on to teach physics and chemistry for many years at St. Eunan's College in Letterkenny, County Donegal, Ireland. After taking early retirement he devoted himself to developing the unified theory of physics presented in his book. His publisher, Trafford Publishing, is based in Canada, and the book is sometimes catalogued as Canadian on that basis; the biographical details published with the book place Kelly himself in Ireland.
