Title Quasar's Gyro-gravity Behavior, Luminosity and Redshift Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Thierry De Mees Keywords quasar , gravitation, luminosity, gyrotation, galaxy, redshift , gravitomagnetism Published 2008 Journal General Science Journal No. of pages 7 Abstract The high redshift value of quasars is generally described by the Hubble constant, related to the Doppler-effect due to the expansion of the universe. In this paper, we look closer to the part of the redshift that is caused by gyrogravitation, which is the analogue application of the electromagnetic Maxwell equations upon gravitation. The result of our analysis explains the possibility of a high value difference between the quasar redshift and the related galaxy redshift due to the quasar's rotation (spin). Moreover, we find results that are within the observed redshifts, based only on the expected quasar-radius of a few light-weeks, without the artifact of an expanding universe. Overview Thierry De Mees applies his gyrotation framework — the Maxwell analogy for gravitation, in which mass currents source a field analogous to the magnetic one — to quasars, arguing that a substantial part of their redshift is produced by their own rotation rather than by cosmic expansion. The motivation is the discrepancy sometimes reported between a quasar's redshift and that of a galaxy with which it appears associated. If part of the quasar's redshift is intrinsic and rotation-dependent, De Mees argues, such a difference is expected rather than anomalous. His analysis proceeds from the same equation set as his other papers on the subject: a gravitational Lorentz force F ⇐ m ( g + v × Ω ), with the gyrotation field Ω sourced by mass currents in analogy with magnetism. Applied to a spinning quasar, this yields a contribution to the redshift depending on the object's rotation.
