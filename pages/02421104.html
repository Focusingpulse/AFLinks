Hendershot is a 1995 book compiled and self-published by Mark M. Hendershot from the personal papers of his father, the inventor Lester J. Hendershot (1898–1961). It gathers documents, notes, correspondence and photographs relating to the Hendershot fuelless generator — the "magnetic motor" that made Lester Hendershot briefly famous in 1928 — and is one of the principal source collections on the device and the controversy surrounding it. Overview Drawing on material Lester Hendershot left behind, the book documents the origin of the fuelless generator (which Hendershot said he stumbled upon while developing a fuelless aviation compass), its public unveiling and the intense press attention it drew, the interest of prominent backers, and the political and commercial difficulties that eventually caused Hendershot to set the work aside. As a first-hand family compilation rather than an independent technical study, it preserves primary material — letters, diagrams and recollections — that would otherwise have been lost, and it remains a key reference within the free-energy community. The device's operation has never been independently verified, and its claimed fuelless output is not accepted by mainstream science.
