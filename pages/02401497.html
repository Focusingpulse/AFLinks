GC2026-STP033 TGS gamma-ray spectrometer on the SINP-MSU-80 small satellite for studying solar activity and space weather effects Vitaly Bogomolov1 , Andrey Bogomolov1 , Anatoly Iyudin1 , Ivan Kucherenko1 , Vladislav Osedlo1 , Sergey Svertilov1 , Nikita Vasiliev1 , Egor Voskresenskov1 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow, Russia bogovit@rambler.ru The small satellite SINP-MSU-80 (formerly known as Scorpion) in the cubesat 16U format was launched on December 28, 2025 into a circular polar orbit with an altitude of about 500 km. The payload of this satellite is a complex of scientific instruments, including gamma-ray and particle detectors, a fast photometer for studying flares in the upper atmosphere, and instruments for studying the effects of cosmic factors on microorganisms. Significant part of scientific research with help of the satellite payload and in particular with TGS gamma-ray spectrometer onboard will be connected with solar activity and space weather effects. The TGS gamma-ray spectrometer consists of four independent modules with an area of 64cm2 each. Two modules are position-sensitive detectors based on detecting pixels with size 10 mm x 10 mm x 20 mm made of GAGG:Ce and YSO, the other two modules are based on large area CsI (Tl) bricks. SiPMs are used as photodetectors in the device. The operating range of the instrument from 30 keV to 3 MeV is divided into 6 monitoring channels, in which the corresponding energy gamma quantum rates are transmitted. One more monitoring channel displays the counting rate of electrons with an energy of more than 300 keV
