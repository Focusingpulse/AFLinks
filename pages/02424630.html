Mafﬁni a a b The Pew Charitable Trusts, 901 E Street NW, Washington, DC 20004, USA Environmental Management Institute, 5610 Crawfordsville Road, Indianapolis, IN 46224, USA a r t i c l e i n f o Article history: Received 3 April 2013 Received in revised form 29 July 2013 Accepted 31 July 2013 Available online xxx Keywords: Food chemicals, Food additives, FDA, GRAS, Safe, Toxicology, Food contact substances, Flavors a b s t r a c t In the United States, chemical additives cannot be used in food without an afﬁrmative determination that their use is safe by FDA or additive manufacturer. Feeding toxicology studies designed to estimate the amount of a chemical additive that can be eaten safely provide the most relevant information. We analyze how many chemical additives allowed in human food have feeding toxicology studies in three toxicological information sources including the U.S. Food and Drug Administration’s (FDA) database. Less than 38% of FDA-regulated additives have a published feeding study. For chemicals directly added to food, 21.6% have feeding studies necessary to estimate a safe level of exposure and 6.7% have reproductive or developmental toxicity data in FDA’s database. A program is needed to ﬁll these signiﬁcant knowledge gaps by using in vitro and in silico methods complemented with targeted in vivo studies to ensure public health is protected. © 2013 The Authors.
