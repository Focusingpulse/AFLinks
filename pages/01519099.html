Title A Dynamical Origin for the Gravitational Constant that Explains Gravitational and Inertial Mass Equality and Rejects Dark Matter and Dark Energy Read in full Link to paper (Internet Archive) Author(s) Lawrence M Stephenson Keywords gravity Published 2003 Journal Physics Essays Volume 16 Number 2 Read the full paper here (archived copy — the original link is no longer available) Abstract There is no accepted theory that explains why the gravitational mass and the inertial mass of a given body are equal. Any new theory that predicted a dynamical origin for the gravitational constant should also predict that the gravitational mass of a body is equal to its inertial mass. Two dynamical theories are outlined. One of these theories does not require the assumptions of dark matter and dark energy to explain cosmological observations. Overview Lawrence M. Stephenson, writing in Physics Essays in 2003, asks why the gravitational and inertial mass of a body are equal, and proposes that both arise from the motion of distant matter — a Machian account which, he argues, also fixes the value of G and removes the need for dark matter and dark energy. He sets out two puzzles. The first is the equality of the two masses, verified to one part in 10 11 at the time of writing and unexplained by either Newtonian theory or general relativity. He is careful about what general relativity does and does not supply: the theory takes the equality as its starting observation rather than deriving it, and "there is nothing in general relativity that either establishes a value for G or proves that G is a universal constant." He notes the 1986 fifth-force episode, in which materials of different nuclear composition were compared and no difference was found. The second puzzle is G itself.
