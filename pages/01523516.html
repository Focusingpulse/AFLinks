Title Newton vs. Einstein Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Marcel Luttgens Keywords redshift , gravitational braking Published 1998 Journal Apeiron Volume 5 Number 1-2 No. of pages 5 Pages 25-30 Abstract This paper demonstrates that the redshift of galaxies and quasars is due to a "gravitational braking" of light and not to a so-called expansion of the universe. It shows further that the velocity of any material body in movement with respect to "the cosmological background" will progressively decrease and eventually cancel out. Moreover, any movement is absolute and independent of an observer, and the GRT gives here erroneous results. Overview Newton vs. Einstein is a five-page paper by Marcel Luttgens of Les Adrets de l'Esterel, France, published in Apeiron vol. 5, nos. 1–2 (1998), pp. 25–30. It argues that the redshift of galaxies and quasars is "gravitational braking" of light in a static universe rather than expansion, and that any body moving relative to "the cosmological background" progressively slows and eventually stops. The argument starts from a passage Luttgens quotes at length from Weinberg's Gravitation and Cosmology on stars of uniform incompressible density. For such a star the surface redshift is (1 − 2MG/R) −1/2 − 1, and the structure of Einstein's equations caps MG/R at 4/9, so the surface redshift cannot exceed 2. From the interior there is no such bound, and light from the centre has 1 + z = 2/[3(1 − 2MG/R) 1/2 − 1], which diverges as MG/R approaches 4/9. Luttgens then substitutes the mean density of the universe for the star's. Writing M = 4πR³ρ/3 gives 2MG/Rc² = K²R²/c² with K² = 4πGρ/3, and for ρ = 2×10 −29 g/cm³ he obtains K = 2.36×10 −18 s −1 , so 1/K = 1.34×10 10 years — "analogous to the Hubble time." A parallel Newtonian calculation gives v² = V² − K²r², which he interprets as an energy loss proportional to distance travelled. Taking z = (v r /c)/(1 − v r /c) with v r = Kr yields his central relation R/R 0 = z/(1 + z), with R 0 = c/K
