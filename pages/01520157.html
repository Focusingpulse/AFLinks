Overview Fundamentals of Gravity is a three-page paper by Bob de Hilster , presented at the 18th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference at College Park, Maryland on 6 July 2011 and published in Proceedings of the NPA volume 8, pages 151–154. Its method is deliberately austere. De Hilster begins from a single assumption — "the universe contains objects that move" — defines space, time and measurement, adopts causality and Newton's three principles, and then builds outward by stating what he takes to be self-evident observations. He is explicit about the limits of the exercise: "This is not a document that explains what gravity is, but only what gravity does. A specific mechanism for gravity is not included." Three observations carry the argument. Gravity passes through objects — it reaches his office through the roof, walls and floor. As it passes through an object it causes the object to move. And gravity is omnidirectional, since wherever one stands on Earth it drives objects toward the centre. From these he restates Newton's first principle: an object remains in uniform motion so long as all external forces are equal and opposite in all directions.
