Hydrocortisone

· archived # 1,459,850
Hydrocortisone is a corticosteroid — a synthetic form of a natural hormone (cortisol) that your adrenal glands make. It’s used to reduce inflammation, itching, redness, and allergic reactions in the body. Common uses Topical cream/ointment (1% over-the-counter): For rashes, eczema, insect bites, mild allergic skin irritation, poison ivy, or inflammation. Prescription strengths or oral forms: For more serious inflammation such as arthritis, asthma, adrenal insufficiency, or severe allergic reactions. How it works Hydrocortisone doesn’t cure the cause of irritation — it simply dampens the body’s immune response in the treated area, calming swelling and itching by reducing the release of inflammatory chemicals. Things to keep in mind Use sparingly and short-term unless a doctor says otherwise; overuse can thin the skin. Avoid broken skin, eyes, or large body areas unless directed. If you’re using a cream on a rash that isn’t improving after a week (or gets worse), stop and get medical advice — some rashes (especially fungal) can worsen with steroids. Oral or injectable hydrocortisone (for systemic conditions) must be tapered under medical supervision.
