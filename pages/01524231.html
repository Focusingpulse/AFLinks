Title The Superfluous System Read in full Read in full Author(s) Lucy Haye ; edited by David de Hilster Keywords Autodynamics , Lorentz transformation , observer, frames of reference, Neutrino Published Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Website https://www.autodynamics.org/superfluous-system/ "The Superfluous System" is a paper by Lucy Haye , edited by David de Hilster , which restates the central objection of Autodynamics to the Lorentz transformation without algebraic machinery. Its argument is that the second coordinate system in the transformation is introduced artificially, and that the artificial system is what produces the excess energy which Pauli 's neutrino was later invented to remove. Overview Haye begins from the simplest possible arrangement: a phenomenon at A , an observer at B , and a signal travelling from one to the other. She argues that this arrangement already satisfies both requirements of relativity — inertial systems in relative motion, and a constant light velocity — so that relativity holds between A and B with no further apparatus. What Lorentz did, she writes, was to insert a third system C in which A is at rest. The paper's claim is that C is superfluous, and that treating a constant distance as if it varied is the source of the trouble. The argument Why the third system carries no information System C moves relative to B but not relative to A . Haye's point is that C can be slid along until it sits on A without any relativity between B and A being lost. Once it has been moved there it is plainly redundant: "System C is totally irrelevant to the phenomenon at A — that is to say, it doesn't have any physical meaning. System C also doesn't have any meaning to System B , because an Observer at B will see the phenomenon directly." The constant distance that is made to vary The technical heart of the paper follows.
