GC2026-SGG052 Laboratory studies of the effect of varying effective pressure on the permeability of oil￾saturated visean sandstones Evgeniy A. Gladkikh1 , Natalia N. Petrova1 1 Perm National Research Polytechnic University gladkih.ea@mail.ru Reservoir pressure typically declines during oil field development, leading to reduced well productivity [1]. This phenomenon is driven by the natural depletion of reservoir energy and geomechanical deformation. Reservoir deformation is characterized by the closure of fractures and pore throats under increasing effective pressure, resulting in decreased permeability. However, induced stress changes can be leveraged to stimulate the near￾wellbore zone. Various technologies utilize stress state modifications to generate microfractures [2]. While extensive research has focused on this process in carbonate rocks, terrigenous formations have received limited attention. This paper presents laboratory results examining the effect of effective pressure on the permeability of oil-saturated terrigenous rocks. Core samples were obtained from Visean-age deposits in the Perm Region. Permeability measurements were conducted using a core holder simulating in-situ confining pressure, pore pressure, and temperature. Effective pressure was varied by altering either the confining pressure (at constant pore pressure) or the pore pressure (at constant confining pressure).
