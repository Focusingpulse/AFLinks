A Discussion of the problems yet to be included (solved) and opportunities for research in the STOE. This is the final wrap-up session of the three-part series. Summary The third and last of John Hodge 's three-part series on the Scalar Theory of Everything, given as a recorded video with live discussion. Where the earlier parts applied the model to particular anomalies, this one is a survey of what remains unsolved — the open problems and the research opportunities. Ian J Cowan hosted, and for the first twelve minutes played the video with his own microphone muted, so the recording carries the slides in silence until Franklin Hu intervened; the group voted to resume from the mathematical section rather than start again. The model in outline Two entities: the plenum , a continuous medium with no particles, and the hod , a two-dimensional magnet with high plenum density on one face and low on the other. There is one force, the gradient of the plenum, and everything else emerges from it at different scales. Hodge's route to gravity runs through his own bench experiments. The divergence of the magnetic field is zero in Gauss's law — no monopoles — but his measurements of the repulsion between like poles show a slight asymmetry, and that asymmetry, he argues, is gravity. The weak flux side of a hod cannot supply flux lines from the other face, so those lines become attractive.
