GC2026-PERM030 Evidence for non-dipole sources in the Devonian Geomagnetic field from analysis of paleomagnetic vectors in rocks of the south-western incarnation of Siberian Platform Margarita E. Luzan1,2 , Dmitriy V. Metelkin1,2 1 Novosibirsk State University, Russia 2 Trofimuk Institute of Petroleum Geology and Geophysics SB RAS Novosibirsk, Russia m.luzan@g.nsu.ru The foundation of tectonic reconstructions based on paleomagnetic data relies on the hypothesis that Earth's magnetic field follows the geocentric axial dipole (GAD) model. However, Devonian paleomagnetic data [1, 2] fit this model poorly, showing significant scatter in paleomagnetic directions in coeval rocks and anomalously low paleointensities [3], suggesting non- GAD field geometry. Accordingly, this limits paleotectonic applications. The goal of this study is to evaluate possible solutions to this problem via paleomagnetic analysis of igneous rocks from the southwestern Siberian Platform margin. Early Devonian rifting affected much of southern Siberia, forming the Altai-Sayan rift system (ASRS) with three main basins: Minusa, Tuva and Delyuno-Yustyd [4]. Predominant magmatic products are bimodal mafic complexes (basalt flows, dolerite dikes and sills) and alkaline associations. Geochronological (U–Pb, Rb–Sr, K–Ar, Ar–Ar) [4, 5] and paleontological data [6] date ASRS magmatism to 405–364 Ma. Primary paleomagnetic signals in Early Devonian dolerite sills and basalt flows had been previously confirmed, but characteristic directions show a pseudo-chaotic distribution [8, 9].
