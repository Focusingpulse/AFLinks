The picture assembled quickly. Vesto Slipher measured large radial velocities for spiral nebulae between 1912 and 1917; Alexander Friedmann found expanding and contracting solutions of Einstein's field equations in 1922; Georges Lemaître connected the solutions to the observations in 1927 and proposed a "primeval atom" origin; and Edwin Hubble published the linear redshift–distance relation in 1929 (see Hubble Constant ). Extrapolated backwards, an expanding universe implies a hot dense beginning — the Big Bang — and with it a finite age, currently quoted as about 13.8 billion years. Four
