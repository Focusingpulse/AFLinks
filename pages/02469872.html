There was a rumor in the Plaza yesterday that it had been served there, but this could not be verified. In the Hotel Astor it was said corn from Virginia and huckleberries from Virginia were on the way and would be served in a day or two. It also was said that spring chickens, those of this spring, weighed at present not more than a pound and a half, and that the price, even to a hotel buying in large quantities, was sixty cents a pound. In the St. Regis a special dish of lobster is served, in which the chef, Emile Bailly, takes pride. He calls it "lobster à l'Americaine." To make this dish, cut the meat of a live lobster in slices, setting at one side the green vein or coral of ting at one side the green vein or coral of the lobster. In a shallow saucepan heat equal parts of olive oil and butter. To this add the lobster, seasoned, and when well colored drain of the fat sauce. Blaze the lobster with brandy, and then add chopped shallots with a flavor of garlic, white wine and tomatoes that have been peeled, chopped fine and partly cooked. Enrich with a light meat glaze and permit the whole to simmer about twenty minutes. Remove the pieces of lobster and place in a deep dish.
