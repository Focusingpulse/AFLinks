The Majority is Always Wrong - What the Minority Can Do Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker David de Hilster Date 5 February 2022 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording The Majority is Always Wrong - What the Minority Can Do was a live session presented by David de Hilster on 5 February 2022 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The majority is always wrong. Change comes from outside. But is the outside its own worst enemy and how can we do better? David de Hilster discusses what he has seen over the decades as some of the biggest problems those outsiders face and why we don't ever seem to make progress. There is a way forward. But do we have the will to do what is necessary? Summary David de Hilster took a business talk on innovation as the starting point for an examination of where the society's own thinking is conventional. The session was interrupted when the streams were suspended for a copyright violation caused by playing the video, and a good part of the audience had to rejoin. The video The talk, by a management speaker, argues that when people hit a wall they do more or less of the same thing rather than something different; that only about three per cent are inclined to do something different; that thinking is expensive, so brains return to automatic pilot and acquire tunnel vision; and that the box we think in is bounded by legal, technological, physical and model boundaries, and by what an industry calls its norms — "norm" being short for normal, so that doing what everyone does produces the results everyone gets. Its examples were London taxis, IKEA and Dell, and it closed with Marcus Aurelius on not siding with the majority.
