08/10/16 - Brain-zapping gadget that could make soldiers STRONGER Elite soldiers in US are trialing Halo neuroscience's headsets. Headset could help soldiers in the SAS and SBS gain extra strength. Device, called Halo Sport, uses pulses of energy to stimulate the brain's motor cortex, improving the brain's response to training. The consumer version of the headset, called Halo Sport, is claimed to be the first neurotechnology designed for athletes and it primes the brain to power a hard workout, in a similar way to how a pre-workout meal fuels muscles. The technology, dubbed neuropriming, involves using pulses of energy to stimulate the brain's motor cortex, improving the brain's response to training. The company said the headset increases the strength and coordination of signals sent from the brain to the muscles. 'When the brain sends more robust signals, motor unit recruitment � or the number of muscle fibres activated � increases,� it explained in a blog post. 'As motor unit recruitment increases, gains in strength and explosiveness - or the rate at which athletes can activate force - increase as well.' Signals usually become weaker when an athlete is tired, but by strengthening them it could help a runner keep their pace up for longer. The idea is that soldiers could wear the headset while training or before a mission, to given them similar advantages in skills that could mean the difference between life and death on the battlefield. The company, which was founded three years ago, claims that during training sessions, users have improved their leg strength by 12 per cent in just two weeks and athletes using the device, including the US Olympic skiing team, have developed �more precise, more co-ordinated and explosive movement.� The headset is available to buy globally for $649 (�493).
