In ex￾panding causal structures, the degrees of freedom can not all be localised in the past, and it is not meaningful to treat these degrees of freedom as initial conditions. As a result, the past no longer determines the future and determinism loses its meaning. This invalidates the argument of free will being incompatible with deterministic laws. 1 Introduction In his Nobel lecture Wigner draws our attention to the duality between laws of nature on the one hand and initial conditions on the other.[1] As Wigner remarks, since Newton's time, the former have become increasingly precise be￾yond anything reasonable; while we know virtually nothing about the latter.
