GTM2026-main022 Optimal design of multi-level control system Alexander Vasin1 , Artem Sivachenko1 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow, Russia vasin@cs.msu.su The present paper continues the analysis of the problem of the optimal organization of state inspections. It belongs to the stream of works on economic mechanisms design (see [1-3 ]). Our study considers inspection designs such that honest behavior of taxpayers and auditors turns out to be stable to deviations of coalitions. The purpose is to minimize the necessary costs for providing stability of honest behavior of taxpayers and auditors. The results are also of interest in context of corporate governance for large companies where employees can collude with clients. For a k-stage inspection, auditing strategy includes a probability of the first-stage audit depending on the tax report, a probability of the second-stage audit depending on the tax report and the report of the first-stage inspector, and so on. At each stage, a probability of the audit depends on the tax report and the results of the previous stages. If some tax evasion is revealed at some stage then the taxpayer and the inspectors audited him at the previous stages pay penalties proportional to the determined evasion. At the top stage all audits are controlled by the head of the inspection. They always reveal the true income of the taxpayer, but the cost of such audit is very high.
