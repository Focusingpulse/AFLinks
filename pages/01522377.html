Age of the Cosmos (ICR Technical Monograph, No. 9) Book Author Harold S Slusher Language English Subject Cosmology; the age of the universe and the solar system; young-earth creationism Published 1980 Publisher Institute for Creation Research, El Cajon, California Pages 76 ISBN 9780932766038 Age of the Cosmos is a 76-page monograph of 1980 by Harold S Slusher , number 9 in the Institute for Creation Research's technical monograph series. It argues that astronomical evidence points to a universe and a solar system thousands rather than billions of years old . It is one of five Slusher monographs catalogued on this wiki, and belongs with The Age of the Earth (ICR Technical Monograph, No. 7) (1978) and Origin of the Universe (ICR Technical Monograph, No. 8) (1980), which take the same approach to the Earth and to cosmogony respectively. The argument Slusher's method here is the one that runs through the series: rather than proposing a cosmology, he looks for processes that should have run to completion if the accepted ages were right, and argues that their persistence sets an upper limit on age. The clearest case is the short-period comets . A comet loses material on every pass close to the Sun, so its lifetime is limited to a few thousand orbits at most. Since short-period comets are still present, Slusher argues, either they have not been orbiting for billions of years or something must be replenishing them — and in 1980 no such reservoir had been observed. He treats planetary cooling rates the same way: bodies that should long since have cooled to equilibrium retain internal heat, which he takes as evidence that less time has passed than is supposed. Comet orbits, and decay processes generally, are handled on the same principle.
