Saturday Morning Science Chat Talk Type Live session Date 28 December 2019 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Saturday Morning Science Chat was a live session on 28 December 2019 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Saturday Morning Science Chat sponsored by the CNPS Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with James Keene , Bob Gray , Ian Cohen, Alistair Riddoch , Carl and Phil, and comments from Cornelis Verhey and viewers. It became the most direct confrontation between Hu and Keene the series has recorded, and it was conducted without rancour on either side. Keene's Physicist's Survival Guide Keene opened with the video he is planning: clicking through the physics literature at random and noting what it says. His complaint is not that the accounts are wrong so much as that they contradict each other. Elementary particles are defined as those with no smaller constituents — and then CERN papers openly track the breakdown products of the heavy quarks. Grand unification presumes a weak force which he does not regard as a force at all: on his account the weak interactions all rest on the unconditional bit operation, and calling it a force is like calling rust one. The speed of light is listed as a constant of vacuum, whereas his own work has it varying, and requires a minimum quantity of quanta present for light to be conducted at all. He also gave his reading of magnetic monopoles, which he says need not be a new kind of particle: on his model the L bits are magnetic monopoles, and the reason a divided bar magnet always yields a north and a south is that the division never gets down to the fundamental length constant. Hu against quarks Hu's objection was blunter and empirical. Quarks have never been isolated, no beam of them can be made, and the reason given — that separating them supplies enough energy to make another particle, so they can never be seen — is too convenient.
