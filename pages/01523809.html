Dishington Born 1919 Died January 15, 2016 (2016-01-15) (aged 96–97) Residence Pacific Palisades, CA, United States Nationality USA Known for The electron as a depletion in the aether; a single conservation law; the primacy of the potentials over E and B Scientific career Fields Physics, aerospace engineering Institutions TRW Roland H. Dishington (1919 – 15 January 2016) was an American physicist who spent much of his career in the aerospace industry at TRW , and who developed over several decades an independent programme in which matter, charge and energy are all distributions and distortions of a single aether . His work is unusually coherent for an independent programme: a small number of commitments, applied consistently across electromagnetism, particle structure and the conservation laws, and set out across four books and a series of papers. He argued the case in the technical language of classical field theory rather than by analogy, and his most-read paper is addressed directly to what he considered errors in the standard textbooks . The aether as the one substance Dishington's starting point is that space and matter are not two things. In The Extended Electron he describes matter and space as disproportionate distributions of the aether — the same substance, differently concentrated — and in Energy and Charge he accounts for energy and charge as distortions of that substance. His stated method is worth recording, because it explains the shape of everything he wrote: If there is no picture to illustrate a theory, constructing one and backing it up with a strong, simple-as-possible mathematical representation is the way to get to new technologies in the shortest time. — Roland H. Dishington, The Extended Electron That is the demand for intelligibility that runs through most of the work catalogued on this wiki — a theory should admit of a picture, and the mathematics should represent the picture rather than replace it.
