kimo2026-main078 Multilateralism in the Global South Rajan Kumar rajan75jnu@gmail.com Multilateralism refers to the coordination of policies among three or more states based on specific rules and principles. Traditional theories of multilateralism, developed in the Western context, underlined the significance of the liberal international order. They lack a cross-cultural understanding of multilateralism. The organisations of the Global South, such as the NAM, BRICS and the SCO, do not fit into their theoretical framework. Therefore, there is a need to redefine multilateralism, considering specific norms, values, economic interactions, and institutions of the Global South. Further, in terms of practice, the Global South's common demand is to reform multilateral institutions such as the UN, the World Bank and the IMF to accommodate their economic, developmental and environmental concerns. The institutions of the Global South, pursue a three-pronged strategy: they cooperate with the existing institutions, such as the UN, the G-20, the IMF and the World Bank; they raise the voice of the Global South for reforming the existing institutions and making them more representative; and they also engage in creating alternative institutions such as the BRICS. The 'Global South' has become central to India's contemporary strategy.
