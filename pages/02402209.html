Melentiev of the Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences max28k@gmail.com the rapidly evolving research landscape of the 21st century, interdisciplinary approaches are increasingly essential for addressing complex scientific and societal challenges. Modern optimization models of large-scale energy systems - such as those implemented in frameworks like PyPSA [1] - have achieved remarkable technical sophistication. However, their outputs often remain opaque to decision-makers, especially in planning contexts marked by deep uncertainty. Current practices for assessing how modelling assumptions influence results are frequently limited to local sensitivity checks or correlation - based analyses, falling short of providing reproducible, end-to-end explanations of which inputs drive variability in key performance indicators and what effects their modification may entail. Aligned with the conference theme "Science Present and Future: Research Landscape in the 21st century", this work proposes an integrated, forward-looking methodology that bridges computational experimentation, global sensitivity analysis [2], and causal effect estimation to enhance the interpretability and decision-support capacity of complex energy models.
