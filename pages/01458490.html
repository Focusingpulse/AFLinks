Coulomb explosion

· archived # 1,458,490
A Coulomb explosion is a mechanism for coupling electronic excitation energy from intense electromagnetic fields into atomic motion . The Coulombic repulsion of particles having the same electric charge can break the bonds that hold solids together. When done with a narrow laser beam, a small amount of solid explodes into a plasma of ionized atomic particles. With their low mass , outer valence electrons responsible for chemical bonding are easily stripped from atoms, leaving them positively charged. Coulomb explosions for industrial machining are made with brief (picosecond or high femtoseconds) laser pulses.
