My Experiments with hydrogen/oxygen gas by Geoff Egel A word of warning If you fill a closed container with an explosive gas mixture, as an example one (1) part oxygen and two (2) parts hydrogen you will have set the stage for accidental explosion. Hydrogen and oxygen can and will explode with violent effect. To know of this, and to teach this, and not carefully warn all parties is to ignore your place as scientific ethical investigator. Hello Geoff, I have read your experiments with much fascination. I see you are very concerned with safety. That is why I am writing this mail: to warn against the use of PVC in these experiments. PVC has the nasty habit of splintering into very sharp pieces when it explodes. That is why the use of PVC for air pressure systems is prohibited. The assembly of PVC is with -special- glue. This is not always leakproof, as you must have noticed. PEHD or HDPE (high density polyethylene) is a very good alternative as is PP (Polypropylene): though at overpressure these will will also burst, they will stay in one piece (no splintering), and they can be welded quite simply, thus forming a homogenous construction after assembly. Both are very resistant to chemicals (resistance to hydrogen has to be checked). Another possibility is ABS, which is used for air pressure systems. It has very similar bursting properties as HDPE. The main advantage over PEHD is that the assembly procedure is very similar to glueing, but the glue is simply ABS made liquid with a solvent. The procedure is called cold welding. So one doesnot need welding equipment nor welding skills. After curing, the assembly is perfectly homogenous. It is less resistant to chemicals (resistance to hydrogen has to be checked). Although not as common as PVC which can be bought on every street corner, still most fittings in HDPE, in PP and in ABS can be bought off the shelf. One manufacturer of ABS and PP tube and fittings is called Durapipe hope this helps for safety greetings frank Hygic
