Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Institute of Earthquake Prediction Theory and Mathematical Geophysics, Russian Academy of Sciences 3 Institute of Marine Geology and Geophysics of the Far Eastern Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences liper@ifz.ru Over the past decades, numerous studies have been published on ionospheric plasma disturbances associated with seismic activity. In studying lithospheric-ionospheric links, most studies have focused on the precursors of strong shallow earthquakes, which are considered mostly destructive. Seismo-ionospheric effects have typically been interpreted as the occurrence of regions of increased or decreased ionization density, more often observed in the temporal and spatial vicinity of earthquakes occurrence than in the background. According to literature, the manifestation time of seismo-ionospheric effects typically ranges from several days before and several days after earthquakes; effects in the F-region were observed for M6+ earthquakes. The dependence of the effect on the time of day using TEC data was demonstrated by use of several examples of strong earthquakes in Greece and Kamchatka [1; 2; 3], where it was shown that an increase in TEC several days before an earthquake is observed predominantly at night.
