Overview Robert de Hilster applies a Le Sage-type pushing-gravity model to Quirino Majorana's gravitational-shielding experiments of 1918–1922, arguing that the model reproduces Majorana's measurements where Newtonian gravity cannot. Majorana placed a 1.274 kg lead ball on a balance and surrounded it with a large mass — a 104 kg mercury cylinder in the first experiment, a 9603 kg lead cube in the second. In both the ball rose, which Majorana interpreted as a loss of mass caused by the screening mass absorbing gravity. Henry Norris Russell objected that if mass were genuinely reduced, tides would vary widely and planetary orbits would show large deflections, neither of which is observed; he accepted that the experiment was well done but held that it needed a different explanation. De Hilster's first move is to separate the two claims.
