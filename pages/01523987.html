Title Systems in Relative Motion Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Autodynamics , velocity addition, kinematics, dynamics, Energy conservation Published Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Website https://www.autodynamics.org/systems-in-releative-motion/ "Systems in Relative Motion" is a paper by Ricardo L Carezani defending the Autodynamics velocity-sum equation against the objection that it gives visibly wrong answers at everyday speeds. It is written as a running exchange with a physicist who raised that objection, and it is the fullest statement of Carezani's distinction between kinematics and dynamics. Overview The Autodynamics velocity sum is β s u m = 1 − ( 1 − β 1 2 ) ( 1 − β 2 2 ) … Carezani's correspondent points out that for two velocities of 1 km/h this returns 1.41 km/h rather than 2 km/h, and concludes that Autodynamics is therefore wrong. Carezani concedes the arithmetic without reservation — "You are right. There is no mistake in your calculation" — and answers that the two calculations are about different things. The argument Kinematics against dynamics The distinction Carezani draws is between composing velocities without expending energy (kinematics) and composing them by expending it (dynamics). Classical mechanics gives 2 km/h and special relativity 1.9999 km/h because both are answering a kinematic question; Autodynamics gives 1.41 km/h because it is answering a dynamic one, in which the second increment of velocity has to be paid for out of the moving body's own mass. His correspondent's rejoinder is the natural one: "in the universe we happen to find ourselves in, if I am traveling at 1 km/hour, and I see someone pass me at 1 km/hour, then a third person on the ground will measure his speed at approximately 2 km/hour … Hence, at least in this case, AD is wrong."
