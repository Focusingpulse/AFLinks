Title Light Speed and the FTL Cesium Experiment Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Speed of Light , superluminal, Autodynamics , caesium, Photon , anomalous dispersion Published 1996 Website https://www.autodynamics.org/light-speed/ "Light Speed" and "FTL Cesium Experiment" are two short companion pieces at autodynamics.org in which Ricardo L Carezani sets out why Autodynamics regards faster-than-light travel as possible in principle, and how he reads the caesium anomalous-dispersion experiment reported in 2000. Overview Carezani's position has two parts. The theoretical part is that Autodynamics contains no barrier at c : having no frames in relative motion, "only 'phenomenon' and 'observer'", it can accommodate superluminal velocities without the paradoxes that trouble special relativity . The practical part is a proposed route: if photons at light speed could be made to combine into a new particle, that particle's decay should yield a "super-photon" travelling faster than light. The argument Why the barrier is not a barrier "Superluminic velocity is not problematic for Autodynamics. The theory has no frames in relative motion: only 'phenomenon' and 'observer.' To shift from one observer to another, the velocity transformation equation is used (velocity sum) that maintains energy and momentum conservation." The argument that c is a limit only because c was chosen as the carrier speed is developed separately in Limit Velocity in Autodynamics . Carezani also notes an internal tension in the orthodox account that he thinks favours him: photons travel at c because their rest mass is zero, yet neutrinos are widely assigned both a small mass and light speed. The same point is made in The Neutrino Spectrum .
