A Brief Look at Atomism Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Roger J Anderton Date 11 January 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording A Brief Look at Atomism was a live session presented by Roger J Anderton on 11 January 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The three theories of Atomism is not taught to physics students, but modern physics is based on that idea of physical reality being made out of particles; and the development of that idea has a long and complicated history. Summary Modern physics rests on atomism — the doctrine that physical reality is made of particles — and Roger J Anderton 's complaint is that a society calling itself natural philosophy has barely discussed it, and that physics students are never taught where it came from. The talk was built around Lancelot Law Whyte 's Essay on Atomism: From Democritus to 1960 , and Anderton noted that Whyte worked on Einstein's unified field theory, met Einstein, and — unlike Einstein — cited his sources. Three atomisms Whyte's central claim, which gave the talk its structure, is that there have been only three distinct and widely successful conceptions of atomism. The Democritean and Newtonian hard atom. Minute, hard, permanent bits of matter with definite size and shape, occupying parts of space and moving through the empty spaces between; occupied and empty space sharply distinguished. Familiar but mathematically awkward, since it involves a discontinuity at the particle's surface, and inescapably tied to the difficulty that matter cannot act where it is not — a problem Newton felt, holding that genuine physical action must be by contact while seeing no way to make that yield his law of gravitation. Boscovich 's point centres (1758). The units are not extended pieces of matter but persisting physical points, evident only as centres of interaction; only their spatial relations enter physics, and every pair is in interaction.
