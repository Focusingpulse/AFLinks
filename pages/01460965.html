Wave fields are bounded by planes of zero curvature, which act as mirrors to reverse all radiation which reaches out to these wave-field boundaries. An example of such a plane of zero curvature is the equator of a bar magnet. Iron filings reaching out from either pole will curve gradually in the ever-changing pressure gradients which surround the poles. Science mistakenly calls these curved lines magnetic lines of force.
