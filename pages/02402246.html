MolPhot2026-main004 A combined theoretical and experimental progress on spectroscopy applications and molecular photonics at Tomsk State University Georgy V. Mayer1 , Olga N. Tchaikovskaya1 , Irina V. Sokolova1 , Olga K. Bazyl1 , Vladimir A. Pomogaev1 , Pavel V. Avramov1 , Elena N. Bocharnikova1 , Nadezhda P. Bezlepkina1 , Julia A. Melchakova1 1 National Research Tomsk State University, Tomsk, Russia bocharnikova.2010@mail.ru Research in the field of spectroscopy and molecular photonics in Tomsk was initiated by N.A. Prilezhaeva, a student of A.N. Terenin, who opened the first spectroscopy laboratory in Siberia in 1935. Since the late 1940s, N.A. Prilezhaeva and V.I. Danilova began developing studies of the spectral-luminescent properties of complex molecules of organic compounds, and since the 1950s, quantum-chemical approaches have been applied. Since the late 1960s, their graduate student V.G. Plotnikov developed studies of nonradiative conversion processes in organic molecules with a focus on establishing patterns of the relationship between spectral-luminescent properties and the nature and relative arrangement of electron states. By the end of the 1980s, the laboratory of V.I. Danilova developed a software package that enabled the implementation of quantum-chemical methods for calculating the rate constants of a full range of photophysical process parameters: radiative decay, internal conversion, and intersystem crossing (note that spin-orbit interaction is calculated using multicenter integrals).
