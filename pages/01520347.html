How Quantum Mechanics Fails - Threshold Model Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Eric S Reiter Date 8 May 2021 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording How Quantum Mechanics Fails - Threshold Model was a live session presented by Eric S Reiter on 8 May 2021 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Eric Reiter, an experimentalist, will be talking about how mainstream's model of quantum mechanics fails and how it is fixed by the "Threshold Model". Summary Eric Reiter on how quantum mechanics fails, and on the threshold model he offers instead — his own beam-split coincidence experiments with gamma rays being the evidence. David de Hilster hosted, and the first fifteen minutes went on getting Reiter's slides onto the stream, which he apologised for at length. The problem Reiter began by collecting the names: quantum mechanics, entanglement, superposition, the measurement problem, spooky action at a distance, the ghost wave, Schrödinger's cat, non-locality. They are, he said, all the same problem. He objected to the double slit as the standard illustration. What matters is not that the pattern builds up from discrete points but that a detection in one place excludes a detection in another. The cleaner arrangement is a beam splitter with two detectors and a coincidence circuit: the source is made weak enough to be called one-at-a-time, and the clicks appear on one side or the other. Recombine the beams and an interference pattern appears, which only waves make. The models are mutually exclusive — waves go everywhere, particles hold together — and a century of trying has produced parallel universes, simulation hypotheses and what he called a list of acts of desperation. He was careful about terminology: a "quantum mechanical particle" is not a particle, and using the word without the qualifier invites confusion.
