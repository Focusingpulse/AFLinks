Don Smith Residence Spring, TX, United States Nationality American Known for Tesla-inspired "resonance energy" over-unity devices Scientific career Fields Free-energy research Don Smith (Don L. Smith) was an American inventor associated with the free-energy movement, known for a large series of Tesla-inspired devices that he claimed could draw usable electrical power from what he called "the ambient background." His designs are widely circulated in the free-energy community, though his over-unity claims are not accepted by mainstream science. Resonance energy devices Smith's devices were based on the ideas of Nikola Tesla , and in particular on Tesla's notion of tapping a "radiant" or environmental energy. He said that reading The Inventions, Researches and Writings of Nikola Tesla had led him to reconstruct and then extend Tesla's apparatus, and over the following years he built what supporters count as dozens of different devices. His designs use resonant induction : a low-voltage source drives a high-voltage stage (typically a neon-sign transformer) feeding tuned coils and capacitors, arranged so the circuit is driven at resonance. Smith claimed that such an arrangement delivers far more energy than it consumes — outputs he described as ranging from kilowatts to megawatts — with the surplus drawn from the surrounding environment rather than from the input battery. He set out his methods in the book Resonance Energy Methods and in a series of public lectures and demonstrations, including a widely circulated 2006 presentation. A claim of this kind — that a device delivers more energy than is supplied to it — describes an over-unity or perpetual-motion machine, which would violate the conservation of energy. No such device by Smith or others has been independently verified under controlled conditions, and this work is not accepted by mainstream physics.
