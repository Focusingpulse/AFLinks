Title Doppler Shifted "In" and "Out" Longitudinal Matter Waves as the Carriers of Particle Relativistic Momentum and Energy Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Dan Wagner Keywords Longitudinal matter wave, sound wave model, Doppler effect, net forward momentum, kinetic energy, rest energy, preferred reference frame, Lorentz-Poincaré viewpoint, gravity Published 2020 Journal Applied Physics Research Volume 12 Number 3 No. of pages 17 Pages 11-27 Read the full paper here ( Applied Physics Research 12 (3), 2020, 11–27; doi:10.5539/apr.v12n3p11) Overview This is Dan Wagner 's attempt to build a mechanical model of a quantum particle out of longitudinal waves, and to recover the relativistic expressions for momentum and kinetic energy from it without invoking relativity as a postulate. The proposal is that a particle is nothing but a standing wave — more exactly, that oppositely directed components of a harmonically oscillating "pseudo standing wave" pass through the particle's centre, and that these components, which he calls longitudinal matter waves , are what carry the particle's momentum and energy. The particle has no separate existence behind the waves. As he puts it, "because a quantum particle is considered to be nothing more than the sum of 'in' and 'out' matter waves focused through its center, whatever happens to these matter waves determines the future location of that center." The engine of the calculation is the Doppler effect . When the centre of this wave system moves relative to the medium, the outgoing and incoming components are compressed ahead and stretched behind, exactly as a moving sound source shifts the wavelengths it emits. Since wave momentum depends on wavelength, the directional imbalance produced by that shift yields a net forward momentum that grows faster than the classical mv as the speed rises — which is what a relativistic momentum formula has to do.
