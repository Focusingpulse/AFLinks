The receiver and transmitter must be "tuned" to the same key — to borrow a musical phrase and simile. That is, if the receiver is capable of recording vibrations of not more than 400,000 a second, while the transmitter is sending out 500,000, it is inoperative. Both must be adjusted to the higher or lower number. The musical likeness to wireless telegraphy is shown in sympathetic vibrations. If a certain note is sounded on a piano, or a violin, or by the human voice, any object in the room which gives that note when struck will vibrate in response, and will echo the sound. It is said that men of strong voices have broken glass dishes by singing their note so long that the extreme vibration cracked them. But it also appears that an output of electrical energy is attended by what, to follow the simile, are called harmonics or overtones, usually the ascending notes of the common chord, such as are sounded by the bugle as the air pressure is increased. These overtones are not often heard, even by watchful ears, but their electrical analogues are likely to make trouble, because they are in regular sequence, say of hundreds or thousands, and a receiver calculated for 500,000 vibration
