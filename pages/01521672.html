Title The Field Approach to Thermodynamics Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Jaroslav G Klyushin , Yegor V Pesterev Keywords Thermodynamics, Field, Uncertainty Principle, quantum mechanics Published 2011 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 8 No. of pages 10 Pages 313-322 Abstract The concept of thermodynamic fields is introduced, and a mathematical apparatus for its description is proposed. Such an approach enables us to shed new light on a lot of problems; for instance, on the second principle of thermodynamics, Planck's formula, and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle. It is an old idea that Brownian motion is essential for description an solution of the Quantum Mechanics problems. Nonstohastic description Brownian motion is also proposed using tensor mathematics. Overview Presented at the 18th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference at College Park in July 2011, this paper by Jaroslav Klyushin and his postgraduate co-author Yegor Pesterev, both of the St Petersburg University of Civil Aviation, proposes to rebuild thermodynamics on the model of electrodynamics. The diagnosis is that electrodynamics prospered because it found a charge and a current, giving the field concept a physical meaning, while thermodynamics never identified a thermodynamic charge and settled instead for ensembles of particles described statistically. Two remedies are offered. The first is a "thermodynamic charge": the particle's spin, with the thermodynamic impulse — mass times a new kind of velocity — set equal to Planck's constant ħ. The second is a deterministic replacement for the statistical description of Brownian motion, built by adding to ordinary space X a second space Y whose axes are the coordinate planes of the first. Motion in Y has a velocity of dimension m² s⁻¹, which the authors call surfalinear, and which they identify as the reason a Brownian particle's elapsed time goes as the square of its displacement rather than as the displacement.
