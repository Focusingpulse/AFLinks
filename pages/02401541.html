GC2026-PERM028 Potential of magnetic force microscopy for research in rock magnetism Alexey Tselebrovskiy1 , Ruslan Rytov2 , Valeriy Maksimochkin1 , Roman Grachev1 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow, Russia 2 Pushkov Institute of Terrestrial Magnetism, Ionosphere and Radiowave Propagation of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow, Russia tselebr@gmail.com Magnetic force microscopy (MFM) is a scanning probe technique that makes it possible to visualize magnetic fields at the nanometer level with a resolution of up to 10-50 nm. In rock magnetism, MFM opens new possibilities for analyzing the microstructures of magnetic minerals, which is critical for understanding the mechanisms of magnetization and evaluating the stability of the magnetization of individual particles. Traditional methods (VSM, SQUID) provide average data, while MFM provides localization of domain structures. MFM is superior to electron (LTEM) and magneto-optical (MOKE) microscopy in resolution or non-invasiveness but is sensitive to topography and requires calibration of the cantilever (CoCr coating) to obtain quantitative estimates. MFM works by measuring the phase shift proportional to the gradient of the vertical component of the magnetic field of the sample. The possibility of applying planar magnetic fields makes it possible to study not only the remanent, but also the inductive magnetization.
