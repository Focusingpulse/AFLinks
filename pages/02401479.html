GC2026-STP027 The average energy of electron and nitric oxide concentration during average aurora Zhanna V. Dashkevich1 , Vladimir E. Ivanov1 1 Polar Geophysical Institute, Russia zhanna@pgia.ru The analysis of variations in the hmax - altitude of the 427.8 nm intensity maximum as a function of electron flux parameters have done using a timedependent auroral ionospheric model. It have found that hmax depends weakly on the shape of the energy spectrum and the pitch-angle distribution of the electrons and is determined primarily by the Eev - average energy of the precipitating electron flux. The dependence of the average energy Eev on the altitude of 427.8 nm intensity maximum was modeled. Based on this obtained dependence and hmax experimental data, a statistically distribution of the average energy of auroral electrons causing auroras in the midnight sector of the polar oval was constructed. The maximum of the Eev distribution is localized in the region from 3 to 4.5 keV. A modeling of the most probable values of nitric oxide concentrations during polar auroras caused by electron fluxes with average energies of 3÷4.5 keV was carried out. It was found that, in order to obtain agreement between the calculated dependence of ratio I(λ557.7)/I(λ427.8) on I(λ427.8) and experimental data of the ratio, nitric oxide concentrations should lie in the range from 4×107 to 2×108 cm-3. Moreover, there is a dependence of [NO] on the index of geomagnetic activity Ap. The nitric oxide concentration increases in the obtained range with an increase in the index Ap from 5 to 40.
