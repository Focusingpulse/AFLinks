Wagner's objection is stated as a matter of physical intuition rather than of formalism. Quantum theory's statistical predictions work, but he supposes there must be a causal story underneath them, and offers the Galton board as his model of what such a story looks like: thousands of beads falling through interleaved pegs produce a Gaussian distribution, and nobody concludes from that curve that the beads were in many places at once. Applied to a polarized electron beam, his position is that "polarized" should mean the spin vectors point into the same hemisphere, not that they all share one identical projection. From that assumption he derives a specific, checkable number . If the spin vectors are distributed over the upper hemisphere rather than fixed at the quantum-mechanical angle, the total angular momentum a polarized beam can deliver along the field direction is about 13.4 per cent less than quantum theory predicts.
