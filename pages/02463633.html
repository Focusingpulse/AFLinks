Predicting the Critical Mass Density and Galactic Structure for Black Hole Formation in the Aether Physics Model Abstract The Aether Physics Model (APM) offers a novel interpretation of black holes as regions where Aether units cease to exist due to extreme space density gradients. This paper adapts the APM's circular deflection angle equation to predict the critical mass density and galactic structure required to trigger a black hole at the center of a galaxy. We use the Hernquist profile to model the mass distribution of the Milky Way bulge and incorporate an exponential gradient to describe the space density. Numerical methods are employed to solve the resulting equations. The calculated length scale provides insights into the critical conditions for black hole formation and suggests further refinements to improve the model's accuracy. 1. Introduction The Aether Physics Model (APM) is a theoretical framework that proposes a quantized structure of space composed of discrete Aether units. In the APM, black holes are described as regions where Aether units cease to exist due to extreme space density gradients. This paper aims to predict the critical mass density and galactic structure required to form a black hole at the center of a galaxy, using the Milky Way as a test case. 2. Theoretical Background The APM suggests that what General Relativity interprets as "spacetime curvature" is better understood as a space density gradient. This gradient is caused by the presence of mass and energy, affecting the Aether units and creating a change in the density of space. The APM states that a black hole forms when the curl of space reaches 1 radian. 3.
