Title Gravity Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Pharis E Williams Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 7 Pages 661-667 Abstract This paper presents a new theory of gravity with the basic premises and fundamental laws of the theory. Then the paper presents some immediate results of the laws and shows how these results lead to an Arrow of Time that has eluded physical theories to date. Isentropic states are then showed to require gauge potentials with specific functional dependences upon space, time and mass. The character of these dependences are then used to compare theoretical predictions with existing experimental data and to discuss new experiments that may be conducted. Overview Williams's Dynamic Theory attempts something more ambitious than a modified force law: to derive the force laws rather than postulate them, from two axioms of thermodynamic type. The first law is conservation of energy in Pfaffian form, đ E = d U + F i d x i . The second is a denial of perpetual motion, stated as: surrounding any state point there exist neighbouring points to which the system cannot go reversibly. Williams then invokes the standard consequence — that the second law guarantees an integrating factor for the first — which yields an entropy-like state function and, he argues, an absolute velocity and hence a definition of time. Working with entropy and the space variables as independent, and requiring the resulting quadratic form to be positive definite, he obtains stability conditions from which a "physical time" τ emerges as a dependent variable. A common multiplicative factor in the expression for dτ he identifies, following Weyl, as a gauge function setting the distance measure of the manifold. The central structural move is dimensional. Counting one variable per independent force plus one more, and admitting a fourth thermodynamic force alongside the three spatial ones, gives five independent variables — space, time and mass.
