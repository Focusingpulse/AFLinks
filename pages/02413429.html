Giovanni Caselli 1815-1891 was an Italian physicist, inventor and priest. He was the inventor of the Pantelegraph of Fax's prerun in 1856. In 1858 the improved version of Caselli was demonstrated by French physicist Alexandre-Edmond Becquerel at the Paris Academy of Sciences. Napoleon (not his big scare) saw a display of Caselli's Pantelegraph in 1860 and placed an order for the service on the French National Telegraph network that started next year. Caselli had access to not only French telegraph lines for his televised fax engine technology, but Napoleon's finances. A test between Paris and Amienne successfully with the signature of composer Gioacchino Rossini, as the image was sent and received, within 140 kilometers. Another test was then taken between Paris and Marseille, 800 km away. Which was also successful. French legislation was adopted in 1864 to be officially accepted. The following year in 1865, businesses started with Paris to Lyon and expanded to Marseille in 1867. The pantelegraph (Italian: pantelegrafo; French: pantélégraphe) was an early form of facsimile machine transmitting over normal telegraph lines developed by Giovanni Caselli, used commercially in the 1860s, that was the first such device to enter practical service. It could transmit handwriting, signatures, or drawings within an area of up to 150 × 100 mm. Wikipedia, Pantelegraph ( click to enlarge )
