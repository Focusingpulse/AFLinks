Now, we shall explore other important concepts. We assume that all binary operations are commutative and associative in this paper. Let ∆ B {β0, β1, . . . , βn} be a nonempty finite set of binary operations, σ : ∆ → Ú a map given by βi ↦→ 2. Then the ordered pair A B ⟨∆, σ⟩ is an algebraic language. Suppose that A is an algebra of the language A. See notation 2.1 for more details. Let Φ be a nonempty subset of ∆, and S a nonempty subset of A. If an ideal M with respect to Φ is the minimal ideal such that S ⊆ M, then the ideal M is said to be generated by the subset S, see definition 3.1 for more details. Let (S) Φ denote the ideal M. For all subset S ⊆ M∁ , if the algebra A is local[4] and M is the maximal ideal, then we have (S) Φ = A, see proposition 3.1 for the details. In definition 3.2, we construct a prime ideal P with respect to a nonempty subset Φ ⊆ ∆. Let P and Q be prime ideals with respect to Φ and Ψ, respectively. We have that Φ∪Ψ = ∆, Φ∩Ψ , ∅, and P∪Q , A implies that P∪Q is a prime ideal with respect to Φ ∩ Ψ, see proposition 3.3 for more details.
