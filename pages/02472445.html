Hardly a month passes in which we escape hearing some new and illusory hope, held out to the most unfortunate sufferers in the community. It is of the unfortunates who are already attacked and who are hoping against hope for cure that my voice is raised. A stricken friend once said to me with tears in his eyes: "It's too bad that I must die with this disease, because the doctors will be curing it five years hence." That was ten years ago, and science couldn't save him to-day. What answer could be made to so pathetic a remark? To tell the invalid that his case was incurable would have been cruelty. To agree with him that science would be able to save unfortunates who came after him would not mitigate the despair in his heart. The papers of this morning contain an announcement that the bacilli of consumption can be destroyed in the human system by the application of one million volts of electricity. Every reader will sincerely hope that this quantity of the electric fluid can be passed through the human frame with safety.
