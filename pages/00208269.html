On a modification of the quantum eraser experiment, the results of which may disprove the absoluteness of measurement in quantum mechanics. R.K. Salimov 1 , 1 Ufa University of Science and Technology, 450076, Ufa, Russia e-mail: salimovrk@bashedu.ru Abstract The article explores a hypothesis concerning nonlocality, which should be described by Lorentz-invariant equations with infinite-order derivatives. It is shown that, despite formal Lorentz invariance, such a description”while preserving causality”necessarily leads to a local violation of the principle of relativity. These violations are confined within the radius of non￾locality associated with each source of nonlocal phenomena. Consequently, while no single preferred reference frame exists, and on average the principle of relativity holds, local devia￾tions are predicted.This hypothesis offers an alternative interpretation of the results from the delayed-choice quantum eraser experiment. To test its validity, a modified experimental setup for the quantum eraser is proposed. If the hypothesis is correct, the experimental outcomes will deviate from the predictions of standard quantum mechanics. Keywords:non-locality, Quantum Eraser Experiment. The quantum eraser double-slit experiment is a variation of the Young’s experiment, demonstrating how the observation of a particle’s path information destroys the interference pattern, and its ”erasure” restores it. The experiment demonstrates wave-particle duality and quantum entanglement. There is also a ”delayed-choice” variant, where the choice to erase the information or not is made after the photon has already passed through the slits, with the interference pattern appearing or disappearing accordingly. This experiment is a variation of the classic double-slit experiment that shows that the decision for a particle to behave as a wave or as a particle can be delayed and even reversed after it has already passed through the slits.
