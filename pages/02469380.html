With the ambitious Intention of raising $1,000,000 in twelve days, the Allied Bazaar opens to-day in Grand Central Palace, and on the eve of its opening gives every promise of reaching, perhaps exceeding the mark set. Advance sale of tickets yesterday reached the 500,000 mark — the greatest advance sale for any event ever held in New York, it is asserted Goods valued at $600,000 have been donated and several new attractions have been announced. Among them is the Booth of the Nine Allies, heralded as the bazaar's crowning feature. The lighting of it is to be pretentious. Nikola Tesla, the inventor, has this matter in charge. Edna May, Marie Tempest, Elsie Janis and Jane Cowl will be the Reception Committee the first evening. Lady Colebrook, in the garb in which she worked in a munitions plant in England, will turrf out shells at a lathe similar to those used abroad. Margaret Mayo's booth will have an interesting exhibit in the pass issued by the Germans to Irvin Cobb in Brussels three days after the fall of Louvain. The booth has been painted to represent a ruined cathedral "somewhere in France." Warslav Nijinsky, the Russian dancer, has been added to the list of artists who will appear at the bazaar. He will appear on Russian day, Friday next. The opening entertainment will be on Italian
