507 Journal of Scientifi c Exploration, Vol. 23, No. 4, pp. 507–516, 2009 0892-3310/09 LETTERS TO THE EDITOR A Puzzling Aspect of the “Global Consciousness Project” The experiment reported by Bancel and Nelson (Bancel & Nelson, 2008; Nelson, 2001) utilizes a global network of binary random number generators (RNGs). Each RNG produces 200 random bits during each 1-second time interval. These 200 bits are added and the deviation from average is expressed by a Z-score. Thus each second of an experimental period (surrounding some major world event) provides an individual Z-score from each RNG. For their primary evaluation the authors add the Z-scores from all RNGs and thus obtain (after normalizing the sum) one “composite Z-score” for each second. Then they add the squares of these composite Z-scores over all seconds of all experimental periods and find that the sum is significantly larger than expected by chance. Among many additional statistical evaluations only one is relevant for the following: If, rather than first combining the Z-scores of the individual RNGs to one composite Z-score, one simply squares the Z-scores of the individual RNGs and sums these values over all RNGs and all test seconds, this sum is not significantly large, i.e., the individual RNGs seem to behave quite normal. The numbers of participating RNGs varied from 3 to over 65. The RNGs were well built and tested, but not all identically designed. And one may assume that the design details of the RNGs are irrelevant.
