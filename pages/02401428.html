GC2026-SGG017 DAS-based Virtual Seismometers: Pros and Cons Valentin V. Gravirov1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences vvg@ifz.ru Distributed Acoustic Sensing technology, known by its acronym DAS (Distributed Acoustic Sensing), is an innovative approach that significantly enhances the capabilities of monitoring and analyzing seismic phenomena. The key aspect of this technology is its ability to transform a standard fiber-optic cable into an extensive network of seismometers, enabling real-time recording of seismic vibrations [1]. The DAS system operates based on the principle of backscatter light, where a laser pulse, when passing through a fiber-optic cable, is partially reflected at locations where there is any impact, such as movement, on the cable. These interactions cause changes in the amplitude and phase of the reflected light, which are then analyzed to extract information about seismic events [2]. This approach provides high sensitivity and allows for the detection of even minimal vibrations, making DAS a particularly valuable tool in geophysical research. Advantages: • High spatial resolution. Virtual seismometers (channels) can have a spatial resolution of the order of several meters, and the length of the active optical fiber, which is a sensitive element, can reach tens of kilometers.
