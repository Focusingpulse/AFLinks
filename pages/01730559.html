Herbalert to the Rescue Toggle navigation Home Article Index Search Contact Online Store Herbalert to the Rescue UK herbal medicines market stands at &pound;240 million per year as an estimated 15 million people choose herbal remedies for everyday ailments. Figures for the global market top $60 billion per year and rising. But is demand for TM/CAM resulting in over-harvesting of the worlds most effective herbal medicines? Sam Burcher reports. The World Health Organisation estimates that 75-80% of the worlds population use plant medicines either in part or entirely for health care [1]. For many, plant medicines are a necessity, as costly pharmaceutical drugs are unaffordable; and for others, the desire to seek natural alternatives with few side effects is preferable to using conventional drugs. This dichotomy has led to important medicinal plants such as Goldenseal ( Hydrastis canadensis ), traditionally used for a range of immune deficiency disorders becoming the fifth most endangered species in the world. In Europe over 200 Medicinal and Aromatic Plants (MAPS) are on the endangered list [2]. Despite attempts by a number of conservation groups such as World Wildlife Fund, The World Conservation Union and Convention on Trade Endangered Species (CTES), further support is desperately needed to save plant species from becoming extinct [3]. David Bellamy, professor and celebrity naturalist, launched the Herbalert campaign in February 2003 for the Natural Medicines Society [See Box 1] at Neals Yard in London [4]. "I am delighted to help spearhead a campaign to put herbal medicine back where it deserves to be an important part of mainstream healing practice in the 21 st Century," Bellamy said. Clearly enthusiastic about the subject, he made an entertaining and impassioned plea to give herbal medicine its due accord.
