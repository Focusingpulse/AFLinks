Scalar Theory of Everything - With John Hodge. The Special Theory of Relativity (STOE) applies where gravitation is insignificant. There are many observations that remain poorly explained by the standard models of either the big of cosmology or the small of Quantum mechanics. Each of the STOE axioms has been used in the development of models of observations in the big and the small. The strength of the Scalar Theory Of Everything (STOE) is its ability to describe an extremely wide range of observations and to predict observations. Summary John Hodge presented his Scalar Theory of Everything, in a recorded talk played by David de Hilster , with a short conversation before and after — interrupted at the start when Hodge's connection froze. How he came to it Hodge took a physics degree at Rensselaer and a master's at Georgia Tech, worked in Texas Instruments' research laboratories, owned an electronics company and sold it in 1991 at forty-nine. He had been interested in cosmology since he was a teenager and returned to it in the late 1990s, taking up the quasi-steady-state cosmology as better than general relativity and then adding a sink to it, from which the rest followed. He has worked on it intensively since about 2002, and taught at Blue Ridge Community College. He described the publication history without complaint: conference papers and a peer-reviewed publication early on, until the model was judged too extreme; then arXiv until about 2010; and now Intellectual Archive, which distributes abstracts to libraries and issues DOIs.
