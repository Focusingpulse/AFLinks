Constant Spacetime Velocity and Time Dilation Stephen W. Hosier Email: ozymandias.swh@proton.me Calgary, Alberta, Canada 5 October 2026 Abstract: Spacetime velocity and related time dilation, are presented in a clearer and simpler format. Many example calculations are shown to demonstrate the simplicity of this approach. In his landmark 1905 paper, "On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies" Albert Einstein presented his theory of "Special Relativity" that was based upon two essential postulates: 1. Relativity: The laws of physics are the same for all observers who are in moving at constant velocity relative to one another. 2. Constant Speed of Light: The speed of light in a vacuum is constant and is the same for all observers, regardless of their relative motion. This theory lead to several profound changes in our understanding of space and time. One such change was time dilation: an object moving through space experiences time more slowly than a stationary object, so a moving clock ticks slower than a stationary clock. Special Relativity also required that space and time be combined rather than treated separately. Hermann Minkowski was a professor of Albert Einstein's who worked with him on special relativity and is largely credited with introducing the concept of spacetime. The views of space and time which I wish to lay before you have sprung from the soil of experimental physics, and therein lies their strength. They are radical. Henceforth, space by itself and time by itself are doomed to fade away into mere shadows, and only a kind of union of the two will preserve an independent reality. (Hermann Minkowski, 1908) When viewed from the perspective of spacetime rather than just space, a profound reality emerges. Everything (light and matter) travels at a constant velocity through spacetime, and the magnitude of that velocity is the speed at which light travels in vacuum (c).
