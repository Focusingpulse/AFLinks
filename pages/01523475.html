Title Missing Momentum & Missing Mass Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Autodynamics , missing momentum, missing mass, decay, special relativity , conservation laws Published Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Website https://www.autodynamics.org/missing-momentum/ "Missing Momentum & Missing Mass" is a white paper by Ricardo L Carezani examining two phrases that recur throughout the decay literature and asking what they presuppose. It is the shortest statement of the central Autodynamics thesis. Overview Carezani begins from a point on which everyone agrees: energy and momentum are conserved. "It is universally accepted because energy is neither created or lost: it is merely transformed. Momentum is conserved because action and reaction are equal." Why, then, do papers on decay routinely speak of quantities as missing? His answer is that the phrases are relative to an expectation, and that the expectation comes from applying the wrong equations. The argument Where the expectation comes from Newtonian mechanics is used for slow particles and special relativity for fast ones. "'Missing mass' or 'missing momentum' appear when SR's equations are used to calculate energy and momentum." In an accelerator, Carezani notes, a particle receives energy from an external field; its kinetic energy rises and, in the standard treatment, so does the mass used to compute its momentum. He objects to the double count — "Even though a 'philosophy' has been invented to explain the 'double increase of energy' (KE + mass increment), to calculate the particle's momentum the increasing mass is real and momentum is bigger than the momentum calculated without the mass increment" — and observes that the same equations are then carried over to decay.
