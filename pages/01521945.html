Title The Thermodynamic Field's Cycles Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Jaroslav G Klyushin , Yegor V Pesterev Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 5 Pages 266-270 Abstract The field approach to thermodynamics, proposed by the authors in [1], is used to analyze field cycles similar to the Carnot cycle. The evaluation of energy losses in such cycles turns out to be qualitatively similar to Carnot but there are some essential differences between them. The field estimation includes dependence on a greater number of parameters and contains not only initial and final temperature. This additional dependence let us formulate recommendations to optimize the work of heat engines and the necessary conditions for the efficiency coefficient to exceed 1. Overview The Thermodynamic Field's Cycles is a five-page paper by Jaroslav G Klyushin and Yegor V Pesterev of the St Petersburg University of Civil Aviation, presented at the 19th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in Albuquerque in July 2012 and printed in volume 9 of the Proceedings of the NPA at pages 266–270. It continues the "field approach to thermodynamics " the same authors set out in volume 8, and applies it to the Carnot cycle. The stated motivation is practical: "deductions following from the Carnot cycle have become an essential obstacle for engineers in their attempts to optimize the work of different heat engines." The authors find it suspicious that an analysis wholly concerned with heat should end in an efficiency formula containing only two temperatures, and they trace the trouble to the historical formation of the subject, in which heat, temperature and entropy were introduced together and never properly separated. Their diagnosis has two parts.
