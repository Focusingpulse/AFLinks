Abstract. The dynamics of COVID-19 cases in Europe shows a novel coronavirus SARS-CoV-2 is able to photoactivate by a natural solar radiation. This means, there are optical-sensitive modes in a spectrum of natural oscillations of the RNA. This paper analyses the dynamics of RNA optical electrons to determine spectral location of the optical-active modes. To do it, we perform the model-independent estimation (what is correct for all varieties of SARS-CoV-2, both known and new ones) as well as model-dependent simulation reveal the location of RNA optical-active modes at several GHz. This allows to use Raman scattering to detect the presence of COVID particles in the ambient air or in a patient saliva sample for fast diagnosis the infection. To enhance Raman signal, we propose to use the photonic crystal as an active Raman substrate.
