AND SHOM MODEL The New Foundations of Cosmo-Dynamics: Static Wave Theory and Static Wave￾Oscillation Model NORBERT MIKLÓS FOREWORD The deterministic foundations of physics and the Wave-Field model Modern theoretical physics faces several open questions that stem from the fundamental axioms of the current standard cosmological model and quantum field theory. The fallacy of the "emptiness" of the vacuum and the classical concept of discrete particles require fundamental revision. The Static Wave-Oscillation Model (SHOM) proposes a strictly deterministic, continuum-mechanical approach to resolving these contradictions. The basic premise of the theory is that physical reality is not a passive geometric "stage," but a two￾level structure: 1. Wave-Field (2D base field): The fundamental, continuous physical medium of the universe. It is an infinitely tense network with a topologically 2D extent, which is the carrier of universal physical laws and the deterministic basis of spacetime. All interactions take place through this field, whose impedance and finite tensile strength (κ) determine the propagation of light and energy. 2. Material Space (3D stress nodes): The matter appearing in the universe does not arise from nothing, but is a manifestation of the Material Space, which is a local, 3D stress concentration (soliton) of the Wave-Field. When the fundamental stress of the Wave-Field reaches the critical limit, the field "twists" and closes in on itself, creating mass. Matter is thus a stable, deterministic stress node of the Wave-Field. The SHOM model uses a Born-Infeld type nonlinear mathematical structure to avoid the divergences of standard field equations. This constraint introduces an upper limit to the stress gradients of the field, preventing physical quantities from becoming singular, whether it be elementary particles or macroscopic structures.
