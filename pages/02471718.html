By BILL NELSON Gazette Staff Writer INVENTOR — Nikola Tesla as he looked in 1896, at a time when his alternating current was winning out over direct current. In the years of success and partial success which followed his bitter disappointment, the public accepted as matter-of-course his inventions, but they rejected Nikola Tesla the man because of the dreams he had for the future of mankind. They labelled him a dreamer and a crackpot. And like many visionaries and creators of fundamental ideas that generate invention, Mr. Tesla was forgotten, while the more practical men like Edison, Marconi and Roentgen, were remembered. They laughed at his prophetic visions of the unborn 20th century.
