Martin Holwerda Residence Dordrecht, Netherlands Nationality Dutch Known for A vortex theory of gravity in a fluid aether ; linking the Podkletnov and Pagès experiments Scientific career Fields Gravitation ; new energy research Martin Holwerda is a Dutch independent researcher from Dordrecht who wrote on antigravity and free-energy devices during the 1990s and ran a foundation publishing conference proceedings on space energy. He is best known here for a short 1997 article arguing that Eugene Podkletnov's reported gravitational shielding, the annular chamber of Marcel Pagès's French patent, and his own vortex model of gravity are all the same phenomenon. The foundation Holwerda founded a Dutch foundation concerned with the practical development of space energy, whose programme proceedings have been available in English since 1987. Its name is uncertain. This page has long given it as the Cosmic Energy Foundation, De Stichting Kosmiese Energie ; but the byline of his own 1997 article in New Energy News reads Stg. Nieuwe Energie — Stichting Nieuwe Energie, the New Energy Foundation. The two may be successive names for one body, or two separate organisations, and no independent source could be found to settle it. Dutch spelling favours Kosmische over Kosmiese , which suggests the form recorded here has been through at least one transcription. Gravity as a vortex in a fluid aether Holwerda's physical picture is stated compactly at the head of the 1997 article: gravity is caused by an excess of horizontal, left-going ether vortices . They are horizontal, on his account, because vortices arriving at other angles are more likely to be absorbed by the Earth before reaching the apparatus. The aether in this model is not a passive backdrop but a hydraulic fluid with real velocities and directions of flow .
