GTM2026-main041 Dynamic Cooperation under Investment Strategies Ndiaye M. Ndiaye1 1 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia mamounepourtoi@gmail.com This paper develops a differential game model to study cooperative investment decisions among firms, incorporating endogenous effort and synergy effects. Players choose production strategies and costly efforts that enhance a synergy function, which multiplicatively boosts joint payoff. Two scenarios are compared: non￾cooperative play and full cooperation with predetermined payoff shares. Theoretical results characterize open￾loop Nash equilibria and cooperative solutions using Pontryagin's maximum principle. Individual rationality conditions define minimum acceptable shares, and a bargaining solution allocates the cooperative surplus based on players' bargaining power. A numerical example with five firms illustrates equilibrium payoffs, reasonable share allocations, and stability under various coalition configurations. Results show that cooperation yields higher payoffs for all players when synergy is sufficiently strong, but leads to higher market prices compared to the non￾cooperative outcome, highlighting the trade-off between firm profitability and consumer welfare.
