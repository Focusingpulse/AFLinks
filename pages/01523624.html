LaViolette Born ( 1947-11-08 ) November 8, 1947 Schenectady, New York, United States Died December 19, 2022 (2022-12-19) (aged 75) Greece Residence Niskayuna, New York, United States; Athens, Greece Nationality USA Alma mater Johns Hopkins University; University of Chicago; Portland State University Known for Subquantum Kinetics , Galactic superwaves, Antigravity and electrogravitics, New Energy , Cosmology Scientific career Fields Systems science, Physics, Astronomy, Climatology Institutions The Starburst Foundation Paul Alex LaViolette (8 November 1947 – 19 December 2022) was an American systems scientist, physicist and author, and president of the Starburst Foundation , an interdisciplinary research institute. He worked across an unusually wide span of subjects — physics, astronomy, climatology, systems theory, psychology and ancient mythology — and is best known for Subquantum Kinetics , a unified field theory in which matter and energy arise as self-organising patterns in a continuously transmuting ether. His work is unified less by a single subject than by a single conviction: that the universe is an open system in which matter and energy are continuously created, rather than a closed one wound up once at a beginning and running down ever since. From that premise follow his cosmology, his account of stellar energy, and his rejection of the Big Bang . Education and career LaViolette was from Schenectady, New York. He took his B.A. in physics at Johns Hopkins University in 1969 and an M.B.A. at the University of Chicago in 1973, and worked for a period at the Harvard School of Public Health. He completed his Ph.D. in general systems science at Portland State University in 1983; his dissertation, on galactic superwaves and their impact on the Earth , set the direction of much of his later research.
