GC2026-PERM035 Rock-and paleomagnetic studies of ceramics from Pereyaslavl-Ryazansky (present-day Ryazan). Archeomagnetism is used in archaeology, and vice versa Natalia Salnaia1 , Pavel Kireev2 1 Geological Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Ryazan Historical and Architectural Museum-Reserve natasavi@inbox.ru Pereyaslavl Ryazansky (present-day Ryazan) was founded on a hill at the confluence of the Trubezh and Lybed rivers. In the 14th–early 16th centuries, the city was the capital of the Ryazan Principality, as well as a major trade and craft center of medieval Rus. One of the main crafts practiced in Pereyaslavl-Ryazansky was undoubtedly pottery. Since the 1980s and 1990s, work has been underway to develop a typology of ceramic products [1] ets. Stratigraphic analysis, dendrochronology, and statistical and typological analysis of pottery have identified 10 types of cooking pots in the Zhitny excavation site on the territory of the Ryazan Kremlin. Currently, the development of ceramic typology continues and extends not only to kitchen pots, but also to tableware and containerware [2] ets. Pereyaslavl-Ryazansky ceramics appear to be a promising subject for archaeomagnetic research, as they are accurately dated within a 50-year range and less from the 12th to 17th centuries and can provide a continuous record of paleomagnetic paleointensity during this period.
