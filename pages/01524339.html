The increased moments of inertia discovered for precessional motion were translated into a series of measurements on pendulums with rotating bobs. Although the discovery of the inertial effects associated with precession and pendulum oscillations were highly suggestive, this author greatly resisted attempts to force him to drop a rotating object for two reasons. Overview Understanding the Dropping of the Spinning Ball Experiment is a five-page paper by Bruce E DePalma dated 3 May 1977. It is the sequel to Gravity & The Spinning Ball Experiment , written seven weeks earlier, and its interest lies chiefly in how far it departs from it. DePalma recounts the sequence that led to the experiment: moment-of-inertia measurements on constrained gyroscopes in forced precession, then pendulums with rotating bobs, and only reluctantly the dropping of a free rotating object — a step he calls "a leap into the dark", since "there was no reason to expect inertial alteration to affect the rate of fall of a released object". The stroboscopic result — a spinning ball rising higher than a non-spinning control launched at the same speed, and then falling faster — "was so startling and anomalous as to have taken me 5 years to understand." The interpretation offered here is new, and it withdraws the gravitational claim of the earlier paper: "The momentous fact is that there is no special interaction between rotation and gravity.
