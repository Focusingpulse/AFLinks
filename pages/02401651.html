GC2026-SGG059 Influence of Lunar-Solar Tides on the Temperature Regime of the Mountain Rock Near the Elbrus Volcano Valentin Gravirov1 , Alexey Sobisevich1 , Dmitry Likhodeev1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences vvg@ifz.ru Fundamental studies of the deep layered-inhomogeneous structures of the lithosphere, which are responsible for the formation and development of the fluid-magmatic systems of the North Caucasus at the present stage, are inextricably linked to the need to obtain new, more reliable information about the entire set of geological and geophysical processes that manifest themselves in regular changes in various geophysical fields. In the context of geophysics and tectonics, fluids should be considered as a liquid or gas-liquid substance that differs from the mineral matter of the lithosphere in terms of its lower density and viscosity, as well as its high migration capacity. The fluid phase and its characteristics vary widely in different parts of the Earth's crust. In shallow horizons, it is water with a more or less significant content of dissolved salts and gases [1]; for the area of the Elbrus Volcanic Center [2, 3], sources of carbonated mineral waters are the most characteristic. In this work, the results of field observations conducted at the North Caucasus Geophysical Observatory of the IPE RAS, at a depth of 3.9 km from the entrance to the tunnel, in a lateral mining cut that is not affected by the forced ventilation system, were used as the initial data.
