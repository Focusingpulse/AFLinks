Title Earth Expansion by Neutrino-Power Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Konstantin Meyl Keywords Earth size, Neutrino, Earth rotation, Core of the Earth Published 2015 Journal Journal of Geology and Geosciences Volume 4 Number 5 No. of pages 5 Pages 219 Abstract The paper contents an approach in the field of basic physics research of the expanding earth and the principle of operation of the growing Earth's core, based on the model for interaction and absorption of neutrino radiation. This has been measured at the Neutrino detector Kamiokande from the difference between day and nighttime. There are numerous indications of the growth of the earth for years. In geography montane formations suggest it. In geology, they ask for the origin of forces that let the mountains grow and that put whole tectonic plates in motion. This paper is about the physical process that makes the earth growing. Overview Konstantin Meyl , a German electrical engineer, sets out to supply expansion tectonics with the thing it has always lacked: a physical mechanism for the mass the Earth would have to gain. He begins with Ott Christoph Hilgenberg 's Vom wachsenden Erdball of 1933, and reads it generously. Hilgenberg, he notes, was probably the first to demonstrate the correspondence between a change of radius and the actual geological formations — magma oozing from the burst crust at the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, compressive cracks and rift valleys where the curvature no longer fits, thrust distortions within the continental plates. But Meyl discards Hilgenberg's explanatory apparatus. Hilgenberg had mass consisting of swallowed ether , atoms as ether sinks and gravity as ether electricity; Meyl points out that the ether proof was already negative from Michelson and Morley in Hilgenberg's own day, that physics dropped the hypothesis, and that "the ether discussion is unhelpful for explaining Earth expansion today as well."
