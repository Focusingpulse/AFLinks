bmde2026-industry013 AI and National Business Culture: Creating a New Organizational Balance in Japan Sergei V. Shaposhnikov1 , Yavar Mansouri1 1 Graduate School of Business, HSE University svshaposhnikov@hse.ru The rapid development of artificial intelligence is transforming organizations worldwide. Algorithms analyze performance, generate forecasts, monitor processes in real time, and even suggest managerial decisions. This has fueled the provocative idea of “management without managers.” If AI can coordinate workflows and process information more efficiently than humans, do organizations still need multiple layers of management? The Japanese case suggests that this question cannot be answered in purely technological terms. The impact of AI is not universal; it is shaped by national institutions and business culture. In Japan, where organizations are historically built on collectivism, consensus-building, long-term employment, and relational trust, AI does not eliminate managers. Instead, it contributes to a new hybrid organizational balance. Technologically, AI enables flatter structures. Digital dashboards reduce information asymmetry, automated reporting removes the need for multi-layered data transmission, and predictive models support planning and risk assessment. These developments weaken the traditional role of middle managers as “information hubs.” Employees can access data directly, and AI systems can flag deviations or suggest solutions without constant human mediation.
