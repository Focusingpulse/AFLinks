Stowe (born 1954) is an American independent researcher in physics, based in American Canyon, California. He develops an aether -based, fluid-mechanical account of physics whose central element is a Le Sage "push" theory of gravity : gravitation arises from the shadowing of a flux in a compressible aether, from which he seeks to derive not only Newton's law of gravitation but also electric charge and the fine-structure constant within a single framework. Scientific work A fluid aether Stowe models space as a real, compressible fluid medium — an aether that he identifies with the zero-point-energy (ZPE) field of the quantum vacuum. On this view the familiar quantities of physics are properties of that medium and its flow rather than features of empty space. He set out this picture in papers such as " A Framework Hypothesis for the ZPE Field " (1996) and " Discussions on the Aether " (1998), treating the vacuum as a physical fluid with definite mechanical properties. Le Sage push gravity Stowe's best-known work applies this medium to gravity through the Le Sage mechanism , the eighteenth-century proposal of Georges-Louis Le Sage that gravity is a push rather than a pull: two bodies are driven together because each shadows the other from an omnidirectional flux, and the resulting imbalance presses them together.
