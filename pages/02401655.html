GC2026-PERM054 Using petrophysical and paleomagnetic data to date the bottom sediments of the Amundsen Basin Irina D. Bazhenova1,2 , Darya V. Elkina1 , Evgeny A. Gusev1 , Alexey L. Piskarev-Vasilyev1,2 , Tansylo I. Khosnullina1,2 1 Federal State Budgetary Institution "All-Russian Research Institute of Geology and Mineral Resources of the World Ocean named after Academician I. Gramberg", Russia 2 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia st088037@student.spbu.ru The Amundsen Basin is characterized by a unique geological structure, which, combined with insufficient information about this region and its difficult accessibility, has generated particular interest in studying the selected area. Paleomagnetic studies play a key role in the study of the Amundsen Basin and the entire Arctic Basin, where biostratigraphic markers and radiocarbon dating materials are virtually absent. The advantages of paleomagnetic data include the long-term preservation of data in sediments, global synchronicity (geomagnetic field reversals), and independence from facies changes. The study is based on data from the North Pole-41 expedition of the North Pole ice platform, a part of the Arctic and Antarctic Research Institute's research fleet. This work is devoted to paleomagnetic and petrophysical studies of bottom sediments in the Eurasian Basin of the Arctic Ocean, with the aim of their subdivision and correlation.
