WORK OF YEARS GONE MUCH NEW AND IMPORTANT APPARATUS BURNED. ALMOST INCONSOLABLE The Celebrated Electrician Had Nearly Completed Inventions Which Would Have Greatly Advanced the Art — His Apparatus for Saving the Energy of Coal. Special dispatch to the Democrat and Chronicle New York, March 13. — By a fire which almost completely gutted the six-story and basement building at Nos. 33 and 35 South Fifth avenue early this morning, Nikola Tesla, the electrician, lost all of the apparatus with which he has been carrying on his professional experiments. Among the things destroyed were several nearly completed inventions which were intended to revolutionize electric lighting. Mr. Tesla occupied the entire fourth floor. When the floor gave way his apparatus fell to the second floor, where it lies in unrecognizable ruin. It was not insured. Gillis & Geoghegan, manufacturers of steam fitters' supplies, who occupied the rest of the building, lost their entire stock, valued at $63,000. They were insured for $50,000. The building, which belongs to Mrs. Matilda W. Bruce, was damaged to the extent of $40,000, which is also covered by insurance. The fire was discovered at 2:30 o'clock.
