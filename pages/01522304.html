A New Approach to Superconductivity Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Stephan J G Gift Date 1 March 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording A New Approach to Superconductivity was a live session presented by Stephan J G Gift on 1 March 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. On the basis that interactions do not necessarily satisfy Lorentz covariance, a theory of magnetism separate from the magnetism associated with the electromagnetic force is developed, and many characteristics of superconductivity unexplained by the Bardeen–Cooper–Schrieffer theory are thus explained. Hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary Stephan J G Gift of the University of the West Indies had twice before argued to the society that the Lorentz transformations are wrong and that Selleri 's are the correct alternative. This talk follows the consequence: if the transformations fail then Lorentz covariance is not a law of nature but a requirement imposed by the relativistic paradigm, and relaxing it opens a line of enquiry that has been closed for a century. What he found there is a theory of magnetism separate from the magnetism of the electromagnetic force — and, with it, a new account of superconductivity. What BCS says, and what is wrong with it The accepted theory has electrons of opposite spin and opposite momentum bound into Cooper pairs by phonon exchange: an electron passing through the lattice draws the positive ions toward it, the local concentration of positive charge attracts a second electron, and at low enough temperature the pairs form and move without collision. Gift's objections were three. The electron–phonon interaction is a weak force, unlikely to overcome the electric repulsion between two electrons even allowing for the screening by surrounding positive charge.
