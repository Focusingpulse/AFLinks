Saturday Morning Science Chat Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Harry Ricker , Ian Cohen, James Keene , Carl, Bob and others, a fortnight into the lockdowns. The first hour went on the pandemic, the second on inertia; the two halves turned out to be about the same thing. Is it legitimate to question a medical consensus? Ian put the question that framed the session. Dissidents make a practice of questioning consensus in physics; medicine now presents an international consensus of the same kind, with politicians deferring wholly to it. Is scepticism appropriate here, or irresponsible? His own answer was that the human pressures are identical — careers ended for stepping out of line, funding tied to producing the expected numbers — and that if anything they operate more strongly in a less exact science. He drew a list of what he called secular apocalypses he had lived through: acid rain, the ozone hole, the exhaustion of fossil fuels, Y2K, global warming and then climate change, SARS and Ebola, weapons of mass destruction — each in its turn the end of the world, each questionable only at the sceptic's cost. Ricker's version was drier: we are at the bottom of the food chain, told to believe the media, which produces experts, who are presumed to know. Whether certified experts can be wrong is the legitimate question, and we cannot tell from here. Hu's own scepticism was narrower and concrete. He could not reconcile the advice not to wear masks with the effort to secure masks for health workers, nor with other countries handing them out; and the shortages had not eased in weeks.
