The dangers of exaggeration, the temptations to adorn a tale by carelessness of the truth and the desire to interest without regard for facts, are causing serious consideration among many readers and some publishers of newspapers with Sunday supplements. Efforts to excel in story telling have brought results that too often smell suspiciously of lying. Contributors to the Sunday issues are, as a rule, unacquainted with the discipline exacted by careful city editors. Their stories grow from suggestions and swell into sensations. The news of the day is too carefully gleaned and chronicled to have left over for Sunday much that can be properly rehashed, with the result that many Sunday supplement staffs are forced to create matter such as "Is love a disease?" and other topics difficult to handle without reckless assertions and careless writing. Marvels of science appeal especially to the Sunday editor. Nikola Tesla is a wonderful help to the Sabbath scribe. The electrician has done so many remarkable things, and asserted that he could do so many others worth doing if they could be done, that he has been of the greatest value to those whose imaginations are their stock in trade, and whose ideas are prone to lead them astray with tempting sensations.
