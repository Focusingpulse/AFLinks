Akito Takahashi Residence Japan Nationality Japanese Known for Cold Fusion Akito Takahashi is a Japanese nuclear engineer, professor emeritus at Osaka University, and one of the principal theorists of low energy nuclear reactions. He chaired the nuclear instrumentation group in the Department of Nuclear Engineering at Osaka, worked for more than twenty years in conventional fusion research before turning to cold fusion in 1989, and has published several hundred papers. He has served in numerous capacities with the Atomic Energy Society of Japan and has received awards for work in both neutron physics and cold fusion. Work Takahashi's contribution to the field is chiefly theoretical, and it addresses the central quantitative obstacle: two deuterons approaching to nuclear range face a Coulomb barrier of a few hundred keV, and thermal tunnelling through it is suppressed by factors of order e⁻⁶⁰⁰⁰. His response has been to look for mechanisms in which more than two deuterons participate at once, so that the two-body barrier problem does not arise in its usual form. The programme developed through a sequence of models. Early work considered three- and four-deuteron resonance fusion in a palladium lattice. The paper Replication Of MHI Transmutation Experiment By D2 Gas Permeation Through Pd Complex (2003) applies an eight-deuteron version, in which octahedral resonance fusion of eight deuterons in the lattice produces an excited ¹⁶O nucleus that breaks into two ⁸Be, each carrying about 47.6 MeV; the ⁸Be is then supposed to be captured by a surface caesium or strontium nucleus before it decays, giving transmutations that raise the mass number by 8 and the atomic number by 4.
