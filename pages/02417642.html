Its output is an exact function of the state. This paper measures what such a reader can and cannot recover from a fully deterministic, exactly reversible integer field, and finds the structure sharper than the statistical version of the same question. The substrate is a one-dimensional lattice gas: 128 cells, each carrying occupancy for a left- and a right￾moving reader together with a four-bit payload, 1280 bits in total. Collisions mix payloads through a unimodular map over Z₁₆, so two readers meeting compute rather than overwrite. Reversibility is exact: 11,146 collisions were run forward and then inverted, recovering every bit of the initial state. Nothing in the dynamics is stochastic. Against this field a reader is a spatial window of s cells and a temporal window of τ steps. Because the coupling is direct, apparent stationarity is not a statistical statement: the field is exactly static to a reader precisely when the whole orbit lies inside a single fiber of that reader's map. Three regimes appear. For s ≤ 8 the reader is saturated — it extracts log₂(T/τ) bits, every distinction its sampling rate permits, and coarsening costs it nothing. Between s = 16 and 64 the field's correlation structure begins to cost it bits. At s = 128, τ = 256 it extracts exactly zero: across the entire orbit the reading never changes once.
