Laboratory Modeling of Meteorite Impact Craters by Z-pinch Plasma

· archived # 1,520,488
Title Laboratory Modeling of Meteorite Impact Craters by Z-pinch Plasma Read in full Link to paper Author(s) C J Ransom Published 2011 Journal Open Astronomy Journal Volume 4 Number Suppl 2-M4 No. of pages 6 Pages 185-190 Abstract Desai, et al. [1] simulated microparticle lunar impact craters using laser impacts. They produced crater diameters of several hundred μm in Al. Ford [2] made observations about lunar craters simulated by spark-machining on metal. These were also very small scale craters. This work expands the size of simulated impact craters from their sub-millimeter craters to craters over a centimeter and expands the type of crater material from metals to substances more likely to be found in large amounts on the surfaces of moons, asteroids and planets. The results support Desai's and Ford's suggestion that these analogs can contribute to the investigation of planetary events. Overview C. J. Ransom, of the Vemasat Research Institute in Texas, reports laboratory experiments in which Z-pinch plasma discharges produce depressions in various target materials resembling planetary craters, and argues that such analogues deserve investigation as models of planetary events. The paper appeared in a special issue of the Open Astronomy Journal . He places the work in a line of predecessors. Desai and co-workers produced crater-like features several hundred micrometres across in aluminium using laser impacts, and concluded that "it seems that there is a universal scaling law governing crater formation." Earlier, Ford used a spark-machining apparatus and reported similarities between laboratory craters and lunar ones, based both on individual crater characteristics and on distribution — crater chains, and the tendency of small craters to sit centred on the rims of larger ones.
