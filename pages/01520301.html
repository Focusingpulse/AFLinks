Title Gravity is A Pushing Force Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Bob de Hilster , Paul Schroeder , Arthur Ramthun Keywords EM wave, Gravity, Pushing Force Published 2015 No. of pages 6 Abstract This document proposes that gravity is caused by an Electromagnetic EM wave that pushes an object as it passes through. There are other models that are based on pushing theories, but the presentation here suggests that the Electromagnetic Wave theory is the most appropriate. Overview This is a working document of the Gravity Subgroup – Wave Theory, part of the Chappell Natural Philosophy Society's Gravity Interest Group, written jointly by Paul Schroeder, Arthur Ramthun and Robert de Hilster. Its proposal is that gravity is a pushing force, in the tradition of Fatio and Le Sage, but with the pushing agent being electromagnetic waves rather than particles. The Le Sage picture is set out clearly. A flux arrives from every direction; a body partially absorbs it, so that the flux emerging on the far side is diminished; two bodies therefore each shield the other, and the reduced pressure between them leaves a net inward push. The authors note the necessary condition — "for us on earth, the pushing particles must come at us from all directions in order to have gravity push us toward the center of the earth no matter where we stand" — and reproduce Le Sage's own diagram. Their contribution is to replace the corpuscles with waves, and much of the document is devoted to explaining how an electromagnetic wave could push at all: the properties assumed for the source, for the "fabric of space", for penetration, interaction and the resulting reduction in flux. The framing is candid about method. "Assumptions are used to help establish the context for explaining the theory. These assumptions may never be proved, but they must not contradict any part of the theory." Assessment Maxwell tried this and abandoned it, for a reason that still holds.
