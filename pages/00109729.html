Light Through the Geometric Memory of Spacetime Optical Signatures in Gravitational Lensing Alejandro Rey Independent Researcher, Argentina reyalejandro311@gmail.com July 16, 2026 Abstract Gravitational lensing does not measure matter directly. A light ray follows the optical geometry encoded in the metric, and any mass distribution is inferred only afterward from the curvature required to explain its path. This paper develops that observation into a weak- eld constitutive hypothesis: the optical geometry sampled by light may contain delayed curvature, left by a gravitational response that relaxes toward the baryonic con guration with a nite time, described by the minimal closure τeffLuq + q = qeq. In relaxed systems the residual is nearly stationary; in young, merging, tidally perturbed, or rapidly reorganized systems the same residual can appear as optical excess or spatial lag. The primary test is weak lensing. The residual template is xed outside the lensing sample by the SPARC/RAR scale; the same scale de nes the reference clock τvac = 36.6 Myr. The leading quadrupolar optical closure then predicts Arel = 19/15 = 1.2667, without tting the lensing amplitude. When the KiDS pro le is projected onto the same xed template it returns Arel = 1.267 ± 0.032, while an independent HSC holdout is consistent within 0.84σ. Population splits show the predicted ordering: redder and more centrally concentrated lenses require a larger optical projection than blue or low-Sérsic lenses. Strong-lensing SLACS systems and eleven gold double-radio relic mergers are treated as supporting checks of the same physical picture: structural optical excess and temporal phase lag.
