Title Problems of Quantum Mechanics Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Thomas E Phipps Keywords Quantum Mechanics Published 1996 Journal Apeiron Volume 3 Number 3-4 No. of pages 5 Pages 121-130 Abstract A recent Special Issue on fundamental problems of quantum physics (Barut et al. 1995) confirmed widespread recognition of the persistent difficulties of quantum measurement theory and its canonical 'Copenhagen interpretation.' The measurement theory problems were faced with ingenuity, fortitude, and a shared hope for 'hidden variables.' What they were not faced with in respect to the latter was consensus. That being the case, there remains room for consideration of still other approaches to hidden variables than those favored by the particular authorities chosen. I shall confine attention here to my own penchant on this subject, which will be summarized in the same spirit of "science criticism" as my previous essay (Phipps 1995), in which I touted the advantages of a Galilean invariant covering theory of Maxwell-Einstein electromagnetism first propounded by Hertz (1892). Overview Phipps identifies what he calls a parametric incompleteness in quantum mechanics, and proposes to repair it by insisting on a stricter correspondence with classical Hamilton–Jacobi theory. The observation is this. Hamilton–Jacobi mechanics carries out a canonical transformation from old variables ( q k , p k ) to new ones ( Q k , P k ) chosen as constants of the motion, and the generating function satisfies three relations: H = −∂ S /∂ t , p k = ∂ S /∂ q k , and − P k = ∂ S /∂ Q k . Both sets are needed classically, and there is a formal symmetry between them. Quantisation uses only the first two. Half the descriptive parameters, Phipps says, "have simply gone fishing, taking their symmetry with them" — and a correspondence that drops an integer number of parameters as one passes continuously from large scales to small cannot be as rigorous as it is advertised to be.
