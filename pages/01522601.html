Title Centrifugal Force Denial Read in full Link to paper Author(s) David Tombe Keywords centrifugal force denial Published 2011 Journal General Science Journal No. of pages 6 Abstract When a mooring line needs to be cast from ship to shore over a lengthy distance, this can be accomplished by utilizing the concept of centrifugal potential energy. Swinging the weight in circular motion in a vertical plane, and building it up to a high angular speed is a means of storing up pressure. The weight can then be released underarm, resulting in a projectile with kinetic energy corresponding to the stored centrifugal potential energy. We can of course choose to deny the existence of this centrifugal pressure and explain the phenomenon on the grounds that we are merely witnessing the tendency of an object to move in a straight line in the absence of an applied force. But we can only indulge in this denial in the absence of a radial or a solenoidal field, and such fields exist everywhere. It will now be shown how centrifugal force corresponds to positive electric charge, kinetic energy, and inertia, and how a magnetic field is a particular manifestation of inertia. Overview Centrifugal Force Denial is a six-page paper by David Tombe of Belfast, dated 5 April 2011 and published through the General Science Journal . It is a companion to his " Centrifugal Force " of November the same year, and goes considerably further. Where that paper argued that centrifugal force is real rather than fictitious, this one identifies it with positive electric charge, kinetic energy and inertia, and presents the magnetic field as "a particular manifestation of inertia." The opening image is a heaving line cast from ship to shore. Swinging the weight in a vertical circle "is a means of storing up pressure," and the projectile's kinetic energy on release corresponds to "the stored centrifugal potential energy."
