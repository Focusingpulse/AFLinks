Cars seized in crimes generate lucrative income for police, governments

· archived # 2,444,259
07/29/16 - Cars seized in crimes generate lucrative income for police, governments When a Plainfield man was convicted earlier this year of DUI in Naperville, authorities sought to seize the 2007 Infinity G35 he was driving at the time and award it to the Naperville Police Department. While ultimately unsuccessful � the car's title was in his wife's name � some might be surprised the effort was made at all, mistakenly believing the state's forfeiture law applies only to vehicles seized in drug cases. In fact, the Naperville Police Department has successfully petitioned the court to have 126 cars forfeited as part of the sentencing process between 2012 and 2015. Of that number, only 26 were involved in narcotics crimes. State law gives a judge the discretion to order the seizure of any automotive vehicle � or boat, vessel or plane � used to carry out a serious felony crime when petitioned by the state's attorney's office on behalf of a local law enforcement agency. While vehicles seized in drug cases might occasionally be kept by law enforcement agencies for their undercover special operations fleet or training exercises, the rest are sold at auction. A percentage of the proceeds goes to the police department that made the arrest, and the balance to the municipality or county that oversees the department, Arres said. Naperville netted nearly $170,000 from the vehicles it sold and from other drug forfeiture proceeds between 2012 and 2015, with the police department receiving $16,635 and the rest going into the city's coffers, Arres said. DuPage County has confiscated 189 vehicles used to commit crimes so far this year, the majority of which were used by people convicted of driving under the influence or for a license violation, said Paul Darrah, spokesman for DuPage County State's Attorney Robert Berlin.
