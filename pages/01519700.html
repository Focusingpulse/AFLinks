Title Dynamic Interaction of Unbalanced Masses Spinning in Vacuum Read in full Link to paper Author(s) V N Samohvalov Published 2011 Journal Galilean Electrodynamics Volume 22 Number S1 No. of pages 4 Pages 3-6 Abstract This paper presents results of experimental research in vacuum into interactions between spinning disks that are close together but not mechanically linked. The research demonstrates that, during co-rotation at large angular velocities, there are interaction forces between the disks, causing precession of the disk spins and elastic deformation of the disk surfaces, in the form of a screw twisting. Thus there is energy transfer from one spinning disk to another initially immobile disk, leading it to gyration, and in their simultaneous gyration, a crossinhibiting action, and heating of the disks. Overview V. N. Samohvalov, of the Samara State University of Means of Communication, reports experiments on two closely spaced but mechanically unconnected spinning discs in a vacuum chamber, and finds interaction forces between them at high rotation rates. The apparatus is described in detail. Two direct-current motors, electromagnetically braked by 18 mm steel plates, carry aluminium alloy discs 165 mm in diameter and 0.9 mm thick, rigidly anchored to the motor flanges; in some runs one disc was wooden. The motors are mounted on plates travelling on four steel columns, allowing the gap to be set and then fixed, with the discs at least 20 mm from the plates. A deliberate skew of the disc axes relative to the motor axes was introduced, "creating upon gyration of the disks a variable quadrupole moment". Initial gaps of 1 to 6 mm and more were used, chosen so that mechanical contact could not occur despite the imbalance. The assembly sat in a vacuum chamber of 300 mm internal diameter and 15 mm wall thickness, evacuated by a roughing pump to about 1 Pa. The reported observations are as follows.
