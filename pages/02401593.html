GC2026-STP069 Study of the Distribution of Proton Enhancements and their Sources in the first 5 years of the 25th Solar Cycle Irina Grigorieva1 , Vadim Ozheredov2 , Alexey Struminsky2 1 Main (Pulkovo) Astronomical Observatory of the Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Space Research Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow, Russia irina.2014.irina@mail.ru A fundamental difference in the ability to accelerate solar energetic particles in the first half of solar cycle 23 became apparent after the same period of cycle 24. The key question was, "Why doesn't the Sun accelerate particles to high energies anymore in solar cycle 24?" [1]. Proton events and coronal mass ejections (CMEs) differed significantly in their characteristics [2]. Now, after the first 5 years of the odd cycle 25, it is logical to compare the conclusions of studies [2, 3, 4] for the same period of cycles 23-24, taking into account their parity, discussed in [5]. Specifically, to check what differences there will be in the distribution of the number of proton enhancements and in the characteristics of their sources (solar flares and CMEs). We draw attention to the already registered 5 Ground Level Enhancement (GLE) events in the first half of the 25th cycle, while in the same period of the 23rd there were 9 GLE, and in the 24th - only one (GLE71 on May 17, 2012). At the same time, about 100 solar proton events were observed during the first 5 years of these cycles, which is roughly consistent with the number of CMEs with a velocity of > 900 km/s.
