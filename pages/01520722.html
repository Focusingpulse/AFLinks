New Experiment Demonstrates Transformation of Visible Photons into Other Visible Photons with Different Energy and Momentum

Title New Experiment Demonstrates Transformation of Visible Photons into Other Visible Photons with Different Energy and Momentum Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Dan Wagner Keywords Compton effect on photons, gamma ray burst, spectral lag, visible photon transformation, photon rest mass Published 2022 Journal European Journal of Applied Physics Volume 4 Number 5 No. of pages 9 Pages 46-54 Read the full paper here ( European Journal of Applied Physics 4 (5), October 2022, 46–54) Overview This paper reports a home-built optical experiment and draws a large conclusion from it: that visible photons of one wavelength can transform into visible photons of another, and therefore that photons carry a small, variable "virtual rest mass" and do not all travel at exactly the same speed. The experiment is disarmingly simple. Wagner displayed three thin vertical lines — green, yellow and red — on an LCD computer monitor against a grey background, and viewed them through a narrow bandpass filter that passes only yellow, in the range 562.5 to 587.5 nm. An LCD has only red, green and blue sub-pixels — there is no separate yellow channel — and the yellow line is produced by driving its red and green sub-pixels together at equal full intensity. Through the filter, the grey background and the red and green lines were almost entirely extinguished, but much of the yellow line remained visible . Wagner's reading is that this is a direct demonstration of photon transformation: red and green photons were annihilated and genuine yellow-wavelength photons created in their place, since only yellow-wavelength light could have passed the filter. He notes that the experiment is cheap and easily repeated, and proposes single-photon counting as the obvious refinement.
