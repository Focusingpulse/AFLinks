Relativity Predicts a Variable G Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Frederic Lassiaille Date 11 October 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Relativity Predicts a Variable G was a live session presented by Frederic Lassiaille on 11 October 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The talk will focus on the mathematical details of the demonstration that Relativity predicts a variable G. Hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary Frédéric Lassiaille 's third appearance of the year, and the one host Ian J Cowan had asked for: having twice presented his "surrounding" gravitational model and its claim that the gravitational constant varies, he came back to show the derivation. He stressed at the outset that the argument is mathematical, not interpretive — he is working inside the mathematical framework of general relativity, with the physical readings set aside, and treating it as the study of a function S that maps an energy distribution to a spacetime structure. The anthropocentric list He framed the claim by analogy. The Earth is flat; the Earth is the centre of everything; the Sun is the centre of the universe; G is a constant. Each was obvious in its day. The talk's object was to show mathematically that the last one is also wrong, and to give the expression for the variation. A single particle in an empty universe The opening move is a thought experiment he illustrated with a photograph of one man alone in a desert, and with the 1686 reception of the Siamese ambassadors at the court of Louis XIV — a room in which everyone bows toward the only person who matters. Put one particle in an otherwise empty universe and there is nothing for spacetime to do but bow toward it everywhere. The scenario is unphysical, so an unphysical answer should be expected, and what he obtains is a singularity throughout spacetime rather than the regular metric the textbooks give.
