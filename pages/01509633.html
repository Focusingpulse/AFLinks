DIY Gauss Meter

· archived # 1,509,633
For my energy projects I needed a meter to measure the magnetic field, induction or flux density. The price of a good Gauss meter in the shop was about 120 dollar. Some study in the field showed me the simple design of such a meter. Anyone can make this was my thought. On internet I found some bad designed circuits. My meter is simple, cheap (10$), sensitive and reliable. The few components are build on a small piece of PCB. Only four main components are there: the voltage regulator (LM7805), a Ratiometric Linear Hall Effect Sensor (SS498A from Honeywell), a mini digital voltmeter and a window comperator (LM339). The hall sensor probe can be used 'free' from the box. There is only one setpoint for the offset between the North (red LED) and the South (green Led) Pole. The only thing to do is to make a table with the conversion numbers between voltage (2.5V is the zero point) and the Gauss and Tesla units. I am really surpriced of the performance of this instrument. By measuring my computer monitor (Imac) I found 22 places with a strong magnetic field.
