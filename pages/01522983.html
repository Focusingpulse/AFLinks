Galina Granek Residence Haifa, Israel Known for A six-part study of the aether in the work of Poincaré and Einstein Scientific career Fields History and philosophy of science Galina Granek is an Israeli philosopher and historian of science. At the time of her published work on relativity she was a member of the Department of Philosophy at the University of Haifa, on Mount Carmel in Haifa, and she gives the University of Haifa as her institutional address in each of the six papers of her Apeiron series. Education Doctoral dissertation on the role of the aether in the thought of Henri Poincaré and Albert Einstein , supervised by Prof. Mara Beller of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. Granek names Beller as her supervisor in the acknowledgements to the series and thanks Prof. Yemima Ben-Menahem for the philosophical discussions behind it; the six Apeiron papers are drawn from the dissertation. Research Granek's published work is a single sustained project in the history and philosophy of physics: an attempt to explain why two of the founders of relativity theory each retained, or returned to, a concept of the aether that the theory is usually said to have made unnecessary. The argument runs across six papers, published in three consecutive issues of Apeiron in 2001 and designated A to F. The first three treat Poincaré. Paper A asks why he retained the aether at all, given that he had stated the principle of relativity and had no use for a detectable state of absolute rest; Granek's answer turns on stellar aberration and his rejection of action at a distance. Paper B characterises what his aether actually was, and finds it built on Lorentz's stationary aether.
