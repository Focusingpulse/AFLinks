Overview Optimized New Alternative Energy Primer is a three-page paper by Jan Peter Roos , a mechanical engineer with an MSME from MIT and a background in energy-conservation work, presented at the 18th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference at College Park, Maryland on 6 July 2011 and published in Proceedings of the NPA volume 8, pages 478–480. It surveys what Roos regards as promising post-carbon energy technologies, specifically ones "an order of magnitude larger in power density than solar, wind and hydro." The framing argument is thermal. Roos notes that all electrical energy ends as heat, holds nuclear power's overall conversion efficiency to be about 20% against combined-cycle plants approaching 60%, and concludes that nuclear's carbon-free advantage "is overshadowed by its waste heat thermal load contribution to global warming" — which he puts at more than 30% of Earth's rate of temperature increase. The alternative rests on an energetic space medium. Roos argues that after the "misinterpreted Michel-Morrison Experiment" the physical properties of the vacuum were set aside, leaving cosmologists with a very small cosmological constant and quantum theorists with an enormous one, while "the engineering value of it is somewhere in the middle" — a figure he takes from Charles Muses as 1.4 × 10³⁰ joules per cubic metre, or 2 × 10²⁶ psi.
