GC2026-PERM009 Rock magnetic indicators of the pyrogenic horizons at the Kostenki-17 archaeological site Varvara Dudanova1 , Fatima Kurbanova2 , Evgeny Konstantinov2 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Institute of Geography of the Russian Academy of Sciences varyanich1212@gmail.com At the Upper Paleolithic sites in the Kostenki-Borshchevo archaeological complex (Voronezh Region), stable stratigraphic levels have been identified in sites since the 1950s – the so-called Upper and Lower Humic Bed (UHB and LHB), usually separated by volcanic ash from the Phlegraean Fields (~40 ka) [1]. The UHB and LHB represent dark, heavily deformed horizons and lenses of varying thickness and prominence, influenced by slope processes. Our study focused on the study of the UHB in one of the most stratigraphically complete sites, Kostenki-17. Here, the UHB serves as the surrounding rocks for the I cultural layer (~32–34 ka) [2]. The nature of these dark layers remains debatable. According to one hypothesis, the UHB is a soils that formed under conditions of a relatively stable landscape, where soil formation was interrupted by slope processes [3]. Another hypothesis links these horizons to interstadials, considering them as evidence of surface stabilization and the formation of vegetation cover [4]. It has also been suggested that the UHB represents soils that have been significantly transformed by anthropogenic activity – Technosols [5].
