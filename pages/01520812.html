Scientist & inventor Mel Winfield claims that today's Hutchison-Effect arose asa validation of effects predicted by his vortex-gravity theory of Nucleonic Energy over 20 years ago,and joins us to talk about his model of physics to offer insight into this compelling mystery. Overview This item is an interview rather than a research paper. Tim Ventura, of the American Antigravity website, questions Mel E. Winfield on 16 January 2006 about his "Nucleonic Energy" theory and its relation to the Hutchison Effect — a set of claimed levitation and materials anomalies associated with John Hutchison's high-voltage apparatus. Winfield's account of gravity is that it arises from the Magnus effect. Heinrich Magnus's 1852 explanation of why a spinning ball curves is, he says, applicable to spinning protons and neutrons: the vortex associated with each nucleon produces a force, directed downward by "the direction of the incoming electrons". Levitation follows from reversing that direction — driving electrons into the atom until, when "enough electrons equal, or better, the energy of the protons", the proton's spin reverses and the force points upward. He states that he knows the frequency at which a proton absorbs energy maximally, and that this frequency or its harmonics is what the method requires. More generally, he holds that all particles are vortices, with vortices inside vortices from the subatomic to the universe, and that their angular momentum is the source of gravity.
