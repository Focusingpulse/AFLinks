The prevailing scientific paradigm operates on a materialist ontology, presupposing that reality is composed of discrete "things"—particles, fields, strings, or branes—that exist independently of the operations that define them. This worldview treats computation as a secondary layer, a map drawn upon the territory of matter. It asserts that the universe is not a computer (a noun), but a computation (a verb). In this framework, the fundamental substrate of reality is not a particle or a field, but a streaming, high-dimensional operator stack. Existence is the emergent result of a self-organizing, self-validating algorithm that continuously unfolds and refolds upon itself.2 This report provides an exhaustive, expert-level analysis of the Nexus Framework.
