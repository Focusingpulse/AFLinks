Peter Graneau Born ( 1921-03-13 ) March 13, 1921 Died February 25, 2014 (2014-02-25) (aged 92) Residence Concord, MA, United States Nationality English Known for New Energy , Newtonian Physics , IAAD , Mach's Principle , Water Arcing Scientific career Fields Research Scientist , Electrodynamics, New Energy Peter Graneau (March 13, 1921 – February 25, 2014) was a British-born American research scientist best known for his experimental revival of Ampère's original force law and for his work on water-arc explosions in the field of New Energy . A physicist and power-engineering specialist who spent much of his career at M.I.T. and Northeastern University, he argued — with his son and collaborator Neal — that a Newtonian, instantaneous "action-at-a-distance" electrodynamics explains a range of phenomena treated as anomalies by mainstream Maxwell–Lorentz–Einstein theory. Early Life and Education Graneau was born on March 13, 1921 in Silesia, then part of Germany (now Poland), where his father was a landowner and businessman. After the Second World War that region was annexed by Poland and his family relocated further west. His studies at the University of Berlin were interrupted by the war, after which he moved to England, became a British subject, and took his degrees at the University of Nottingham: B.Sc. (First Class Honours), University of Nottingham PhD, University of Nottingham He was later appointed a Fellow of the (British) Institute of Physics. Career in Power Engineering After university, Graneau joined the industrial laboratory of British Insulated Callender's Cables as assistant research manager, where he worked to foster collaboration between industry and academia — then an uncommon practice — initiating joint projects in standard electrical cables, novel forms of electrical energy transmission, and the electrification of Britain's railway network.
