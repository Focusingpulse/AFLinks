Introduction In 1938, Ives and Stilwell published the results of an experiment designed to test whether a transverse, second-order Doppler effect applied to the linear 'transmission' of light, by measuring the light emitted forward and backward (direct and reflected Doppler lines) from canal-rays accelerated through a potential drop of 8-43 kV [1]. The experiment was billed as a test of time-dilation, and its results variously interpreted as either confirmatory of Special Relativity (SR), or confirmatory instead of Larmor-Lorentz Relativity (LLR).
