Sparks, Lightning, Cosmic Rays: An Anecdotal History of Electricity Book Author Dayton C Miller Language English Subject Electricity — history Published 1939 Publisher The Macmillan Company, New York Pages xvii, 192 Sparks, Lightning, Cosmic Rays: An Anecdotal History of Electricity is a 1939 book by the physicist Dayton Clarence Miller , published by The Macmillan Company of New York. It is a popular history of electricity , and it is not a research monograph: the text is that of a series of Christmas week lectures for young people which Miller gave at the Franklin Institute in Philadelphia in 1937, under the John Mapes Dodge Lecture Foundation. Overview The book belongs to the tradition of the Christmas lecture for a young audience — the same form as the Royal Institution lectures in London — and its manner follows from that. "Anecdotal" in the subtitle is precise rather than modest: the history is carried by episodes and demonstrations rather than by mathematics, and the sweep implied by the title runs from the spark of static electricity, through lightning , to the cosmic rays that were among the most actively discussed phenomena in physics at the time the lectures were given. It runs to xvii + 192 pages and is illustrated. The Library of Congress classified it at QC507 — electricity, history — and catalogued it under the single subject heading Electricity — History . Context The dates are worth setting out, because they place the book at the end of a long career rather than in the middle of one. Miller had retired from the Case School of Applied Science in 1936, after forty-three years as head of its physics department. He delivered these lectures in the Christmas week of 1937 , the book appeared in 1939 , and he died in February 1941 . It is among the last things he published.
