Title The Principle of Relativity and the Ether Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Jozef Kajfosz Keywords principle of relativity , Ether , Special Relativity Published 2013 No. of pages 5 Abstract In the alternative, Lorentzian interpretation of special relativity the ether is a necessity. This has some far-reaching consequences for fundamental research in physics and for the future unification which under this new paradigm shall be much easier. Overview Józef Kajfosz, retired from the Institute of Nuclear Physics at Řež and from the Institute of Nuclear Physics in Cracow, argues that the Lorentzian interpretation of special relativity requires an ether, and that adopting this reading would make future unification in physics considerably easier. His method is to examine what enters a physical description. Writing a description as D ( G , S X , F Y , I Y , U Y ) — an object G in a state of inertial motion, described using a frame F Y , a synchronisation assumption I Y , and units U Y — he formulates the principle of relativity as the statement that any such description depends only on the relative velocity and not on the choice of state of motion or of frame. Because this concerns descriptions rather than properties, he calls it a formal relativity, and argues that physics must go beyond descriptions to conclusions about the objects themselves. The pivotal step distinguishes the three conventional elements. The choice of frame F Y and of units U Y play, he says, "just an organizing role": the origin, axis directions and unit length can be altered without affecting any conclusion about the objects measured, since no assumption about physical reality enters them. The assumption I Y is different in kind, because it contains a statement about the speed of light relative to a particular state of motion.
