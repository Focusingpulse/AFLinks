Log in I forgot my password Sympathetic Vibratory Physics &raquo; C E Ammann Page actions PDF Print Share Send link C E Ammann ( click to enlarge ) In 1921 on the streets of Denver, Colorado, a man named C. E. Ammann drove an electric car without batteries, fuel, or any visible power source. He claimed it ran on aether! At the centre of the vehicle sat a compact cylindrical device, topped with two brass spheres and containing nothing more than steel wires and minerals. Yet with that, the car moved silently and effortlessly, even uphill. Onlookers, including trained engineers, confirmed that the car worked and no one could explain how. Ammann’s generator didn’t need charging. It harvested energy from the air itself, or more precisely, from the aether, the same medium Nikola Tesla spent his life trying to tap. Tesla’s Wardenclyffe Tower was built to do just this - extract limitless power from the environment using resonant frequency and high-voltage design. But Ammann’s device was smaller, portable and proven. “There’s more electricity above your head than in all the wires ever strung,” he said. Many believe Ammann had rediscovered what the Tartarians once mastered - wireless, field-based energy systems that powered vehicles, lit cities and transcended the crude mechanics of combustion. Some even say this technology goes back further, to the age of Atlantis, where energy was drawn directly from the earth and sky in harmony with natural forces. But like Tartaria and Atlantis, Ammann’s generator vanished. Shortly after the Denver demonstration, he brought the device to Washington, D.C., hoping to patent it.
