If the nose of Cleopatra had been shorter the whole face of the earth would have been changed. — Pascal. All physiognomists agree in attaching the greatest importance to the nose, which has been called by one of them “the sign post that points to the character,” and in reading your neighbor’s disposition through his face you may consider that, with the exception of the nose, the weakness of every other feature may be balanced and redeemed by the strength and force of another; thus a weak mouth is controlled, as it were, by a strong, firm chin, a weak chin by a broad intellectual forehead, and any or all features are in a way fortified by a strong, powerful nose. A noticeably small nose, however, is pronounced by all character readers from Lavater, the earliest of physiognomists, to the present day, as incompatible with any great moral, artistic or even physical accomplishment. Nosologists declare there are but three national noses — Roman or aquiline, Greek and Jewish; all others belong to the mixed classes and are known as angular, parrot-beak, bulbous, turned up and snub.
