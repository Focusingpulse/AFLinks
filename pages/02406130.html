Part II.—SYSTEMS CONTAINING ONLY A SINGLE NUCLEUS Part III.—SYSTEMS CONTAINING SEVERAL NUCLEI Introduction. IN order to explain the results of experiments on scattering of a rays by matter Prof. Rutherford[2] has given a theory of the structure of atoms. According to this theory, the atoms consist of a positively charged nucleus surrounded by a system of electrons kept together by attractive forces from the nucleus; the total negative charge of the electrons is equal to the positive charge of the nucleus. Further, the nucleus is assumed to be the seat of the essential part of the mass of the atom, and to have linear dimensions exceedingly small compared with the linear dimensions of the whole atom.
