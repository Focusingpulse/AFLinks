One of the most remarkable of Tesla’s inventions is his so-called “oscillator,” which is a contrivance consisting of a steam piston vibrating at a tremendous rate of speed against electromagnets. The oscillation, though so rapid, is through so small a space that the piston seems merely to tremble as it vibrates. By means of this device very powerful currents are generated and energy is utilized more economically. In other words, less energy goes to waste. Tesla employs his oscillator for lights and for running machinery. Another of his feats is to charge himself with electricity in such quantities that his body is luminous in a dark room. Thus charged, he takes an electric light bulb in one hand and ignites it by placing a finger of his other hand upon a generator. Marvels are being accomplished in telegraphy these days. There is the new Delaney multiplex system, by which 72 messages can be sent over one wire simultaneously. It is already in use in England, but for practical purposes only six messages are attempted. You have heard of course of Prof. Gray’s telautograph, by which, over a wire, one may reproduce his own handwriting at a distance of 1,000 miles.
