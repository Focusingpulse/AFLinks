Keywords: Cold fusion, excess energy, nanomaterial, preloading. Aqueous cold fusion augmented by nanomaterials LATTICE assisted nuclear reactions [LANR, also known as cold fusion (CF) and LENR] use hydrogen-loaded alloys to generate heat and other products1–3 by enabling deuterium fusion to form an excited de novo helium nucleus at near-room temperature under difficult-to￾achieve conditions. The ‘excess heat’ observed is thought due to energy derived from coherent de-excitation of molecule D2 to ground state 4He, with the large 24 MeV quantum fractionated into optical phonon vibrations near 65 meV. Usually, in the past, successful LANR required engineering of multiple factors including loading, ade￾quate confinement time, loading rate and prehistory (with careful avoidance of contamination and materials and operational protocols which quench performance).
