The first type of experiment used a surface-barrier particle detector positioned a few millimeters from the foil to provide a very sensitive monitor for possible fusion-generated protons at 3.02 MeV. The detection limit for this arrangement is estimated to be 10 -24 fusions/deuterium/s, assuming a bulk fusion effect. These experiments included cells with 5- and 25-1,m-thick Pd foils, 10-txm Ti foils, parallel experiments with 0.1 M LiOD (heavy water) in one cell and L/OH (light water) in another, current densities up to 0.5 A/cm 2, and run times as long as 22 days. No evidence for fusion products was seen. The second type of experiment using these cells, both as an adjunct to the fusion tests and to provide new information, was the use of external beam nuclear reaction analysis to monitor directly the loading and unloading of deuterium in the foil of an operating cell. Using a 1.5-MeV 3He ion beam in air, the deuterium in the outer 2 tzm of the exposed Pd foil was measured for the first time using the D(~He,p) nuclear reaction.
