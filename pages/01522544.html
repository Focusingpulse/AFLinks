Bob Johnson Residence Oxford, United Kingdom Nationality English Known for Electric Universe Robert J. "Bob" Johnson is an independent researcher based in Oxford, England, and a long-standing advocate of the Electric Universe and Plasma Universe view. He wrote the original outline for the Essential Guide to the Electric Universe , the introductory reference issued by the Thunderbolts Project, and has presented at Electric Universe conferences and at Natural Philosophy Alliance meetings between 2011 and 2013. Approach The Electric Universe position Johnson works within holds that electromagnetic forces, and plasma behaviour in particular, play a far larger role in astrophysics and geology than conventional accounts allow, with gravity correspondingly demoted. Johnson's own papers apply that outlook in two quite different directions, and both share a method: he takes a phenomenon whose standard explanation he judges incomplete, and proposes an alternative drawn from outside the relevant discipline. Evidence for the Anisotropy of the Speed of Light (2011) joins Petr Beckmann 's proposal that light propagates relative to the locally dominant field to the Electric Universe contention that the Earth carries a net charge, and reads the small non-zero drift velocities reported in Michelson–Morley-type interferometry as an electromagnetically induced anisotropy rather than an ether wind. The Origin of Nodular Flint (2012) turns to geology, arguing that the diagenetic account of flint formation in chalk leaves the banding and the shapes of nodules unexplained, and proposing instead that flint is produced directly from chalk by biological transmutation — the Kervran effect — converting calcium to silicon and carbon. Both papers end by proposing tests rather than declaring the case closed, which is a consistent feature of his writing.
