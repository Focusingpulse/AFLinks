Tsao Chang Born ( 1942-04-22 ) April 22, 1942 (age 84) Shanghai, China Residence Beijing, China Nationality Chinese Alma mater Fudan University (nuclear physics, 1965) Known for Gravitodynamic formulation of gravitation; the proposal that neutrinos are superluminal Scientific career Fields Gravitation , relativity , space physics, superluminal particles Tsao Chang (born 22 April 1942, Shanghai) — who publishes on gravitation as T. Chang — is a Chinese-American physicist who has worked on the theory of space and time for more than fifty years in both China and the United States. Two lines of work run through it: a gravitodynamic formulation of gravitation, written on the model of electrodynamics rather than of curved spacetime, and the proposal that the neutrino is a superluminal particle with a tiny imaginary mass. Education Nuclear physics, Fudan University, Shanghai, 1965 Career 1980–1982 — Visiting scholar, Department of Physics, University of Massachusetts Amherst 1982–1985 — Physics instructor, Department of Physics, Shanghai University of Science and Technology 1985–1989 — Visiting professor, Utah State University and the University of Alabama in Huntsville 1989–2002 — Researcher in space physics, University of Alabama in Huntsville 2002 onwards — Independent theoretical work on space and time He is credited with more than thirty research papers and two books. Scientific work Gravitation on the model of electrodynamics Chang's gravitational work starts from Einstein's principle of equivalence taken at face value: if the inertial forces of an accelerated frame really are equivalent to a gravitational field, then a formulation giving those inertial forces explicitly must give the gravitational force too. General relativity supplies no such force — motion follows from the geodesic equation — and recovering one is Chang's object.
