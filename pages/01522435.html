Chubykalo Born ( 1959-04-30 ) April 30, 1959 (age 67) Polotsk, Byelorussian SSR Residence Zacatecas, ZAC, Mexico Nationality Russian Alma mater Kharkov State University Known for Electrodynamics ; instantaneous action at a distance; the separated-potentials method Scientific career Fields Professor of Physics ; classical field theory; solid state theory Institutions Universidad Autónoma de Zacatecas Andrew Evgenievich Chubykalo (born 30 April 1959) is a theoretical physicist at the Universidad Autónoma de Zacatecas in Mexico, known for a sustained argument that the standard set of solutions to Maxwell's equations is incomplete and that classical electrodynamics contains an instantaneous, action-at-a-distance component alongside the retarded fields of Faraday and Maxwell. The claim was made in the mainstream literature, in Physical Review E in 1996, and was contested there; the resulting exchange is the reason his name is known outside Zacatecas. He has since edited two collections on the question and, with Valeri V Dvoeglazov and Vladimir Kadyshevsky , the proceedings of the 1999 Zacatecas workshop The Lorentz Group, CPT and Neutrinos . Biography Chubykalo was born on 30 April 1959 in Polotsk, then in the Byelorussian SSR — a town on the Western Dvina in what is now the Vitebsk Region of Belarus, and one of the oldest recorded settlements of the East Slavs. Education MSc in Physics , Kharkov State University, Ukraine, 1986 PhD , Institute for Single Crystals, Academy of Sciences of Ukraine, Kharkov, 1992 Career He was a postdoctoral and then research fellow at the Instituto de Ciencia de Materiales in Madrid from 1993 to 1995, working with Roman Smirnov-Rueda , with whom the Physical Review E papers were written. He then moved to Mexico, where he has since been Professor of Physics at the Universidad Autónoma de Zacatecas, latterly in its Unidad Académica de Ciencia y Tecnología de la Luz y la Materia.
