Scientific Theory Name Autodynamics Type Alternative relativistic mechanics (proposed replacement for Special and General Relativity) Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Special Relativity , Lorentz transformation, neutrino , pico-gravity, Compton Effect, Bohr atom Year 1940s–present Website https://www.autodynamics.org/ Autodynamics ( AD ) is an alternative theory of relativistic mechanics developed by the Argentine physicist Ricardo L. Carezani beginning in the 1940s, and promoted since 1994 by the Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics (SAA). Its central claim is that Einstein's special relativity is built on a redundant use of two reference frames, and that removing the redundant frame yields a simpler, single-frame mechanics which Carezani held to replace both special and general relativity. Autodynamics is not accepted by mainstream physics, which regards it as a fringe or pseudoscientific theory; on this wiki it is documented as a dissident alternative to relativity. Core ideas Frame reduction. Carezani argued that the Lorentz "systems in relative motion" from which special relativity is derived contain a second reference frame that is mathematically and physically redundant. Removing it — the Carezani Frame Reduction — is said to give a new set of transformation equations that remain finite at the speed of light, reduce to Newtonian mechanics at low speed, and contain no paradoxes. Mass decreases with velocity. In place of the "relativistic mass increase" of special relativity, Autodynamics holds that a moving body must eject part of its mass in order to accelerate — much like a rocket — so that its rest mass decreases and the lost mass is converted to kinetic energy. No neutrino. Carezani held that the neutrino was invented only to preserve energy conservation within the relativistic equations of beta decay.
