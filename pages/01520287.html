Title Gravity and Revolution Rates within Galaxies Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Paul Schroeder Keywords galaxies, gravity, solar system, dark matter , Dark Energy Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 3 Pages 514-516 Abstract Mankind did not understand the motions of planets and moons until Copernicus, Kepler, and Newton defined the structure and workings of the solar system. Our understanding of galaxy motions is in that early stage today. There is a mystery surrounding the constant rotation rates observed for stars orbiting within galaxies unlike the solar system where more distant planets orbiting the sun do so more slowly than inner planets. Some suggest there something different about gravity in galaxies and they invented dark matter, dark energy and MOND to explain it. But proper analysis of gravity finds it is constant though its environment may vary. So the answer is otherwise. Overview Paul Schroeder addresses the flat rotation curves of galaxies, arguing that neither dark matter, dark energy nor MOND is required, and that the explanation lies in what he calls External Gravitation. His framing is historical. Just as the motions of planets were not understood before Copernicus, Kepler and Newton, our understanding of galactic motion is at a comparably early stage. The specific puzzle is that stars orbit at nearly constant speed regardless of radius, unlike the solar system where outer planets move more slowly than inner ones. He notes a structural difference between the two systems: a galaxy consists of stars broadly similar in size, with a dense central region that is nevertheless not a central body, whereas the solar system has one dominant mass surrounded by small ones. His External Gravitation model concludes that "the rotation of central bodies pushes other bodies gravitationally," so that two rotating bodies drive each other in their orbits.
