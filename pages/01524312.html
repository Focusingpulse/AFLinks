Overview Time out for Time Dilation and Light Quanta is a four-page paper by John-Erik Persson of Hägersten, Sweden, published in 2014. It is a survey of what he takes to be "different interpretational errors in physics", each stated compactly and referred back to his longer monograph Light Without Energy . The opening argument concerns the swimmer analogy for the Michelson–Morley experiment. Persson holds that the transverse arm suffers no ether-wind delay at all, because the mirrors' boundary conditions fix the wavefronts parallel to themselves: "Light takes the shortest path in the frame of the ether." On this reading the √(1−β²) factor for the transverse arm is spurious, Stokes introduced it wrongly, and Einstein then invented time dilation to conceal the error. The apparent slowing of moving clocks is instead a property of clocks. An ether wind distorts the Coulomb field in one direction only, the bound electron adapts to the distorted field, and its orbital period changes by (1−β²) −1 . There is no dilation of time itself. The rest of the paper sorts phenomena into first- and second-order effects. First order: stellar aberration is "useless" as an ether probe since it depends on the observer's change of motion, while the Sagnac corrections in GPS and light bending near the Sun are genuine first-order signatures. Second order: Michelson–Morley is nulled because a crystal contracts by the same 1−β² as the light path, so no contraction of space is needed; atomic clock rates, the Pioneer anomaly and gravity itself are real second-order effects.
