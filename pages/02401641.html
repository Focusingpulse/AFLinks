GC2026-SGG058 Ionospheric effect of 8.8 Kamchatka earthquake and its powerful 6.9 aftershock Svetlana Riabova1,2 1 Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia 2 Sadovsky Institute of Geosphere Dynamics of Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia riabovasa@mail.ru The Kuril–Kamchatka arc extends more than 2,000 km from the island of Hokkaido (Japan) to its junction with the Aleutian Arc and is the largest tectonic structure in the Kamchatka region, characterized by high seismicity. Its high dynamics (subduction rate of 7-8 cm/yr) determines a significant number of strong earthquakes with magnitudes exceeding 7. An earthquake with a magnitude of Mw = 8.8 according to USGS data occurred on July 29, 2025, in the Kuril–Kamchatka Trench in the subduction zone between the Pacific and Okhotsk tectonic plates at a depth of ~35 km (GEO: 52.498° N; 161.264° E). The strong earthquake of July 29, 2025, not only caused numerous aftershocks but also led to increased volcanic activity in Kamchatka. The data from the pett station were used for study the coseismic response of the ionosphere to the Kamchatka megaquake of July 29, 2025, and its powerful aftershock (magnitude 6.9). The satellites that were less than 450 km away at the time of the main shock and its powerful aftershock (5 minutes before and 15 minutes after the events) were selected. Data processing and analysis revealed that plasma density disturbances in the F-layer near-field are observed 10-15 min after the main shock and its powerful aftershock.
