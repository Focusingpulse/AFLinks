Published 2011 No. of pages 4 Abstract The mysterious ether is discussed regarding transmission of light and gravity. Michelson-Morley's experiments (MMX) are explained. Overview Ethereal Ether and Michelson's Mystery is a four-page paper by John-Erik Persson (2011), companion to Dilatory Dilation from the same year and drawing on the same framework. Its argument is that the ether debate has been posed wrongly. Persson distinguishes an autonomous ether — a preferred frame , as most ether theorists assume — from an entrained ether, a preferred field v(r) determined by the distribution of matter, which he advocates. The autonomous version, he argues, is refuted by planetary motion without retardation, and by the fact that GPS reduces its data in an Earth-centred frame while VLBI uses a Sun-centred one, so that "we use different frames for different heavenly bodies." The entrained ether is taken to be falling toward each body, which supplies gravity along Le Sage lines. Persson notes the standard difficulty and sets it aside: "Le Sage predicts gravitational shielding, but Newton does not. The effect of gravitational shielding is however very small and experiments to confirm the idea have failed." The paper's distinctive proposal is its explanation of the Michelson–Morley null result. Atoms in a crystal, he suggests, hold their spacing by exchanging signals through the ether, and those signals suffer the same second-order two-way delay as the light in the interferometer. So the apparatus contracts by exactly the amount needed to cancel the effect being sought: "The searched effect in light is therefore compensated in the crystal. This explains the hundred years of zero results with MMX, since MMX is useless." A closing hypothesis relocates the effect.
