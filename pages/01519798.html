Overview The Electrodynamic Algorithm of Core of Stars and Black Holes is a two-page paper by Nikos Alexandris of Thessaloniki, posted to the General Science Journal in 2014. It sets out a procedure, drawn from his book Modified Hawking Field , for calculating the electrodynamic properties of a star's core or a black hole from its mass and radius, and applies it to Sagittarius A*, the Sun and the Earth. The idea is credited to Alfvén and Carlqvist: "we can predict the parameters of super massive stars and black holes by electrodynamic parameters. Gravity energy is equalised with electrodynamic energy and the magnetic field is produced by an LC circuit." The basic hypothesis is that "electric charge is equal with mass by a coefficient", which Alexandris writes as k = (G/2πK e ) 1/2 = √(2Gε 0 ) = 3.43745×10 −11 C/kg "different of Stoney's coefficient as well as Hawking's coefficient but in agreement with Hawking theory". A charge density follows as ρ c = kρ m , and two further "mathematical parameters", β and t c , lead through a Hooke's-law elastic coefficient to an oscillation force F and an acceleration g = F/m. From these, the algorithm proceeds step by step: a capacitance C, an electric charge Q, a rotation period T = 2π√(λ/g) with λ = 2πR, a self-inductance L = T²/4π²C, an energy E = kGmQ/R, a current I = √(2E/L), and finally a magnetic field B = E/(I·2πR²/n), where n is the ratio of the body's radius to its core's — 1 for black holes, 5 for the Sun, 2 for the Earth.
