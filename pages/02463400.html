The Golden Angle in Aether Physics: From Quantum Geometry to Atomic Structure Abstract This paper examines the fundamental role of the golden angle (approximately 137.508°) in the Aether Physics Model (APM), focusing on its manifestations in quantum geometry, subatomic particle structure, and atomic configurations. We demonstrate that the golden angle, derived from the golden ratio φ, appears as a key organizing principle in the geometric structure of Aether space, electron shell configurations, and nuclear arrangements. The relationship between the golden angle and electron binding energies suggests a deeper connection between geometry and quantum behavior than previously recognized. This analysis provides new insights into atomic structure and offers potential explanations for observed quantum phenomena. 1. Introduction The golden angle, approximately 137.508 degrees, emerges as one of nature's most intriguing mathematical constants. Defined as 2π(1 - 1/φ), where φ represents the golden ratio ((1 + √5)/2 ≈ 1.618034), this angle appears throughout natural phenomena, from the arrangement of seeds in sunflowers to the spiral patterns of galaxies. While its presence in macroscopic systems has been well documented, its potential role in quantum physics and atomic structure has remained largely unexplored.
