New Energy (also free energy , alternative energy or breakthrough energy ) is the umbrella term used in the dissident research community for work on energy sources that mainstream physics regards as impossible, unproven or already refuted. It is not a single theory. It is a loose federation of research programmes — cold fusion and low-energy nuclear reactions, the extraction of usable energy from the vacuum or zero-point field, "over-unity" motors and generators, and unconventional water, hydrogen and plasma processes — held together chiefly by the shared conviction that the accepted account of where energy comes from is incomplete. Mainstream science's position is straightforward and should be stated accurately. Devices that deliver more energy than they consume are held to violate the first law of thermodynamics; the vacuum's zero-point energy is held to be a ground state, from which no net work can be extracted precisely because it is the lowest state available; and cold fusion is held to have failed the replication test in 1989 and to have earned its place as a standard example of pathological science. Two US Department of Energy reviews, in 1989 and 2004, declined to recommend a dedicated programme. Almost none of this work appears in the major physics journals, and most of it is funded privately or not at all. The researchers documented on this wiki do not all agree with each other, and several of them are among the field's sharpest critics. What the literature collected here shows is a body of experimental and theoretical work — some careful, some not — that has continued for four decades outside the institutions, and the rest of this article sets out that work.
