Ferrock – or iron-rich ferrous rock – is made primarily from steel dust, a waste product from various industrial processes, and silica made from ground-up recycled glass. Above: Ferrock is made up of 95% recycled material, primarily steel dust and crushed glass and is up to five times stronger than concrete ( image courtesy of Guy Shovlin). When mixed together, the iron in the steel dust reacts with CO2 and rusts to form iron-carbonate fusing the components together. Like concrete, Ferrock cannot return to its liquid state once hardened. However, unlike the manufacturing of cement which creates large amounts of carbon dioxide, the hardening process of Ferrock actually absorbs and traps CO2, creating a carbon-negative product. While advantageous as a concept, the large-scale implementation of Ferrock does have limitations.
