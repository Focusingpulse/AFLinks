It is quoted in kilometres per second per megaparsec, and in standard cosmology it is read as the present-day rate of expansion of the universe. The redshift–distance relation was published by Edwin Hubble in 1929, building on radial-velocity measurements made by Vesto Slipher from 1912 onward and on Hubble's own Cepheid distances; Georges Lemaître had derived essentially the same relation from theory two years earlier. Hubble's original slope was about 500 km/s/Mpc, which implied an age for the universe smaller than the known age of the Earth — an embarrassment resolved only when the distance scale was repeatedly revised downward over the following decades.
