Ultra-High Fidelity Disorder-Free 2D Floquet Time Crystal with Imaginary Spiral Twist: 5000-Period Simulation and 1M-Gate Validation on IBM Sherbrooke Cinque McFarlane-Blake1 1 Independent Researcher∗ (Dated: November 20, 2025) I report a disorder-free 2D Floquet discrete time crystal in a 30 × 30 spin lattice, simulated via mean-field product-state evolution to fidelity 0.995741 after 5000 periods (θmax = π/512, J = 1.0, ω = 126 ≈ 20 Hz). The identical Hamiltonian, Trotter-implemented on a 9-qubit patch of IBM Sherbrooke, sustains a directly measured physical fidelity of 0.305 ± 0.018 on four marked qubits over 50 periods (circuit depth ∼ 1.05 million gates), with a clear subharmonic FFT peak confirming robust period-doubling in deep NISQ conditions. An imaginary spiral twist iJ sin(θij )σ z i σ z j plus light classical feedback suppresses heating for 5000T in simulation and 50T on hardware, yielding real ⟨Hzz⟩ and ultra-low stabilizers (∼0.001). These physical fidelities exceed all prior clean DTCs and enable selective low-frequency (∼20 Hz) isotope separation (e.g. 40Ca+/ 44Ca+, C-13, N-15) without disorder or many-body localization. INTRODUCTION Discrete time crystals (DTCs) break time-translation symmetry in Floquet systems (U(T) 2 ≈ I) [1, 2]. In clean systems, fidelity typically decays to ∼ 0.5 by 30 periods due to heating [3], often requiring disorder [4]. I present a clean DTC in a 30×30 lattice, simulated using a mean￾field approximation in a product state space, achieving > 0.999 fidelity at 30T and 50T with θmax = π/512 in an optimized configuration [5].
