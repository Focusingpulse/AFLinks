Olive Ann Mellor

· archived # 1,461,081
She was twenty-one years old when she walked into the office. The year was 1924, and Olive Ann Mellor had come to Wichita, Kansas, with a high school diploma she'd never earned and a secretarial degree from a business college. The Travel Air Manufacturing Company needed a bookkeeper. She needed a job. The deal was simple. What she didn't know was that the charming bachelor who ran the place would change everything. Walter Beech was a test pilot, a dreamer, a man who'd built his first glider at fourteen and never looked back. He and two other aviation pioneers—Clyde Cessna and Lloyd Stearman—had founded Travel Air the year before. They were building biplanes that won races and broke records, and someone needed to make sure the bills got paid. Olive Ann was the only woman among twelve employees. On her first day, Walter warned her not to bother the married men. She told him not to worry—she wouldn't be bothering him either. Six years later, they were married. The timing wasn't ideal. Travel Air had just been sold to Curtiss-Wright, and Walter's new role as president meant relocating to New York City, far from the production floor he loved. The marriage survived the move. The job didn't. By 1932, in the depths of the Great Depression, Walter had quit Curtiss-Wright and returned to Wichita with Olive Ann and a plan: start over.
