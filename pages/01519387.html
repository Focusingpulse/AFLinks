Barry John Setterfield Born ( 1942-04-15 ) April 15, 1942 (age 84) Residence Grants Pass, OR, United States Nationality Australian / USA Known for The claim that the speed of light has decreased over time, and its later restatement in terms of the zero-point energy Scientific career Fields Astronomy , geology , physics Barry John Setterfield (born 15 April 1942) is an Australian-born writer on astronomy and cosmology, living in Oregon, who is known for a single sustained claim: that the speed of light has not been constant, but has fallen since creation, and that the history of measurements of c since the seventeenth century shows it. The proposal matters chiefly within young-earth creationism, where it would answer the difficulty of light from distant galaxies reaching a young Earth — if light once travelled very much faster, the journey need not have taken billions of years. In later work Setterfield restated the idea in terms of the zero-point energy of the vacuum, arguing that a rising zero-point energy would slow light, lengthen atomic timekeeping and shift the light of distant galaxies toward the red, so that redshift is not evidence of an expanding universe. Biography Education Double major in geology and physics — University of Adelaide Career Setterfield was born in Australia in 1942. He took part in a full mineralogical survey of South Australia, and lectured in astronomy for six years for the Astronomical Society of South Australia. He later moved to the United States, and is the astronomer at the New Hope Observatory at Grants Pass, Oregon, where he lives with his wife Helen. His papers are collected on his own site at setterfield.org . The declining speed of light The claim was set out at length in The Atomic Constants, Light, and Time , written with the Flinders University mathematician Trevor Norman and issued in August 1987 as a technical report of the School of Mathematical Sciences at Flinders University in Adelaide.
