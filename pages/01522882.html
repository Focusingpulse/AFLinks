Title Evolution of Quasars into Galaxies and its Implications for the Birth and Evolution of Matter Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Halton C Arp Keywords satellite, redshift quasars, galaxies, evolution, matter, general relativity Published 1998 Journal Apeiron Volume 5 Number 3-4 No. of pages 7 Pages 135-141 Abstract In recent years satellite observations have recorded a number of point X-ray sources in the sky. Such sources are overwhelmingly identified with medium to high redshift quasars. These quasars can now be shown to originate in low redshift, active galacies which eject them along their minor (rotation) axis. It can be empirically demonstrated that this is how galaxies are born and evolve. The observations invalidate the assumptions of Friedman and Einstein in General Relativity and require a more general, Machian solution of the field equations in flat space-time. Overview Evolution of Quasars into Galaxies and its Implications for the Birth and Evolution of Matter is a seven-page paper by Halton C Arp , written at the Max-Planck-Institut für Astrophysik in Garching and published in Apeiron volume 5, numbers 3–4 (July–October 1998), pages 135–141. It was presented at the International Conference on Special Relativity and Some of its Applications in June 1997. Arp was a professional observational astronomer of long standing — twenty-nine years on the staff at Palomar, and compiler of the Atlas of Peculiar Galaxies whose numbering is still in daily use. This paper is a compact statement of the position that cost him his observing time: that quasar redshifts are not distance indicators. The argument is observational. Working with H.-D. Radecke on archival X-ray fields around a complete sample of nearby Seyfert galaxies, Arp reports an excess of point X-ray sources around them relative to control fields, with almost all of the identified sources turning out to be quasars. The configurations recur: quasars appear in pairs aligned across the parent galaxy
