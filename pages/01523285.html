Karl Teppo Born 1928 Residence Mermaid Waters, Queensland, Australia Nationality Finnish Australian Known for A "top-down" account of the elements, in which cold neutron matter breaks down rather than light elements fusing up Scientific career Fields Cosmology; the origin of the elements Karl Teppo (born 1928) is a Finnish-Australian writer on cosmology, living at Mermaid Waters in Queensland, who has published a series of papers since the 1980s on the origin of the elements. He describes himself as an amateur, and his papers are unpublished pieces circulated through the Natural Philosophy Alliance rather than journal articles. His work is more coherent than the three separate titles below suggest: they are three applications of one idea. Biography Nothing is recorded about Teppo's education or working life; the World Science Database gives his occupation simply as "Retired". He attended the 18th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in 2011. Ideas The neutron as the fundamental particle Teppo's starting point is a refusal of the assumption that the fundamental constituent of matter must be small. Because a free neutron decays in about fifteen minutes into a proton, an electron and an antineutrino, he argues, physics has given "too much importance to the bits" and overlooked the neutron itself. His motivating observation is that the heavier elements of the periodic table contain substantially more neutrons than protons. Top-down instead of bottom-up From that he derives what he calls Top-Down element evolution, set against the standard account he attributes to Eddington, Bethe and Fred Hoyle, in which hydrogen fuses into heavier elements in stars and supernovae and matter accretes into stars and planets from cold clouds. Teppo reverses the direction.
