This is the 2nd video in a 3 part series on the history and future of the construct of "Time" in physics. Part 2 looks at the transition in the concept of "Time" from the late 19th century's (post-MMX) Lorentz Aether Theory (LAT) to Special Relativity. Whether the viewer is familiar or not with LAT, looking at Special Relativity in the context of this transition should be very enlightening. Further, we will look at this transition through the lens of clock slowing, since clock slowing involves cumulative effects. Summary Part two of Nick Percival 's three-part series on the past, present and future of time in physics, on the transition from Lorentz's ether theory to special relativity. David de Hilster hosted and played the recorded presentation. Percival recapped part one first. It rested on Lee Smolin 's Time Reborn — Smolin being a respected mainstream physicist who is also, in Percival's reading, a serious critical thinker, having torched the Higgs search as pseudoscience — and on its history of how the idea of time has evolved through several paradigm changes over thousands of years, which should discourage anyone from treating the current theory as final. His illustration was the thousand years it took to get past the intuition that the earth is stationary, and Tycho Brahe's inability to break with it entirely even after concluding the planets orbit the sun. Smolin's own conclusion was that relativity was the pinnacle of removing the essence of time from physics — a first step, Percival thought, that did not go far enough.
