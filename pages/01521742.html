Title The Kinetic Theory of Electromagnetic Radiation Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Charles Kenneth Thornhill Keywords electromagnetic radiation, speed of light , electromagnetic waves, particle, Planck Published 1983 Journal Speculations in Science and Technology Volume 8 Number 4 Pages 263-272 Abstract It is shown that Planck's energy distribution for a black-body radiation field can be simply derived for a gas-like ether with Maxwellian statistics. The gas consists of an infinite variety of particles, whose masses are integral multiples n of the mass of the unit particle, the abundance of n-particles being proportional to n -4 . The frequency of electromagnetic waves correlates with the energy per unit mass of the particles, not with their energy, thus differing from Planck's quantum hypothesis. Identifying the special wave-speed, usually called the speed of light, with the wave-speed in the 2.7 o K background radiation field, leads to a mass 1/2 ? 10 -39 (kg) for the unit ether-particle, and an average number of about 360 ether particles per cubic centimetre in the background radiation field, whose density is about 0.2 ? 10 -30 (kg)/m 3 . Overview The Kinetic Theory of Electromagnetic Radiation is a ten-page paper by Charles Kenneth Thornhill , received in August 1983 and published in Speculations in Science and Technology volume 8, number 4, at pages 263–272. Thornhill was an applied mathematician who spent his career on gas dynamics and detonation physics at the Royal Armament Research and Development Establishment, and the paper reads as the work of a professional fluid dynamicist turned on a problem in the foundations of radiation theory. Its purpose is to remove what Thornhill takes to be the two standing arguments against a material ether . The first is that electromagnetic waves are transverse and so any medium carrying them would have to be an elastic solid.
