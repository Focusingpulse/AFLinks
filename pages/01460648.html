Marilyn vos Savant

· archived # 1,460,648
She had an IQ of 228. The highest ever recorded. Higher than Einstein. Higher than Hawking. Higher than anyone in documented history. The Guinness World Records recognized her as the person with the highest intelligence quotient on Earth: Marilyn vos Savant — a name that sounded like destiny itself. By the age of ten, she had already read all twenty-four volumes of the Encyclopaedia Britannica. Not just read — memorized them. Most prodigies follow a predictable path: elite universities, research labs, scientific breakthroughs. Marilyn didn’t. She chose the most unexpected route: She became a writer. A communicator. A thinker who refused to be boxed in. Starting in 1986, she began answering questions from readers around the world in her iconic column Ask Marilyn for Parade magazine. Letters poured in — logic puzzles, philosophical dilemmas, mathematical quirks, paradoxes that kept people awake at night. Then came 1990, the year her name exploded globally. She publicly solved the infamous Monty Hall problem. Her solution was correct — mathematically sound — but so counterintuitive that thousands of mathematicians, statisticians and university professors wrote to tell her she was wrong. That she “didn’t understand math.” And then the proofs arrived. Clear, undeniable, elegant. She had been right all along.
