Saturday Morning Science Chat Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Ian Cohen, James Keene and Cornelis Verhey , with Bill Lucas commenting from the YouTube stream. It started from a small puzzle Ian had been chewing on and ran, by a chain of ordinary steps, to the structure of the vacuum and the Michelson–Gale experiment. Viscosity, and what it says about mass Ian's puzzle: liquids grow less viscous as they warm — treacle in January against treacle in June, the oil in an engine — but gases do the opposite, and he had checked it twice, latterly against Jeans's Dynamical Theory of Gases , because it sounded wrong. Nobody in the room could account for the inversion; Hu had expected heat to loosen the bonds in both cases. Hu's way of thinking about it was mechanical: a rack dragged across a table crowded with billiard balls, the resistance set by how many balls are in the way; add glue to the balls and the sticking matters more when they move slowly, which gives the liquid behaviour. The discussion passed through what distinguishes a solid from a liquid — Hu's criterion being that the bonds hold their geometry regardless of external energy, which is why one solid will not pass through another — and Ian's correction that the state is not a property of the substance, since water takes all three. That produced the session's key move. If a spoon meets more resistance in molasses than in water, and mass is defined by force over acceleration, then the mass of a marble is not a property of the marble but of the marble against its medium.
