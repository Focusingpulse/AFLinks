INTRODUCTION On March 23, 1989, Fleischmann and Pons1 and Jones et al.2 reported the observation of anomalous products during constant-current electrolysis of D20 on palladium. Specifically, they reported production of excess heat, tritium, neutrons, and gamma rays. Since that time, research has been performed throughout the world in an attempt to reproduce and confirm these claims. There has been widespread dis￾appointment in that the phenomena cannot be switched on and confirmed in a simple way. Even those with electrochemical experience and plentiful resources (such as those at national laboratories) have reported negatively on the grounds that phenomena were visi￾ble only in sporadic bursts and "nothing consistent" could be measured.
