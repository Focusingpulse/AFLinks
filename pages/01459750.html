Harry Hoxsey

· archived # 1,459,750
In 1956, the United States government printed warnings about a herbal formula and posted them in 46,000 post offices across the country. Every state. Every town. 46,000 buildings. Not for a drug. Not for a poison. For herbs. Herbs that had been used for centuries. Herbs that supported a system in your body your doctor has never mentioned — the system that controls your swelling, your stiffness, your inflammation, and your fatigue. The system she got 30 minutes of training on in four years of medical school. The man who used those herbs treated thousands of patients. Was arrested 100 times. Sued the medical establishment and won. Even his own prosecutor secretly used the herbs for his family — then switched sides and became his lawyer. They didn't ban the formula because it was dangerous. The AMA admitted it themselves: "He came into the community and got the business." And the reason you've never heard this story is the most infuriating part. In the mid-1800s, Hoxsey's family developed an herbal formula — a combination of plants that had been used for generations to support the body's ability to heal. They didn't invent the herbs. The herbs had existed long before them, documented across cultures, used by physicians and healers for centuries. The family just put them together. The formula worked well enough that Harry Hoxsey, the great-grandson, started treating people with it. Word spread. People came from other towns. Then other counties. Then other states.
