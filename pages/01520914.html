Origin and Destiny of the Earths Magnetic Field (ICR Technical Monograph, No. 4) Book Author Thomas G Barnes Published 1973/1989 Publisher Master Books Pages 74 ISBN 089051013X Origin and Destiny of the Earth's Magnetic Field is a short monograph by the American physicist Thomas G. Barnes (1911-2001), issued as Technical Monograph No. 4 of the Institute for Creation Research. In it Barnes argues that the Earth's magnetic field is produced by a freely decaying electric current in the Earth's core rather than by a self-sustaining dynamo, that the observational record of the past 130 years shows that field decaying exponentially, and that extrapolating the decay backwards sets an upper limit on the age of the Earth. He presents that limit as evidence for a recent, Biblical creation. The book is the most widely cited statement of what became known in creationist literature as the geomagnetic decay argument. It is also, more unusually for that literature, a piece of applied classical electrodynamics: Barnes uses the same model to derive figures for the electrical conductivity of the core, the current flowing in it, and the heat that current produces. Summary Barnes begins from the observation that the Earth's dipole magnetic moment has been declining measurably since systematic magnetic observations began. He treats this decline not as one phase of a longer cycle but as the principal fact to be explained, and asks the question the publisher's description put directly: when will the magnetic field vanish? His answer rests on adopting the model of Sir Horace Lamb , who showed that an electric current induced in a conducting sphere will decay freely, dissipating its energy as ohmic heat, unless something acts to sustain it. On Barnes's reading, the geomagnetic field is exactly such a current, started once and running down ever since. No dynamo is invoked, and on this account none is needed.
