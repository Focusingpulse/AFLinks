Christo I. Christov Born 1951 Died March 16, 2012 ( 2012-03-16 ) Lafayette, Louisiana, United States Residence Lafayette, Louisiana, United States Nationality Bulgarian-American Alma mater University of Sofia; Institute of Theoretical and Applied Mechanics, Novosibirsk Known for Nonlinear continuum mechanics of space (the "metacontinuum"); solitons and dissipative structures; spectral methods for nonlinear evolution equations Scientific career Fields Applied mathematics; continuum mechanics; solitons; numerical analysis Institutions Bulgarian Academy of Sciences; Université Pierre et Marie Curie; Universidad Complutense de Madrid; Université Libre de Bruxelles; Stanford University; University of Louisiana at Lafayette Christo Ivanov Christov (1951 – 16 March 2012) was a Bulgarian-American applied mathematician, SLEMCO Endowed Professor of Sciences in the Department of Mathematics at the University of Louisiana at Lafayette. He worked across continuum mechanics, soliton theory, dissipative structures and numerical analysis, and published on the order of two hundred and fifty papers. He belongs in this catalogue for a programme he pursued alongside that mainstream career: a nonlinear continuum mechanics of space , in which space itself is treated as a material continuum — a "metacontinuum" — whose deformations are to yield electromagnetism, and eventually a unified field theory, without recourse to geometry as a primitive. He set this out in Natural Philosophy Alliance sessions in 2009 and 2010 and was, in the same period, one of the most regular attenders of the society's video meetings. Biography Christov studied at the University of Sofia in Bulgaria and took his doctorate in 1980 at the Institute of Theoretical and Applied Mechanics of the Siberian Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences in Novosibirsk — the route from Sofia to Novosibirsk that gave Gérard Maugin the title of his 2011 tribute in Wave Motion .
