Overview The Photon with a Classical Field is a paper by Richard Oldani , published in the Annales de la Fondation Louis de Broglie vol. 5 (1980), pp. 225–231. It belongs to a debate that was live when it was written: whether the quantised electromagnetic field is necessary, or whether a semiclassical theory — classical radiation with quantised emission and absorption — would suffice. Oldani proposes a middle model and, more substantially, argues that the experiment usually taken to settle the matter does not. His photon "possess[es] the properties of a classical wave packet, but with a discrete energy and a cross sectional area equal to the square of the wavelength". Detections are attributed to "the instantaneous field intensity of single photons or superposed photons", so that interference fringes arise "due to an overlapping of photons", and "each detection event originates in both sources". The paper's real weight is in section II. The evidence against such a model, he says, "is comprised of a single, much discussed experiment, the interference of low intensity light" — Taylor 1909 and its successors, in which fringes still form when a beam is attenuated so that photons should arrive one at a time. He argues the attenuation criterion is misconceived: The estimate used is N = c/L c ≈ 10 9 photons/s, based on the time-averaged beam intensity.
