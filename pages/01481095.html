Hughes We here trace the twelve harmonies developing in succession . Notice how exactly they all agree in their mode of development ; also the use of the chasms between E and F, B and C. Remark also the beautiful results from the working of the double tones , especially C# - D? , and E# - F? , causing the seven tones of each harmony , when ascending , to rise one tone , and, descending , to reverse this movement . F# - G? is the only double tone which acts as F# when a key-tone , and G? when the root of D? . The root of each harmony is the sixth and highest tone in each succeeding harmony , rising one octave ; when it is a double tone , it sounds according to the necessity of the harmony . The intermediate tones are here coloured, showing gradual modulation . The isolated fourths (sounding sevenths ) were the previously developed key-tones ; these also alter when they are double tones , according to the necessity of the harmony . Beginning with B, the isolated fourth in the harmony of C, the tones sound the twelve notes of a keyed instrument , E# being F? , and the double tones , some flats , some sharps .
