From Symbol to Physical Operator: The Ontological Leap As Euler’s formula reveals, the imaginary unit is not a symbol but an agent of rotation in space¹. Yet abstraction turned this act into notation—the living geometry into a mere sign. To recover what was lost, theory piled algebra upon algebra in the attempt to describe spatial non-commutativity. The bivector in geometric algebra hinted at the forgotten geometry, yet even there, its physical meaning remained unseen². What is uncovered here is not a new algebraic consistency or a completion of an existing formal system. It is the nature of space itself. Space possesses both continuity and orientation—it is the medium where translation and rotation coexist as fundamental expressions of motion. This conceptual transition marks an evolution of understanding: we no longer describe geometry through algebra; we recognize algebra as the reflection of geometry.
