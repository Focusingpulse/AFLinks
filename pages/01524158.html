The Motion of Mercury's Perihelion: A Reevaluation of the Problem and its Implications for Cosmology and Cosmogony Book Author Harold S Slusher , Francisco S Ramirez Series ICR Technical Monograph, No. 11 Published 1984 Publisher Institute for Creation Research Pages 101 ISBN 093276617X The Motion of Mercury's Perihelion: A Reevaluation of the Problem and its Implications for Cosmology and Cosmogony is a 1984 monograph by Harold S Slusher and Francisco S Ramirez , issued as number 11 in the Institute for Creation Research's Technical Monograph series. Its subject is the classic anomaly in celestial mechanics: the perihelion of Mercury advances by about 43 arcseconds per century more than Newtonian gravitation predicts once the perturbations of the other planets are accounted for. That residue is the first and best known confirmation claimed for general relativity , and Slusher and Ramirez set out to show that it does not require general relativity to explain it. The subtitle indicates how far they take the argument: if the classic test of relativity can be met by other means, the cosmology and cosmogony erected on relativity are open to reconsideration. Background: the anomaly The problem is genuine and long-standing. Le Verrier established in 1859 that Mercury's perihelion advances faster than Newtonian theory allows, and the unexplained residue — about 43 arcseconds per century — resisted every classical remedy tried on it, including the hypothetical intra-Mercurial planet Vulcan. Einstein's 1915 calculation, which recovered the figure from general relativity without adjustable parameters, is generally regarded as the theory's first and cleanest empirical success. Anyone proposing to remove general relativity from the account must therefore supply the 43 arcseconds from somewhere else. This is what the monograph attempts.
