The Role of the Hubble parameter in Galactic Rotation Curves and Spiral Morphology E.P.J. de Haas1, a) Kandinsky College, Nijmegen, The Netherlands We investigate the influence of the Hubble parameter H(z) on galactic dynamics and mor￾phology. By introducing the cosmic expansion as an effective limit on Newtonian grav￾itation, we obtain a redshift-dependent critical radius that constrains both spiral structure and rotation curves. Galactic bars are interpreted as frozen spirals of high-z epochs nested inside extended low-z spirals. This framework naturally explains the coexistence of bulges, bars, disks and halos as the outcome of metric inflow and bulge reset events. The result￾ing morphology and kinematics provide a direct and testable connection between galactic structure and the cosmic expansion. We argue that this approach opens a new possibility to empirically derive H(z) from galaxy morphology and rotation curves and allows recon￾structing the expansion history of the universe on galactic scales. Galactic spirals contain a frozen memory of cosmic epochs through the constraint of the time dependent Hubble expansion on the Newtonian gravitational potential. The critical distance, in its time dependent form with H(z) instead of H0, tightens galactic spirals and reduces their extent in space.
