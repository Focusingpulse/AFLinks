Title About the law of gravity Read in full Link to paper Author(s) John-Erik Persson Published 2013 No. of pages 3 Abstract The paper carries no abstract. It is a short contribution to the Natural Philosophy Alliance gravity group, written in response to Robert de Hilster's question about what is wrong with Newton's law of gravity. Persson argues that the law should properly be written as a summation or integration over mass elements; that Le Sage's pushing gravity gives almost the same expression but with an exponential attenuation with range, and hence a maximum possible gravitational field; and that the anomalous gravimeter readings reported during the 1997 Chinese solar eclipse support such an exponential, shielding model. The note also restates his view that pre-publication review should be replaced by extensive review after publication. Overview This three-page note of 2013 was written for the Natural Philosophy Alliance's gravity group, in answer to a question put by Robert de Hilster: what is the problem with Newton's law of gravity? John-Erik Persson 's answer is that the familiar form g ~ GM / r 2 hides its own approximations, and that Le Sage's pushing gravity differs from it by an exponential factor which is small but, he argues, already detectable. The note opens with a statement of method that is worth quoting because it describes the venue as much as the physics: "It is therefore a very good idea that NPA and GSJournal has no reviewing before publishing. We must accept that this leads to a diversified quality in the contributions. However, it is very important that we have extensive reviewing after publication… Remember that it is such negative critique that has the positive effects." He closes hoping "that this article can provoke some people to state a different opinion."
