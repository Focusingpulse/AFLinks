Vector kinematic analysis of Keplerian velocity from Hamilton’s hodograph Hervé Le Cornec* Dated 15/08/2025 Abstract: Many authors in the literature focused on Hamilton's hodograph of the Keplerian motion but none of them developed a vector kinematic analysis of the peculiar velocity shown by the hodograph, so we perform it here. As expected, the analysis predicts Kepler's laws and the mathematical structure of Newton's acceleration. It also leads to new expressions for the eccentricity and the classical mechanical energy. We then discuss the relationship between the geometric acceleration given by Hamilton's kinematics and the physical one stated by Newton. Introduction In 1845 W.R. Hamilton proposed a new way of representing the motion of a body submitted to Newton's gravitation law, the hodograph[1]. Many authors have since discussed this approach[2-8] . A most interesting result issued from the hodograph of the Keplerian motion is that the velocity of a Keplerian orbiter is always the simple addition of a uniform rotation velocity vector, let call it vR, and a uniform translation velocity vector, let call it vT. The overall velocity is then always given by : v=vR + vT (1) Because the authors were focused on the structure of the hodograph rather than the kinematics of this peculiar velocity, we could not find in the literature any kinematic analysis of this very simple vector expression, therefore we decided to develop one. We will show that, as expected, we are able to confirm that such a velocity is in full agreement with the three laws of Kepler and with the mathematical expression of Newton's gravitational acceleration. However, Newton's constant physical factor GM (G being the constant of gravitation and M the mass at the focus of the orbit) is replaced by another constant factor, purely kinematic.
