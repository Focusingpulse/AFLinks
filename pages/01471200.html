flash Joule heating

· archived # 1,471,200
Here’s how a little known technology could break China’s rare earth stranglehold. Surprisingly, it’s neither particularly difficult nor expensive. Rice University chemist and professor Dr. James Tour and his team have pioneered a method known as flash Joule heating that can unlock rare earth elements from things we already have abundantly available: industrial, electronic, and mining waste. “You put a high voltage and a high current through a material that is not highly conductive. It's much like your toaster. Your toaster [has] this coil and you run this current through, but there's enough resistance that the coil gets red hot very quickly. That's flash joule heating,” he told me. Due to the high voltage used in flash Joule heating, the waste materials heat up extremely rapidly, often exceeding 3,000 degrees Celsius in milliseconds, followed by rapid cooling, and they can separate out the rare earths one by one. And they found that adding chloride makes the process work much better. This process allows rare earth metals to be “flashed” out of e-waste, industrial waste and mining tailings - all of which are plentiful in America. [Facebook post]
