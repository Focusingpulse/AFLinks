caption Wikipedia Dispute : wikipedia:Gravity This Natural Philosophy wiki page disputes content found on Wikipedia page wikipedia:Gravity Gravity is the tendency of massive bodies to approach one another. It is the most familiar phenomenon in physics and the least explained: every account of how it works — as an occult attraction at a distance, as a pressure, as a flux of particles, as the curvature of spacetime — has been contested since Newton, including by Newton. This wiki holds one of the largest collections anywhere of work that disputes the standard account. Category: Gravity catalogues over 1,200 items. This page maps that literature; the individual arguments are set out on the pages linked from it. For Pharis E. Williams's 2012 paper of this name, see Gravity (2012 paper) . The two orthodoxies, and what is disputed Two accounts hold the field. Newton's law of universal gravitation gives an inverse-square attraction proportional to the product of the masses — exact enough to land spacecraft, and silent on mechanism. Einstein's general relativity replaces the force with the curvature of spacetime: bodies follow geodesics in a geometry shaped by their own mass-energy. The critics collected here divide over which of these to reject, and the division matters. Some accept Newton's law as an accurate description and object only to Einstein's replacement of it, holding that a real physical cause must be found rather than a geometry. Others reject attraction itself as unphysical — the objection Newton made against his own theory in the letter to Bentley of 1693, that action at a distance through a vacuum is "so great an Absurdity that I believe no Man who has in philosophical Matters a competent Faculty of thinking can ever fall into it." On that view, whatever pushes must do so by contact, and the task is to identify the medium. Push gravity and the Le Sage tradition The oldest live alternative is that gravity is a push , not a pull.
