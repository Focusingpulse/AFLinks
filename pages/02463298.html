The APM proposes that space consists of discrete Aether units with specific geometric properties, and introduces the distinction between electrostatic and magnetic charge as distributed (squared) quantities. This framework successfully predicts electron binding energies across the periodic table with a mean absolute error of 0.54%, provides a geometric explanation for the Casimir effect, and offers new interpretations of the quantum Hall effect and other phenomena. The theory derives all fundamental force constants from a single universal force (Gforce) and the properties of Aether units, achieving a level of unification that has long eluded physics. Key predictions include specific fine structure constants for protons and neutrons, a maximum mass density related to the Schwarzschild radius, and precise relative strengths of fundamental forces. These predictions align with existing experimental observations and suggest new avenues for experimental investigation. The APM's mathematical framework employs straightforward dimensional analysis and simple equations while maintaining consistency with observed phenomena. This approach bridges quantum and classical physics, potentially resolving long-standing issues in theoretical physics such as quantum gravity and the unification of fundamental forces. The model also offers new perspectives on dark matter, dark energy, and the matter-antimatter asymmetry in the Universe.
