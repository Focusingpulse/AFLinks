caption Wikipedia Dispute : wikipedia:Physics This Natural Philosophy wiki page disputes content found on Wikipedia page wikipedia:Physics Physics is the study of matter, motion, and the forces between them. On this wiki it is also the name of a dispute: most of the researchers catalogued here hold that twentieth-century physics took a wrong turn, and that the turn was philosophical before it was empirical. This page is an entry point. Over 3,000 researchers and some 20,000 papers are catalogued on this wiki, and the substantive arguments live on the topic pages linked below. For Roland H. Dishington's 1989 book of this name, see Physics (Dishington book) . The common thread The researchers here disagree with one another about nearly everything, but a recurring theme can be stated fairly. It is that physics abandoned the requirement of a physical model — a picture of what is actually happening — in favour of formalisms that predict without explaining. On this reading, relativity replaced a mechanism for gravity with a geometry; quantum mechanics replaced a description of the electron with a probability amplitude; cosmology replaced an account of the universe with a set of fitted parameters. Each move gained predictive power and gave up something the critics regard as the point of the subject. Dishington's book, which formerly occupied this title, opens with the complaint in its usual form: that the student "is told to abandon his intuition, cause and effect and the deterministic world", and is offered instead "a statistical numbers game, a space and time done with mirrors". Whether that is a diagnosis or a nostalgia is the argument. It should be said plainly that the theories objected to are the best-tested in the history of science, and that this wiki records the objections rather than adjudicating them.
