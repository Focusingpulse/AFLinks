, Dornach To-day it is my intention to describe those sounds which have not yet been considered. To begin with I shall take s and z , for the nature of these sounds is such that they may almost be said to be in a category by themselves. Later, as opportunity arises, I can deal with any of the sounds which up to now have been omitted. S was always felt, at a time when such things had not yet been lost, as a sound penetrating specially deeply into the very essence of language.
