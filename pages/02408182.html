Materials. ; ; . . : B 33 256 89 INTRODUCTION This book attempts to bring to those readers whose knowledge of tools is very limited an insight into some of the more generally used hand tools and their applications. Tools are powerful weapons in the hands of the skilled person, so start off by buying good-quality ones ;treat them well and they will give a lifetime’s service. Those who have no separate workshop, or who cannot monopolise a room in the house for this purpose, will have to make do with a corner near a window or source of artificial light. In the selection of a suitable space bear in mind that a certain amount of mess, noise and even smell is inevitable in a work- room. Readers who can acquire a separate workshop should arrange for good natural and artificial lighting positioned effectively. A gas supply is very useful for soldering and the heat treatment of metals, but failing this convenience a blow-lamp will suffice. Electric points for soldering irons and possibly for motorised tools and a water supply can be added in due course as the reader gets more ambitious. Tool storage will depend upon the workroom space.
