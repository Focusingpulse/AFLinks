A fellow was in prison for 12 years after embezzling $200,000. When the fellow was released from prison, the detective who by now was a captain who had handled the case 12 years earlier had never been able to recover the money, so he vowed to follow the parolee so he would know he would NEVER get to spend the $200,000. The detective came to the parolees room right off the bat and made idle chatter about noticing how the parolee had spent so much time looking over travel and vacation brochures all his life and especially in prison, leading everyone to think he fully planned to spend the $200,000 on travel for the rest of his life. A large suitcase was on the bed in plain sight and the detective asked him where he thought he was going. The parolee replied, just got out of prison so I'm moving in. After about 10 minutes of discussing the marvels of travel that he'd always wanted to experience, the parolee seemed resigned to his fate of always being watched. He sat on the bed and told the detective he'd decided he just wanted to be left alone for the rest of his life and if the state would do that, he would give them back the entire $200,000. The detective was of course dumbfounded (as was I) because what was the point, 12 years of his life wasted in prison, only to give up what he had so carefully kept secret about. He projected an air of being so tired and just wanting to be left alone.
