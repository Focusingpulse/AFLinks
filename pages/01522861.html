Eric W. Crew Born 1917 Died 2006 (aged 88–89) Residence United Kingdom Nationality English Known for Electrical discharge explanations of violent astronomical events Scientific career Fields Electrical Engineer Eric W. Crew (1917 – 2006) was an English electrical engineer who, in retirement, wrote extensively on the part played by electricity in astronomy. Taking up the electrical discharge theory of C. E. R. Bruce , he argued that the sudden and violent events astronomers observe — stellar flares, jets, the behaviour of novae and quasars — are lightning on a cosmic scale, produced by the same physics as a lightning stroke in the Earth's atmosphere. His paper "Lightning in Astronomy" appeared in Nature in December 1974. He was a Fellow of the Royal Astronomical Society and a council member of the Society for Interdisciplinary Studies, and his work is part of the tradition now labelled the electric universe , though his own claim was a good deal narrower than that label suggests. Biography Crew was born in Stoke-on-Trent and trained as an electrical engineer. He joined British Petroleum in London and travelled widely installing switchgear in power stations. He was a Fellow of the Institution of Electrical Engineers, and in retirement he developed a fire protection system for cables used in industrial plants and underground railways. Passionately interested in all aspects of science, and especially astronomy, he became a Fellow of the Royal Astronomical Society and a council member of the Society for Interdisciplinary Studies (SIS). A prolific writer with a wide circle of correspondents, in his later years he was in contact with the Hungarian scientist László Körtvélyessy on the electrical aspects of the Sun, and the two published together in 2002. Körtvélyessy recalled that Crew's "theory of the universe was based on the fact that electricity can shape matter", and that Crew identified many celestial bodies whose features he attributed to electrical effects.
