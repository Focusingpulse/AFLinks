Title Dynamics of Black Holes and Structure Formation in the Hotson - Westergard Universe Model Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Billie Westergard Keywords Angular Momentum, Antimatter, Black Holes , Beta Decay, BEC, Bose-Einstein Condensate, Degenerate matter, Degenerate Hydrogen Well, Dirac's Equation, Galaxy Harmonics, General Relativity , Gravitation Published 2009 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume Vol. 6 Number 2 No. of pages 40 Pages 334 - 373 Abstract The theory of the object known in general relativity as a Black Hole is not fully worked out and remains a source of various controversies. This paper shows that true black holes do not exist in nature due to forces that prevent the formation of singularities and event horizons. A reconstruction of the derivation of the Einstein field equations of general relativity shows that a force causes a phase-change transition from matter to energy near the Planck scale. This phase-change is the cause of an ejection process from the nuclear region of galaxies and ultimately results in the formation of gas, dust, stars, and other structures throughout the Universe. Overview Presented at the Natural Philosophy Alliance conference at Storrs, Connecticut in 2009, this is a long synthesis paper — roughly forty printed pages, much of it consisting of extended verbatim quotation from other authors, which Westergard assembles into a single argument. His own contribution is the closing third: the dynamics of what he renames "Degenerate Hydrogen Wells," and the account of cosmic structure formation that follows from them. The claim is that the black hole of general relativity is an artifact rather than an object. Gravitational collapse, on this view, does not run to a singularity; it stops at a definite limit where matter undergoes a phase change into pure spin, and the stored angular momentum is eventually ejected — supplying the gas, dust, stars and galaxies of a steady-state, non-expanding universe.
