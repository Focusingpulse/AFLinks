GTM2026-main014 On a multistage spatial fish war model with finite-horizon Denis Kuzyutin1,2 , Nadezhda Smirnova2 , Maria Bulgakova1 1 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia 2 National Research University Higher School of Economics, Moscow, Russia nadezhda.v.smirnova@gmail.com Keywords: renewable resource extraction, dynamic game, fisheries, Subgame perfect equilibria, cooper- ative solution, migration Dynamic game-theoretical models provide suitable tools to describe and study worldwide fishery in competitive setting as well as to design fishing policies to guarantee sustainable long-term exploitation of fishery resources (see, e.g., [4, 5, 7, 6, 9, 1, 8]). Considering spatial fishery management problems where water basin is divided to several regions with different marine biological conditions, fishery regimes e.t.c., one need to take into account the fish migration process between the regions. A two-player, two-region dynamic fishery management model with migration where the regulator controls the size of each fishery area was introduced in [7, 6]. The paper [2] explored an extended version of this single-criterion model with migration between three marine areas, namely, an open access fishing area with n players, a MPA with limited fishing activity of only one player and a no-take area (”fish bank”). The multicriteria extention of these models that takes into account the players’ environmental concern was introduced and explored in [3].
