HIMSTEDT IN "WIEDEMANN'S ANNALEN." The author has succeeded in reproducing most of Tesla's experiments with very simple apparatus. He utilized at first the Lecher method of producing electrical oscillation, but later on used the still more common Leyden jar. The pole of a powerful Rubenkorff coil, 50 centimeters long and 20 centimeters in diameter, and used with five or six accumulator cells, was connected to the inner coatings of two medium-sized Leyden jars insulated from one another by standing on paraffine blocks, and also to a spark micrometer. The outer coatings of the jars were connected together by a copper wire four millimeters thick and 120 centimeters long, bent into U shape. A 16-volt glow lamp put in parallel with this wire was soon brought to incandescence when the spark micrometer was suitably adjusted. When jars 16 centimeters in diameter and 4: centimeters high were used, as many as three lamps could be made to glow — a 65-volt lamp below, a 16-volt lamp in the middle and a 2-volt lamp at the top. If the nipple of such a lamp be filed off it ceases almost entirely to glow. Tesla ascribes this fact to the alteration in the molecular bombardment in the lamp; but the author considers that it is due to the connection of heat by the air admitted.
