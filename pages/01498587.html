Belarusian State University, Minsk, Belarus: Fusion Information Center, Salt Lake City. A BRIEF SURVEY OF USEFUL INFORMATION ABOUT HYDROGEN IN METALS R.A. Oriani Department of Chemical Engineering and Materials Science University of Minnesota Minneapolis, MN 55455 USA INTRODUCTION Because cold fusion phenomena are notoriously erratic, and the parameters necessary to obtain reproducible and consistent results are poorly understood it is important to be aware of what is known about the state of hydrogen in metals and of the dynamics of its entry into and release from a metal. This short paper cannot do more than indicate some of the important areas; the interested reader can obtain more information by reading the references (1-3). EQUILIBRIUM ASPECTS Hydrogen dissolved in metals at low concentrations exhibits a linear relation between the concentration and the half power of the pressure, p, of the molecular gas. This is proof that hydrogen dissociates when it enters the metal. Much experimental information and theoretical considerations demonstrate that in transition metals the electron accompanying the proton of the dissociated hydrogen enters the s and d-bands of the host metal changing the density of states at the Fermi surface and causing shifts of the energy bands. The Fermi electrons concentrate about the proton, closely screening the positive charge and producing what is effectively a neutral atom (4). These electronic reactions produce both short-range and long-range interactions between the dissolved hydrogen species. Hydrogen occupies interstitial sites in the metal lattice, as shown by neutron diffraction in many cases.
