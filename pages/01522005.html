"A Theory of Electricity" is not a paper but a serial, running to seventeen instalments in the Toth-Maatian Review between 1988 and about 1994; the page's abstract field lists them all. The linked PDF is not the instalment the infobox cites: the infobox gives Volume 6, Number 1 (1988), pages 2955–2979, while the file is Volume 9, Number 2 (July 1990), pages 4413–22. It is also a scan of aging print, and the optical character recognition has substituted r for r and l throughout, so the text is hard going. What the instalment contains is a piece of experimental history followed by a replication attempt. Milnes reviews Arago's 1824 demonstration that a swinging copper pendulum deflects a magnetic needle, and his 1825 finding that rotating discs of copper, zinc, brass and lead drag a permanent magnet far more strongly than they attract it at rest. He follows the reception — Babbage and Herschel repeating it in London within weeks, Christie contesting Arago's claim that non-conductors did the same — and then Faraday's thorough 1832 investigation.
