The plumage is ample and compact, often iridescent in color, but handsome. They feed mainly on fruit, seeds, etc. Most of them fly swiftly and have a cooing voice. — Webster. But if you are not satisfied with Webster, idle about a bit on the library steps and Boswellize stray thoughts. It’s a capital place to loaf — that long, paved terrace, aloof from the blatter and squawk of Fifth Avenue and Forty-second Street. There lie Potter’s lions in lithic contempt of trotting humans. Forefronting the assured chastity of the deep portico, those two gigantic urns curve to contours that bring balm to the inner eye. From the capitals and ledges above comes the hundredfold cooing of pigeons, and the air whispers in wide circles with the beat of their wings. A hundred wingless sons and daughters of the Lord scrape past and see neither lions nor steps, neither urns nor portico, nor pigeons. Praise Allah for these virtuous ones, children of affairs who make the world turn round, straining to the click of a stock ticker or the tick of a telegraph.
