Paleogravity is a new area in paleontology that studies ancient fossils and geology to determine the strength of gravity in ancient times. Stephen Hurrell is the world’s leading expert in this area and will be talking about some new evidence using data from dinosaurs and animal anatomy to show that gravity must have been significantly less in the ancient past. Summary Stephen Hurrell , presenting from near Liverpool, on whether the strength of the earth's surface gravity in the past can be calculated. His connection failed repeatedly at the start and David de Hilster was preparing to read the slides himself before Hurrell got through. The method Measuring surface gravity needs only two things: a weight and a mass. An astronaut on the moon weighs a sixth of what they weigh on earth while their mass is unchanged. So if one could establish the weight and the mass of something that lived in the past, one would have the surface gravity of that time without needing a time machine. Both can be had from a fossil. The weight follows from the leg bone: Anderson and colleagues derived a formula predicting the weight of a land animal from the dimensions of its femur, and Hurrell showed the plot of predicted against actual weights for living animals to demonstrate it works.
