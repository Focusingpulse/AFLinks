Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Paul Schroeder Keywords external gravitation, pushing gravity, aberration, rotation, space, cosmology Published 2014 No. of pages 7 Overview A Flowing View of the Nature and Geometry of Space. is a seven-page paper by Paul Schroeder , dated 2014, setting out the reasoning behind his " external gravitation " model, in which gravity is a push delivered by long-wavelength radiation arriving from all directions rather than an attraction exerted by masses. The paper is preparatory rather than technical: its aim is to establish a way of picturing space from which, Schroeder argues, a pushing gravity follows naturally. He presented the material as a live session in February 2014, with a second part in April. Schroeder's own summary of the motivation is direct. "The idea that Newtonian gravity is viewed as an attraction and Relativity gravity is space bending retards logic. A force is something that provides a source for motion. Gravity is called a force and therefore it provides the impetus rather than an attraction for motion. Physical interactions imply contact resulting in a pressure from a push." What follows, he writes, "considers many little physical issues for which a solution leads to a development of pushing gravity." The paper belongs to the tradition running from Le Sage , but with a distinctive substitution: where Le Sage used a flux of ultramundane corpuscles, Schroeder uses long-wavelength electromagnetic radiation, on the explicit ground that particle impacts "would generate too much heat and disturb orbits." The argument Starting from space rather than matter Schroeder's opening move is methodological. Most accounts of the universe begin from matter — from massive bodies such as the Earth — which he grants is natural, since human experience and the philosophical tradition both start there.
