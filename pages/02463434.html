Systems that appear locally conventional may possess global constraints that fundamentally alter their correct description. This distinction is not merely philosophical; it determines whether measured quantities are interpreted as intrinsic properties or as projections of a higher-order geometry. The Möbius strip provides a canonical example [Munkres, 2000]. A sufficiently small segment appears two-sided, yet the global object is single-sided. The apparent duality is therefore not fundamental, but arises from restricting observation to a local region. Crucially, the continuity of a traversal does not imply structural unity.
