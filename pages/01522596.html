Title Central Body Rotation Drives Orbital Revolutions Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Paul Schroeder Published 2012 No. of pages 7 Abstract Prior to the development of physics as a science it was sufficient to have motion that offsets a perpendicular gravitational force and continues forever. The total absence of any friction could be assigned to the ?void' of space. But perpetual motion and empty space are now seen as fallacies. Continuous motion needs a driving force for impetus. Overview Paul Schroeder's "External Gravitation" is a push-gravity theory in which space is filled with beams of particles he calls paeps — "particles applying external pressure" — travelling at c and pressing inward on matter from all directions. What we call attraction is the imbalance left when beams passing through a body emerge diminished. This paper applies the framework to a question Schroeder takes to be unanswered: what keeps planets going round. His premise is that "perpetual motion and empty space are now seen as fallacies", so "continuous motion needs a driving force for impetus", and his answer is that the central body's rotation supplies it. Gravity beams exiting the Sun are bent by its rotation, acquire a sideways component, and push the planets along their orbits. From this, he says, comes the explanation of Kepler's third law — which he describes as having "worked for the first 6 planets". The paper opens with a numbered list of thirty-one propositions setting out the wider theory, in which magnetism is the net of redirected beams, the strong force is "simply the sum of gravitation pushing from all directions", electrons are beam crossings, charge is the direction of flow, and the solar wind is solar gravity beams passing the Earth. The argument Push rather than pull Gravity is pictured as lines rather than fields, because "lines help analyze and contemplate a linear push" while "pressure gradients summarize the situation but inhibit analysis".
