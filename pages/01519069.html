Overview Charles Lucas presents a classical electrodynamic model of elementary particles as continuous loops of charge — "charge-fibers" — twisted and intertwined into stable configurations. It is the second part of a series and a companion to his work on atomic and nuclear structure, and it belongs to the wider Common Sense Science programme, which aims to rebuild physics on nineteenth-century electrodynamics and to dispense with quantum theory, relativity, and the Standard Model. The empirical basis he cites is threefold. Compton published a series of papers in 1917 arguing that hard X-ray and gamma-ray scattering was consistent with thin flexible rings of charge, and derived Owen's law for fluorescent absorption on that model. Winston Bostick, one of Compton's last graduate students, produced plasmoids in plasma-focus devices from the 1950s onward — droplet-like structures built from charge-fibres, stable under a balance of internal forces, and found by scattering to have considerable tensile strength.
