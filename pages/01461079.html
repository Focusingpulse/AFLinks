Ole Knutson

· archived # 1,461,079
"In the spring of 1896, a Norwegian-American dairy farmer named Ole Knutson drove his milk wagon into Stearns County, Minnesota and discovered something that his neighbors had been discovering independently for months without anyone putting it together in a single conversation. The creamery in St. Cloud was testing his milk at eight percent butterfat and paying him accordingly. His neighbor Hans Bergstrom was being tested at seven percent for milk from the same breed of cows on the same feed in the same valley. August Schroeder, three farms north, was testing at nine percent one week and six percent the next with no change in his herd or his management.Ole was a methodical man. He had been a schoolteacher in Norway before he immigrated in 1881 and he retained from that life a habit of writing things down and a suspicion of explanations that required you to ignore evidence. He began collecting the test results from every dairy farmer in his township — going door to door on Sunday afternoons, sitting at kitchen tables, writing numbers in a composition book while the farmer's wife brought coffee and the farmer looked at Ole's accumulated data with the expression of a man seeing clearly for the first time something he had been sensing in the dark for years.The numbers were unambiguous.
