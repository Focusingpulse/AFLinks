LENR

· archived # 1,460,268
LENR (Low-Energy Nuclear Reactions) can be precisely defined as: A class of nuclear reactions occurring in condensed matter systems under specific conditions, characterized by: Generation of excess heat beyond what can be explained by chemical processes Production of nuclear products (including helium, tritium, or transmutation elements) Absence of strong neutron radiation or gamma emissions typical of conventional nuclear reactions Operation at temperatures far below those required for thermonuclear fusion This definition distinguishes LENR from both conventional nuclear fission (which requires radioactive materials and produces radioactive waste) and hot fusion (which requires temperatures of millions of degrees). It also differentiates LENR from purely chemical reactions, which cannot produce the energy densities observed in successful LENR experiments. Multiple theoretical frameworks have been proposed to explain LENR phenomena, with several gaining scientific traction: Quantum Nuclear Effects in Condensed Matter: This framework proposes that quantum effects in metal lattices can overcome the Coulomb barrier that normally prevents nuclear reactions at low temperatures. The metal lattice creates conditions where hydrogen nuclei can achieve sufficient proximity for nuclear interactions without requiring high temperatures.
