Overview A New Theorem in Relational Mechanics is a seven-page paper by Jorge A Guala-Valverde of the Fundación Julio Palacios in Neuquén, Argentina, published in Apeiron vol. 8, no. 3 (2001), pp. 132–138. Its thesis is that gravitational mass and inertial mass are not merely different in value but different in kind — "a primary magnitude" against "a derived, secondary magnitude" — and that the two carry different physical dimensions. The equality m g = m i that general relativity assumes is, on this account, an artefact of a unit convention which conceals a Machian mechanism. The theorem itself is short. From a relation asserted in earlier work, m ik = Φ GV m gk for every particle k in the universe, with Φ GV proportional to the mean gravitational mass density ρ g of the universe, he sums over the N particles in a volume V and divides by V, obtaining ρ i ∝ ρ g ² He reads this as identifying two contributions to inertia: a "cumulative effect" from more galaxies, present in classical and Einsteinian mechanics alike, and a distinct "Machian" effect by which increasing the density of gravitational mass increases the individual inertial mass of each particle. The dimensional argument follows. Writing Newtonian gravitation without G, as F = m g1 m g2 /r², forces [m g ] = M 1/2 L 3/2 T −1 which is exactly Maxwell's dimensional formula for electric charge in electrostatic units, quoted at length. From this Guala-Valverde derives that the MKS and cgs standards of gravitational mass stand in the ratio 31,623 rather than 1,000, and proposes to name them the palacios and the schrödinger .
