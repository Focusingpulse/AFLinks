Title A Model of the Electron Based on Its Magnetic Moment Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Bill Stubbs Keywords magnetic moment, electron , Bohr Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 3 Pages 588-590 Abstract The magnetic moment can be expressed as the product of a 'moment of charge' and a frequency. Using this, the Bohr magneton appears to be the electron magnetic moment generated when the electron is modeled as a hollow sphere. It is shown that, by treating the electron as a small charged particle in a rotating high-speed orbit instead of a hollow sphere, a correction to the hollow-sphere moment of charge approximation appears that produces an electron magnetic moment much closer to the actual value than the Bohr magneton. Overview A Model of the Electron Based on Its Magnetic Moment is a three-page paper by Bill Stubbs of Port St. Lucie, Florida, presented at the 19th conference of the Natural Philosophy Alliance and published in the Proceedings of the NPA volume 9 (2012), pages 588–590. It is written as an account of how the author arrived at a model in 1979, and retains that character throughout: it explains the reasoning, the constraints of working without a good library, and the author's own reservations. The starting observation is dimensional. Magnetic moment is quoted in joules per tesla, and those units reduce to coulomb metres squared per second — a charge times an area, per second. Stubbs reads this as a product of a "moment of charge," the charge analogue of the moment of inertia, and a rotation frequency: µ = I q ν. For a hollow sphere the moment of inertia is (2/3) mr ², so the moment of charge would be (2/3) qr ², and using that for the electron reproduces the Bohr magneton. Since the measured electron moment is 9.28476 × 10⁻²⁴ J/T against a Bohr magneton of 9.27401 × 10⁻²⁴ J/T — a ratio of 1.00116 — Stubbs proposes that the hollow sphere is an approximation.
