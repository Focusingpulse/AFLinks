The Hundred Year Club held its monthly meeting at the Hotel Majestic, Seventy-second street and Central Park West, last evening. Dr. A. L. Wood read a paper, entitled, "The Influence of Water Upon Health and Longevity." The discussion following was opened by Dr. W. R. C. Latson, editor of Health Culture, followed by Mrs. Leon Harvier, the Rev. Joseph Newton Hallock, D. D. Dr. F. E. West, professor of clinical medicine Long Island College Hospital; Dr. Olive F. McCune, Dr. David Allyn Gorton and by Dr. M. L. Holbrook, formerly professor of hygiene, New York Medical College. George W. Dithridge is secretary and Dr. Carleton Simon, president of the club. The Rev. Joseph Newton Hallock, D. D., editor of The Christian Work, said in part: "When I mentioned that pure water was to be the subject of discussion at this meeting, a friend remarked, 'Pure water. Why there's nothing in it.' I think we can easily succeed in showing that there may be altogether too much in it; at least for the health of those who drink it, or use it internally in any way. Why there have been times when our city water, in Brooklyn at least, and I doubt not here in New York, has been so offensive to the sight, taste and smell as to be unfit, even for bathing purposes.
