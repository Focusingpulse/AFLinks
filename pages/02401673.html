GTM2026-main016 Dynamic Pricing Around Thresholds: Asymmetric Adjustment Speeds and Equilibrium Regimes Anton Bondarev1 , Regis Chenavaz2 1 Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University, China 2 Kedge Business School, France anton.bondarev@xjtlu.edu.cn Many pricing environments feature a reference threshold, such as a manufacturer's suggested retail price, a psychological price point, competitor parity, or a regulatory band, around which observed price changes are asymmetric: adjustment is rapid on one side of the threshold but sluggish on the other. Standard continuous-time sticky-price models impose a uniform adjustment speed and are silent on how such asymmetry shapes equilibrium pricing dynamics. We develop a continuous-time oligopoly model that endogenizes this asymmetry by letting the speed at which price adjusts toward its demand-implied target depend on whether the current price is above or below the threshold. This single modification turns an otherwise standard sticky-price differential game into a piecewise-smooth (hybrid) dynamic system. The model delivers a small number of qualitatively distinct results: convergence to a regime-specific steady state, price trapping near the threshold in ever-faster micro-oscillations, and endogenous regime-switching cycles. The results are governed primarily by the threshold location and the asymmetry ratio of adjustment speeds above and below it.
