Title Galilean Simplification (Frames in Relative Movement) Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Autodynamics , Galilean transformation , frames of reference, observer, relativity of motion Published Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Website https://www.autodynamics.org/galilean-simplification/ "Galilean Simplification" , subtitled Frames in Relative Movement , is the first of the two derivations by which Ricardo L Carezani arrives at Autodynamics . Unlike the Frames Derivation , it uses no calculus: it works entirely within the Galilean transformation, and its conclusion is not a formula but a definition of what a physical system is. Overview Carezani sets up three parallel coordinate frames and asks how many of them are doing any work. His answer is that at most two are, and that of those two one is always the observer and the other is always the observed phenomenon. From this he derives the position that the "two frames in relative motion" of special relativity contain one frame too many — the claim that the rest of Autodynamics is built on. The argument Three frames, one redundant With frames F , F ′ and F ″ and a point P whose abscissa in F is x , the Galilean transformation gives x ′ = x + v t and, adding the third frame, x ″ = x + ( v + v 1 ) t . Carezani then argues the redundancy twice over. If P is at rest in F — the configuration used in the relativistic derivation — then an observer in F can never observe P at all, because "self observation of a point is absolutely impossible." Frame F is a geometrical system attached to the object, and "with point P and a geometrical system F linked to it, we have no physics whatsoever." If instead P moves within F at v x , then its coordinate in F ′ is x ′ = x + ( v x + v ) t — an expression Carezani observes is "totally equivalent" to the three-frame result.
