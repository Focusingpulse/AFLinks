Craig Newmark

· archived # 1,458,493
One nerdy programmer with an email list destroyed a $16 billion industry A self-described “nerd patient zero” chose to give away 90% of his potential wealth. And built one of the most influential websites on Earth anyway. Craig Newmark lost his father to lung cancer at 13. His mother, a bookkeeper, moved him and his younger brother into an apartment in New Jersey. He wore taped-together black-rimmed glasses and a pocket protector. Had what he called “social dysfunction.” Struggled to connect with people his entire childhood. Everyone saw a nerdy kid with no future. He didn’t see it that way. Newmark taught himself to code. Worked at IBM for 17 years as a programmer. Never climbed the ladder. Never got the corner office. Just showed up, did his job, and went home. At 40, he moved to San Francisco for a contract job at Charles Schwab. Didn’t know anyone. Felt isolated. Wanted to “cultivate a bit of community.” So he started sending emails to friends. Just a list of local events. Art shows. Tech meetups. Places to meet people. By mid-1995, his email list hit 240 addresses. That was the limit his email program could handle. So he built a simple website instead. Called it Craigslist. It looked like garbage. No design. No features. Just text on a page. People loved it. By the end of 1997, the site was getting a million page views per month. Then Microsoft showed up. They wanted to run banner ads. Pay him real money.
