Lebedev State Research and Development Center for Expertise of Projects and Technologies, Moscow, Russia Abstract Experimental studies of pulse electrical explosion of thin titanium foils in water, with discharge power of ∼0.2 GW are described. Production of a considerable amount of molecular hydrogen is revealed whose origin cannot be explained either by water decompo￾sition or by known chemical reactions. A nuclear mechanism for the formation of the observed molecular hydrogen upon electric explosion is hypothesized. Emphasis is laid on some measurements confirming the hypothesis. © 2011 ISCMNS. Introduction It has long been known that high-current electrical explosions of metal wires in liquid produce strong magnetic fields (H ∼ 1 MGs) and high pulse pressures (P ∼ 105 atm) [1,2]. Meanwhile, there is no doubt that such strong external fields can significantly change the probability of nuclear decays and even the conditions of nuclear stability [3].
