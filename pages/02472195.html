In this article an attempt is made to criticize some of my inventions, notably those which you have described in your issue of August 6, 1890. The writer begins by stating: "The motor depends on a shifting of the poles under certain conditions, a principle which has been already 1 employed by Mr. A. Wright in his alternating current meter." This is no surprise to me. It would rather have surprised me to learn that Mr. Wright has not yet employed the principle in his meter, considering what, before its appearance, was known of my work on motors, and more particularly of that of Schallenberger on meters. It has cost me years of thought to arrive at certain results, by many believed to be unattainable, for which there are now numerous claimants, and the number of these is rapidly increasing, like that of the colonels in the South after the war.
