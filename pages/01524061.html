The Cosmic Microwave Background CMB Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Harry Hamlin Ricker Date 5 October 2024 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording The Cosmic Microwave Background CMB was a live session presented by Harry Hamlin Ricker on 5 October 2024 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The CMB will be discussed from its discovery, and interpretation, to modern satellite measurements including COBE, WMAP, and Plank. Summary Having given a session on Lambda-CDM cosmology, Harry Ricker found he could not answer a simple question — what exactly is the cosmic microwave background? — and set out to trace it from the theory to the satellites. His conclusion is about the story rather than the physics: the standard account is constructed by hindsight, and the details do not fit it. The session began half an hour late after StreamYard problems forced him to restart his computer. Who actually predicted it The textbooks say Gamow predicted the background and that Penzias and Wilson confirmed it by accident. Ricker went to the sources and found otherwise. Gamow's 1946 paper contains nothing about microwave background radiation; it postulates neutrons decaying into protons and electrons in a hot early state. Alpher and Herman's 1948 paper, which corrects Gamow, gives about 5 K — but for the remnant gas , not for any radiation. The prediction Ricker credits comes from the Princeton group. Dicke's own account has the hot cosmology in disfavour — probably, Ricker thought, because it could not synthesise the heavier elements — and revived by Hoyle and Tayler's work on helium around 1962. What the Princeton group added, and what is now forgotten, is that their motivation was an oscillating universe: a closed universe contracting to a hot bounce and re-expanding, which avoids having to assume the energy of the universe at t = 0.
