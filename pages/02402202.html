GTM2026-main060 Personalized persuasion in the era of AI: The role of various types of human data in LLM-generated arguments. Olga Zalesskaia1 , Ilya Sukharev2 , Arseniy Khlytchiev1 , Dina Rusinova1 , Ilya Astapenko3 , Yan Tashe2 , Olga Dymarskaya4 , Evgeniya Lukinova5 , Alexander Gubanov1 , Alexander Petrov2 , Ivan Kozitsin1 1 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (State University) 2 V. A. Trapeznikov Institute of Control Sciences of Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia 3 Russian Presidential Academy of National Economy and Public Administration, Russia 4 National Research University Higher School of Economics in Saint Petersburg, Russia 5 University of Nottingham, United Kingdom zalesskaia.oiu@phystech.edu With the development of generative artificial intelligence and the emergence of large language models (LLMs), a new threat of adaptive informational influence on individual opinions on social media arises. We present the pilot results of a human-LLM debate experiment. In this experiment, we prompt an LLM to persuade a human subject using available information on this subject. We manipulate what kind of information is digested to the LLM demographic, psychometric, textual, etc. Our main objective is to identify which types of human data enable LLMs to generate the most persuasive arguments. Our results will make it possible to understand the informative value of various types of user data for personalized persuasion, which in the future can be used to develop protection mechanisms against destructive AI-influence in the online domain.
