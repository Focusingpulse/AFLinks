Lee Smolin Born ( 1955-06-06 ) June 6, 1955 (age 71) Residence Toronto, Ontario, Canada Nationality American Known for Quantum gravity , Loop quantum gravity, Cosmological natural selection, critique of String Theory Scientific career Fields Theoretical physics Lee Smolin (born June 6, 1955) is an American theoretical physicist and a founding faculty member of the Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics in Waterloo, Ontario. He is an adjunct professor of physics at the University of Waterloo and a member of the graduate faculty of the philosophy department at the University of Toronto. Smolin is a mainstream physicist best known for his work on quantum gravity and for his widely-read books criticising the dominance of String Theory in fundamental physics and calling for a greater diversity of approaches — a theme that has made his work frequently cited in dissident-physics circles. Education Study — Hampshire College Ph.D. in theoretical physics — Harvard University, 1979 Biography He held postdoctoral positions at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, the Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics in Santa Barbara, and the University of Chicago, and was a faculty member at Yale, Syracuse and Pennsylvania State universities before becoming one of the founding members of the Perimeter Institute in 2001. Research Smolin has worked mainly on approaches to quantum gravity that do not depend on string theory. He was one of the initiators of loop quantum gravity and of deformed (or "doubly") special relativity . He is also known for the idea of cosmological natural selection , a proposal in which universes reproduce through the formation of black holes and their physical parameters are "selected" for producing many black holes.
