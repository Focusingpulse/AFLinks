Adolphe Martin Died 2007 Residence Montreal, QU, Canada Nationality Canadian Adolphe Martin (died 2007) was a Canadian independent researcher based in Montreal, Quebec, who developed a kinetic ether theory in which all of physics is reduced to the mechanics of a universal hard-sphere gas. The cosmonic gas Martin's framework, set out in a series of papers from the early 1990s onward, posits a gas filling the whole of space — including the interiors of elementary particles — whose constituents he named cosmons . Cosmons are given the barest possible properties: a definite diameter, zero spin, no internal structure, no rest mass and no fields, with absolute void between them. They interact only by elastic encounter, exchanging the velocity component along the line of centres, so that physics at that level is reduced to pure kinematics and the familiar quantities — mass, momentum, energy — appear only as statistical properties of the gas. From this substrate he derived accounts of elementary particles as structures in the gas, of electromagnetism as its mechanical behaviour, and of gravitation as a pressure effect arising from the excluded-volume term in the equation of state. The associated treatment of light propagation is Galilean rather than relativistic, and two of his papers address the reception of light signals in Galilean space-time. Publications and conferences His work appeared in Apeiron and in the Apeiron volume Einstein and Poincaré: the physical vacuum (2005), and he presented at the Frontiers of Fundamental Physics conference in 1993, at the International Conference on Special Relativity and Some of its Applications in 1997, and at the 7th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in 2000. His last recorded address was 10299 avenue Bois de Boulogne, Montreal; earlier, 2235 Brébeuf, Longueuil, Quebec.
