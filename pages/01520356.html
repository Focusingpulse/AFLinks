How the magnetic field works Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Date 10 January 2021 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording How the magnetic field works was a live session on 10 January 2021 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. This will describe what a magnetic field is made out of and how it causes the deflection of charges it a very mechanical level. Summary A recorded presentation by Franklin Hu giving a mechanical account of the magnetic field, drawn from a paper newly posted on his site. He opened with the observation that the magnetic force is one of the most mysterious in nature and that the mainstream, pressed on what the force actually is , would shrug: here is how it behaves, here is the mathematics, and that is as far as it goes. His own answer is offered with an explicit disclaimer — none of it will be found in a textbook, it is his theory, and since there is little agreement anyway his idea may be as good as any other. He also said plainly why he was re-recording: a few weeks earlier, arguing about induction by email, he had found a problem that his long-standing version of the theory simply could not accommodate, and had had to rebuild it. What a field is made of He began with the word. A field of corn is an area containing corn plants; a magnetic field, he suggested, is better thought of as a field of flags with a wind blowing through it, all pointing one way. Scatter compasses around a magnet and they line up and trace out what we call the field — so he takes the field to be an actual physical arrangement of things that point, and the deflection of an electron to be the mechanical result of its striking them, as ping-pong balls thrown at flags are thrown aside. What plays the part of the flags is his answer to what space is made of.
