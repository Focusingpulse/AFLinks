The effect was predicted in 1948 by the Dutch physicist Hendrik Casimir, then at Philips Research Laboratories in Eindhoven, in the course of work with Dirk Polder on the forces between neutral atoms. Casimir's argument is that the presence of the plates restricts which electromagnetic field modes can exist in the gap: only wavelengths that fit between the plates survive, while the region outside supports the full continuum.
