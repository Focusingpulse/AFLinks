A great sensation was produced 10 or 12 years ago, when a prominent American electrician, Nikola Tesla, exposed himself to the effects of a current whose pressure was said to be a million volts. Well, there may have been some exaggeration in that statement, observes the New York Tribune, but there seems to be no question that the voltage was far beyond what was then regarded the limit of safety. A trolley current has a pressure of between 500 and 600 volts, and, while it cannot well prove fatal, it is a thing to be treated respectfully. For the execution of the death penalty, at Sing Sing and Auburn prisons, it is customary to employ only about 1,500 or 1,800 volts. Now, there were two things about Tesla’s experiment which, in combination, afforded comparative immunity. One was that he did not use the continuous, or direct, current, but an alternating current. That is to say, the electricity first flowed in one direction and then in the opposite, the changes occurring with extraordinary frequency. If they had been produced at the rate of only one or two or 25 or 30 per second, serious harm would have resulted.
