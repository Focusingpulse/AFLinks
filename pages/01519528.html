Computational Electromagnetism with Moving Matter Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Halim Boutayeb Date 24 May 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Computational Electromagnetism with Moving Matter was a live session presented by Halim Boutayeb on 24 May 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The analysis of electromagnetic problems with moving objects has many applications: RF Doppler radars, astrophysics, GPS, electromagnetic gyroscopes… This seminar proposes an original and thorough analysis of the behavior of electromagnetic waves in the presence of moving bodies by using the Finite Difference Time Domain (FDTD) method. Hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary Halim Boutayeb 's first talk to the society, and the fullest account of his programme: solve Maxwell's equations numerically in the presence of moving bodies, using no transformations at all, and see what comes out. He dedicated it to the memory of Wolfgang Engelhardt , with whom he had discussed classical electromagnetism and relativity on ResearchGate between 2016 and 2019. Equations have a context He opened with two preliminaries that carry the whole argument. The first: an equation is written with a context in mind. Measure a building's height as it rises, h = h 0 + α t , and one may of course invert it to give t as a function of h — but nobody would conclude that time stops when construction stops. Time was the input, not the output. The second: auxiliary variables are not physical quantities. Plotting free-fall distance against ½ t 2 to read the acceleration off the slope is convenient, and the quantity plotted could be called anything at all without acquiring a meaning.
