Silly. New York Sun August 11th, 1912 Page number(s): 11 Dr. Humphries So Characterizes Proposed Tesla Plan. Dr. H. R. Humphries, neurologist at the Post Graduate Hospital, does not think very highly of Nikola Tesla's scheme to improve the mental condition of defective children by electric stimulation. As a neurologis for ten years at the Vanderbilt Clinic and for five years at the State Hospital for the Insane, Dr. Humphries has had experience with mental diseases and he feels very strongly on this particular subject. "As I understand the proposed scheme," he said, "the plan is to charge the walls of the schoolroom where the defective children are with electricity and so to set up in the air a high grade frequency current. It is believed that this current will stimulate the cerebral cells of the defective child and so improve mentality. The proposition is silly. "In my opinion, based upon years of ob- servations and experiment, there would be no value in such a proceeding. If we thought there was any value in it we could supply every defective child at our hospitals with a skull cap in which could be set up such a high grade frequency current, but we know that there would be no use in trying it. That would be to overestimate the therapeutic value of electricity.
