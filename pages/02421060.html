Alan L. Francoeur (the surname is sometimes rendered Francouer , including in the 2004 Tesla Tech conference programme) is a Canadian independent inventor and researcher based in Penticton, British Columbia. He is known for the Interference Disc electric generator, for a stainless-steel fuel vaporizer system for internal combustion engines, and for his work collecting and reverse-engineering the surviving hardware of Edwin Gray's EMA motor technology. He works entirely outside academic and corporate research institutions. Biography Francoeur has described conceiving the interference-disc concept in 1985 while working in the mining industry at Yellowknife, Northwest Territories, and building and testing a first prototype in a Calgary, Alberta machine shop in the summer of 1986. He has stated that the development consumed several decades of part-time research and roughly $35,000 of his own funding. He trades as Magnetic Energy Systems (also given as EMA Magnetic Energy Systems Inc.), through which he develops and demonstrates his generator and vaporizer work. Alongside the energy research he has described a broader interest in self-sufficiency, including greenhouse horticulture for year-round food growing in colder Canadian regions. Francoeur is unusual among independent energy researchers in that he has published detailed construction specifications for his generator rather than keeping them proprietary, stating that he released the material publicly in order to advance the field.
