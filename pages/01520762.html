Nikola Tesla On His Work With Alternating Currents and Their Application to Wireless Telegraphy, Telephony, and Transmission of Power: An Extended Interview Book Author Leland I Anderson (editor), Nikola Tesla Published 1992; 2002 printing Publisher Twenty First Century Books Pages 240 ISBN 1893817016 Nikola Tesla On His Work With Alternating Currents and Their Application to Wireless Telegraphy, Telephony, and Transmission of Power: An Extended Interview is the first volume of the Tesla Presents series, edited by Leland I Anderson and published by Twenty-First Century Books. It is not a book Tesla wrote. It is the transcript of a three-day interview he gave in 1916 to his own legal counsel, in preparation for expert testimony in the impending radio patent cases — an account never intended for publication, and therefore one of the least guarded technical statements he ever made. Its value to researchers is as a primary source. Speaking to his lawyers rather than to the press or a lecture audience, Tesla traces his own work in sequence: from the first high-frequency alternators built at his Grand Street laboratory in New York and their associated tuned circuits, through the Colorado Springs experiments, to the Wardenclyffe plant at Shoreham, Long Island. The Tesla researcher James F Corum has described it as a "veritable Rosetta stone" for tracing the technical thought of one of the most distinguished engineering scientists. Background By 1916 the priority of Tesla's wireless work was a matter for the courts rather than the journals, and his counsel needed his account on the record. The interview was conducted as preparation for expert testimony, which shapes the document in two ways that matter to a reader. First, its purpose was legal rather than expository: the questioning drives at what was built, when, and in what order, so the text is unusually specific about dates, apparatus and priority.
