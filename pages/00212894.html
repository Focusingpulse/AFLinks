On the Existence of a Prime Number in the Interval [n 2 , n2 + n/2] Hashem Sazegar Department of Mathematics Islamic Azad University Branch of Mashhad, Iran. email:h.sazegar@gmail.com Abstract Oppermann’s conjecture states that for every positive integer n, there exists at least one prime number between n 2 and n 2 + n. Prior to this, Legendre had conjectured that there is always at least one prime number between n 2 and (n + 1)2 . In this paper, we not only claim to prove Oppermann’s conjecture but also propose a smaller interval, asserting that there exists at least one prime between n 2 and n 2 + n/2. Keywords: Bertrand–Chebyshev theorem, Landau’s problems, Gold￾bach’s conjecture, twin primes. 1 Introduction Bertrand stated that for every positive integer n, there is always at least one prime between n and 2n [1]. The first proof was given by Chebyshev in 1850, now known as the Bertrand–Chebyshev theorem [2]. Ramanujan later provided a simpler proof in 1919 using properties of the Gamma function [3]. In 1932, Erd˝os gave an elementary proof using the Chebyshev function and binomial properties[4]. Legendre’s conjecture states that for every positive integer n, there is at least one prime between n 2 and (n + 1)2 [5, 6]. Landau highlighted this as one of his famous problems [7].
