The Consistency Between Statistical Entropy and Kolmogorov-Sinai Entropy of a Stellar Three-Body System Author: Zhi Cheng Email: gzchengzhi@hotmail.com Abstract We propose a macroscopic "social thermodynamics" framework in which three solar-mass stars, each composed of hydrogen molecules, are treated as gas molecules of a new statistical ensemble. A "social Boltzmann constant" is constructed by multiplying the ordinary Boltzmann constant by the number of hydrogen molecules in a single star. The social temperature and entropy of the three-body system are then calculated using the kinetic theory of gases. Remarkably, this social entropy increase, arising from the free expansion of stellar positions from a confined initial volume to the full accessible volume, is shown to be quantitatively consistent with the entropy production predicted by the Kolmogorov-Sinai (KS) entropy of the underlying chaotic three-body dynamics. Using a concrete numerical example, we find that the total entropy increase computed by the two independent methods agrees within the same order of magnitude (∼ 1036 J/K). This agreement reveals a deep connection between the statistical thermalization of a self-gravitating few-body system and the chaotic phase-space mixing characterized by Lyapunov exponents. 1. Introduction The gravitational three-body problem is a paradigm of deterministic chaos. Minute differences in initial conditions diverge exponentially, characterized by positive Lyapunov exponents [1-3] . This chaotic mixing drives the system towards an effectively random, ergodic-like state on long timescales. On the other hand, statistical thermodynamics describes the irreversible evolution of many￾body systems towards maximum entropy. For systems with a huge number of particles, the connection between microscopic chaos and macroscopic irreversibility is well established [4-5] .
