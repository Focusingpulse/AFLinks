Title SN 1987A and the Neutrino Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords SN 1987A, Neutrino, Supernova, Kamiokande, IMB, Mont Blanc, Autodynamics Published Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Website https://www.autodynamics.org/sn-1987a/ "SN 1987A and the Neutrino" is a paper by Ricardo L Carezani challenging the neutrino detections associated with the supernova of February 1987 in the Large Magellanic Cloud — the observation generally regarded as the founding event of neutrino astronomy. Carezani sets out seven numbered "facts" against it. Overview Carezani begins from a published summary of the evidence: "The coincidence between the two results provides OVERWHELMING evidence that the neutrinos, formed at the instant the supernova's core collapsed, did INDEED come from this star". His objection is first to the form of the argument — "We suppose what we are trying to prove: neutrinos!" — and then to the data behind it. The argument The seven facts Fact 1. Of four detectors then operating, one — Mont Blanc — recorded the first claimed burst, at 02:52:36.8 UT. IMB, Kamiokande II and the South Dakota experiment did not. Twenty-five per cent agreement, Carezani argues, is not overwhelming evidence. Fact 2. Even granting simultaneity between Mont Blanc and Kamiokande II, two of four detectors saw nothing, so the agreement is at best 50 per cent. Fact 3. Producing the assumed flux from 180,000 light years away requires converting 10 to 15 per cent of a solar mass to neutrinos. No mechanism for this is supplied, and the mechanism would differ from anything known in stars. Fact 4. To explain neutrinos arriving at Kamiokande II twelve seconds after the first burst, a "neutrino sphere" some tens of kilometres in radius is invoked to trap and release them.
