Title Mechanical Dimensionalities of Electrodynamic and Gravidynamic Fields Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Jaroslav G Klyushin Keywords fields, electric field, permittivity, density, Ether Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 3 Pages 257-259 Abstract A system of mechanical dimensionalities is proposed for electrodynamics. It turns out that permittivity has the dimension of mass density. Therefore, the electric permittivity constant is interpreted as ether mass density. The vacuum magnetic permeability has dimension of compressibility, and it is therefore interpreted as free ether compressibility. The electric field has dimension of velocity. That enables us to compare its qualities with those of gravity, which has dimension of acceleration. Overview Mechanical Dimensionalities of Electrodynamic and Gravidynamic Fields is a three-page paper by Jaroslav G Klyushin of the University of Civil Aviation, St. Petersburg, presented at the 17th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference and published in Proceedings of the NPA volume 7 (2010), pages 257–259. Its motivation is stated clearly and is a reasonable one. "A system of dimensionalities is actually the language in which we express our ideas in physics," and different branches use different languages, so that connections between them are obscured. Newtonian mechanics works in mass, metres and seconds; thermodynamics introduces temperature and heat as separate concepts; and electrodynamics is worse still, with several competing systems in circulation. Klyushin's aim is to express electrodynamic quantities in purely mechanical dimensions and thereby clarify the relations among electrodynamics, gravity and hydrodynamics. His starting observation is that the gravitational field g = γ M / r ² has dimensions of acceleration, since the gravitational constant carries m³/kg·s².
