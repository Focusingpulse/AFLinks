Nathan Stubblefield Born ( 1860-11-22 ) November 22, 1860 Died March 28, 1928 (1928-03-28) (aged 67) Residence Murray, Kentucky, United States Nationality American Known for The earth battery; early wireless telephony Nathan Beverly Stubblefield (22 November 1860 – 28 March 1928) was an American inventor and self-taught electrician of Murray, Kentucky, remembered for two things: a claim to priority in wireless voice transmission, and the earth battery that powered it. The earth battery Stubblefield's ground cell consisted of coils of wire buried in damp soil, drawing a continuous current from the electrolytic action of moisture and minerals in the earth. The claim that made it interesting to later researchers is that the output appeared disproportionate to any ordinary chemical action, and the device has been studied ever since by workers on unconventional energy, John Bedini among them. His coil is compared directly with the Rodin coil in work catalogued here: Rodin Coil vs Stubblefield Coil (2011 conference) and Rodin Coil vs Stubblefield Coil (2012 conference) . Wireless telephony Stubblefield began experimenting with wireless voice transmission around 1892 and gave public demonstrations in early 1902 of a battery-operated wireless telephone, portable enough to be used from boats. His early apparatus worked by conduction rather than radiation; the patent he eventually received in 1908 covered a wireless telephone system using magnetic induction . The patent was delayed in part because the trunk containing his apparatus was lost on a journey to Washington and he had to build another. He died in poverty and obscurity in 1928. Murray State University holds a collection of his papers.
