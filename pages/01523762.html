Title Relativity and the Formation of Black Holes Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Paul Marmet Keywords Black Holes , Schwarzschild radius, Big Bang cosmology Published 1990 Journal Apeiron Volume 1 Number 7 No. of pages 6 Pages 20-27 Abstract In order to form Black Holes, matter has to move across the Schwarzschild radius. We demonstrate here that according to Einstein general relativity, matter cannot have the time to form a Black Hole when we consider either the proper time or the Schwarzschild time. Black Holes are incompatible with the time-limited Big Bang cosmology. Overview Relativity and the Formation of Black Holes is a six-page paper by Paul Marmet , then at the Herzberg Institute of Astrophysics of the National Research Council of Canada, published in Apeiron in the summer of 1990. It argues that general relativity itself forbids black holes from forming within the age of a finite-age universe, so that "Black Holes cannot exist unless the Big Bang theory is erroneous and the universe is infinite". The argument runs through two standard equations from Misner, Thorne and Wheeler's Gravitation . For matter falling radially onto a mass, the proper time τ of the falling body is related to the radius by r = (9M/2) 1/3 (α − τ) 2/3 , reaching r = 0 at the finite proper time τ = α; the Schwarzschild coordinate time t, meanwhile, diverges as r approaches the Schwarzschild radius 2M. Marmet notes correctly that for a radially infalling particle the two are related by dt/dτ = (1 − 2M/r) −1 , and observes that the falling observer crosses in moments while the distant observer never sees the crossing at all. He then makes the argument quantitative with a worked figure. Taking a star that forms "in 10 million years in proper time", he synchronises the two clocks at large r and follows the infall.
