Re-interpreting the experiments that formed the base of Special Relativity. Summary John Hodge supplied a recorded video applying the Scalar Theory of Everything to the observations on which special relativity was built, and intended to answer questions afterwards. His connection failed repeatedly before, during and after the recording; he was never able to speak for more than a few seconds, sent no answers to the chat, and the session was ended early. Roger J Anderton hosted, with Franklin Hu filling in. The video Hodge's method is to go back to the nineteenth-century experiments — Fizeau's on the index of refraction of moving water, stellar aberration, and Michelson–Morley — and to read the data rather than the conclusions drawn from them. Fizeau. Light speed increases with the flow and decreases against it. Hodge calls this carry-along rather than drag: the speed of light depends on the local plenum density, greater density giving greater tension and a higher speed, so a photon moving with the fluid spends more of its time on the relative peak and one moving against it in the relative valley. Michelson–Morley. Hodge put up the graph from the original paper and made an observation about its shape. The differences are not in the east–west direction, where the result is very nearly null, but in the directions corresponding to the Sun–Moon combination — which, he suggested tentatively, makes the residual a tidal effect rather than a motion-through-the-ether effect, and another instance of carry-along from nearby bodies: the table, the room, the Sun and the Moon.
