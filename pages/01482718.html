psilocybin

· archived # 1,482,718
A recent Oregon pilot study, led by former NHL player Daniel Carcillo, gave psilocybin to eight athletes (boxers, MMA fighters, BJJ practitioners, surfers, bobsledders) with long-term concussion symptoms. Using EEG scans and cognitive tests, researchers saw more balanced frontal lobe activity, stronger P3 signals (better focus and decisions), and drops in depression, anxiety and PTSD. The changes suggest improved energy distribution in the brain, hinting psilocybin may repair metabolic dysfunction (glucose disruption, mitochondrial damage, inflammation) caused by TBI, not just neurons. Emerging research links psilocybin to metabolic pathways, inflammation, and gut microbiome shifts, pointing to whole-body repair rather than symptom masking. The paper isn't peer-reviewed and the sample is tiny. But coordinator Nige Netzband called it compelling evidence of recovery. For millions with mild TBI, blast injuries, or treatment-resistant depression, the implications could be enormous. We're watching mushrooms become medicine in real time.
