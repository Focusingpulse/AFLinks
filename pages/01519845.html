Title Electromagnetic Wave Energy and Time Read in full Link to paper Author(s) John Linus O'Sullivan Keywords Cosmology, Space-Time, standing waves Published 2015 No. of pages 3 Electromagnetic Wave Energy and Time is a 2015 paper by John Linus O'Sullivan proposing that space and its contents arise from two distinct kinds of energy — a finite energy that carries time, and an infinite energy that does not — and that time itself exists only as the transfer of finite energy. It is part of the author's wider effort to give a single wave-based account of mass, light and gravity. The model is the author's own and is not part of accepted physics. Overview O'Sullivan argues that space is built from two types of energy. The first is finite energy — energy that is bound up with time. In his scheme this is mass-energy, tied to light and gravity and generated by "open-ended standing waves." Finite energy is held to expand as mass in the electric field, while light and gravity act in the magnetic field; gravitational forces located at the anti-nodes of the magnetic field are what shape the mass-energy waves in the electric field, always at the constant speed of light. Photons, in this picture, are the particle aspect of the magnetic-field waves, carrying energy according to Planck's relation E = hf . The second is infinite energy — energy without time. O'Sullivan defines infinity as energy that has no beginning and no end, toward which the open-ended standing waves are always moving as they approach static equilibrium. The speed of light is presented as the common constant linking the finite and the infinite. The central conclusion is that time does not exist outside of finite energy : time is simply the transfer of finite energy, so where there is no finite energy there is no time.
