PDF Print Share Send link monoatomic gas Why do monoatomic gases have an unusually low heat capacity? Unlike water which has a very HIGH heat capacity, noble gases like Helium , Neon & Argon have a very LOW one. But why? What are monoatomic gases? They are single, unbonded atoms. No molecules, no hydrogen bonds, no complex structure. Just He, Ne, Ar flying freely. The Secret: 3 Degrees of freedom only Water has to break its extensive hydrogen bond network to get hot. That takes a HUGE amount of energy. Monatomic gases have NO bonds to break, NO rotation, NO vibration . They have only translational ( oscillation ) movements (x, y, z). When you add heat, 10% of the energy goes directly into making the atom move faster. Temperature shoots up instantly. Result: Very little energy is stored. Molar heat capacity is only compared to water's Water is like a sponge for heat, monatomic gases are like a dry stone — they heat up super fast! See Also inert gas Created by Dale Pond.
