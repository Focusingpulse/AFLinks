Martin Residence Boulder, CO, United States Known for Analysis of spatial flow theories of gravity, and the hollow shell Cavendish experiment proposed and performed to test them Scientific career Fields Gravitation , general relativity Thomas D. Martin , who publishes as Tom Martin , is an American researcher in gravitation based in Boulder, Colorado. His work concerns spatial flow theories of gravity — accounts in which gravitation is represented as the motion of an underlying physical substratum, the class to which the Painlevé–Gullstrand form of the Schwarzschild solution belongs — and it is unusual in this literature for being pursued in both directions: he develops the flow picture as a complement to general relativity , and he then designs and performs an experiment whose result tells against one of its assumptions. Work Two complementary approaches Martin's programme is set out in General Relativity and Spatial Flows: I. Absolute Relativistic Dynamics (2000), which treats the standard approach to relativistic physics and the substratum-flow approach as two complementary and equally important accounts rather than as rivals. Reviewing and extending earlier results on the flow approach, he relates it more closely to the formalism of general relativity and derives an absolute relativistic dynamics in which energy and momentum take on absolute significance with respect to the substratum — and notes possible new effects on satellites. The mathematical groundwork appears in On the Motion of Free Material Test Particles in Arbitrary Spatial Flows (1998), which shows that the motion of free test particles in an arbitrary flow can be determined within ordinary vector calculus.
