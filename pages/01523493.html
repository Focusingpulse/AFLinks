Title NASA Explores Space Travel Concepts Author(s) Charles A Yost Keywords alternative propulsion, Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Workshop, Casimir effect, gravity, inertial electrostatic confinement, Marc Millis, NASA, rotating superconductors, quantum tunneling, warp drive, zero-point energy Published 1998 Journal Electric Spacecraft Journal Number 23 No. of pages 19 Pages 20-38 Abstract NASA's first Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Workshop took place August 12-14, 1997 at NASA's Lewis Research Center in Cleveland, OH. It was organized by Marc Millis to survey revolutionary concepts for space travel. An outline of the proceedings and summaries of the papers presented follows. Overview This is a report, not a research paper: Charles Yost used nineteen pages of his own journal to tell its readers what NASA had just done. The occasion was unusual enough to deserve it. For three days in August 1997 a United States government laboratory convened a meeting on warp drives, wormholes, vacuum energy and antigravity, published the results as a NASA conference publication, and set out to decide which of them were worth funding. Yost was well placed to report it, and not only as an editor. He attended, and contributed a poster paper of his own — "Electric Field Propulsion Concepts from Independent Researchers" — which carried into a NASA proceedings the sort of work ESJ existed to publish. The article that appeared in issue 23 the following March gives the outline of the proceedings and summarises the papers presented. The workshop The Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Workshop was held at the NASA Lewis Research Center in Cleveland, Ohio, on 12–14 August 1997, chaired by Marc G. Millis of Lewis, with Richard Ziegfeld as logistics chair.
