Saturday Science Chats sponsored by the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Dennis Allen , Ian Cohen, Bill Lucas and James Keene , prompted by a popular article on warp drive in the week's physics headlines. Is faster-than-light travel possible? Hu put the question round the room before showing the article. Ian Cohen had no objection in principle: the speed of light is an arbitrary speed much as the speed of sound in air is, being the speed of electromagnetic radiation in the ether, and there is no reason nothing should exceed it. He drew a distinction that ran through the session — between exceeding c by orders of magnitude, which is the science-fiction case, and exceeding it slightly, which needs only a refractive index below unity. His own example is the total internal reflection of radio waves at the ionosphere, where the phase speed exceeds c by a few per cent. He also holds, as a standing position, that gravitational interaction propagates far faster than light. Bill Lucas framed it as a question about the medium: c reflects the tensile strength of the electromagnetic field, as measured by Hooper , so the question is whether that strength can differ — and he has seen no answer. Dennis Allen brought a 1995 Galilean Electrodynamics paper by a NACA physicist whose manuscript had been supplied by his daughter, arguing that classical physics extended without curved spacetime reproduces the results credited to general relativity. Its relevant claim is a distinction he thought decisive: a body cannot be accelerated past c by forces applied from without , but may be by propulsion from within .
