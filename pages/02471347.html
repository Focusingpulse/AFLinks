Niagara Falls Gazette June 9th, 1931 Page number(s): 6 LAFAYETTE, Ind., June 9 — Two million volts of flashing, crackling, burning electricity pass through a man — and he lives to try it again! C. E. Fisher, holding rod, and W. P. Hallstein, at the controls, are shown here “playing” with 2,000,000 volts of electricity. This voltage is a thousand times that used for execution in the electric chair, but C. C. Fisher, of Sheridan, and W. P. Hallstein, Indianapolis, repeatedly take such a charge as one of the experiments with the Tesla coil, work which they have been conducting as a thesis for graduation from the school of electrical engineering at Purdue University. The investigators say that allowing this voltage to pass through the body produces only a slight tingling sensation, less than the penny arcade shocking machine, and leaves no ill effects. Yet there is power enough in the charge of electricity which they take to ignite a stick of wood or to break a half-inch plate of glass when placed in the five-foot electric spark which is discharged into the air from the large coil. The apparatus consists of a condenser, transformer, and two large inductance coils, so arranged that they “step up” the power of an ordinary 110-volt electric light circuit to the extremely high potential of two million volts.
