Submitted to Nature 14 August 2020, accepted 24 May 2021 The innermost regions of accretion disks around black holes are strongly irradiated by X-rays that are emitted from a highly variable, compact corona, in the immediate vicinity of the black hole [1, 2, 3]. The X-rays that are seen reflected from the disk [4] and the time delays, as variations in the X-ray emission echo or ‘reverberate’ off the disk [5, 6] provide a view of the environment just outside the event horizon. I Zwicky 1 (I Zw 1), is a nearby narrow line Seyfert 1 galaxy [7, 8]. Previous studies of the reverberation of X-rays from its accretion disk revealed that the corona is composed of two components; an extended, slowly varying component over the surface of the inner accretion disk, and a collimated core, with luminosity fluctuations propagating upwards from its base, which dominates the more rapid variability [9, 10].
