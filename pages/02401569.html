GC2026-STP060 Role of the Solar Wind v z -component in Substorm Initiation Marina Kubyshkina1 , Vladimir Semenov1 , Igor Kubyshkin1 1 St Petersburg University m.kubyshkina@spbu.ru The question of when and why a substorm begins remains not fully resolved to this day. Although it is well established that the primary trigger for the explosive phase is the accumulation of a critical magnitude of magnetic flux in the magnetotail, there exist no universal quantitative criterion for this critical value. In this work, we examined how the amount of magnetic flux accumulated before the onset of the explosive phase depends on the dipole tilt angle, and how it is affected by the direction of the solar wind (SW) velocity. For this study we used the OMNI webpage data with 5-minutes averages from 1995 till 2017 and a substorm list for the same period from SUPERMAG cite. We have confirmed that at large dipole tilt angles, the onset of a substorm — identified by a sharp decrease in the SML index — occurs at a lower accumulated magnetic flux. Under the nonzero transverse component of solar wind velocity the distant magnetotail follows the SW flow direction, and the effective dipole becomes larger or smaller by a value equal to atan(v z /v x ). Therefore, if the v z -component of the solar wind velocity increases the effective dipole tilt angle, substorm onset happens earlier under a smaller magnetic flux input.
