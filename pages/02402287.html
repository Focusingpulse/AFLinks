MolPhot2026-main044 IR spectra of nitrogen adsorbed on zeolites Yan Kotik , Alexey Tsyganenko1 1 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia atsyg@yandex.ru Nitrogen is used for the specific surface area measurements as a gas physically adsorbed on all the surfaces. Nevertheless, on oxides it forms a weak coordinate bond with unsaturated surface metal ions [1], where the band of induced absorption is slightly shifted to the higher wavenumbers. With hydroxyl groups of silica or zeolites linear H-bonded complexes are formed whose linear structure is supported by intensity measurements of the bands of simultaneous transitions [2]. When adsorbed on silicalite, where cationic or hydroxyl sites are absent, considerable amount of nitrogen not visible in IR spectra is adsorbed on the framework walls. However when adsorbed on HZSM-5 zeolites with protons substituted by NO+ ions, where the hydroxyls or metal cations are also absent, the band of nitrogen was clearly seen. In our work we measured the integrated absorption coefficient ε of nitrogen following the method described previously [3] in order to obtain information on the nature of nitrogen interaction with the surface. The obtained values decrease in the series ZnY, HZSM-5, NO+ZSM-5, aerosil, silicalite, and are equal to 0.78, 0.021, 0.011, 0.004, and 0.005 cm/μmol, respectively. The intensity of the bands of induced absorption is proportional to the square of electrostatic field tension of a surface site and the derivative of polarizability on the normal coordinate, which depends on molecule orientation with respect to t
