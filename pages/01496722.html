three-body problem

· archived # 1,496,722
The three-body problem has a habit of humbling geniuses. It resisted Isaac Newton, tangled up Henri Poincaré (who, in trying to solve it, ended up laying the foundations of chaos theory), and stayed stubbornly unsolved for centuries. Then in 2000, Alain Chenciner and Richard Montgomery uncovered something unexpected: a perfectly choreographed figure 8—three bodies chasing each other forever in a delicate gravitational dance. By 2013, Milan Šuvakov and Veljko Dmitrašinović expanded the landscape, discovering 13 more families of these strange, stable motions. Every clip you’re seeing is a raw numerical integration of F = G·m₁m₂ / r²—no tricks, no shortcuts. Just gravity, time, and precision. More than three centuries after Newton wrote down his laws, the universe is still revealing hidden choreographies inside them. [extract: From Infinity with Love]
