James Doohan

· archived # 1,460,017
In 1990, the dean of the Milwaukee School of Engineering faced a fascinating dilemma. After surveying the incoming freshman class, the results were nothing short of extraordinary. Nearly half of the students credited the same person for inspiring their career choice. It wasn't a Nobel laureate, a famous inventor, or even a real engineer. It was a fictional character from a science fiction series. James Doohan had spent years portraying Montgomery "Scotty" Scott, the miracle-working chief engineer of the USS Enterprise. Week after week, viewers watched Scotty save the ship from impossible odds, coaxing "more power" from the engines and solving technical crises with sheer ingenuity and a thick Scottish accent. What Doohan hadn't realized was that his performance was quietly shaping an entire generation. These young viewers weren't just being entertained; they were being recruited. They saw engineering not as dry calculations, but as creative problem-solving, heroism, and an essential calling. The university realized they had to acknowledge this unique reality. Doohan had arguably done more to inspire future engineers than any traditional outreach program. His character made the profession look exciting, accessible, and vital to hundreds of their students. Breaking from academic tradition, the school decided to award Doohan an honorary doctorate in engineering. It wasn't for research or technical innovation, but for the power of inspiration.
