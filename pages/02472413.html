An electrocution permits the best possible test of the question whether electricity kills or merely suspends animation. If Dr. Gibbons should be successful he would open the way for saving many lives. Daily workers with electricity are shocked to apparent death. Dr. Gibbons’ experiment on Wilson would show whether prompt efforts at restoration would save them. A French physician, Dr. D’Arsonval has long maintained that the shocks do not kill. The Philadelphia Record shows how has suggestions have saved at least one life. Superintendent James E. Cullen, of a Pittsfield, (Mass.) manufactory, in October last grasped by mistake the two terminals of a current with the high pressure of 4,000 volts. This force completely paralyzed him; and if his companions in the room had not hastened to apply the D’Arsonval method of artificial respiration he might now be lying in his grave instead of rehearsing the story of ‘how it feels to be killed by electricity.’ One instinctively wonders what fate might otherwise have been that of the surcharged victim, and whether he would have been practically buried alive.
