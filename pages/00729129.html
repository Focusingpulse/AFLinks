We establish a closed-form expansion of the Riemann zeta function ζ(s) at any complex point s = a + ib in terms of an auxiliary real-analytic function L(a) built from the Bernoulli numbers and the rising factorial. Introduction The Riemann zeta function ζ(s) = X∞ n=1 1 ns , Re(s) > 1, admits an analytic continuation to C \ {1} with a simple pole of residue 1 at s = 1, and is the central object of analytic number theory [6, 16, 9, 10]. The Riemann hypothesis (RH), formulated in [14], asserts that all non-trivial zeros of ζ lie on the critical line Re(s) = 1/2; it is one of the seven Millennium Problems [2] and remains open despite extensive numerical and structural progress [5, 4, 3]. The classical Euler–Maclaurin expansion of ζ(s) [8, 12, 1] provides one of the standard tools for both the asymptotic study and the high-precision numerical evaluation of the zeta function. The aim of this note is to recast (1) in a form whose analytic content is carried entirely by an auxiliary real-analytic function L on the real line, and whose combinatorial content is encapsulated by the elementary symmetric polynomial function K p n(a) evaluated on consecutive integers.
