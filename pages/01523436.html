Overview Mercury's Perihelion Advance is Caused by Our Milky Way is a four-page paper by Thierry De Mees (dated December 2004, updated July 2007), published in the General Science Journal . It restates the first result of his earlier Did Einstein Cheat? The framework is the Maxwell Analogy for gravitation — Heaviside's 1893 proposal that gravity, like electromagnetism, has a second field generated by moving mass. De Mees writes the four analogue equations, calls the second field gyrotation Ω, and adds one departure from Heaviside and Jefimenko: the velocity entering the induction must be a "local absolute speed" defined relative to "the local stationary gravitation field", not relative to an observer. "Only gravitation fields can be regarded as 'local immobile' references." Applied to Mercury, the argument runs: The Sun's own spin is too slow to matter, but the Sun moves through the Milky Way at v 1 ≈ 235 km/s. That motion generates a gyrotation field, giving Mercury an extra attraction which, at orbital angle α to the direction of galactic motion, is G mM v 1 ²cos²α / 2r²c². Mercury's own orbital speed is v 2 = 47.9 km/s, so v 1 ² = 24 v 2 ², turning the extra term into 12 G mM v 2 ²cos²α / r²c².
