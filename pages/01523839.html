Saturday Morning Science Chats Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Alistair Riddoch , Ian Cohen, James Keene , Bill Lucas , Harry Ricker and Toby. It began with Riddoch's model of the vacuum and turned into an argument about what would count as doing science at all. Infinity, and immutability Riddoch opened with two things he had been turning over. The first was density: he had learned that relativity has the centre of a black hole infinitely dense, and wanted to know whether infinite density is even a possible thing. Hu's answer was that an infinity in a result is a red flag that something has gone wrong, and he made the same complaint about renormalization, which he calls dividing one infinity by another and keeping the answer. Ian agreed — thou shalt not divide by zero — and extended it to the big bang's infinitely small interval and infinitely great density, and to the Copenhagen picture, which appears to require an infinite speed of transmission where relativity has a finite one. The second was the word immutable . Riddoch's own model has Planck-scale building blocks that have always existed and never change: not permeable, not compressible, not flexible. What changes is only their relative orientation, spin polarity and rate of rotation. Because they are not perfect spheres — their surfaces are wave-deformed — the gaps between them do change, alternating between hyperbolic tetrahedra and hyperbolic octahedra as the blocks turn. Those gaps are genuine void, with no properties at all except a shape, and the shape is defined entirely from outside: as he put it, it is the doughnut that defines the hole, not the hole.
