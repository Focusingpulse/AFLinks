The central premise is not that the nervous system reads out a static memory store, but that awareness-relevant processing operates on a temporally delayed field of dynamically available mnemonic structure. This field may be generated online from current sensory input together with the system’s immediately preceding representational trajectory, rather than preserved as a passive buffer. In the present formulation, awareness-relevant operations are carried out through overlapping four-step local kernels of 4 × 25 ms = 100 ms embedded within a broader delayed regime on the order of approximately 500 ms, consistent with memory-based and retrospective-access accounts of consciousness and with gamma-scale temporal discretization (Budson et al., 2022; Libet et al., 1979; Buzsáki & Wang, 2012). Functional awareness is therefore defined not as immediate access to the physical present, but as the structured availability of a recently reconstructed and dynamically regenerated past. In short, functional awareness is the synchronized curvature of a remembered present. Within this architecture, ΔΓ does not function as a metaphysical identity claim about consciousness, but as a compact descriptor of memory-curvature dynamics across successive local kernels within the delayed field. The framework proposes that awareness-relevant organization depends not merely on the occurrence of relevant ingredients somewhere within the delayed mnemonic interval, but on their StrongSync￾compatible co-instantiation within a bounded objective regime.
