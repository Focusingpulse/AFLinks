Title An Interpretation of Faraday's Lines of Force Read in full Link to paper Author(s) David Tombe Published 2019 Abstract The magnetic field is solenoidal, yet the Biot-Savart Law which is the textbook equation for the magnetic field, indicates the existence of a singularity owing to the fact that it involves an inverse square law in distance. This dilemma is solved within the context that an individual magnetic line of force constitutes a double helix of sinks and sources closed on itself to form a toroidal ring vortex. Overview Frederick David Tombe sets out to resolve what he sees as a conflict at the heart of magnetostatics: the magnetic field is solenoidal, yet the Biot–Savart law contains an inverse-square dependence and therefore, he argues, a singularity. His resolution is that an individual line of force is a double helix of sinks and sources closed on itself into a toroidal ring vortex. The construction is the double helix theory developed in his earlier papers. A rotating electron–positron dipole is an electron and a positron in a mutual central-force orbit, with the rotation axis perpendicular to the line joining them. Stacking these along their rotation axes, electrons above positrons and angularly synchronised in a twisted rope-ladder fashion, gives a helical spring. Such springs are magnetic lines of force, channelling a Coulomb tension along a double-helix alignment within a neutral electron–positron sea. The angular momentum vector H of the rotating dipoles measures magnetic intensity, and the circumferential speed of the electrons and positrons "is what determines the local speed of light." Magnetic repulsion follows from centrifugal force.
