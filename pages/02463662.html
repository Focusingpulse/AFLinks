It develops the Aether Physics Model (APM) 1 INTRODUCTION 1 Abstract Raman longitudinal optical phonon–plasmon coupled (LOPC) features in SiC are widely used as empirical carrier-density and resistivity proxies, but multilayer wafers and finite depth of field convolute spectral responses and reduce such workflows to regression without falsifiable invariants. This paper upgrades SiC LOPC metrology to a ledger-first closure test in Quantum Measurement Units (QMU) by normalizing Raman observables into dimensionless QMU form and enforcing the Aether Rotational Self-Identity (Ledger One) as the propagation anchor. Two closure targets are defined: (i) a spectrum-only invariant based on a QMU-normalized LOPC inversion combined with a linewidth-normalized quotient, and (ii) a transport-coupled invariant that incorporates a unitless resistivity proxy. A decisive resolving-power experiment is then specified: mixed bulk depth spectra should fail closure as depth varies, while multivariate curve resolution (MCR) component spectra extracted from Dual Spatial Filtration (DSF) depth stacks should satisfy closure within uncertainty when they correspond to physical layers. Where raw spectral matrices are unavailable, a published feature-space proxy closure coordinate is constructed from reported intensity ratios and linewidths, enabling the same bulk-versus-component falsification on industrial datasets. A depth-axis modulation bound is extracted (in SI-free, unitless form) from open supplementary depth-of-analysis tables in a quantified Raman–eddy-current resistivity mapping study of 4H–SiC.
