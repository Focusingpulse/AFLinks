The committee, organized by James G. Marshall, 88-year old retired Electro Metallurgical Co. works manager, decided at a meeting at Hotel Niagara Monday to make overtures to the federal and state governments, electrical equipment manufacturers and historical societies for help in making the old plant a permanent museum. The Adams plant is to be taken out of service when the new power plant at Lewiston goes into operation in 1961. First AC Transmission It was from the Adams plant in 1895 that alternating current was first transmitted over a long distance. Power from the Adams plant was used to illuminate the new street lights in Buffalo — 25 miles away. Martin J. Travers was named chairman of the executive committee that will push the project. Other members of the committee are R. Wolcott Hooker, senior vice president of Hooker Chemical Corp.; Walter Eggert, vice president of Moore Business Forms; Kenneth K. Burke, vice president of the Niagara Falls Gazette, and Mr. Marshall. John Daley of Kimberly-Clark Corp. will serve as secretary.
