Chapter 3 Loading Measurements 3.1 Background and Literature Review Given the fantasy and biasing that exists around studying LENR, Callisto sought to ground their research program in principles of physics and chemistry. Within this framework, one of our primary thrusts was to understand, control, and maximize deuterium loading in palladium and other relevant metals. Regardless of observing any excess heat, we could certainly develop methods for enhancing the fractional loading of hydrogen isotopes. Success depended on accurately measuring the hydrogen loading, exploring methods for increasing this loading, and ultimately optimizing these methods to yield the highest possible fractional loadings near ambient conditions. The focus of this section is on measurement techniques that can be used to estimate the fractional loading of hydrogen in a metal hydride. The majority of our work was performed on palladium protide (PdH) and palladium deuteride (PdD), although Callisto did investigate a few other metal systems including palladium alloys, nickel, and several rare-earth metals. Not only is palladium considered a model system, but it is also widely believed to be the most relevant material for LENR. Herein, we will emphasize methods and literature that work well for palladium hydride, although these methods can be extended to other metal hydride systems. We can classify loading measurements as either direct or indirect. In a direct loading measurement, the moles or mass of hydrogen is tracked directly. Typically, direct measurements offer higher measurement accuracy, given there is less error propagation. However, direct methods are typically more constrained, making it hard to apply an arbitrary form factor or in situ to an experiment. In contrast, indirect loading measurements offer more flexibility in experimental design, albeit at the cost of lower certainty.
