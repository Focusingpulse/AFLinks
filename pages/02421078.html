Charles Ostman Residence Berkeley, California, United States Nationality American Known for Nanomaterials and "greentech"; bioconvergence; futurist analysis of nanotechnology Scientific career Fields Nanotechnology, nanomaterials, electronics, materials science, artificial intelligence, futures studies Institutions Institute for Global Futures; NanoSig; Silicon Valley Nano Ventures; formerly Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and Los Alamos National Laboratory Charles Ostman is an American technologist, analyst and futurist specialising in nanotechnology, nanomaterials and the convergence of biological and engineered systems. Often described — and describing himself — as a "historian of the future", he argues that nanotechnology has already moved out of speculative research and into markets and applications, and that its greatest near-term value lies in environmentally driven "greentech". He presented this thesis to the independent-inventor community at the Tesla Tech ExtraOrdinary Technology Conference in 2008. Biography Ostman has stated decades of experience in electronics, computing, artificial intelligence and materials science, including eight years working with the United States Department of Energy at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory at the University of California, Berkeley, and at Los Alamos National Laboratory. He is a senior fellow at the Institute for Global Futures, a consulting group providing strategic research, analysis and technical due diligence to corporations and institutions. He chairs the NanoElectronics and Photonics Forum of NanoSig, an organisation whose stated charter is facilitating investment in nanotechnology ventures, and has served as a senior consultant with Silicon Valley Nano Ventures. He has also been listed on the management team of Fourth Venture, which sought to translate former Soviet and Eastern Bloc military technologies into clean energy and nanotechnology applications, on the scientific advisory board of Legendary
