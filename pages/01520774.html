Title Non-Hertzian Waves May Involve Subquantum Particles Read in full Link to paper (Internet Archive) Author(s) Rochus Boerner Keywords ether , longitudinal waves, scalar waves, Tesla , light Published 1995 "Non-Hertzian Waves May Involve Subquantum Particles" is a short expository piece by Rochus Boerner on the question of whether longitudinal electromagnetic waves can exist in vacuum — the waves variously called "non-Hertzian", "longitudinal" or "scalar". It is a summary rather than a research paper: about a thousand words, written for a general reader, with four references to the technical literature. It was hosted on the web page collection of Robert Neil Boyd at rialian.com, where the original has since disappeared; the text survives in the Internet Archive. Read the full text here (archived copy) . What it argues Boerner sets out the case in four steps. The nineteenth-century aether could carry two kinds of wave. If light is a mechanical oscillation in a medium, that medium should support both transverse waves, oscillating across the direction of travel, and longitudinal waves, oscillating along it — as water carries both surface waves and pressure waves, and at different speeds. Only the transverse kind survived. Hertz demonstrated transverse electromagnetic waves experimentally, and those are the only ones mainstream physics deals with. Boerner is careful and correct about why: from the standard Maxwell equations "it can be mathematically proved that only transversal waves are possible in a vacuum." He is equally careful to note that longitudinal waves in a material medium — plasma waves in an ionised gas — are entirely orthodox. Tesla claimed to have made the other kind. Tesla said he had generated longitudinal electromagnetic waves, and that they could not be shielded by a Faraday cage, travelled faster than light, did not fall off as the inverse square of distance, and could carry energy wirelessly.
