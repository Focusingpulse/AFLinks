Rebigsol (also published as Cameron Wong ) is a Chinese-American independent researcher living in Vancouver, Washington, known for a series of papers arguing that special relativity defeats itself on its own mathematics. He has contributed to the meetings and proceedings of the Natural Philosophy Alliance and its successor the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society since the 1990s, and is the author of Aqua Soil (Xlibris, 2009), which sets his objections to relativity alongside arguments about human evolution and offers a $50,000 award to anyone who can talk him out of them. Background Rebigsol is retired, and describes his research as the resumption of interests he had no chance to pursue when younger — among them travel, ballroom dancing, music, history, social study, evolution, literature and physics. He writes that the subjects he has found hardest to make sense of are relativity, evolution and American democracy, each of which he regards as monopolised by the theories of elites despite inconsistencies and contradictions, and each of which he believes calls for new ideas. Scientific work Criticism of special relativity Rebigsol's principal claim is that special relativity refutes itself on its own mathematics, before any question of experiment arises. In Relativity's Length Measurement Inconsistency ( Proceedings of the NPA 7, 2009) he works through a case study of length measurement and reports arriving at the result 1 = 0.
