aeolipile

· archived # 1,469,107
In Alexandria, Egypt, during the 1st century AD, a mathematician sat watching a boiling cauldron. Most people in the ancient world just saw water turning into vapor. But Hero of Alexandria saw the future. He didn't know it at the time, but he was looking at the exact same energy source that would one day power locomotives, factories, and the electric grid. The Roman Empire was at the absolute height of its power. Their domain stretched from the rainy coasts of England to the burning sands of Africa. They possessed the greatest army on earth and the most advanced architecture the world had ever seen. They had paved roads, massive aqueducts, and marble temples. But the entire economy relied on one thing: muscle. The empire ran on the backs of millions of slaves and manual laborers. Hero, however, was a man who liked to tinker with the impossible. He decided to build a device that moved on its own. He took a hollow copper sphere and mounted it delicately on a pair of standing tubes. He connected those tubes to a sealed cauldron filled with water. He placed a fire beneath it and waited for the pressure to build. As the water boiled, steam rushed up through the tubes and injected into the ball. Then, the physics took over. Two bent nozzles on the opposite sides of the sphere blasted the steam outward in opposite directions. The sphere began to spin. And then it spun faster.
