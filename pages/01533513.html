"It may be difficult, and it would certainly be rash, to venture to judge recent scientific work on the basis of history. But it is possible to discover in the history of heat and thermodynamics certain outstanding features which seem to be lacking in the modern scientific scene, and whose absence may represent a defect in our arrangements for science. Three key figures in our narrative - Carnot, Joule and Mayer - were in strict terms amateurs, or devotees, of science. They stood outside the 'establishments' of their days and because their ideas were unorthodox they were ignored. Carnot died unrecognized and Mayer was driven to despair, while Joule succeeded only through good fortune and very favorable personal circumstances. These men and the independent lines they took were essential for the progress of science. If we consider the strong and complex pressure which modern society exerts on all its members, scientists included, it is reasonable to wonder whether such men could work effectively today and, if not, whether science can continue to progress satisfactorily without them.
