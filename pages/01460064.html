John H. Gregory

· archived # 1,460,064
In May 1859, prospector John H. Gregory made one of the most significant discoveries in Colorado history: he uncovered the first hard-rock gold (lode gold) ever found in the Rocky Mountains. While following a small gulch about 28 miles west of the fledgling settlement of Denver City, Gregory located a rich gold-bearing quartz vein. On May 6, 1859, he and a small group of companions panned decomposed vein material from the hillside and washed out roughly four dollars’ worth of gold from their very first pan, clear evidence of a genuine underground lode rather than the loose placer gold that had been found earlier along the streams. This strike in what quickly became known as Gregory Gulch (near present day Central City and Black Hawk in Gilpin County) sparked an immediate rush of miners into the mountains. Gregory’s discovery shifted the focus of the 1859 Colorado Gold Rush from surface panning to hard-rock mining and quartz vein exploration. It led to the rapid establishment of the Gregory Mining District, Colorado’s first formal mining district, and helped transform the central Rockies into one of the West’s most productive gold regions. The area around Gregory Gulch soon boomed with tent camps and log cabins, eventually growing into the bustling mining towns of Mountain City, Central City, and Black Hawk.
