Michael Leas Known for Tesla wireless energy demonstrations, the Magnevex generator, Nikola Tesla's "Electric Universe" Scientific career Fields Physics Michael Leas is an independent inventor and researcher who builds and demonstrates working wireless-energy apparatus based on the work of Nikola Tesla . He describes himself as "an inventor, visionary artist, metaphysical scientist and inter-dimensional explorer," and is the developer of the Magnevex motor/generator and head of the Magnevex Research Project. He works entirely outside academic physics, presenting his experiments to the independent-inventor and Tesla Technology community. Scientific contributions Leas's central practical work is a small-scale wireless energy display which he presents as a bench-top analogue of the power system Tesla intended for the Wardenclyffe tower. In his conference demonstrations the display is energised by his own Magnevex motor/generator, and is used to illustrate what he calls the "lost concepts" of Tesla's Electric Universe — the idea, taken from Tesla's own articles and letters, that immense currents can be moved through the Earth itself by a magnifying transmitter, and that electrical systems can be connected to what Tesla called the "wheel work of nature." Leas's use of the phrase is drawn from Tesla's own writing on wireless power and should be distinguished from the Electric Universe plasma-cosmology programme associated with Wallace Thornhill and David Talbott; the sources for his talks do not connect him to that body of work. The name Magnevex is derived from "magnetic vortex." Leas states that the device functions on energy principles drawn from the writings of Tesla, Walter Russell and Ernest L. Norman, founder of the Unarius Society — a set of ideas he frames as "4-dimensional electromagnetic physics of cyclic motion instead of linear reaction," in which energy is tapped as a pulse, a wave or a regenerative cycle rather than as a one-way linear reaction.
