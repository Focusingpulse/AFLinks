Title Autodynamics' Universal Gravitation: Application to Allais' Anomaly Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani and Lucy Haye Keywords Allais Effect , Autodynamics , pico-graviton, solar eclipse, torsion pendulum, Gravity Published 1999 Website https://www.autodynamics.org/allias-anomaly/ "Autodynamics' Universal Gravitation: Application to Allais' Anomaly" is a paper by Ricardo L Carezani and Lucy Haye , dated Long Beach, California, 10 July 1999, applying the Autodynamics pico-graviton mechanism to the anomalous behaviour of pendulums reported during total solar eclipses. Overview The Allais effect is the claim that a pendulum's period changes measurably during a solar eclipse. The paper works against a specific dataset: the torsion-pendulum measurements made by Erwin J. Saxl and Mildred Allen at Harvard during the total eclipse of 7 March 1970 and published in Physical Review D3-4, 823 (1971). The authors note at the outset that "neither Classical Mechanics nor General Relativity explains the phenomenon." The argument The shadowing mechanism In Autodynamics gravity is the pressure of pico-gravitons absorbed by matter. The Sun, absorbing them, casts a cone of umbra and penumbra in which the flux is depleted. When the Moon enters that cone it receives a reduced flux, so its own gravitational pull on the Earth's surface weakens — and since the Moon's pull ordinarily reduces the net downward acceleration at the Earth's surface, weakening it makes the net acceleration increase . The prediction is therefore that gravity at the surface rises slightly during totality, shortening a Foucault pendulum's period and lengthening a torsion pendulum's. The numbers Carezani and Haye work the budget explicitly. The Sun's flux produces 27398 cm/s 2 at the Sun and reduces terrestrial gravity by 0.593069844 cm/s 2 ; the Moon's produces 162.2 cm/s 2 and reduces it by 0.003439282 cm/s 2 .
