GTM2026-main025 The role of intergroup links on aggregate effort in the social-norm model Ilya Petrov1,2 1 National Research University Higher School of Economics, Moscow, Russia 2 V.A. Trapeznikov Institute of Control Problems of the Russian Academy of Sciences. ilyapetrov22@gmail.com For the social norms model, we consider the case of interaction between two groups of agents with different levels of productivity. In this case, a benevolent central planner tries to maximise the aggregate effort of the agents. Adding links between the clusters of agents with different productivity can increase the total effort of players, albeit reducing the sum of their payoffs. In particular, it has been shown that increasing the number of links between clusters redistributes social norms, resulting in a significant increase in the efforts of agents with low productivity at the expense of a small reduction in the efforts of more productive agents. Numerical experiments confirm theoretical findings, showing that small changes in network structure can significantly affect equilibrium outcomes. Future research could focus on developing incentive mechanisms to compensate agents for network structure changes. We study network interventions in which individuals strategically adjust their actions based on the decisions of their neighbours, while a central planner seeks to optimise an objective function in equilibrium.
