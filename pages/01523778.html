Ricardo L. Carezani Born ( 1921-04-11 ) April 11, 1921 Died 2016 (aged 94–95) Residence Long Beach, CA, United States Nationality Argentine / American Known for Autodynamics , Special Relativity Scientific career Fields Physicist Ricardo Libertario Carezani (April 11, 1921 – 2016) was an Argentine-American physicist and the originator of Autodynamics (AD), an alternative theory of relativistic mechanics that he proposed as a replacement for Einstein's special and general relativity. Biography Carezani was born on April 11, 1921 in Córdoba, Argentina. He entered Tucumán University in 1939 to study electro-mechanical engineering, and from the outset regarded Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity as ill-founded. Around 1940 he came to believe that the "two observers in relative motion" postulate underlying special relativity was redundant; removing that redundancy, he argued, left a theory in which a single observer describes a phenomenon in its own coordinate system. By about 1945, at the age of 24, he considered the foundations of Autodynamics complete. In 1951 Carezani published his first book on Autodynamics, which he said was immediately blacklisted by Argentina's National Atomic Energy Commission; only about 300 copies were printed. In August 1954 the Buenos Aires daily Crítica (which ceased publication in 1963) published an account of his theory; Carezani recounted that the paper's publisher was warned not to print anything further by him or face being shut down. He attributed the long delay in developing and publicising Autodynamics to this political climate — his struggle, as he put it, was waged against Perón on one side and the Einstein "myth" on the other. After Argentina's political reopening, Carezani emigrated to the United States around 1979–1981, settling in California, where he worked as a mathematician and physicist.
