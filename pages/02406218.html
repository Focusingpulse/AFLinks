The Secret River By This Writer A huge river, allegedly with twice the volume of the Columbia, runs underground from Alaska to the Sea of Cortez. It emerges near Cancun and forms an offshore lake of fresh water. This Writer calls it "Alph", after Samuel Coleridge's famous poem-interruptus “Kubla Khan”. Here follow some relevant old newspaper articles that defy denial and demand to be reread. The earliest known specific report of a huge-now-lost cavern (albeit without a river) in California appeared in The Banner Liberty (June 1860): Mammoth Cave in California A letter dated Negro Hill, Cal., on the 18th ult., and published in the Sacramento Standard, explains in detail the discovery of a mammoth cave in El Dorado County. While a party of men were excavating for a lime-kiln, they broke through into a cave of immense size. Mayor Swan, of Sacramento, subsequently explored it for two hours, and visited several large rooms of several hundred feet in length, by nearly as broad, until he was brought to a halt by a lake, the extent of which is as yet unknown. The floor, as well as stalactites are all of a beautiful white crystallized marble. Thirty years later this similar snippet, minus a river, was printed in the Brooklyn Daily Eagle (October 13, 1894): Mammoth Caverns, Southern California An Interesting Discovery Which Rivals the Kentucky Wonder. The series of the Rocky sides of Canjon peak, a spur of the caverns recently discovered by prospectors in Guyamaca range, have been partly explored, and are found to rival the Mammoth cave of Kentucky in interesting features as well as in the size of the chambers.
