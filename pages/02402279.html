MolPhot2026-main037 Three stages in the luminescence decay kinetics of nanocrystals with traps Евгений Н. Бодунов1 1 Петербургский государственный университет путей сообщения Императора Александра I, Россия evgeny.bodunov@inbox.ru Measuring the luminescence decay kinetics, I(t), of semiconductor nanocrystals (NCs) is a fundamental method for studying their optical properties. In recent years, a number of studies have recorded long-lasting NC luminescence over long time scales - from hundreds of picoseconds to seconds - with luminescence intensity decreasing by up to seven orders of magnitude [1]. At longer times, I(t) was approximated by an inverse power law of 1/t b+1 (b is a constant). This paper presents an analysis of the luminescence decay kinetics of NCs using two models of NC ensembles: with identical and non-identical charge carrier (CC) traps [2-4]. It is shown that, regardless of the model used, three stages (three time segments) are observed in the NC luminescence decay kinetics I(t). In the first, initial stage, rapid decay of I(t) occurs. This is caused by radiative and nonradiative transitions of the CC from the emitting NC state to the ground state and the CC trapping. The second and third stages are the delayed luminescence, I d (t), caused by the CC detrapping to the emitting NC state.
