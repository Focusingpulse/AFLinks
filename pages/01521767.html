Three hundred years after Newton's formulation and one hundred years after Einstein's special relativity calculation, we have been no closer to understanding the cause for the force of gravity. A case is now made for the well known atomic mass defect as the mass sink that forces mass to be attracted to mass. Theory by Dr. T. Van Flandern and experiments by Dr. E. Podkletnov are discussed that serve to support the nature and superluminal velocity of the gravity force. Overview Thomas Lockyer proposes that gravity is caused by the nuclear mass defect. When nucleons bind, energy is released and the resulting nucleus weighs less than its constituents; Lockyer takes that shortfall to be a literal absence — a "mass sink" — and argues that gravity is the mass-energy of one body seeking to fill the deficit in another. Since binding energy is always released and never absorbed, the deficit always exists, which he offers as the explanation for the one feature of gravity that distinguishes it from electromagnetism: it is exclusively attractive. From this he constructs a replacement for Newton's constant.
