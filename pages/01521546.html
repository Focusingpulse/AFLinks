Title The DNA of Electromagnetic Radiation (The Electron-Positron Dipole) Read in full Link to paper Author(s) David Tombe Keywords aether , electron-positron dipole Published 2006 Journal General Science Journal No. of pages 16 Abstract It is proposed that space is a dynamic aethereal medium of unknown substance in which electrons constitute sinks, and in which positrons constitute sources. Negative and positive electric charge is merely a measure of the rate of flow of the aether into the sinks and out of the sources. Standard hydrodynamical theory indicates that this arrangement not only accounts for the irrotational radial flow (inverse square law force) of aether into electrons and out of positrons, but that it also yields three additional components of force. Overview This is the third of David Tombe's papers in the collection and the one that sets out his picture of the medium. The companions — " The Centrifugal Force and the Coriolis Force " and " Gravitation and the Gyroscopic Force " — argue the kinematics; this one asks what the aether is made of. The answer is a densely packed sea of rotating electron–positron dipoles . Electrons are sinks in the aether and positrons are sources, with electric charge simply measuring the rate of flow in or out. Each dipole is an electron and a positron in mutual orbit, with aether streaming across from source to sink and swirling into a vortex as it goes. Tombe holds these dipoles to be many orders of magnitude smaller than an atom. From standard hydrodynamics he extracts four force components. The irrotational radial flow gives the inverse-square law, and he takes this to cover Coulomb's law and Newton's law of gravitation alike — arguing that if the two were unrelated one would need two separate aethers, and a positron could not be a sink in one and a source in the other.
