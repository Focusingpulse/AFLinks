A simple proof of the generalized Poincare conjecture. Johan Noldus∗ November 29, 2017 Abstract We show that any closed n dimensional topological connected and simply connected space (or piecewise linear manifold) is homeomorphic to the n sphere. 1 Proof Any topological space M can be endowed with a path metric; take a point p ∈ M and consider the level surfaces Sr = {x|d(p, x) = r}. For a n dimensional topological space, those will all be homeomorphic to n − 1 spheres for r sufficiently small. Since M has no boundary, there must ex￾ist a critical r0 such that Sr0 is not homeomorphic to a n − 1 sphere; out of continuity arguments, it is either (a) a point (b) pointlike contractions and point identifications. In case (a), we are done since we have obtained a topological S n . In case (b) the contractions will create disconnected components whereas the identifications will create “tori”; every discon￾nected component being a closed n − 1 dimensional topological space. In case of the “tori”, this will create closed non-contractible curves; the “tori” cannot shrink to a point given that this would violate the condition that it is a topological space of dimension n. Identifying different com￾ponents or unpinshing the tori (which should happen eventually) leads to non-contractible curves. Therefore, the generic situation is that of a tree graph with points blown up to a n − 1 sphere except at the endpoints. Such topological space is clearly homeomorphic to S n .
