It is the most technically developed of Carezani's published applications of Autodynamics and the one in which the electromuon — a heavy electron-like particle proposed in place of the neutrino — is introduced. Abstract A brief explanation of autodynamics principles is given. The autodynamics equations are explained, and their application to muon decay is analyzed. Autodynamics can explain the results obtained at SLAC and CERN. The author concludes that the problem of muon and pion decay remains to be resolved by the experimenters. Overview Carezani's starting position is that the special relativity expressions for kinetic energy, mass and momentum were derived for particles receiving energy from outside and are being misapplied to decay, where a particle's energy comes only from its own rest mass. Applied to the three-body decay of the muon , the relativistic expressions return more energy and momentum than experiment finds, and the shortfall is assigned to neutrinos. The Autodynamics expressions, he argues, return the experimental values directly. The Autodynamics relations are E c = m 0 c 2 [ 1 − 1 − β 2 ] , m v = m 0 1 − β 2 and p = m v v . Carezani draws attention to two of their properties, both of which are essential to the paper: the momentum rises to a maximum near 0 . 7 c and then falls, and the kinetic energy rises to a ceiling equal to the particle's rest energy.
