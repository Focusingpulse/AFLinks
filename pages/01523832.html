Saturday Morning Science Chat with David de Hilster Summary David de Hilster hosted a second week in Franklin Hu 's absence, again streaming to both the society's channel and his own Dissident Science , and set the topic deliberately: is charge real? The case against charge De Hilster opened with a question few could answer — who first put the plus and minus signs on things? — and gave the answer as Benjamin Franklin. His argument follows from that. The signs are a notation adopted to record which things attract and which repel; they have since become so ingrained that people take charge for an innate property of matter. He regards it instead as a magical attribute imposed on a particle, and asks anyone who uses it for a physical account of what a field actually is. The model he and his father Robert de Hilster are writing up needs no charge at all: the nucleus holds what orbits it for mechanical reasons, everything is Newtonian, and by Occam's razor the burden falls on whoever wants the extra ingredient. He noted that Glenn Borchardt 's neomechanics likewise dispenses with it. Hu, watching from the road, wrote in twice. First that charge is too complex a phenomenon to be an assumption — which de Hilster read back as an argument for his own side, since an assumption is precisely what one declines to explain. Then that charge needs to emerge as the result of something like ordinary Newtonian physics, at which de Hilster cheerfully declared they agreed.
