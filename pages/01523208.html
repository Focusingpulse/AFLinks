James Marsen Born 1948 (age 77–78) Residence Ridgefield Park, NJ, United States Nationality USA Known for The Tron Theory, the local-ether model, a proposed Michelson-Morley experiment in space, the Brillet-Hall residual Scientific career Fields Independent Researcher , Physics James Marsen (also published as Jim Marsen , born 1948) is an American independent researcher and Columbia University-trained engineer working outside the academic mainstream on aether physics. He is best known as an author of the Tron Theory , a proposed unification of physical science built on a new sub-electron particle called the tron , and as an advocate of local-ether models of light propagation as an alternative to Einstein's Special Relativity . Education Marsen was educated entirely at Columbia University in New York City: BA, Columbia College, Columbia University, 1970 BSME, School of Engineering and Applied Science, Columbia University, 1972 MSME, Plasma Physics, School of Engineering and Applied Science, Columbia University, 1975 PhD candidate, Plasma Physics, Graduate School of Arts and Science, Columbia University, 1976-1979 Biography He lives in Ridgefield Park, New Jersey, and describes his current work as independent research. He maintains profiles on Academia.edu and ResearchGate, where his papers on the Tron Theory and on aether-drift experiments are posted, and he is a member of the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society, in whose online seminar series he has presented repeatedly. Scientific contributions The Tron Theory The Tron Theory, developed with Richard A. Marsen and set out in the papers "Introduction to the Tron Theory" (2014) and "The Tron Theory: A novel concept for the Ether and Matter" (2016), is offered as a unifying framework for physical science. Its central posit is a new fundamental particle, the tron , several orders of magnitude smaller than the electron and of minute mass, with a simple spherical-like structure.
