Does the Cosmic Microwave Background Disprove Einstein's Relativity? Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Roger J Anderton Date 20 September 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Does the Cosmic Microwave Background Disprove Einstein's Relativity? was a live session presented by Roger J Anderton on 20 September 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Ideally the Cosmic microwave background should disprove Einstein's relativity, but because the theory is not properly defined what is alllowed/permitted in relativity is obscure. Hosted by Roger Anderton. Summary Roger J Anderton opened not with his own material but with a clip from André Assis , on the history of predictions of the temperature of the cosmic microwave background. The point of the clip is that the big bang's predictions were poor — Alpher and Herman's 5 K in 1948, Gamow's 7 K in 1953 and 50 K in his 1961 book, against the 2.7 K eventually measured — while a series of workers reasoning from an infinite, non-expanding universe came far closer, and much earlier: Charles Édouard Guillaume's 5.6 K in 1896, Eddington's 3.2 K in 1926, Regener and Nernst in the 1930s, McKellar's 2.8 K in 1941, Finlay-Freundlich in 1953. All used the Stefan–Boltzmann law and the measured starlight energy density to find the equilibrium temperature of a body in interstellar space. Anderton's interest was in the omission: none of this appears in the textbooks, because it does not suit the narrative. The talk itself then set that argument aside and granted the big bang, in order to ask a different question. An argument for a preferred frame Anderton's own proposal, developed as usual in dialogue with ChatGPT, is an ontological one. If the universe began in a highly uniform state, all matter was initially at rest in a single frame. Everything now moving relative to that frame must at some point have accelerated.
