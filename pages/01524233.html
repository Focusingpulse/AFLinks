Overview The Telegrapher's Equations is a four-page paper by David Tombe of Belfast, dated 22 October 2008 and published in the General Science Journal . It is a companion to his Displacement Current of a fortnight earlier. Its thesis is that two phenomena usually described by the same equations are physically distinct: "Wireless EM radiation relates to magnetization while the waves that travel alongside the conducting wires in transmission lines relate to linear polarization." Tombe reads Maxwell's wave equation ∇²H = με∂²H/∂t² as describing "the propagation of angular acceleration through a sea of tiny aethereal vortices", with A the circulation within each vortex and time-varying induction the overflow between neighbours. The telegrapher's equations, derived by Heaviside in the 1880s from the self-inductance and capacitance of a line, he takes to describe something else — a capacitive, linear-polarisation effect in the dielectric between the wires, with Q = CV replacing D = εE. He then raises a difficulty with his own reading: "since experiments tend to confirm that the speed of an electric signal along a wire is indeed in the order of the speed of light, it would seem like a coincidence if the telegrapher's equations weren't in fact applicable in the context." His proposed resolution is an aethereal fluid.
