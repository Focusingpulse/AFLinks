GC2026-PERM045 Paleomagnetic results of early Jurassic volcanic rocks from western Transbaikalia Ivan Fedyukin1 , Andrey Shatsillo1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences ivan_f88@mail.ru For the testing rigidity of Northern Eurasia in Mesozoic time, we studied early Jurassic volcanic rocks in western Transbaikalia. More than 25 natural outcrops have been studied in the middle and lower reaches of the Chikoy River and 160 oriented samples have been selected. The rocks are mainly represented by basalts, sometimes with dacite flows. The rocks form a synclinal structure, the wings are oriented in the north-west and south-east directions. Geochronological studies of zircon from dacites have been carried out and the age of 181 million years has been determined, which corresponds to the Toarcian stage of the Lower Jurassic. Most of the studied samples contain an interpreted paleomagnetic signal. In all sites, there is a layered characteristic component of magnetization, which we interpret as primary. The magnetization component is bipolar (positive reversal test). The obtained paleomagnetic pole coincides with European apparent polar wander path of the same age [1] and indicates that in the earlier Jurassic period, Transbaikalia as a frame of the Siberian Platform and the East European Platform were fully consolidated and represented a single rigid structure – the North Asian continent. This conclusion indicates the inconsistency of models suggesting the rotation of the "Siberian domain" in the North Asian continent. References 1. T. H. Tor
