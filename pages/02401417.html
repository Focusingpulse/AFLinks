GC2026-SGG013 Seismic effects of industrial explosions in a granite quarry in Belarus Kseniya Tsiareshchanka1 , Viktoriia Bialiayeva1 , Yuliya Martsinonich1 , Arkady Aronov1 1 Geophysical Monitoring Center National Academy of Sciences of Belarus, Republic of Belarus tsiareshchenko@cgm.by The study is based on seismological observations carried out in three stages at the Mikashevichi granite quarry from October, 2021 to May, 2025. At each stage different arrangements of seismic stations were used. A total of ten stations have been installed during the entire observation period, seven of which were located each time at different points, and one station was located within the same private residential building at each of the three stages. The observations were carried out by digital stations consisting of LE-3DLite/1s short-period three-component seismometer-velocimeters [1] and GeoSig GMSplus [2] and Delta-03M [3] seismic signal recorders. Fourteen explosions were recorded from August 20, 2021 to October 22, 2021; 103 explosions – from July 14, 2023 to December 15, 2023; and 93 explosions – from August 2, 2024 to May 30, 2025. Seismic recordings from 210 explosions were used in the research process. The explosions selected for analysis were divided into 11 groups depending on the distance between the detonated block and the recording station. The group 1 had a distance of less than 1 km, followed by the groups 2- 10 with 300 meter increments. The group 11 had a distance of more than 3.7 km.
