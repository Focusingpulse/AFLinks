ITS VALUE IN DWELLINGS. Wizard Says the Lamp Will Last Forever and Is Different from Tesla's. THOSE CRYSTALS IN TUBE. Dr. Frances Williams Makes a Guess That Edison Found What He Wanted in Iceland Spar. Mr. Edison Explains His New Lamp and Its Possibilities. Mr. Edison was, if possible, more confident yesterday than he was the day before of the success of his newly discovered light. Of course, the great thing is to make it of commercial value, and this will come about, it is expected, in a short time. I visited the great electrician at his laboratory in West Orange. Since the day before he had changed the apparatus and was fitting up something different for the purpose of making measurements. He did this with the aid of an electrical expert, but not until next week will he have completed the observations. "Can this light," I asked Mr. Edison, "be used in a dwelling house?" "Yes," he said, "but of course not now, because it is not perfected — that is the apparatus to utilize it for domestic or other purpose has to be simplified and made capable of general use. There is the light, but somebody else may invent the means of making it marketable." HE IS GOING TO TRY. "Why not do it yourself?" I suggested. "Well," said the wizard, "I am going to try.
