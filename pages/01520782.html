It is shown that the use of noncovariant contracted length leads to violation of the laws of electric charge and energy conservation. Overview Nonconservation of Charge and Energy as Consequences of Contracted Length Noncovariance is a four-page paper by Vyacheslav N Streltsov of the Laboratory of High Energies at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research, Dubna, published in Apeiron 5(1–2) in 1998. It argues that the standard Lorentz contraction is not merely wrong in emphasis but inconsistent, and should be replaced by an "elongation formula" in which moving bodies grow longer. The paper opens with a mathematical objection. For a rod along the x axis, Streltsov forms the four-component quantity l n c = (cΔt, Δx, 0, 0) = (0, l c , 0, 0), whose interval is s c = l*√(1 − β²). At rest the same quantity gives s* = l*. "The comparison of (3) and (4) tells us directly about the noninvariance of ‘contracted interval.’ In other words, the four-component quantity l n c is not a 4-vector." Since "the relativistic interval is the main invariant of relativity theory", he concludes that "we are obliged to refuse the generally accepted representation of the contraction of longitudinal sizes in motion as it contradicts the very substance of relativity theory." Two physical arguments follow. The first concerns a neutral current-carrying wire. In the wire's rest frame S* the electron and ion densities cancel, so Δq* = ρ*ΔV* = 0.
