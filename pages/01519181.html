Title A Potential Electromagnetic Source for the Observed Wang Eclipse Anomaly Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Barry Springer Keywords Wang eclipse, electromagnetic susceptibility, gravity anomaly Published 2012 No. of pages 3 Abstract Researchers have been straining to find explanations for the anomalies recorded by Wang, et al, during the 1997 Mohe eclipse, based on the assumption that the anomalies were of gravitational origin. An examination of the energetic nature of the solar chromosphere-corona regions, the shielding geometry of the eclipse and the instrumentation design suggests that electromagnetic, rather than gravitational, effects may have produced the recorded data anomalies. Overview A Potential Electromagnetic Source for the Observed Wang Eclipse Anomaly is a three-page paper by Barry Springer of Falcon, Colorado, dated 5 September 2012. It belongs to the cluster of NPA papers on the gravity variations Wang and colleagues reported from the Mohe observation centre during the total solar eclipse of 9 March 1997, alongside Robert de Hilster 's The Wang Eclipse and Thierry De Mees 's analysis. Springer's thesis is that the other treatments all assume the anomaly is gravitational, and that this assumption should be tested. His alternative is that the recorded dips were an electromagnetic artefact of the gravimeter rather than a change in gravity. The argument has three strands. First, the physical premise: drawing on Donald Scott's The Electric Sky , he takes the chromosphere and corona to be the seat of the Sun's electromagnetic activity, so that lunar occultation of the solar limb would remove a large electromagnetic source from the observing station's view. Second, the timing: he reads the two recorded valleys as occurring before first contact and after fourth contact, with a reduced effect during totality, and argues this matches the volume of chromosphere–corona shielded.
