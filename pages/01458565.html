Daniel Tammet

· archived # 1,458,565
Daniel Tammet can recite 22,514 digits of pi from memory because numbers have personalities to him. The number 289 is ugly. The number 333 is beautiful. He sees math as three-dimensional landscapes. He's the only savant who can explain exactly how his mind works—and that makes him the most scientifically valuable savant alive. Most savants can't tell you how they do what they do. They can perform extraordinary feats—calculate massive equations instantly, memorize entire books, play piano concertos after hearing them once—but they can't explain the process. Their abilities are mysterious, even to them. It just... happens. Daniel Tammet is different. He can do everything other savants can do: memorize tens of thousands of digits, learn languages in days, calculate complex math problems instantly. But unlike other savants, Daniel can describe exactly how he does it. He can explain what he sees, what he feels, what's happening inside his mind when he performs these impossible tasks. That makes him the Rosetta Stone of savant syndrome—the one person who can translate genius into language the rest of us can understand. Daniel Tammet was born in London in 1979. He had a normal childhood, mostly. He was quiet, a bit odd, struggled socially. But so do a lot of kids. Nobody realized he was autistic until he was 25 years old.
