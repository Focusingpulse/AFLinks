GTM2026-main017 Efficient Distributed Learning in Stochastic Non-Cooperative Games Without Information Exchange Haidong Li1 1 University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, China haidong.li@ucas.ac.cn In game theory, the scenarios of non-cooperative game include that individuals autonomously optimize their selfish objectives, e.g., congestion control, Cournot oligopoly, and resource allocation auctions. The individual cost function of each player depends on not only its own strategy but also its rivals’ strategies. The Nash equilibrium (NE) is a solution of non-cooperative game. In general, players do not have complete information of the game and thus cannot compute an NE in an introspective manner. For learning the NE, players need to take actions in repeated games, and adjust their strategies in response to their rivals’ strategies for seeking a lower cost. In most existing work on multi-agent learning, a center that has bidirectional communication with all players is required or agents are allowed to exchange information with their neighbors in a network. Learning the NE without local information exchange is considered much more challenging and has not been well studied. In this paper, we study how each player can learn the NE based on only its own cost observations. We consider stochastic non-cooperative games where each player only has access to a noisy but unbiased estimate of its cost function. Such stochastic setting is known as black-box or zeroth-order.
