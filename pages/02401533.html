GC2026-STP044 Diagnostics of the average energy of auroral electrons used photometric observations of the 427.8 nm emission Zhanna V. Dashkevich1 , Vladimir E. Ivanov1 1 Polar Geophysical Institute, Russia zhanna@pgia.ru The precipitating electrons are the main energy sources in the ionosphere during auroras. So, evaluation of the auroral electron flux parameters is a pressing issue in a diagnosing the ionospheric plasma state in polar region. The methodologies of estimating the Eev - average energy of auroral electron flux used on photometric observations of a just one auroral emission are presented. The first method for determining the Eev uses the hmax - height of the maximum intensity at a wavelength of 427.8 nm. The 427.8 nm emission is excited only by direct impacts from primary electrons and ionization cascade electrons. So, the altitude of the 427.8 nm intensity maximum is the region where maximum energy dissipation of the precipitating electron flux occurs. Modeling of the I427.8 - 427.8 nm emission intensity revealed that height hmax weakly depends on the shape of the energy spectrum and the pitch-angle distribution of the electron flux and is determined by Eev . The dependence of the height hmax on the average energy Eev was modeled. This dependence can be used to diagnose the average energy of of the precipitating electron flux.
