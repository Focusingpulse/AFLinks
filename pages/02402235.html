Urban Heritage and the Great Tea Route in Irkutsk: A Youth-Oriented Mediation Project Abstract The Great Tea Route, a historic Sino-Russian trade corridor, left a lasting mark on Irkutsk’s urban fabric. This study analyzes the city’s tea-trade heritage through an educational project for young audiences using comic books, digital mapping, and gamification. It argues that urban heritage analysis must blend historical authenticity with modern formats to engage younger, international visitors. Urban Heritage and the Tea Trade During the 19th and early 20th centuries, the tea trade transformed Irkutsk from a regional center into a prosperous mercantile hub. Wealth financed estates, churches, schools, and warehouses that still define the historic core. Yet these traces remain under-interpreted. Traditional guidebooks fail to engage youth or non￾Russian speakers. To bridge this gap, a student project combined historical research with product design. The theoretical framework emphasized five guidebook functions: educational, entertainment, communicative, advertising, and aesthetic.
