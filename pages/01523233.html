Jesse Babcock Born 1915 Residence United States Nationality USA Known for "True suction" theory of the origin of matter and gravity Scientific career Fields Gravity , cosmology, theory of energy Jesse Babcock (born 1915) is an American self-taught theorist associated with the Natural Philosophy community. He is known for an unorthodox cosmological framework he calls the "true suction" theory, which he developed to explain the origin of matter, energy and Gravity outside the framework of mainstream physics. Biography Babcock reports having only an eighth-grade formal education, a fact he presents as central to his outlook, arguing that it left him unencumbered by conventional scientific training. By his own account he became interested in perpetual motion as a child before 1923, and at around the age of fifteen concluded that a working perpetual-motion device could not be built until "the secret of the universe" was understood. He states that experiments imagining spring-and-spoke wheel mechanisms convinced him that any purely mechanical arrangement will always balance out, ruling out perpetual motion in practice. Babcock dates the origin of his theory to 1953. He recounts hearing a radio report that high-frequency sound waves from jet engines were damaging the lungs of ground crews, which led him to reconsider the idea that sound is "nothing." Reflecting on empty space, he concluded that space must be endless and that it is the only source from which anything could arise. Work Babcock's central claim is that space itself possesses a dynamic characteristic he terms "true suction," from which all matter and energy originate. In his view "true suction" is not an object or substance but a property of space, and everything that exists ultimately derives from space and this characteristic.
