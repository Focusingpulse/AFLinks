When Jeff Kooistra first reviewed Wizard: The Life and Times of Nikola Tesla for Infinite Energy #12, he said he was "a veteran of several Tesla reviews." After plodding through no less than three biographies of electricity's greatest showman, I confess to feeling somewhat battle fatigued. Unless you are a total Tesla freak, don't, I repeat, don't try this at home. My entree was Prodigal Genius by John J. O'Neill [Reprinted 1968, Spearman, ISBN 6-81610-1]. This was a good choice as starter. I chose it mainly because O'Neill was a close friend of Tesla and claimed to know him better than anyone. This book is the definitive biography and one from which the other authors clearly lifted much of their material on Tesla's personal life and eccentricities. It is heavily, and quite unashamedly, biased in favor of reflecting only the positive aspects of Tesla's character. O'Neill has a tendency to gloss over the inventor's mistakes and misjudgments, focusing only on his achievements and his incredible insight into technologies, the realization of which still lay far ahead in the future. I could probably have written the above paragraph based merely on reading the author's preface. O'Neill writes: "The rising star of Tesla's genius shot across the electrical heavens like a meteor.
