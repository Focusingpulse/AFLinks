GC2026-SGG047 Mantle transition zone of the Russian Far East from receiver function data Andrey Goev1,2 1 Sadovsky Institute of Geosphere Dynamics, Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia 2 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences goev@idg.ras.ru Mantle transition zone (MTZ), occurring at depths of approximately 410 and 660 km, is the region where olivine transforms into its denser states upon reaching specific P-T conditions. Accordingly, the boundaries at which these transitions occur are characterized by a sharp increase in seismic velocities. It has been established that when these boundaries interact with a cold slab (for example, in the Kuril-Kamchatka zone), their depths can vary significantly. One of the most effective tools for studying the local characteristics of these boundaries is the analysis of Ps converted waves (the PRF method). However, to achieve reliable results, it is necessary to analyze long-term records of broadband seismic data. For this reason, detailed studies of the depths of these boundaries in the Russian Far East zone were practically absent. In recent decades, the seismic networks of the Kamchatka and Sakhalin branches of the GS RAS have developed significantly, and now it compose more than 50 broadband seismometers. Processing of this data has, for the first time, made it possible to estimate the local relief of the 410 and 660 km discontinuities in the Russian Far East zone.
