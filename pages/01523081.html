Harvey L. Morgan Born ( 1920-10-01 ) October 1, 1920 Devoe, South Dakota, United States Died July 25, 1998 (1998-07-25) (aged 77) Residence Deming, NM, United States Nationality American Known for Claimed superluminal transmission of electrical signals; a momentum-field theory of gravity Scientific career Fields Chemical engineering , electronics, invention Harvey Lenard Morgan (1 October 1920 – 25 July 1998) was an American chemical and electronics engineer, inventor, and technical writer. After a long career in the aircraft and defense-electronics industries he became, in retirement, a contributor to the Natural Philosophy Alliance , where he argued from his own laboratory measurements that electrical signals can be made to travel faster than light and that gravity is best understood as a field of momentum rather than as an attraction or a curvature of spacetime. Biography Education Morgan was born in Devoe, South Dakota, on 1 October 1920. He took both of his degrees at the University of Idaho: 1942 – B.S. in chemical engineering, University of Idaho 1947 – M.S. in physics, University of Idaho Career Between his two degrees Morgan worked in the aircraft industry as a mechanical engineer from 1942 to 1945, then served in the armed forces. On completing his master's in 1947 he entered the electronics industry, where he spent the rest of his working life, much of it in the defense sector. His employers included several aircraft companies, General Electric (1960–1967), E-Systems (1967–1970), ARA Manufacturing (1977–1980) and Technical Development Corporation (1980–1989). He was a member of the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) from 1948, and between 1957 and 1998 published twenty-four technical articles in engineering and electronics trade journals, among them the IEEE Aerospace and Electronic Systems Magazine .
