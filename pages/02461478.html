Semantic Subtext in the Bubble Sort Algorithm Olegs Verhodubs oleg.verhodub@inbox.lv Abstract. The bubble sort algorithm is one of the simplest and most well‑known in programming. That is why this algorithm was chosen as an experimental algorithm for studying the meanings embedded in it and their transformation. This research is part of a broader study whose final goal is to develop a system for the automatic generation of algorithms. Automatic algorithm generation can be implemented in various ways, but in this case, preference is given to a semantic approach — more precisely, an approach based on Semantic Web Technologies. The advantage of this approach lies in the direct, “willful” movement (from one concept to another) towards the result, as well as in the ability to trace the entire path from the initial data to the generated algorithm. To achieve the final goal, it is proposed to develop an ontology of the semantic subtext of the algorithm. This ontology will serve as a source of rules for an expert system for the automatic generation of algorithms. Keywords: Algorithms, Bubble Sort Algorithm, Algorithm Generation, Python, Ontology, Rules from Ontology, Semantic Web I. Introduction Philosophical thought has been developing for many centuries, but the ancient Greek philosopher Plato was and still remains one of the greatest philosophers of all time. Many hundreds of years have passed since his life and death, and over this entire time we have grown accustomed to Plato’s idea that, in addition to the material world, there is also an immaterial world — namely, the world of ideas. According to Plato, the world of ideas is the world of ideal prototypes, that is, the models of all things, while the world we observe is merely a pitiful copy of some absolutely true being that is inaccessible to our senses [1].
