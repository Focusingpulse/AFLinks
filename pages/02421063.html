Ord-Hume Residence Surrey, England Nationality English Known for The standard history of perpetual motion; reviving the Luton Minor and the British homebuilt aircraft movement; authority on mechanical musical instruments Scientific career Fields Mechanical and aeronautical engineering; history of technology; mechanical music Institutions Royal Air Force; Handley Page Ltd; The Library of Mechanical Music & Horology Arthur Wolfgang Julius Gerard Ord-Hume is an English mechanical and aeronautical engineer, aircraft designer and historian of technology. He appears on this wiki as the author of Perpetual Motion: The History of an Obsession (1977), the standard history of the subject — but that is one book out of some fifty , published across nearly six decades, and it is not the field he is best known for. He has had three careers. He designed aircraft, he revived a light aeroplane that founded a movement, and he became one of the world's leading authorities on mechanical musical instruments . He is Director of the Library of Mechanical Music & Horology and lives in Surrey. Aviation After leaving the Royal Air Force, Ord-Hume worked as a designer at Handley Page Ltd on civil and military aircraft. His lasting contribution, however, was to amateur aviation. In March 1958 he and the designer C. H. Latimer-Needham formed a company to take over the design rights to the Luton Minor , a small pre-war single-seat monoplane that had lapsed into obscurity. Ord-Hume rebuilt the pre-war example G-AFIR and built G-ASAA, among the first of the post-war machines, and the pair developed the redesigned Luton LA.4a Minor and later the LA.5a Major . The consequence was out of proportion to the aeroplane. Plans for the LA.4a were sold worldwide and the type was built by amateurs in many countries, and Ord-Hume is credited with laying the foundations of the post-war British homebuilt aircraft movement .
