For that reason the conversation with the director of the Nikola Tesla Museum, Dr. Aleksandar Marinčić, had little of a museum’s “historical patina.” In many ways it was direct contact with the present, or even the future (!), because we also spoke of projects such as “star wars” and the “death ray,” on which work is only now being done. It probably came out that way because Dr. Aleksandar Marinčić, as the professional organizer and the author of a paper at the symposium marking the 135th anniversary of Nikola Tesla’s birth, held during our meeting at the Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts from 23 to 25 September, had gained an excellent view of what is happening today with the great scientist’s work. Some American journalists, at the mention of Tesla’s name, used to say that he “offers fewer stories and more realized projects.” Today, when so many of his projects are part of everyday life, and when some have yet to be studied and realized, one sees how far ahead of his time our countryman was. — There is a view that much of Tesla’s life and work remains unilluminated, and that many papers have yet to be studied.
