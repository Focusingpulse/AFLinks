Title Centrifugal Force and the Electron-Positron Sea Read in full Link to paper Author(s) David Tombe Published 2015 Journal General Science Journal Abstract We often hear it said that centrifugal force is not a real force and that it is only the effects of inertia resulting from a body undergoing its uniform straight line path, as per Newton’s first law of motion. These effects of inertia however are very real and this suggests that the inertial path itself must have an underlying physical cause. This cause will now be ascribed to a background elasticity that is rooted in electrostatics. The electric dipole, with its inverse cube law field, will be proposed as the primary physical cause of centrifugal force, while the inertial path and the Mach Principle will be seen to result from the compound effect of a dense multitude of superimposed dipole fields filling all of space. Overview Frederick David Tombe argues that centrifugal force is a real physical force rather than an artefact of describing motion from a rotating frame, and that its cause is a background elasticity rooted in electrostatics. Space, on this account, is densely packed with picometre-scale electron–positron dipoles, each a rotating pair whose field falls off as the inverse cube of distance; the pressure of that sea against itself is what defines the inertial path and what underlies Mach's principle. The argument's hinge is a comparison of exponents. Writing acceleration in polar coordinates gives centrifugal and Coriolis terms, and in a central-force problem the radial equation takes Leibniz's form r̈ = − k / r ² + l / r ³, with the centrifugal term going as the inverse cube. An electric dipole's field also goes as the inverse cube. Tombe takes the coincidence as an invitation: "the fact that it follows the inverse cube law makes it reasonable to suspect that the inertial path is caused by a dense sea of tiny electric dipoles."
