Introduction Let E(n) : 1 = s0 < s1 = 2 < . . . < sh = n be an addition chain leading to n ≥ 3. Addition chains provide nat￾ural, combinatorial recipes for forming integers by repeated summa￾tion of previous terms [1]. Their length and internal structure arise in complexity theory and algorithmic number theory, but here we raise a different, purely arithmetic question: Under mild regularity, how many primes must an addition chain contain? In this paper, we state and motivate Conjecture 2.1 and provide a compact and persuasive heuristic for it. Informally, the conjecture predicts that any addition chain whose upper half follows a reasonably regular linear profile contains at least h 2 log( h 2 ) (1 + o(1)) primes, as n → ∞ (with h growing with n).
