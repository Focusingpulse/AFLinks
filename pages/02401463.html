GC2026-SGG025 Tectonic earthquakes with NDC sources Ekaterina A. Marshakova1,2 , Vladimir B. Smirnov1,2 , Valentin O. Mikhailov1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Lomonosov Moscow State University katya-marshakova@mail.ru Although the DC (Double Couple) model adequately describes pure shear slip on a planar fault in a homogeneous isotropic medium, a significant number of earthquakes exhibit a Non-Double Couple (NDC) component in their seismic moment tensor. The origin of this component remains debated, as it may reflect either real physical processes in the source, for example crack opening or collapse [1], the presence of subevents with different mechanisms [2], phase transformations [3], or artifacts related to inversion methodology and mismodeling of the Earth structure [4]. In this study, a global statistical analysis of tectonic earthquakes that deviate from the classical DC source model is performed using the Global Centroid Moment Tensor (GCMT) catalog for the period 1976–2025 (Mw ≥ 5) [5]. The deviation of an earthquake source from the DC model is quantified by the sine of the angle α between the slip vector and the rupture plane. This parameter is computed from the principal values of the seismic moment tensor and accounts for uncertainties in the moment tensor components. To ensure robustness, only events satisfying |sin α| > 0.5 and having a relative uncertainty below 30% are classified as NDC type earthquakes.
