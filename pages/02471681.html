The article, " A Close Look at Charge Deposition from a Pulsed Tesla Coil System ," by Richard Hull and Scott Fusare , ESJ 35, pp. 20-26, is a good piece of investigative research. In summary, it reported how a Keithley 610BR electrometer was giving a false indication of charge deposition on an isolated capacity from a Tesla coil system in which neither spark nor corona were created. I fully support the conclusions presented in the main article, which state that the tests must be conducted with a non-sparking Tesla coil. However, the statements regarding the operation of the Tesla coil in the last sidebar are vague, because they don't make the distinction between sparking and non-sparking coils. In the sidebar, entitled "Mea Culpa," the statement, "no net charge is placed on remote conductors by a functioning Tesla coil," is correct only in the context of non-sparking coils.
