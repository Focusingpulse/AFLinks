On March 23, 1989, in a news conference at the University of Utah, the electrochemist B. S. Pons (also speaking for bis colleage M. Fleischmann) claimed (1] that nuclear energy, in the form of heat, was liberated by a room temperature "table-top" apparatus that used a palladium (Pd) cathode to electrolyze heavy water (D20). In a paper submitted for publication a few days earlier [2], the two chemists had written that "the bulk of the energy release is due to a hitherto unknown nuclear process or processes (presumably ... due to clusters of deuterons)". The immediate - and thereafter unrelenting - reaction of the hot fusion community was disbelief based on the absence at the expected intensities, of the customary signs of a DD reaction, such as neutrons (d + d -n + 3He) and high energy y-rays (d + d - y + 4He).
