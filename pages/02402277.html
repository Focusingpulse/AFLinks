MolPhot2026-main035 Polymetallic nanoclusters on biopolymer matrices. Varvara G. Kubenko1 , Nikolai V. Shekhovtsov1 , Egor I. Alekseenko1 , Tomash S. Sych1 , Andrey A. Buglak1 , Alexei I. Kononov1 1 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia alexei.i.kononov@gmail.com Metal nanoclusters (NCs) offer unique possibilities in a wide range of fields. In particular, their electronic structure with an energy gap being much more than kT energy enables their luminescence capability. They can be used as luminophores for bioimaging and sensing applications [1]. Various approaches are used to increase brightness of NCs. One of them is the use of different metals in the synthesis of clusters. The synthesis of bi- and trimetallic NCs in solution protected by phosphine ligands was started more than 30 years ago. Since then, a lot of bi- and trimetallic NCs have been synthesized [2]. In some cases, enhancement in the luminescence quantum yield (QY) was observed upon alloying due to the synergistic effect. In this presentation, a summary of the main advances on the luminescent polymetallic NCs stabilized by thiols and biomolecules are reviewed. They include NCs protected by thiolates, peptides, proteins and DNA. Biomolecule-templated NCs can provide biocompatibility and low toxicity in comparison with conventional luminophores such as dyes and quantum dots. Essentially, the specificity of biomolecular interactions provide the necessary selectivity of biosensors based on NCs. Possible mechanisms of the luminescence enhancement are discussed.
