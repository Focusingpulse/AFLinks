B. Haggin Sued by Princess Who Painted Portrait New York Herald June 26th, 1913 Page number(s): 22 Sat in Chair Used by Kaiser, and Artist Values Her Work at $25,000. Princess Lwoff-Parlaghy , through her attorney, Alton B. Parker, brought an action yesterday against James B. Haggin, mining man and horse fancier, for $25,000, which she alleges he agreed to pay to her for painting his portrait. To explain the reason for the litigation the Princess consented to see me in her atelier in the Plaza Hotel. She said that Mr. Haggin, who is more than ninety years old, agreed to pay $25,000 to her for painting his portrait, and when it was finished he told her all he could afford to give was $10,000, which, the Princess asserted, she refused. Princess Lwoff-Parlaghy has painted portraits of several of the crowned heads of Europe, and a picture which gained for her much renown as an artist was one she made in Bismarck. In the mellow light of the spacious room of her apartment I found the Princess seated comfortably on a Louis XVI. sofa of red and gold. Her gown was of green and gold — her hair is true titian — and on her corsage she wore a crown of rubies and diamonds, which she said was a gift from Prince Bismarck.
