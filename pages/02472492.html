McKay on Its Properties and Functions. ONE OF THE MYSTERIES OF THE PHYSICAL UNIVERSE — THE THEORY OF ITS EXISTENCE A DEDUCTION FROM NATURAL PHENOMENA. An entertaining and carefully prepared lecture on the subject of “The Ether, Its Properties and Functions” was delivered before the Institute Department of Physics by Prof. John S. McKay, president of the department, at the Art Building last evening. There were a number of stereopticon illustrations. The theory of ether, said Prof. McKay, was formulated about a hundred years ago, principally in order to account for the transmission of light through space. The Newtonian theory that light consisted of luminous particles shot out in straight lines at incredible speed was superseded by the modern wave or undulatory conception. Sound and heat are supposed to travel in a like method. A large number of phenomena are now explained, and cannot otherwise be explained, by the deduced existence of the etherial medium. Within comparatively recent years its importance in the universe of which we are cognizant has been much recognized by men of science, until to-day it is considered as one of the ultimate mysteries. Ether is that which pervades all space, meaning also what we know as material substances. It is, of course, invisible.
