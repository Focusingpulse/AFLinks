SPAF-2026-main022 CDU: 550.3 Current challenges in electrical prospecting within the arctic and subarctic regions of eastern russia and approaches to their resolution Darya F. Absalyamova1,2 1 Institute of Earth Crust of the Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Vetvistoe LLC, Karla Marksa 40, Irkutsk, Russia dasha.absalyamova@mail.ru The development of the mineral resource base and infrastructure in the Arctic and subarctic territories of Eastern Russia requires reliable delineation of the permafrost layer and a robust assessment of underlying thawed horizons. In continuous and discontinuous permafrost, shallow transient electromagnetic (sTEM) sounding, vertical electrical sounding (VES), and electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) encounter interrelated methodological challenges that have not been systematically addressed for this region. This review identifies the key issues and outlines pathways for overcoming them through substantiated integrated interpretation of methods. The thick high‑resistivity layer of frozen rocks (up to 10⁶ Ω·m and above) creates fundamentally different difficulties for methods of different physical principles. For VES and ERT, it acts as a nearly impermeable screen: current spreads near the surface, depth of investigation is severely limited, and conductive taliks at depth are either not detected or generate spurious anomalies due to lateral current redistribution bypassing the frozen layer.
