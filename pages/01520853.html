Title On the Continuing Relevance of Lorentz Ether Theory in the Age of Relativity Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Doug Marett Keywords Lorentz , Ether , Special Relativity , reference frame Published 2011 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 8 No. of pages 5 Pages 397-401 Abstract With the advent of relativity the Lorentz Ether theory has been relegated to not much more than a historical footnote. What is less well known is that virtually all optical experiments to date to test the validity of special relativity cannot distinguish between the predicted outcomes of the two theories. We review the historical development of the Lorentz theory as it evolved to address the results of key optical observations of the 19th century. We then examine how modern optical experiments attempting to detect the relative inertial motion of an observer with respect to a preferred reference frame for light remain consistent with Lorentz's predictions. Overview Marett's thesis is that Lorentz ether theory has been relegated to a historical footnote without ever having been refuted, and that virtually no optical experiment can distinguish it from special relativity. This is correct, it is the standard view among historians and philosophers of physics, and the paper argues it competently. The historical review is accurate. Stellar aberration, explained by Bradley in 1728 on a corpuscular picture, was accommodated in wave theory through Huygens' principle with an immobile ether.
