Harvey Scribner Known for Transverse relative time shift; time dilation as a two-signal time delay; classical geometric alternatives to Special Relativity Scientific career Fields Physics , Relativity , Electromagnetism Harvey Scribner is an independent researcher working in relativity and electrodynamics who argues that the time and length effects usually attributed to Special Relativity can be derived from ordinary Euclidean geometry and Galilean transformations applied to signal propagation. He has published his work through the viXra preprint archive and has presented in the online seminar series of the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society (CNPS). Scientific contributions Scribner's central claim is that the standard derivation of relativistic time dilation contains what he calls an undiscovered "physics disconnect": in his reading, the signals used to obtain the time-dilation expression are effectively signals from fixed sources in space rather than from the moving source itself. On that basis he reinterprets time dilation as a two-signal time delay — a difference in the arrival times of two separately emitted signals — rather than as a change in the rate of time itself. This argument is set out in his paper Relativity Time Dilation a Two Signal Time Delay Theory (2023), and formed the core of his 2023 CNPS presentation, in which he examined two textbook cases, the light clock at rest and the light clock at rest with a moving observer. The geometric machinery behind this reinterpretation is what Scribner calls the transverse relative time shift . He considers a source S moving with constant velocity along an axis in Euclidean space, emitting spherical signals that expand at the velocity of light c relative to the position from which they were emitted. An observer placed transversely to the source's line of motion receives successive signals at different times, and the measured difference between those arrival times is the transverse relative time shift.
