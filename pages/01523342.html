Title A General Discussion on Limit Velocity Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Speed of Light , Autodynamics , relativistic mass, klystron, cyclotron radiation, superluminal Published 2000 Website https://www.autodynamics.org/limit-velocity/ "A General Discussion on Limit Velocity" is a white paper by Ricardo L Carezani written in response to a question raised during the lecture he and David de Hilster gave at California State University, Sacramento on 16 June 2000. Its subject is why the speed of light is a limit, and its claim is that Autodynamics and special relativity give different reasons — with different consequences. Overview Carezani's target is the standard account: that a particle cannot reach c because its mass grows without bound as it approaches. He argues that mass and kinetic energy cannot both increase, that only the kinetic energy does, and that the light-speed limit is therefore a property of the carrier used to accelerate the particle rather than a universal ceiling. The argument Energy or mass, not both "If an Electron beam has an energy equal to 1 MeV all its energy is carried only as Kinetic Energy. It is calorimetrically measured. There is no increasing energy that is equivalent to an increasing mass." The relativistic kinetic-energy formula, Carezani observes, contains m 0 as a constant and v as a variable; the energy rises because the velocity rises, not because the rest mass changes. Applying a separate mass equation alongside it, he argues, double-counts and violates energy conservation. Why acceleration gets harder His alternative explanation appeals to the inertia of energy, which he notes both theories accept. The Stanford linac has 230 klystrons, each delivering the same force f . The first raises the electron's velocity by some amount and gives it energy E .
