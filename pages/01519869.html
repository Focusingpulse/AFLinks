Title Electron Spin and the Emission of Photons Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Mitch Emery Keywords Electron , Photon , Spin, Waves, Planck , Photoelectric Effect Published 2007 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 4 Number 1 No. of pages 2 Pages 58-59 Abstract The awkward spin of an electron escapes rational understanding. But by principles from my previous study, the spin is portrayed as a dual spin—with one spin perpendicular to the other. A dual spin as such serves to explain the emission of photons, along with their wave-like pattern, and the speed limit c. Planck's radiation law and the photoelectric effect are also discussed. Overview Electron Spin and the Emission of Photons is a two-page paper by Mitch Emery of Maumee, Ohio, presented at the 14th conference of the Natural Philosophy Alliance and published in the Proceedings of the NPA volume 4, number 1 (2007), pages 58–59. It builds on his earlier "New Physics Based on Force-Free Circular Motion," and proposes that the electron's spin is a dual spin — two rotations at right angles, one at twice the rate of the other — and that this accounts for photon emission, the wave pattern of light, the speed limit c , Planck's radiation law and the photoelectric effect. The foundation is a notion of circular motion requiring no force. "A body moves in a straight line, but its linear momentum is carried and turned effortlessly by a spin from the body itself." Emery is careful that this cannot be produced by force — "force-free circular motion cannot be created by force. The very idea contradicts itself" — and concludes it must be a pre-existing feature of the universe: "force was not the cause of the effect because the effect was already here." He offers the Moon as an instance: "The Moon has an underlying straight-line motion, but its linear momentum is carried and turned by a force-free spin from the Moon itself.
