Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels

· archived # 1,460,124
The Parasite Prophet and His Poisoned Heirs: Karl Marx’s Life of Dependency, Racial Hatred, and the Socialist Road to Eugenic Culling [Socialists Have A Proud Heritage Indeed] Karl Marx’s life was socialism in miniature: a man who lived as a permanent charge upon the industry of others while his own household paid the bill in misery. He preached the overthrow of the capitalist order yet survived only because one capitalist Friedrich Engels, heir to a Manchester cotton mill kept sending cheques. Engels’ factory profits, extracted from the very system Marx denounced, paid the rent in Soho, bought the coal, and kept the British Museum reading room open to the theorist who never once had to meet a payroll or balance a ledger of his own. Marx produced mountains of manuscripts; he produced almost nothing that fed his children. The results were predictable. Of the seven children born to Jenny Marx, four died in childhood or infancy. The family moved from one damp, overcrowded lodging to the next. Jenny, raised in a Prussian aristocratic household, pawned her clothes and silver, nursed the dying, and watched her husband’s chronic illness and chronic debt devour what little security remained. Marx himself spent long stretches in bed or in the reading room while the women of the household managed the actual grinding work of survival.
