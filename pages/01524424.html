Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Marvin Eli Kirsh Keywords scientific cultural social political economic evolution, abstraction, representation of nature, marxism, capitolism, relativity , model of volume, aceticism, religion and science, space and volume Published 2009 No. of pages 15 Abstract A conceptual parallel is created between studies in anthropology and natural science. A space occupied by "the conceptualization" is argued to not only parallel symbolically the scientifically conceived value of volume as space, but to have an equal meaning- the spaces of the conceptual ordering of nature as manifestations of energy are held to be paralleling manifestations of the same energies that mold the environment. A growing historical asceticism attributed to the individual in struggles with nature and himself becomes destructive universally as the consequence of scientific activities that are focused from raw curiosities that are distant from real symbolic meaning. Overview This is a philosophical and anthropological essay rather than a physics paper, written by an anthropologist at California State University Los Angeles. Its subject is the act of scientific description itself: the paper asks whether, when the physicist reports "what is", he is asking a question of nature or telling nature what it must be. Kirsh's title carries that double sense deliberately — "What's happening", he writes in the conclusion, "is more of a self willed acceleration to what happened." The claim is that modern physical science has quietly converted its measuring instruments into products of its own abstractions, so that the abstraction now certifies the ruler rather than the ruler the abstraction. Kirsh's example is the definition of the metre in terms of a fixed speed of light , as recorded in the NIST edition of The International System of Units (Taylor and Thompson, 2008).
