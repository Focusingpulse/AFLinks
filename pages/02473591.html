Solving a quintic equation using two de Moivre’s formulas Tai-Choon Yoon1 (Dated: Oct. 15th, 2026) Abstract It is well known that general solutions cannot be found for higher-degree equations, such as quintic and sextic equations. This is because solving a quintic equation requires solving a sextic equation, and vice versa. However, if the quintic equation encountered while solving a sextic equation is a de Moivre quintic (DM quintic), then the sextic equation can be solved. Although the condition is specific, solving the sextic equation allows for the solution of the quintic equation as well. While standard factorization methods fail to solve equations of the fifth degree or higher, solutions can be obtained by utilizing two forms of de Moivre’s formula that are solvable via root formulas. Specifically, if a general 𝑛th odd-degree equation can be decomposed into two or more 𝑛th degree de Moivre equations derived from de Moivre’s formula, then the sum of their respective roots constitutes a root of the original 𝑛th degree equation. Therefore, if the relationships between the coefficients of the odd degree de Moivre formulas and those of the 𝑛th degree equation can be established, the solution to the 𝑛th degree equation can be found. A. Introduction Methods for solving equations include factoring and using the quadratic formula. Solutions for quadratic and cubic equations can be found using the quadratic or cubic formulas. However, equations of the quartic or higher degree generally cannot be solved using a root formula. Therefore, such equations are typically factored into lower-degree equations—such as quadratics or cubics— whose solutions can then be found using the appropriate root formulas. Factoring a quartic equation into two quadratic equations entails solving an intermediate cubic equation.
