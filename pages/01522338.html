Title A Survey of Anomalous Redshifts Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Hilton Ratcliffe Keywords redshift , quasars , galaxies, expansion, cosmology, anomalous, peculiar. Published 2008 No. of pages 41 Abstract One of the greatest challenges facing astrophysics is derivation of remoteness in cosmological objects. At large scales, it is almost entirely dependent upon the well-established Hubble relationship in spectral redshift. The comparison of galactic redshifts with distances arrived at by other means has yielded a useable curve to an acceptable confidence level, and the assumption of scale invariance allows the adoption of redshift as a standard calibration of cosmological distance. However, there have been several fields of study in observational astronomy that consistently give apparently anomalous results from ever-larger statistical samples, and would thus seem to require further careful investigation. This paper presents a review summary of recent independent work, primarily (for galaxies and proto-galaxies) by teams led by, respectively, D. G. Russell, M. Lopez-Corredoira, and H. C. Arp, and for galaxy clusters and large-scale structures, those of N. A. Bahcall, J.C. Jackson, and N. Kaiser. Included also are several other important contributions that will be fully cited in the text. The observational evidence is presented here per se without attempting theoretical conclusions or extrapolating the data to cosmology. Overview The PDF carries the title "A Review of Anomalous Redshift Data"; it is the same paper. Hilton Ratcliffe writes as an independent researcher, and the piece is deliberately a review rather than a theory: he collects observational results that sit badly with the assumption that spectral redshift is a reliable distance calibrator, and declines to draw a cosmological conclusion from them.
