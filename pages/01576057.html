Feasibility-Boundary Analysis of a Speculative Bio-ISRU Regenerative Logistics Architecture for Reusable Solar-Electric Cargo Tugs Sam DeRenzis Independent Researcher Bolingbrook, Illinois, USA waitandhope123@gmail.com 2026 Abstract Reusable solar-electric propulsion (SEP) cargo tugs are candidate elements of cislunar and Mar￾tian logistics architectures, but they depend on repeated delivery of Earth-supplied electric￾propulsion (EP) propellant, a recurring driver of launch mass and campaign complexity. This paper analyses a deliberately speculative Symbiotic Tug Boundary-Case Architecture (STBCA), in which a conventional reusable SEP tug carries a low-TRL bio-ISRU subsystem that generates candidate propellant precursors from local CO2, water, and waste during long parked dwells, downstream conditioning and upgrading stages, and an EP-compatibility quali cation chain. Biological outputs are treated strictly as candidate precursors requiring quali cation, not as ight-ready EP propellant, and no replacement of xenon supply chains is assumed. The contribution is a mission-level feasibility-boundary methodologynot a viability claim, design proposal, or implementation plan. Expressing the lifetime Earth-launched mass dierence as a function of reuse count N, we show it is linear in N, so feasibility is governed by the sign of a per-cycle mass credit, not by any single quantity. Two results follow: within this linear model, below a critical derated fraction no amount of reuse closes the mass balance; and the dominant attrition is a derating chainreliability, EP-compatibility quali cation retention, and a newly introduced EP performance-equivalence factor κEPnot bioreactor productivity. A Monte Carlo propagation over largely literature-sourced priors leaves the architecture unable to close in approximately 98% of Monte Carlo realisations under the assumed priors, with per-cycle recurring mass and κEP the two decisive drivers.
