Saturday Science Chats Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Date 1 August 2020 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Saturday Science Chats was a live session on 1 August 2020 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Lead by Franklin Wu sponsored by the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society. Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Ian Cohen and Bob, and one of the more technical of the series — on where the electromagnetic wave equation comes from and whether it is entitled to. Reading list Ian opened with J. P. Wesley , recommended to him by Dennis Allen : the large volume Scientific Physics , now obtainable only from the family, Wesley having died. Bob had worked through Wesley's books some years ago and gave a useful assessment — much of it Weber electrodynamics, but Wesley wanted to drop the acceleration terms, and Bob had followed the derivation and found an error in it, adding that if Wesley were alive he would show him the terms need not be dropped and that Weber electrodynamics works well with them. Ian returned the favour with Alfred O'Rahilly , a countryman of his and once president of University College Cork, whose nine-hundred-page Electromagnetics is freely available and challenges Einstein throughout; Bob has a copy and has not found the lifetime it would take to work through. Bob added Thomas E Phipps 's Heretical Verities as a comparable alternative account. Where the displacement current comes from Bob offered the session's best piece of history. The textbook story is that Maxwell added the displacement term to Ampère's law to complete the symmetry of the equations — the genius spotting what was missing. Bob recalled reading in Assis , who works from original papers, that a letter of Maxwell's says he had made a calculation error, and that correcting it is what produces the term.
