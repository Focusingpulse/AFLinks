Title Reforming Electromagnetic Units, Equations, and Concepts: An Extension of Ivor Catt Theory Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Forrest Bishop Keywords electromagnetic, Units, speed of light , physical constants, electric charge Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 12 Pages 38-49 Abstract Some of the problems, paradoxes, and clutter within the mathematical physics of the past few centuries are briefly reviewed. Catt's concept of illusory static fields created by TEM waves replaces the electrostatic and magnetostatic field concepts, removing any number of these paradoxes at once. Z 0 , the vacuum wave impedance, is a physical constant as fundamental as the speed of light; together they replace permittivity and permeability. A units system- 'MNSZ', is proposed in which either the ohm or Z 0 replaces the ampere, allowing physical constants and equations to be rewritten without reference to coulomb, generally in a more compact form. By replacing the dielectric constant with the index of refraction, the common electrical equations are reduced to more compact forms that highlight the wave nature of the purported electric charge. Several example applications are shown, including a compact new expression for the voltage of the photon. Recent experimental results are presented which refute the exponential-decay model of a discharging capacitor. Overview Forrest Bishop's 2012 NPA paper is an extension of Ivor Catt 's "theory C", the claim that there is no such thing as a static electric or magnetic field: what looks static is always a pair of counter-propagating transverse electromagnetic (TEM) waves — "energy current" — moving at the speed of light in the dielectric. On this view electric current in the conventional sense is not a measured quantity at all but a survival of the medieval doctrine of invisible fluids, "an enshrined ghost" for which a base SI unit has been named.
