Frederick David Tombe Born 1958 (age 67–68) Residence Belfast, Northern Ireland, United Kingdom Nationality British Alma mater Queen's University Belfast Known for Double helix theory of the magnetic field; electron-positron sea aether model Scientific career Fields Electromagnetism , Centrifugal Force , Coriolis Force , Aether , Gravity Frederick David Tombe (born 1958) is a British independent researcher who writes on electromagnetism , the aether , and the foundations of Maxwell's equations. He is known for advocating the revival of the nineteenth-century luminiferous aether, which he models as a dense "electric sea" of rotating electron-positron dipoles, and for his "double helix theory of the magnetic field." His views on special relativity , the luminiferous aether and the reality of centrifugal force place his work outside the mainstream of modern physics. He is among the most prolific contributors catalogued on this wiki: the list below runs to 122 papers written between 1984 and 2024, and he has continued to publish since. Education B.Sc. in physics, Queen's University Belfast , 1978–1982 — graduating July 1982, with astronomy and applied mathematics as subsidiary subjects Biography Tombe has described how, early in his undergraduate studies, exposure to Einstein's special relativity alongside the concept of stellar aberration led him to become skeptical of relativity theory, and how his study of vector field theory in 1981 left him dissatisfied with the standard textbook treatment of Maxwell's displacement current. In 1982 Tombe encountered the writings of anti-relativity authors including Professor Richard A. Waldron of the Ulster Polytechnic and, through him, the aether-drag work of Dr. Carl A. Zapffe of Baltimore, whom he visited in Minnesota shortly after graduating.
