David F. Roscoe Residence Sheffield, United Kingdom Nationality English Known for A "hard-line" reading of Mach's Principle ; the fractal D = 2 inertial universe; discrete dynamical classes in spiral galaxy discs; a proposed theoretical basis for MOND Scientific career Fields Applied mathematics , galaxy dynamics , Cosmology Institutions Department of Applied Mathematics, University of Sheffield David F. Roscoe is an English applied mathematician who taught in the Department of Applied Mathematics at the University of Sheffield . Over some twenty-five years he pursued a single line of argument — a deliberately uncompromising reading of Mach's Principle — from its philosophical roots in Berkeley and Leibniz through to a cosmology, a theory of gravitation, and a large statistical study of galaxy rotation curves. He is unusual among the scientists catalogued on this wiki in that the work was published in the mainstream literature : Astronomy & Astrophysics , General Relativity and Gravitation , Astrophysics and Space Science and The European Physical Journal D , together with chapters from Cambridge University Press, Kluwer and World Scientific. The ideas are heterodox; the venues were not. Biography Roscoe spent his academic career in the Department of Applied Mathematics at the University of Sheffield , on Hounsfield Road, which was later absorbed into the School of Mathematics and Statistics; within it he belonged to the Particle Astrophysics and Gravitation research group, giving galaxy dynamics as his research interest. He is listed by the School as retired academic staff. He has also taught mathematics part-time for the Open University. He continued to post papers after retiring, the most recent in 2020. The 1993 Frontiers of Fundamental Physics conference at Olympia in Greece was a formative meeting for him and for several others catalogued here. Martin Kokus later recalled that on the last day Halton Arp took a group of astronomers and cosmologists — Bill Napie
