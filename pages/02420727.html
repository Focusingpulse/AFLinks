Quirin Schiermeier investigates whether carbon offsetting can really save the planet. Russ George, founder and president of the offsetting company Planktos, based in Foster City, California, is one those thinking big. He plans a 10,000-hectare 'climate park' in Hungary. Plant ecologists with the Hungarian Academy of Sciences have conducted a feasibility study of the environmental impact and permanence of the project, and planting is to begin in 2007. Planktos is also offering credits for its controversial ocean eco-restoration projects, which involve fertilizing plankton growth with iron. Bottom line George believes voluntary offsetting could solve the problem of climate change. "We're expecting to save the world and make a little money on the side," he says. "In that order."
