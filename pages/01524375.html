It validates the explanation of dark matter mystery detailed in this article. Overview Frédéric Lassiaille sets out to test, against real data, the modified gravitational law proposed in his earlier paper "A solution for the 'dark matter mystery' based on Euclidean relativity." Where that paper used an estimated matter-density law for a hypothetical galaxy, this one applies the law to five real objects: NGC 3310, NGC 1068, NGC 157, NGC 7541 and NGC 7331. The calculation is carried out in a MAPLE program, reproduced in full. The modified force uses e = √(8 R / x ) (√ r + √ρ 0 )/(√ r + √ρ) where ρ 0 is the matter density at the Sun's position in the Milky Way, ρ is the density in the target galaxy as a function of galactocentric distance x , R is a Schwarzschild-type radius MG / c ² for the galactic centre, and r is "the square of the extra-galactic luminous contributions." The last is fixed by assuming the gravitational constant outside galaxies exceeds that inside by a factor G ′/ G = 10, whence √(ρ 0 / r ) = √( G ′/ G ) − 1 = 2.16. For NGC 3310 he takes ρ 0 = 0.03 solar masses per cubic light-year and a central mass of 0.07 × 10 23 solar masses, read from the galaxy's published integral mass profile. He is direct about the difficulty this creates. "The issue with the calculated force profile above is that there are as much positive (attractive) as negative (repulsive) values." His first approach is to compute v = √( Fx / m ) only where F > 0 and set the rest to zero, noting that "the global shape of the resulting curves is not very interesting since the negative (repulsing) values of the force are forced to 0."
