Title: An alternative equation for effective nuclear charge that is applicable to all atoms Ikechukwu Iloh Udema* Department of Chemistry and Biochemistry, Research Division, Ude International Concepts LTD (862217), B. B. Agbor, Delta State, Nigeria. *Corresponding author: udema_ikechukwu99@yahoo.com ORCID: https://orcid.org/0000-0001-5662-4232 1 Abstract Although the advent of ionization energies predated Slater’s rule, there was unawareness of the utilitarian value of the former for the computation of effective nuclear charge (ENC for short) until recently and in this research. The study aimed to derive another equation in which the average ionization energies of hydrogen and any other multielectron atom are independent variables. The objective was to answer the question in the literature that states, "Are Slater’s Rules the Best Approach for Calculating Effective Nuclear Charge for Undergraduates?" (Moore & Birnbaum, 2014) and compare the graphical portrayal of chemical periodicity between outcomes using data generated using Slater's rules for some elements and data generated in this research. By exploring theoretical and computational methods and the data in the literature, the study showed that ENC (1.34458-3.32446) for the first 30 elements computed using the 2021 CODATA fine structure constant (FSC, for short) was higher than those (1.344023.32309) without it; however, the equation containing the computed FSC using e^2/20 hc and other equations gave the same value of ENC. The use of Slater’s result (atomic radii) for the computation of ENC (1.29859-5.06996) and average ionization energy, IE (553.21-2108.13 kJ), gave higher values than other methods. All plots using Slater’s values of ENC and IE versus atomic number (Z) did not show chemical periodicity (greater linearity), unlike the use of other values (less linearity or lower R^2).
