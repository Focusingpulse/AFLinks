Title Comparing Spinning Mossbauer, GPS, and VLBI Experiments Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Ronald R Hatch Keywords GPS , Global Positioning System, speed of light , Sagnac effect , atomic clocks Published 1994 No. of pages 8 Comparing Spinning Mossbauer, GPS, and VLBI Experiments is an eight-page paper given by Ronald R Hatch at the 1st Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference , held within the Pacific Division meeting of the American Association for the Advancement of Science at San Francisco State University in June 1994. Hatch gave his affiliation simply as "Global Positioning System Consultant" — he had left Magnavox the previous year and had not yet co-founded NavCom Technology. The paper takes three experimental programmes routinely cited as confirming special relativity — the spinning Mössbauer experiments, the Global Positioning System , and Very Long Baseline Interferometry — and argues that all three are moot on the question of an ether drift rather than decisive against it. In each case, he contends, an effect that would reveal a drift is exactly cancelled by a second effect on clock rates, so the null result carries no information either way. It is also the paper in which Hatch publicly abandons the position he had taken two years earlier in his book Escape from Einstein . Abstract Spinning Mossbauer experiments, with gamma ray source and detector on a spinning disk, are frequently cited as providing strong evidence in support of the special theory. However, as Hayden has shown, the claims are generally based upon two separate phenomena. Ruderfer suggested that one could detect the variation of the transit time across either the radius or diameter of the spinning disk if an ether wind were present. Turner and Hill looked for a change in the frequency of the gamma rays as a function of the source velocity.
