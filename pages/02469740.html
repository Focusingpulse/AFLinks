I know this sounds like a mouthful. It also sounds unimportant. But in this strange and relativistic world, it is really the fantastic, the microscopic and the unbelievable which is important. I happen to be very much interested in the electron; and I’m interested for the same reason that I’m interested in Bach, Cézanne, steak Chateaubriand and pandas. The electron is part of the slipstream of living. Look at what we , with our uniquely American mechanical genius, are doing: We are taking the almost-imperceptible electron, slipping it into harness, sending it up to detect airplanes, sending it down from airplanes to detect submarines, shooting it through smokestacks to detect and measure tell-tale smoke. But recently , in the Westinghouse Lamp Laboratories at Bloomfield, N. J., I saw what to me is one of the strangest and most awesome demonstrations of electronics yet to be made: something that puts even the marvels of television into the shade. I saw a Klystron in action. Tesla’s Dream The Klystron, girls and boys , is a simple, high-frequency electronic tube.
