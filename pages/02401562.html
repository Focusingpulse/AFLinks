GC2026-STP057 Verification of magnetospheric models T-01 and T-03 using GLE technique Yury V. Balabin1 1 Polar Geophysical Institute of the Kola Scientific Center of the Russian Academy of Sciences balabin@pgia.ru Solar cosmic ray (SCR) are recorded by ground-based neutron monitors (NMs). Such events are designated as Ground Level Enhancements (GLEs). SCRs are characterized by strong flux anisotropy and high intensity, sometimes exceeding the flux of galactic cosmic rays by orders of magnitude. Using data from the global NM network, the inverse GLE problem is solved - namely, determining the parameters of the SCR energy spectrum. The inverse problem technique developed at PGI is based on calculating the response of each NM to an anisotropic SCR flux and selecting spectral parameters that best fit the NM data during a GLE, as is shown in Equation. ΔN = ∫ S(R) D(R) F(θ(R)) dR where R is the particle rigidity, ΔN is the NM count rate increase, S(R) is the SCR spectrum, D(R) is the NM yield function, and F(θ(R)) is the angular function that accounts for the mutual orientation of a particle with a given pitch angle and the direction of acceptance. It is note the set of all such directions is the asymptotic cone of acceptance (AC). As can be seen, the spatial orientation of the AC enters directly into the expression determining the NM response to the SCR flux. In case of an incorrectly calculated AC position leads to an incorrect NM response and subsequently makes it impossible to determine SCR flux characteristics consistent with the observations from the NM network.
