Gauss Academy of Science Press Pages 293 ISBN 0971881103 The Planck Aether Hypothesis: An Attempt for a Finitistic Non-Archimedean Theory of Elementary Particles is a 2002 book by Friedwardt Winterberg , published by the C.F. Gauss Academy of Science Press. Its cover asks the question the book is built around: "Is the Secret of the Universe hidden in Negative Masses?" Winterberg is not an outsider to physics. He took his doctorate under Werner Heisenberg, is a research professor at the University of Nevada, Reno, has published more than 260 papers, and holds the Hermann Oberth Gold Medal for work on nuclear rocket propulsion. The Planck aether is the heterodox strand of that career, and it has appeared in refereed journals as well as in this book. The hypothesis The proposal is that space is densely filled with an equal number of positive and negative Planck-mass particles , interacting locally over a Planck length with the Planck force, and obeying a law of motion that is exactly non-relativistic — Newtonian mechanics at the smallest scale there is. Everything else is then meant to emerge from that substratum rather than be assumed. Quantum mechanics and Lorentz invariance appear as low-energy approximations rather than as first principles. Einstein's gravitational waves and Maxwell's electromagnetic waves come out as the symmetric and antisymmetric modes of a vortex lattice in the aether. Electric charge is attributed to the zero-point fluctuations of Planck-mass particles bound in vortex filaments, and the Dirac spinors of ordinary particle physics to gravitationally bound positive–negative mass quasiparticles, with two particle families falling out and masses very small compared with the Planck mass.
