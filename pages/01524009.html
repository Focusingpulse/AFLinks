Article Title Nobel Prize Statement Press Release Author(s) Members of the Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Published in Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Date 1995 Subject area Physics Topics neutrino, Nobel Prize, Reines-Cowan experiment, autodynamics Read online Read the full article The 1995 Nobel Prize press release is a statement issued by members of the Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics objecting to the award of that year's Nobel Prize in Physics to Frederick Reines for the detection of the neutrino . It is the SAA's most direct public intervention against the neutrino hypothesis, predating the 2015 New York Times advertisement by twenty years. Overview The statement opens by declaring the Society "outraged by the award of a Nobel Prize for Physics to highly speculative work on the Neutrino Hypothesis", and takes as its target the Reines–Cowan experiment of the 1950s and the Nobel committee's own account of it. The argument The Reines–Cowan experiment The statement quotes the Nobel announcement — "There were struggles with the low counting speed and high background. During the experiments a few events were recorded per hour" — and reads the admission against the award: "'Struggles' indeed. The experiment was little more than smoke and mirrors. Any number of particles could (and did) react with the target medium to produce the observed results." The scale argument The sharpest point is a comparison of scales. "If 400 liters of target sufficed for detecting neutrinos in the 1950's, why are we building million gallon tanks for detection of neutrinos in the 1990's? Was the technique 40 years ago so much better than today's? Was the flux of neutrinos from the Savannah nuclear reactor substantially greater than that of the Sun? No, they are comparable, within an order of magnitude."
