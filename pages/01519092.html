Title A Description of Physical Reality Without Mass Stuff, Charge Stuff, and Field Stuff Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Gary L Johnson Keywords energy , information, infoton, graviton, gravity, charge, field, Ampère force law Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 6 Pages 245-250 A Description of Physical Reality Without Mass Stuff, Charge Stuff, and Field Stuff is a six-page conference paper by Gary L. Johnson , professor emeritus of electrical engineering at Kansas State University, presented at the 19th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in Albuquerque in July 2012 and printed in the Proceedings of the NPA , volume 9, pages 245–250. It proposes that physical reality contains only energy and information — that mass, charge and fields are not substances but bookkeeping, and that what we observe as forces is energy in motion, directed by information. Johnson presents it explicitly as "a general conceptual outline of how things might be put together", not a quantitative theory: "Considerable work remains to flesh out the concepts." Abstract It is speculated that physical reality consists of energy and information only, and not any mass stuff, charge stuff, or field stuff. Masslike particles are concentrations of energy. Writing E = mc 2 suggests that m is 'real' while E is 'calculated'. Rewriting as m = E / c 2 suggests that E is 'real' while m is 'calculated', which is the point of this paper. Energy travels from place to place in the form of photons and quite possibly in other forms that have not yet been fully identified. Information travels from place to place as a packet which might be called an infoton. There are hints that infotons travel much faster than light. Observed forces (gravity, electric, magnetic, etc.) are produced by an infoton causing a masslike particle to expend a small portion of its energy as propulsion in a specified direction.
