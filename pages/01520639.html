Title Microwave Power Measurements Author(s) Leslee A Kulba Keywords Microwave power, bolometer, directional coupler, mismatch uncertainty Published 2010 Journal Electric Spacecraft Journal Volume 48 Pages 27-29 Microwave Power Measurements is a three-page article by Leslee A Kulba , published in 2010 in issue 48 of the Electric Spacecraft Journal , the journal's final double issue. It is the eighth part of the ten-part handbook on perpetual motion and its testing that Kulba, the journal's technical editor, contributed to that issue, and it is the most specialised of the ten. The text of the article has not been recoverable, and this page describes its subject and its place in the series rather than its argument. The subject Why microwave power is a separate problem At low frequencies, power is found by measuring a voltage and a current at a point. At microwave frequencies that stops working: the wavelength becomes comparable with the apparatus, voltage and current are no longer well defined along a line, and what can be measured instead is power flow — how much energy per second is travelling in each direction along a transmission line. Every technique below is a way of turning that flow into something a meter can read. The subject earns its place in a handbook on energy claims because a good deal of the relevant experimental work — plasma devices, resonant cavities, and the microwave-driven arrangements in parts of the cold fusion and discharge literature — is done with radio-frequency and microwave excitation, and the input power is exactly the quantity a claim of excess output depends on. Sensors Thermistor and bolometer sensors absorb the microwave power and detect the resulting change in resistance. Their virtue is DC substitution : the instrument determines how much direct-current power produces the same heating, so the measurement is referred to a DC standard and is the most accurate method available.
