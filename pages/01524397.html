Streltsov Residence Dubna, Moscow Oblast, Russia Nationality Russian Known for The claim that moving bodies elongate rather than contract; a Lorentz-covariant "gravidynamics" offered in place of general relativity Scientific career Fields Experimental particle physics; foundations of relativity; theories of gravity Institutions Laboratory of High Energies, Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (JINR), Dubna Vyacheslav N. Streltsov was a Russian physicist in the Laboratory of High Energies at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (JINR) at Dubna, the international laboratory north of Moscow, and one of the most prolific critics of general relativity catalogued on this wiki. His central claim is unusually sharp and easy to state: that special relativity, correctly read, implies that a moving body lengthens rather than contracts. From that he developed a criticism of the standard treatment of length, and a Lorentz-covariant theory of gravity — "relativistic gravidynamics" — which he offered as a replacement for general relativity. Career Streltsov's early work at JINR was experimental particle physics . The literature database INSPIRE-HEP records him from 1968, with papers such as "Study of the formation of (e + e − ) pairs by 1.25-GeV to 4-GeV electrons" and, in 1979, "Space correlations in the three-particle interaction reaction." The turn toward the foundations of relativity begins as early as 1973, with "The Liénard-Wiechert potentials and the relativistic length conception," and becomes his main occupation from the late 1980s. From then until the end of the 1990s he produced a steady stream of JINR communications and preprints on length in relativity, the "radar formulation" of the theory, gravitational red shift, antigravitation and the Lorentz-covariant theory of gravity.
