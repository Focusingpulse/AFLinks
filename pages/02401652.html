GC2026-STP089 Building a Training Dataset of Solar Active-Region Evolution from MDI Magnetograms Sanjar Madimarov1,2 1 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (State University) 2 Space Research Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences madimarov.s@phystech.edu Long-term space weather forecasting can be based on so-called Toy Models, where the evolution of large-scale and small-scale magnetic fields on the Sun is described through interactions of simple objects. One such class of objects is fragments of solar active regions observed in MDI magnetograms. Information about the emergence, disappearance, and motion of these regions forms the core of the training dataset for a Toy Model. Current status: Primary visualization has been performed: magnetic-field maps and value distributions were produced, confirming the expected structure, namely the dominance of weak fields and heavy tails of strong values associated with active regions. Active regions and their fragments can be represented as a set of objects with parameters such as area, magnetic flux, centroid, polarity, and shape, and used to construct trajectories and event sequences on magnetogram time series, including emergence, splitting, merging, decay, and disappearance. Event types have been defined: emergence and disappearance, merging and splitting, and changes in the dominant polarity. Threshold-based segmentation and connected-component labeling have been implemented.
