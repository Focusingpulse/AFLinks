Science Chat Saturdays Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Bill Lucas , Ian Cohen, James Keene , Alistair Riddoch and Dennis Allen . Hu opened with a question rather than a topic: it is February 2020, a date that once sounded futuristic — why are there still such holes in physics, and why does nobody, orthodox or otherwise, know what gravity is? Lucas on why Lucas's answer was historical and political. He traced it to the American settlement between the established colonial churches and the revival movements, and argued that from about 1850 the separation of church and state was used to license a science defined by opposition to religion: what Maxwell and Ampère had called the Spirit of God became the electric field, and quantum mechanics took the universal wave function rather than the field as its object. He credited Poincaré with a meta-theory of theorems from logic applicable to all scientific theories, said Poincaré published relativity a year before Einstein and then showed by those theorems that it was false, and that this was why the theory was carried elsewhere. He gave his own experience as evidence of the cost. He had held the highest-paid postdoctoral position in the country at Catholic University, running a National Science Foundation grant to treat a thousand inoperable brain tumours with negative pion beams made at Los Alamos — the pion stopping in the brain, replacing an electron, entering the nucleus and breaking it up — with 990 of the thousand tumours destroyed and no notable side effects; and he was dismissed by the NSF and the Department of Energy after giving a paper to the American Physical Society arguing that logic falsifies special and general relativity.
