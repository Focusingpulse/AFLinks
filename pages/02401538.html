GC2026-STP046 Daily Variations of Observed SDO/HMI Doppler Velocities in Strong Magnetic Fields Aidar M. Sadykov1,2 , Viacheslav M. Sadykov3 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University 2 Skobeltsyn Institute of Nuclear Physics 3 Georgia State University, United States of America sadykov.am19@physics.msu.ru SDO/HMI data are widely used to study Doppler velocities and magnetic fields in the solar photosphere. However, their interpretation requires careful consideration of systematic effects associated both with the observables reconstruction algorithm and with the orbital kinematics of the spacecraft. In particular, for HMI it is well established that the spacecraft’s orbital velocity affects the measured line-of-sight (LoS) observables. Because SDO operates in a geosynchronous orbit, its radial velocity relative to the Sun exhibits a daily variation with an amplitude of up to approximately ±3.5 km/s [1]. In our study of HMI Doppler velocities, we identified pronounced deviations in regions of strong magnetic field that are modulated with a period of about 24 hours. Daily (and also 12-hour) periodicities and artifacts in HMI products in strong-field regions have been reported previously and attributed to the spacecraft’s orbital velocity combined with calibration and/or algorithmic effects [2]. In our study, we augment the existing analysis of this effect by quantifying how the amplitudes of orbital velocity-induced Doppler shifts depend on the magnetic field strengths in the region.
