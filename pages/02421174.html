Beck Born ( 1925-01-25 ) January 25, 1925 Dallas, Texas, United States Died June 23, 2002 (2002-06-23) (aged 77) California, United States Residence Santa Ana, CA, United States Nationality USA Alma mater University of Southern California Known for ELF magnetometers; the Brain Tuner cranial electrical stimulator; the "Beck Protocol" Scientific career Fields Physicist , Inventor ; electronics, scientific photography, electromedicine Robert Cary Beck (25 January 1925 – 23 June 2002), known as Bob Beck , was an American electrical engineer, inventor and instrument designer. He is remembered for two quite different bodies of work. The first is documented and uncontroversial: from the late 1940s he was a working engineer and scientific photographer, holder of a 1950 patent on portable electronic flash equipment, a designer of lighting effects for the film industry, and later a consultant on extremely low frequency (ELF) magnetic instrumentation for defence laboratories, where he built sensitive portable magnetometers. The second is the "Beck Protocol" , a set of self-administered electrical and chemical health procedures he devised and promoted from the early 1990s, centred on passing microcurrents through the body and on home-made ionic colloidal silver. These he presented as treatments for a wide range of infectious disease including HIV. No part of the protocol has been shown by controlled clinical trial to treat any disease , and the colloidal silver component carries documented harms; see Medical assessment below.
