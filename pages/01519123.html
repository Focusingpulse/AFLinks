Title A Model of Gravity Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Bill Stubbs Keywords gravity Published 2012 No. of pages 5 Abstract The mechanisms that cause bodies to move toward each other due to gravity remain a mystery. It is shown that the typical dimensions of the gravitational constant are reduced to simplest form. Through analysis they are restored to a more useful form. Once restored, they give insight into why masses are drawn to each other. They support a gravity model where a fluid filling all of space and surrounding all mass is essentially consumed by mass, creating currents of the fluid that ultimately move the bodies consuming it toward each other. Overview William Stubbs, writing from Port St. Lucie in Florida, proposes that gravity is the drag of an invisible fluid. Bodies consume the vacuum; the consumption creates an inward flow; and objects caught in that flow are carried toward the consuming mass, as soap bubbles are carried toward a bath drain by water so clear that the water itself cannot be seen. The paper's method is dimensional analysis of the gravitational constant, and this is what makes it unusually checkable. In SI units G has dimensions m 3 kg −1 s −2 . Stubbs regroups these as (m 3 /s)/kg, reading the numerator as a volumetric flow rate and concluding that "the rate at which something flows is tied to the mass that a body possesses." He then expands further. Writing G in terms of newtons gives kg·m 3 /(kg 2 ·s 2 ), which he splits as [(m 3 /s)/kg]·[(kg/s)/kg]. The second bracket has no obvious meaning, so he multiplies its numerator by m 3 /m 3 — "multiply by 1" — and obtains a third factor with dimensions kg/m 3 . The result is G = a G 2 b G , with a G a vacuum consumption rate per kilogram and b G the mass produced per cubic metre of vacuum consumed. Two further steps complete the model.
