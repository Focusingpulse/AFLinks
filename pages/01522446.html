When a particle meets its antiparticle the pair can annihilate, converting their rest mass into radiation. The standard account Antimatter entered physics through Dirac's 1928 relativistic wave equation for the electron, whose solutions included negative-energy states. Dirac's initial interpretation was a filled "sea" of negative-energy states whose holes appeared as positive particles; by 1931 he had identified the hole as a new particle of electronic mass and opposite charge. Carl Anderson found it in cloud-chamber cosmic-ray tracks in 1932 and named it the positron.
