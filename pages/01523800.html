Robert W. Bass Born ( 1930-08-01 ) August 1, 1930 Residence Pahrump, NV, United States Nationality USA Known for New Energy , Cold Fusion Scientific career Fields Physicist , Inventor Dr Robert W. Bass (Bob Bass) (b. August 1930) is an American mathematician, principal inventor of the Topolotron, and a retired professor of physics and astronomy at Brigham Young University, where he taught from 1971 to 1981. He later ran the aerospace company Innoventech, Inc. of Pahrump, Nevada, and practised as a registered patent agent. Work Bass's published work falls into three groups, spread over half a century. The first is his professional field, the qualitative theory of nonlinear differential equations and control, beginning with his 1955 Johns Hopkins dissertation on the singularities of nonlinear systems. The second, in the 1970s and early 1980s, is a series of papers in Pensée , Kronos and the SIS Review on the celestial mechanics of Velikovsky's Worlds in Collision . Their recurring theme is that the classical "proofs" of the stability of the solar system prove less than they are taken to, and that the question of whether planetary orbits can change catastrophically is open rather than settled. The third, from the mid-1990s, concerns low-energy nuclear reactions and zero-point energy. It includes Plasma-Injected Transmutation (1996), Experimental Evidence Favoring Brightson's Nucleon Cluster Model (1997), The Physical Basis of Zero-Point Energy? (1998), Planck's Constant From Hubble's Constant: Cosmological Origin of Terrestrial ZPF & Zitterbewegung (1999) and Five Frozen Needles CF Protocol (2002). His patents include the Topolotron and the Plasmasphere, both issued, and a cold-fusion process filed as QRT.
