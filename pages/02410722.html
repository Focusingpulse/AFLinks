Over thousands of hypnotherapy sessions, she kept encountering the same strange detail; a huge number of people have nothing going on inside their own heads. No inner monologue. No “voice” talking them through decisions. No private debates in the mind. Just silence. When asked how they make choices, these people described simply doing the next thing in front of them, like a cursor moving down a checklist. There was no inner questioning, no “what if”, no self-reflection. It wasn’t laziness or lack of education, it was as if that part of their mind had never existed. Cannon realised something chilling. These weren’t rare cases. She believed a staggering percentage of the population could be like this, perhaps fifty, seventy, even eighty percent. If she was right, most of the people you see every day might not have a mind like yours at all. The signs are subtle. They never replay conversations in their head. They don’t plan beyond the most obvious next step. They copy opinions, tone and even facial expressions from the group around them. If you keep asking them “why”, you’ll often get a blank stare or sudden irritation; the mental equivalent of hitting a firewall. Gamers call them NPCs, Non-Player Characters, background figures that repeat the same lines and follow simple behavioural loops. Cannon’s “background people” are essentially the same thing in real life. And this is where my own research in Rise of the Clones: The Cabbage Patch Babies collides with hers.
