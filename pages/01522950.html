Frank Twiss Born 1968 (age 57–58) Residence Sammamish, WA, United States Nationality American Known for An electromagnetic account of matter and forces; critique of distant-universe observation Scientific career Fields Physics, Cosmology Frank Twiss (born 1968) is an American independent researcher in physics and cosmology, based in Sammamish, Washington. In the early 1990s he published two papers in the dissident journal Galilean Electrodynamics , in which he argued for an electromagnetic basis for matter and the fundamental forces and questioned the conventional interpretation of observations of distant astronomical objects. His conclusions are his own and are not part of mainstream physics. Research An electromagnetic theory of matter and forces In " Nonlinear Electromagnetic Field Theory " (1994) Twiss argued that the conservation of energy requires the electromagnetic field to be nonlinear, and that this nonlinearity causes the field to take on properties normally attributed to "matter." On this view, elementary particles may themselves be understood as electromagnetic field patterns, so that the forces acting on them — the gravitational force, the Lorentz force and the nuclear or strong force — are all ultimately electromagnetic in origin. The paper is an example of the "everything is electromagnetic" line of dissident unified-field thinking. A limit on how far back in time we can see In " Limitations on Viewing Distant Astronomical Objects " (1991) Twiss challenged the common assumption that in observing very distant objects we are seeing back in time almost to the origin of the universe. He noted that some distant galaxies appear more evolved — that is, older — than that assumption would predict, and argued that, taking the expanding-universe theory on its own terms, we cannot in fact see so far back in time; distant objects are therefore not being seen in their infancy.
