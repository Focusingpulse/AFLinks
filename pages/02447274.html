On the Boundary Problem in the Origins of Matter, Life, and Consciousness Sergey B. Yurchenko Brain and Consciousness Independent Research Center, Andijan, Uzbekistan s.yucko@gmail.com https://orcid.org/0000-0001-8582-003X Abstract Science has made great strides in answering many questions, but three major mysteries remain unsolved. Although these mysteries belong to different fields of science – physics, biology, and neuroscience – they all confront with the boundary problem. These are scientific investigations into the origins of matter, life, and consciousness, which extend beyond empirical and rational methods in the search for definitive answers. Here, this problem is presented as the search for the lowest boundaries for three forms of existence: physical, biological, and conscious. They are then translated into the context of Big Bang cosmology, abiogenesis, and the neuroscience of consciousness, each representing an ontological singularity with its own epistemological event horizon. Keywords ontological singularity; abiogenesis; life-mind continuity; consciousness 1 Introduction Three major mysteries of science and philosophy revolve around three fundamental forms of being: physical existence, biological existence, and conscious existence. Each form is believed to emerge from the previous one in this sequence. Accordingly, the questions that should be answered are all concerned with their origins. 1. How and why did the universe come into existence? 2. How and why did life arise from inanimate matter? 3. How and why did consciousness occur in living systems? These questions have been debated for centuries, from ancient philosophy to modern science. For all of these questions, an ontological problem arises that can be generalized as the “empty room argument”. Suppose we are wandering through a castle, searching for an empty room.
