Published 2010 Volume ROAJ Vol. 20 Number 2 No. of pages 10 Abstract GRT predicts the existence of relativistic corrections to the static Newtonian potential, which can be calculated and verified experimentally. The idea leading to quantum corrections at large distances consists of the interactions of massless particles, which only involve their coupling energies at low energies. Using the quantum correction term of the potential we obtain the perturbing quantum acceleration function. Next, with the help of the Newton-Euler planetary equations, we calculate the time rates of changes of the orbital elements per revolution for three different orbits around the primary. For one solar mass primary and an orbit with semimajor axis and eccentricity equal to that of Mercury we obtain that Delta-omega(qu) = 1.517*10?81 deg/cy, while DeltaM(qu) = ?1.840*10^?46 rev/cy. Overview Ioannis Iraklis Haranas of York University and Vasile Mioc of the Astronomical Institute of the Romanian Academy compute the effect of the quantum correction to the Newtonian gravitational potential on the elements of an orbit. The starting point is the effective-field-theory treatment of general relativity. Treating gravity as a quantum field theory valid at low energies yields calculable long-distance corrections to the static potential, arising from the exchange of massless particles whose contribution involves only their coupling energies at low momenta. The correction has two pieces: a classical relativistic term of order G ² M / c ² r and a genuinely quantum term of order G ħ/ c ³ r ², the latter carrying the Planck length squared.
