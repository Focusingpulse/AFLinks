Joe E. Champion Nationality American Known for Claims of low-energy nuclear transmutation of base materials into gold and other precious metals by chemical means; the "Philadelphia Project"; Twentieth Century Alchemy Scientific career Fields Chemistry, Cold Fusion , Low-energy nuclear transmutation Joe E. Champion is an American independent researcher and author best known for his claim that ordinary chemical reactions, carried out under the right thermal and electromagnetic conditions, can drive low-energy nuclear transmutation — the conversion of one element into another, and in particular the conversion of base materials such as mercury, copper and silver into gold and other precious metals. He worked outside the university system for most of his career, and his work belongs to the chemical- and biological-transmutation strand of the wider cold fusion / LENR and free-energy research community. He presented his results at the independent-inventor conferences run by Tesla Tech and its predecessor, the International Tesla Society. Biography Champion came to public attention shortly after the March 1989 cold fusion announcement by Martin Fleischmann and Stanley Pons, when he began building his own low-energy nuclear apparatus and circulating claims of anomalous nuclear effects. In September 1989 he contacted the electrochemist John O'M. Bockris, a distinguished professor of chemistry at Texas A&M University who was at that time among the most prominent academic investigators of cold fusion, and the two began a correspondence about Champion's process. In April 1992 that contact became a formal collaboration. A research team known as the Philadelphia Project , headed by Bockris and Champion, was set up at Texas A&M with a $200,000 donation to the university's development foundation from the California businessman William Telander. Over the following months the group ran the two procedures Champion advocated: bombardment of mineral samples with electromagnetic resonance
