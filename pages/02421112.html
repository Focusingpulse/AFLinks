Title How Peace Activists Can Be More Effective Author(s) Buryl Payne Keywords magnetic field, Paradigms, earth, Sun How Peace Activists Can Be More Effective is an article by Buryl Payne arguing that peace activism could be made more effective by taking account of what he describes as the cyclic, externally-triggered nature of war, and by the deliberate use of collective "thought power." The claims below are the author's own and are not accepted by the scientific mainstream. Peace activities are all too often reactive rather than proactive. Only after an international battle or war has occurred are attempts made to ameliorate situations by constructing treaties, agreements, or the curtailment of nuclear weapons, cluster bombs, poison gas, biological weapons, and so on. This is political symptom treatment. Historical analysis shows this approach is only partially successful: when wars start up again, peace treaties go out the window. Effective treatment of warring behavior may be aided by a more modern understanding of the nature of war. Section I. Wars come and go in cycles Warring behavior can be considered as a kind of mass psychosis — insane behavior. Individuals who behave that way are locked up and treated by psychiatrists. The most important finding of scientists, based on observations rather than theories, is that wars come and go in cycles. These cycles were discovered by the late Professor Raymond Wheeler, a psychologist and historian at the University of Kansas. As part of his larger historical analysis he found that international battles happen in approximately 11-year cycles, and have done so for 2,500 years. His work was later statistically validated by Edward Dewey, Director of the Foundation for the Study of Cycles, who considered the analysis of war cycles the most important work of his life.
