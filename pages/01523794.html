Robert G. Adams Born ( 1920-09-02 ) September 2, 1920 Died May 10, 2006 (2006-05-10) (aged 85) New Zealand Residence Whakatane, Bay of Plenty, New Zealand Nationality New Zealander Known for The Adams Motor , a switched-reluctance motor-generator claimed to run over unity Scientific career Fields Electrical engineering ; motor design; aether energy research Robert George Adams (2 September 1920 – 10 May 2006) was a New Zealand electrical engineer and inventor, known for the Adams Motor — a permanent-magnet switched-reluctance motor-generator which he claimed produced more energy than it consumed, drawing the excess from the aether . He worked on the machine for some forty years from his home at Whakatane in the Bay of Plenty, and in 1993 filed a British patent on it jointly with the English physicist Harold Aspden . The design was widely replicated by hobbyists in the 1990s through a manual sold by NEXUS magazine, and it remains among the better-documented over-unity claims of that period — both because Adams published enough for others to build it, and because their results are on record. Professional background Adams's conventional engineering career is well attested. He was: Founder of the New Zealand Section of the Institute of Electrical and Electronic Engineers (IEEE), 29 May 1968 Chairman of Region 10 of the New Zealand IEEE, 1969 and 1970 Fellow of the New Zealand Electronics Institute, elected 1969 a member of the Royal Society of New Zealand, the New Zealand Electrical Institute, the Auckland Institute and Museum, and the Scientific and Medical Network (UK) He also listed a Doctor of Science (honorary) and the Ancient Royal Assyrian Order of Merit.
