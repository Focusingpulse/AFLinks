TO THE EDITOR OF THE SUN — Sir: I have read with deep astonishment in THE SUN of yesterday a statement of Prof. Slaby of Berlin which I can scarcely believe to be accurate. Only two years ago he wrote me a letter generously acknowledging my pioneer work and calling me the "father of wireless telegraphy." I am at a loss to imagine what has occurred since then that Prof. Slaby, who has considered me a seer, should now look upon me as a fanatic. I am still more disappointed to notice that in his recent lecture, upon which you have commented and which I have just perused in a German technical journal, he does not give me credit for a beautiful invention which he calls the "multiplier," and which alone makes multiple and selective wireless telegraphy practicable. Have I not first described this device and given rules for its construction and attunement? Was I not the first to apply it in wireless transmission? What are the resonating tuned circuits with their nodal points and terminals of maximum pressure and their fixed lengths in my wireless system but "multipliers?" I will not reply to Prof. Slaby in a critical spirit, but will, on the contrary, gladly acknowledge the high quality of his labors.
