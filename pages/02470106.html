Schenectady Gazette July 13th, 1934 Reading like a page from one of the fiction books so popular half a century ago comes the announcement by Nikola Tesla, noted inventor, of the perfection of a defensive weapon capable of wiping out a fleet of airplanes, or an army of a million men, hundreds of miles away. According to the statement, only the curvature of the earth limits the distance at which the “death beam” is effective." If the claims made by the inventor for his invention are substantiated when given the thorough trial to which they must be submitted, the weapon will prove the most effective force for peace ever produced. Tesla’s contention is that this beam is purely defensive, and cannot be used for offensive purposes. The reason is simple. Its large size makes it immovable. Hence it must be placed on the borders or coast of the land to be defended. Its function will be to destroy attacking forces. A warship, according to the inventor, might be equipped with a miniature form of the weapon, enabling it to destroy attacking airplanes. But the vessel, approaching shore in order to make an attack, would in turn be wiped out by batteries guarding that territory. The suggestion appears on first thought to be fantastic.
