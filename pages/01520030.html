Experimental Difference Between Magnetic Attraction And Repulsion

· archived # 1,520,030
Title Experimental Difference Between Magnetic Attraction And Repulsion Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Jaroslav J Kopernicky Keywords repulsion, gravitational constant Published 2003 No. of pages 8 Abstract Gravitation's property of attraction only makes it appear different from electrical or magnetic force. The observations and reasoning in this paper attempt to find an explanation for the gravity as the difference between vectors of attractive and repulsive forces of the total energy "content" in favor of attraction. This difference is supposed to be related to the gravitational constant. Overview Experimental Difference Between Magnetic Attraction And Repulsion is an eight-page paper by Jaroslav J Kopernicky of Brampton, Ontario, dated 21 June 1999 and revised 18 February 2003, presented at the 7th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in June 2000. Its subtitle is "Leading to the notion of gravitation as an electromagnetic attractive force", and it opens with epigraphs from Eddington and Faraday. The paper reports a measurement. Two flat disc magnets about 6 inches in diameter and ¾ inch thick, magnetised in halves and sintered in hard rubber, were mounted on a non-ferromagnetic stainless steel shaft running on linear ball bearings, with forces and torques read off electronic digital scales. Attraction and repulsion were measured in 1 mm increments from 1 to 25 mm, and Kopernicky reports that attraction always exceeded repulsion at the same separation, the difference growing as the magnets approached: "an almost linear drop of repulsion with increasing distance and a slightly steeper initial drop of attraction". He notes that varying the shape or type of magnet made little difference, and expresses surprise that the effect is not already known: "This author is puzzled why nobody (to this author's knowledge) found it a long time before."
