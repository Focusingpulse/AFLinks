GC2026-SGG050 Deep neural network to classify anthropogenic seismic events in the Khibiny Massif Alexander Y. Motorin1 1 Kola Branch, Federal Research Center “Geophysical Survey of Russian Academy of Sciences”, Russia ayumotorin@gmail.com Long-term seismic monitoring of rockburst-prone sites in the Khibiny Massif has been conducted using automated rock mass seismicity control systems. In result, an extensive dataset of heterogeneous seismic records has been acquired at underground mines [1]. Seismic signals have been traditionally classified by experts at the Kola Branch of JSC Apatit based on integrated metadata on an event (time, location, and energy), as well as a detailed analysis of waveforms. The latter comprises the frequency content, P-to-S amplitude ratios, presence of infrasonic components, and other attributes [2]. However, a heavy volume of recorded signals up to several hundred per day combined with their partially overlapping features significantly constrains the efficiency and unambiguity of real￾time classification by experts. Recently, deep learning techniques have been increasingly applied to automatically process and classify seismic signals. These approaches have proved strongly efficient in analyzing complex waveforms and extensive datasets, including scenarios characterized by incomplete or inaccurate labeling [3-6]. The paper presents a deep learning model designed for the automated classification of seismic signals recorded in the underground mines of the Khibiny Massif.
