A Thermodynamic Cycle and Thermo-electric Converter utilising the Magneto-calorific Effect Remi Oseri Cornwall BEng MSc 152 Grierson Road Forest Hill London SE23 1NX r.o.cornwall@city.ac.uk ? Remi Cornwall 1999 Abstract This paper discusses a thermodynamic cycle of a ferromagnetic material in a magnetic field about the ferromagnetic/paramagnetic phase transition point to transform heat into electricity. The theory of the device's operation is presented and it is shown how the cycle can be performed many times per second giving good conversion rates. Practical issues of electrical circuitry, materials, material configuration and practical limitations are then discussed. The conclusion summarises the work done and presents areas for further study. Introduction This paper is laid out as follows: A brief preamble on the magneto-calorific effect, ferromagnetism and paramagnetism; a thermodynamic cycle utilising the magneto-calorific effect for transforming heat to electricity is presented, modelled and justified. Practical issues are then discussed, electrical circuitry, materials, material configuration and the like. The conclusion summarises the paper and presents further work. An appendix 1 (page 9) containing a proof that, dependent fluxes (a flux which is some function of the current) merely allow transformer action (double coil arrangement). This shows that the thermodynamic cycle discussed has an independent flux and isn't transformer action. An appendix 2 (page 11) further developing the idea of an independent flux in a single coil arrangement showing that the ideas discussed in this paper aren't merely 'inductor' action. A precise requirement for a dependent flux is shown (and hence merely inductor action) to which the thermodynamic cycle doesn't adhere and hence can generate power.
