GC2026-STP031 Spectrum of solar cosmic rays in the GLE77 event Yury V. Balabin1 , Aleksey V. Germanenko1 , Boris B. Gvozdevsky1 1 Polar Geophysical Institute of the Kola Scientific Center of the Russian Academy of Sciences balabin@pgia.ru A large solar cosmic ray (SCR) event occurred during the declining phase of Solar Cycle 25. SCRs are characterized by strong flux anisotropy and high intensity. Since 2012, such events (Ground Level Enhancements, GLEs) have had small amplitudes of about 3-5%. The new event, GLE77, was recorded by the global neutron monitor (NM) network on November 11, 2025. GLE77 is produced by a flare in active region 4274 with coordinates N23W24. In the optical range, the flare was classified as 3B. It was accompanied by X-ray emission with a maximum at 10:04 UT, classified as X5.1, and by a type II radio burst. The interplanetary conditions at the time of the flare and during the following hours were quiet; the Earth’s magnetosphere was also in a quiet state. However, approximately 12 hours later near the end of November 11, a new coronal mass ejection (CME) reached the Earth, producing an intense geomagnetic storm and a 10% Forbush decrease (according to Apatity NM data). This CME originated from a previous flare two days before GLE77. The earliest increase (based on 1-minute NM data) began sharply at 10:12 UT at the Mawson and Newark stations. In the next interval, 10:15–10:20 UT, a number of stations showed a sharp rise reaching up to 40%. The largest amplitude among all NMs was observed at Nain (~150%) and Mawson (~130%).
