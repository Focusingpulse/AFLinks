VIEW OF ELECTRICITY'S FUTURE Great Cheering for Edison When "the Wizard's" Name Is Mentioned by the Speaker. There was a noteworthy gathering of men prominent in the scientific world last night in Columbia University at a reception for Lord and Lady Kelvin. The large hall adjoining the gymnasium was decorated handsomely with Columbia's colors and was filled with men and women who had come to honor Lord Kelvin for what he had done in the interests of science. In the throng were Thomas A. Edison and Nikola Tesla. Dr. Francis Bacon Crocker, who presided, Introduced Dr. Nicholas Murray Butler, who said he was proud to have Columbia share with representatives of leading societies the honor of welcoming a guest who had been proved a master of science. “It is one of the eminent distinctions of our guest,” he said, “that he has made himself understood. Lord Kelvin has not only gone to the outskirts of the Unknown, but he has come back with tales of what he has done.” Professor Elihu Thomson of the American Institute of Electrical Engineers said that he regarded Lord Kelvin as the father of electrical engineering, and that all the great progress in this science for the last thirty-five years had its start in mathematical calculation.
