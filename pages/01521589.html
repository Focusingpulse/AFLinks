Title The Electrodynamic Origin of the Force of Inertia, Part 3 Read in full Link to paper (Internet Archive) Author(s) Charles William Lucas Keywords Inertia, centrifugal force , Mach's principle, Weber electrodynamics, universal force law, gyroscopic motion Published 2008 Journal Foundations of Science (Common Sense Science) Volume 11 Number 2 No. of pages 6 "The Electrodynamic Origin of the Force of Inertia, Part 3" is a 2008 paper by Charles William Lucas , the third in a series arguing that inertia is electrodynamic — that the resistance a body offers to acceleration, including the centrifugal force , is not a primitive fact about matter but the residual electric force between the vibrating charges inside it. It is an attempt to deliver what Mach wanted and, on Lucas's reading, neither Newton nor Einstein supplied: an account of inertia in purely relative coordinates, with no absolute space and no action at a distance. The paper appeared in Foundations of Science , the publication of the Common Sense Science group, and should not be confused with the mainstream philosophy journal of the same name. Abstract A review of Newton's Principia shows his dependence on his Existence Theorem for absolute space and time in order to explain the force of inertia and the centrifugal force in terms of absolute coordinates. A review of the history of Einstein's General Theory of Relativity reveals his failure to establish the basis of inertia and the centrifugal force in terms of relative coordinates as Mach had envisioned. In this work the force of inertia, including the centrifugal force, is derived from the universal electrodynamics force law based on relative coordinates. From the electrodynamics perspective the inertial force is an average residual force between vibrating neutral electric dipoles consisting of atomic electrons vibrating with respect to protons in the nucleus of atoms.
