logarithm

· archived # 1,481,359
In mathematics , the logarithm is the inverse operation to exponentiation . That means the logarithm of a number is the exponent to which another fixed number, the base, must be raised to produce that number. In simple cases the logarithm counts factors in multiplication . For example, the base 10 logarithm of 1000 is 3, as 10 to the power 3 is 1000 (1000 = 10?×?10?×?10 = 103); 10 is used as a factor three times. More generally, exponentiation allows any positive real number to be raised to any real power, always producing a positive result, so the logarithm can be calculated for any two positive real numbers b and x where b is not equal to 1.
