Saturday Morning Science Chat sponsored by the Chappell Natural Philosophy Society Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Ian Cohen, James Keene , Stoyan Sarg , Cornelis Verhey , Bob and Jim, with comments from Harry Ricker , Curt Renshaw and Alistair Riddoch on the stream. The topic had been running all week on the email list: the standard textbook picture of an electromagnetic wave — electric and magnetic vectors at right angles, in phase, propagating through empty space — and whether anything of the sort actually happens. Hu put the diagram on screen and asked the room. The objections to the picture Bob had two. The first is energy: square the two fields and add them, and the total goes to zero at the nodes and back to a maximum, which he cannot reconcile with conservation. The second came from his reading of Jefimenko , who argues that Maxwell's equations are not causal. Once retardation is put in properly, a changing electric field does not cause a changing magnetic field, nor the reverse; the charges are the source of both, and the two merely propagate together, which is why they look like cause and effect. He added that the picture omits the longitudinal Coulomb component that an oscillating source charge must produce. Hu pressed the same point geometrically. If there is a source, the field should fall off as the inverse square, which the picture does not show; and the only way to get vectors pointing uniformly perpendicular, rather than radially from a point, would be a flat charged plate cycling between plus and minus five volts, with a rectangular magnet reversing alongside it.
