Title A Solution for the "Dark Matter Mystery" Based on Euclidean Relativity: long version Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Frederic Lassiaille Keywords gravity, relativity , Newton , matter Published 2009 No. of pages 36 Abstract The study of this article suggests an explanation for the "dark matter mystery". This explanation is based on a modification of Newton's law. This modification is conducted from an Euclidean vision of relativity. Concerning the mystery of the velocity of the stars inside a galaxy, the study calculates a theoretical curve which is different from the one coming from Newton's law. This theoretical curve is very close to the measured one. Concerning the mystery of the velocity of a galaxy inside its group, the explanation is more direct. For this mystery, the study calculates a greater value for G, the gravitational constant. Overview This is the extended, 36-page version of Frédéric Lassiaille's argument that the phenomena attributed to Dark Matter are artefacts of an incomplete gravitational law rather than evidence of unseen mass. Lassiaille builds a Euclidean rather than Minkowskian picture of space-time: four dimensions ( x , y , z , ct ) carrying an ordinary Euclidean metric locally, with the invariance of the space-time interval deliberately abandoned as a global principle. Everything else he keeps — isotropy of space, reciprocity between inertial frames, constancy of c in every frame, and the general-relativistic idea that energy deforms space-time. From that base he derives a correction to Newton's law in which the gravitational force at a point depends not only on the attracting mass but on the surrounding matter distribution. Inside a galaxy, where the density of neighbouring stars is high, the correction is large; in empty intergalactic space it is negligible, and the ordinary inverse-square law is recovered.
