Garuda

· archived # 1,459,585
In Theosophy, Garuda is interpreted not merely as a mythological figure, but as a profound symbol representing the highest spiritual principles, the "Wings of Wisdom," and the vehicle of the divine consciousness (Vishnu). Madame Blavatsky interprets Garuda within the context of esoteric knowledge, linking the creature to the "thread of Wisdom" that unites the lower self with the Higher Self. "Garuda is represented with the head, wings, talons, and beak of an Eagle, and the body and limbs of a Man. His face is white, his wings golden, and his body red." ― Helena Petrovna Blavatsky - Collected Writings Garuda (Sanskrit: गरुड) is a multifaceted figure in various traditions, primarily represents a mythical bird, most notably the vehicle of Vishnu. In Hinduism, Garuda symbolizes speed, strength, and divine intervention, appearing in numerous roles from a mount to a symbol of power and protection. The name Garuda has deep roots in Hindu mythology, where it represents a divine Eagle-like being, often depicted as the mount (Vahana) of the god Vishnu. In Sanskrit, "Garuda" translates to "Eagle" or "one who carries a heavy burden," reflecting his role as Vishnu's carrier and his strength. He is revered as a powerful protector and a symbol of courage and swiftness, embodying the virtues of devotion and fearlessness. The name also appears in Buddhist traditions, maintaining similar symbolic associations. In Vaishnavism, Shri Garuda is a divine figure recognized as the mount of Lord Vishn
