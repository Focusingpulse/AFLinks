OceanEmbed: A U-Net Framework for Subsurface Ocean Temperature Reconstruction in the North Indian Ocean

· archived # 2,443,842
OceanEmbed: A U-Net Framework for Subsurface Ocean Temperature Reconstruction in the North Indian Ocean Team Intell Alpha Chaitanya Bharathi Institute of Technology Hyderabad, India Abstract—Reconstructing 3D subsurface ocean temperature profiles from surface satellite observations is a fundamental underdetermined inverse problem in physical oceanography. We present OceanEmbed, a U-Net-based deep learning framework that reconstructs daily temperature fields across 15 discrete depths (0.49 m to 902.34 m) on a 0.25◦ grid over the North Indian Ocean (5–30◦ N, 45–105◦ E). The core methodological contribution is an anomaly formulation that predicts temperature deviations relative to a day-of-year climatology rather than absolute temperature, yielding a ∼10× reduction in test RMSE (from 5.08◦ C to 0.525◦ C). Through controlled ablation experiments, sea level anomaly (SLA) is identified as the dominant predictor, accounting for 87.5% of the total performance improvement across all additional input variables. Evaluated against a 2022 GLORYS reanalysis hold-out set, OceanEmbed achieves an overall RMSE of 0.5246◦ C and R2 of 0.9980. Independent validation against 8,883 in-situ ARGO float profiles from 2020–2022 confirms consistent performance across seasons and sub-basins, yielding an overall ARGO RMSE of 1.5719◦ C. We additionally document negative results regarding auxiliary physical loss terms and architecture configurations. Index Terms—Ocean temperature reconstruction, U-Net, Satellite remote sensing, Sea level anomaly, North Indian Ocean, ARGO validation I. I NTRODUCTION Satellites observe only the upper boundary of the global ocean—roughly the top 10 µm for infrared radiometers and the upper 5 cm for altimeters.
