GC2026-SGG044 Experimental study of nanoparticle transport in sand-porous media Anton P. Petrovykh1 , Alexey G. Parshakov1 , Evgenii Kozhevnikov1 1 Perm National Research Polytechnic University anton.petrovykh@yandex.ru Particle migration processes in porous media have a significant impact on subsurface hydrology and the ecological condition of aquifers. The relevance of this research is driven by the widespread adoption of nanotechnologies in the oil industry, particularly the method of nanoflooding, where nanoparticles are used to enhance oil recovery by altering rock wettability and mobilizing residual oil. Simultaneously, nanoparticles are actively used in industry – in the production of catalysts, photocatalytic coatings, sensors, and sunscreen materials – which leads to industrial wastewater containing nanoparticles entering soils and aquifers. The key problem is that as various particles penetrate the structure of a porous medium, gradual pore clogging occurs, leading to colmatation and a reduction in the filtration characteristics of the formations. This paper presents an experimental study of the filtration of unstabilized aqueous nanoparticle solutions through sandy porous media. The aim of the study was to investigate the mechanisms of nanoparticle retention and assess their impact on the filtration characteristics of aquifers. Experiments were conducted on two sand packs with different initial permeabilities during the injection of solutions at a constant concentration.
