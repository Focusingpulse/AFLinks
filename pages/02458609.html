ABSTRACT We are led to believe that from the beginning of the enlightenment in Europe in the 17th and 18th centuries CE, science has moved forward with an abandonment of all forms of irrational prejudice. While we are aware that socio-cultural factors control large areas of science, particularly with regard to the allocation of public funds, we often forget to as￾sess the consequent societal damage. This is true particularly in relation to the biggest questions of science such as the origin of life and the origin of the Universe. In the inter￾ests of science, it is important to recognize the role of such influences in the assessment of competing theories, particularly those relating to the origin of life.
