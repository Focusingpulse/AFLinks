This returning electric current enters the generator just as if it were entering a motor. In an electric motor, current enters at a higher voltage than the exiting current. This causes the motor's rotation to accelerate, or to push forward. In generators however, returning current enters at a lower voltage than exiting current. This produces not a push, but a pull: the generator's rotation decelerates. By pushing like a motor in reverse, the generator paradoxically acts as a brake to its own forward motion. The more current the generator produces, the stronger this braking action or "pull backwards" becomes.
