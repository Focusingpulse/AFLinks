Scheduling of Petroleum Refinery Operations Manne

· archived # 2,406,725
ACTA 39003004692959 Second Printing In the belief that the economist and the petroleum industry planner can effectively help each other, this book uses mathematical economics to approach representative problems in oil refinery scheduling. In concise terms, Alan S. Manne reviews conventional refinery cost accounting methods, and then presents four case studies. They cover the allocation of individual crude oils to several refineries at different locations; next, naphtha reforming; then, gasoline blending; and last, scheduling for a small integrated plant that performs cracking, recycling, and blending. For this full-scale refinery problem, not only a running plan is considered, but also a case involving the construction of new capacity. As a result of the individual calculations, it is possible to obtain a better understanding of how a refiner might respond to changes in several kinds of variables: the market prices of refined products, the quali- ty specifications of these products, and the amount of capital available for investment in new equipment. With patience and with (Continued from front flap) some additional laboratory work, it is prac- ticable to analyze the manufacturing process activities within any required degree of detail. To keep expository material to a minimum, it is assumed that the reader is capa- ble of solving a set of simultaneous linear equations and also the calculus problem of maximization under constraints. Inasmuch as the book centers upon the economic implications of mathematical models rather than upon the mechanics of computation, Mr. Manne has not dealt extensively with the simplex method of linear programming. However, the book contains a description of parametric linear programming, along with several illustrations of its use. A glossary of technical terms, tables, figures, and diagrams complement the text.
