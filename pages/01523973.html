Twentieth century Physic's equivalent of Fantasia has been the Neutrino. During the last 67 years (1998-1931) some of the most fantastic explanations and experiments were invented involving Neutrinos: Pauli's creation to save SR's inability to explain energy and momentum conservation in the historic RaE experiments. Overview Ricardo Carezani, the originator of Autodynamics, argues here that the neutrino does not exist and that Super-Kamiokande's own published data show it. The paper was written in 1998, discussing the Super-Kamiokande solar neutrino measurement then newly posted to the arXiv, and its framing is polemical throughout: the neutrino is "Twentieth century Physic's equivalent of Fantasia", proposed by Pauli "to save SR's inability to explain energy and momentum conservation" in beta decay. The technical case has two parts. First, a list of what Carezani calls failures in the Super-Kamiokande results: the detected solar flux is only about 38 per cent of the standard solar model prediction; the night-time rate exceeds the day-time rate by 2 per cent; and the annual variation is much larger than the 7 per cent expected from the Earth's orbital eccentricity. Second, and this is the paper's centrepiece, a re-analysis of the collaboration's angular distribution. Super-Kamiokande plots recoil-electron events against the cosine of the angle to the Sun and reports "a clear peak from the solar neutrinos". Carezani re-bins the same data into equal intervals of angle and finds the peak gone: "there are more events at any other interval than at 10°. This is especially true around 90°."
