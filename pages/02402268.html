MolPhot2026-main026 Investigation of mixture of thioacetamide and tributyl phosphate as a safe precursor for lead sulfide quantum dots synthesis Dmitry V. Dyomkin1 , Anton A. Mikhailov1 , Vladimir F. Razumov1,2 1 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (State University) 2 Federal Research Center for Problems of Chemical Physics and Medical Chemistry RAS dyomkin.dv@phystech.edu Colloidal quantum dots (CQDs) are currently used in a variety of applications, including solar panels, light￾emitting diodes, medical biomarkers, photodetectors [1], and more. Lead sulfide is a narrow-bandgap semiconductor with a bandgap of 0.41 eV, making it attractive for creating IR photodetectors. Its high oxidation stability makes it a convenient material for developing conductive thin-film structures. PbS CQDs are most often produced using high-temperature colloidal synthesis, where a separately prepared sulfur precursor is injected into a reactor containing a hot lead precursor. The lead consists of either lead chloride in oleylamine, or lead oleate in a mixture of oleic acid and octadecene. A solution of bis(trimethylsilyl) sulfide (TMS) in octadecene is most commonly used as a sulfur precursor [2]. This reagent is one of the most used due to the high quality of the resulting CQDs, which is reflected in a narrow exciton absorption band, as well as the excellent performance of photosensitive devices.
