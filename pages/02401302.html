kimo2026-main114 Digital Silk Road in South Asia: Pakistan's Technology Choices amid US-China Tech Competition Mubashra Shaheen mubashragondal@gmail.com The Digital Silk Road (DSR), China's ambitious strategy to extend its technological influence alongside physical Belt and Road Initiative infrastructure, has emerged as a critical battleground in Sino-American technological competition. This paper examines Pakistan's navigation of competing technology frameworks offered by China and the United States, with particular focus on telecommunications infrastructure, digital payment systems, e￾governance platforms, and surveillance technologies. As a frontline state in the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) and a long-time US security partner, Pakistan faces distinctive pressures in its digital technology choices. The analysis reveals that Pakistan has pursued a strategy of selective technological hedging, predominantly embracing Chinese digital infrastructure while maintaining limited American technological engagement in specific domains. This pattern reflects Pakistan's economic constraints, deepening strategic alignment with China, deteriorating relations with the United States, and domestic governance priorities that favor surveillance and control capabilities. The paper argues that Pakistan's digital technology choices carry profound implications for its economic development trajectory, democratic governance prospects, data sovereignty, and strategic autonomy.
