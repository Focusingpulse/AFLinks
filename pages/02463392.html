Existing geomagnetic observatory networks are used as a preliminary proxy for distributed RMFD stations in order to characterize magnetic transients, estimate environmental background structure, and evaluate coincidencediscrimination methodology. Second-resolution geomagnetic data from multiple INTERMAGNET observatories are analyzed for a quiet reference interval and for the major geomagnetic storm interval of 2024 May 10. The effective RMFD environmental proxy is constructed from the temporal magnetic variation, dBtot , dt which represents the leading environmental quantity entering the pickup-coil voltage chain. During the quiet reference day, measured RMS temporal magnetic variations occupy the approximate range 10−11 –10−10 T/s, whereas storm-time backgrounds increase to approximately 10−10 –10−9 T/s. Station-dependent variation is observed, with high-latitude observatories showing stronger magnetospheric coupling. Power spectral density analysis further identifies a broad storm-time enhancement structure in the approximate frequency range 10−2 –10−1 Hz, providing a preliminary detector-design constraint for future RMFD filtering and coincidence strategies.
