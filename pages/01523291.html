Ken Shoulders Born 7 March 1927 Died June 7, 2013 (2013-06-07) (aged 86) Residence Ukiah, CA, United States Known for Electron beam lithography; field-emission cathodes; charge clusters (EV / EVO) Scientific career Fields Microelectronics, vacuum nanoelectronics, energy research Institutions Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Stanford Research Institute Kenneth Radford Shoulders (7 March 1927 – 7 June 2013) was an American experimental physicist and inventor with two distinct careers. In the first he was a pioneer of microelectronics : he established the microelectronics programme at Stanford Research Institute, is credited as an early developer of electron beam lithography , and has been called the "father of vacuum microelectronics" for his work on field-emission devices. In the second, pursued independently from the 1980s onward, he investigated what he called charge clusters — compact, highly organised bundles of electrons which he named Electrum Validum (EV) and later Exotic Vacuum Objects (EVO), and which he held to be a potential source of anomalous energy. It is the second body of work that brought him into the cold fusion and new-energy community documented on this wiki. The first is the reason his papers are held as an archival collection by the Science History Institute. Biography Shoulders entered the electronics industry directly, working for a succession of firms — among them Magnavox, Texas Instruments and Collins Radio — from the middle 1940s to the middle 1950s. From about 1955 he was a research staff member at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology , working on microminiature data-processing components and, with Dudley Allen Buck , on thin-film cryotron integrated circuits. In 1958 he moved to California as a senior research engineer in the Applied Physics Laboratory that Charles Rosen had created at the Stanford Research Institute , where he established SRI's microelectronics programme.
