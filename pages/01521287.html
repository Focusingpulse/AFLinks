Title Secondary Field Theory and Photons Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Richard Oldani Keywords photons , magnetic field Published 2008 No. of pages 4 Abstract The field diagram for an electromagnetic wave shows that electric and magnetic field vectors violate Gauss laws. This is corrected and wave motion is explained by introducing a dipole-field geometry for the photon. The dual wave-particle nature of photons is also accounted for by means of classical fields and vector addition. Overview Secondary Field Theory and Photons is a four-page paper by Richard Oldani , presented at the 15th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in April 2008. Its starting point is a complaint about a picture. The standard textbook diagram of an electromagnetic wave shows E and B as arrows of varying length standing on the propagation axis, and Oldani observes that these arrows are open-ended: they begin nowhere and terminate nowhere. Since Gauss's laws require magnetic field lines to close (∇·B = 0) and electric field lines to begin and end on charges (∇·E = ρ/ε 0 ), he concludes that "in figure 1 electric and magnetic fields are shown as open-ended vectors which do not conform to either equation" — that the standard wave picture violates two of Maxwell's four equations. He raises a second objection to the Ampère–Maxwell law. Writing it as ∇×B = μ 0 J + μ 0 ε 0 ∂E/∂t, he identifies J as the displacement current, calls it "an imaginary quantity that is proportional to the change of an electric field" which "does not exist as a real current", and argues that its presence makes the two curl equations asymmetric. His replacement is a photon conceived as "a magnetic dipole in rectilinear motion whose axis is aligned with its path", carrying a constant B field of closed loops extending to infinity.
