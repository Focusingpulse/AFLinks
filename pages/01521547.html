Overview The Death of Electric Current is a three-page article by Ivor Catt , subtitled "A contribution to electromagnetic theory," published in Wireless World in December 1982, pages 79–81. Its programme is stated as a succession of theories. "A major advance in electromagnetic theory, which I shall call the transition from Theory N to Theory H, was made by Oliver Heaviside a century ago. What is proposed here is a transition from Theory H to a third theory, Theory C." Catt opens with a long quotation from Heaviside, which he reproduces accurately and which is worth having: "By the way, is there such a thing as an electric current? Not that it is intended to cast any doubt upon the existence of a phenomenon so called; but is it a current — that is, something moving through a wire?" Heaviside goes on to describe a telegraph line from London to Edinburgh in which "the energy is poured out of the battery sideways into the dielectric at a steady rate," travels through the dielectric rather than the wire, and enters the instrument at the far end having "travelled wholly through the dielectric." Under Theory H, Catt writes, "the field E × H, travelling along between the wires at the speed of light, what Heaviside called the 'energy current', is the cause, then electric charge and electric current are merely what define the edge" of that wave.
