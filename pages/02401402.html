GC2026-STP013 Determination of lightning discharge height by radio interferometric method Evgeny I. Malkin1 , Evgeny A. Kazakov1 , Boris M. Shevtsov1 , Nina V. Cherneva1 1 Institute of Cosmophysical Research and Radio Wave Propagation FEB RAS Kazakov@ikir.ru The trigger for high-altitude discharges observed in the mesosphere above thunderclouds at altitudes of 50 to 90 km is positive cloud-to-ground lightning, which excites VLF/ELF atmospheric discharges [1,2]. The existing connection between high-altitude discharges and paired discharges indicates that the second VLF/ELF pulse in a pair is generated by electric currents in the sprite itself [2,3]. To verify this assertion, a comparative analysis of 27,511 paired discharges over a seven-year period (up to and including 2021) was conducted using data from WWLLN and the IKIR FEB RAS VLF direction finder. In the work [4], the height of an electric discharge was determined using a VLF radio direction finder using dual-beam interferometry. This paper presents a method for determining the height of a lightning discharge using multi-beam interferometry, which involves comparing the simulated and actual power spectra of a convolution signal. The height of the second discharge in a pair of positive discharges coincides with the region of the stratopause, where the temperature has a local maximum, facilitating the formation of a high-altitude electrical discharge.
