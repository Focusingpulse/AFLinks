GC2026-STP015 On the transport of high-energy protons in the solar atmosphere and the associated gamma-ray emission Alexey V. Kuzmitskiy1 , Alexey A. Kochanov1 1 Institute of Solar-Terrestrial Physics, Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Irkutsk, Russia a.kuzmitskiy@iszf.irk.ru Solar flare activity is responsible for many phenomena that can cause space weather disturbances. These events lead to disruptions in the operation of various ground-based and space-based systems and pose a serious threat to human activities and health. Among them are solar energetic particles (SEPs), especially protons, accelerated to energies ranging from fractions of MeV to tens or hundreds of MeV, and reaching several GeV during ground-level enhancements (GLEs) of cosmic ray intensity. Unlike electrons, which manifest across the entire electromagnetic spectrum, protons can only be remotely detected through gamma-ray emission generated in nuclear reactions involving them and identifiable by its characteristic spectral shape. This requires reliable numerical modeling of proton spectra and their evolution in the solar atmosphere. In this work, we solve the non-stationary proton transport equation, accounting for energy losses due to ionization and inelastic nuclear reactions, along with the processes generating neutral pions, which are the primary sources of gamma-ray emission. Solutions obtained for the thick-target regime were compared to classical calculations of gamma-ray emission spectra and modern Monte Carlo calculations.
