Harold W. Milnes Born ( 1925-06-09 ) June 9, 1925 Died December 21, 2003 (2003-12-21) (aged 78) Residence Lubbock, TX, United States Nationality USA Known for Superluminal propagation of electrical signals on fine wires; Galilean-invariant electrodynamics; criticism of relativity; founding and editing The Toth-Maatian Review Scientific career Fields Mathematics, Electromagnetism , Relativity Institutions Texas Tech University; editor and publisher of The Toth-Maatian Review Harold Willis Milnes (1925-2003) was an American mathematician, experimenter and publisher who became one of the most persistent critics of Special Relativity and of the received interpretation of Maxwell's equations. Trained as a numerical analyst and later a professor of mathematics at Texas Tech University, he argued from both theory and bench experiment that electrical signals can propagate along very fine wires at velocities exceeding c , and — with Thomas E Phipps — that electromagnetism is better expressed in a first-order Galilean-invariant (Hertzian) form than in the Lorentz-covariant one. He is equally remembered as the founder, editor and publisher of The Toth-Maatian Review (1981-1994), an unrefereed journal that gave dissident scientists worldwide a place to publish. Education M.A. in mathematics — Wayne State University, 1952 Ph.D. in mathematics — Wayne State University, 1955 Biography Harold Willis Milnes was born and grew up in Detroit, where he was educated at home by his parents and studied operatic singing as a young man. He then worked for various institutions including General Motors, Boeing, and NASA, and eventually became a professor of mathematics at Texas Tech University in Lubbock, Texas. In his professional mathematical career he published on the numerical solution of partial differential equations, notably the "boundary contraction" method developed with R. B.
