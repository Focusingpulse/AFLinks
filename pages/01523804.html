The Rodin coil is a toroidal winding pattern devised by Marko Rodin , in which the conductor is wound around a torus in a repeating star-like sequence rather than in the usual evenly spaced circular loops. It is one of the most widely replicated devices in the amateur new energy community, and a substantial body of experimental reports on it is catalogued on this wiki. The winding A conventional toroidal coil is wound so that successive turns lie side by side around the ring. In the Rodin pattern the winding instead skips a fixed number of positions on each pass, so that the conductor traces a closed star polygon over the surface of the torus and returns to its starting point only after several circuits. The result is that the current paths cross one another at intervals and the turns are not parallel, which is the feature its advocates regard as essential. Rodin derived the sequence from what he calls vortex-based mathematics , a scheme built on the repeating digit-sum patterns that appear when the numbers 1–9 are doubled repeatedly (1, 2, 4, 8, 7, 5 and back to 1, with 3, 6 and 9 standing apart). He describes the winding as following "the longest free-flowing path of the electron", and identifies the 3–6–9 triad as governing a flux perpendicular to the coil surface. Claims Rodin and those following him have attributed a wide range of properties to the coil, including: an unusually strong and well-collimated magnetic field along the torus axis, described as a toroidal pinch ; applications to fusion confinement, brushless motors, high-sensitivity antennas and propulsion; in the stronger versions of the claim, gravitational effects and energy gain.
