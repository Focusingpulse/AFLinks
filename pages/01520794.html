Nuclear Alternative: Redesigning Our Model of the Structure of Matter

Nuclear Alternative: Redesigning Our Model of the Structure of Matter Book Author William L. Stubbs Published 2008 Publisher Xlibris, Philadelphia Pages 146 ISBN 1436329272 Nuclear Alternative: Redesigning Our Model of the Structure of Matter is a 2008 book by the nuclear engineer William L. Stubbs . It is the full statement of his alpha-beta model of the atomic nucleus: the proposal that nucleons are built not from up and down quarks but from beta particles — electrons and positrons — and that nuclei hold together not by a strong force but by nucleons sharing those particles, in the way atoms share electrons in a covalent bond. From that starting point the book builds models of more than 250 stable nuclei out of a small set of repeating modules, tabulates their masses against measurement in an appendix, and then uses the same models to give accounts of alpha decay, beta decay, fission and fusion that dispense with both the strong and the weak nuclear force. Stubbs had reached the central idea in 1993 while working as a reactor engineer; the book was written after he retired, and he presented its argument to the Natural Philosophy Alliance in 2010 as " The Alpha-Beta Model of the Atomic Nucleus ". The publisher's description states its aim: The Standard Model has been the model of choice for nuclear structure since the late 1960s. However, 40 years later, scientists are still struggling to validate it. Nuclear Alternative offers an alternative explanation for the structure of matter that uses particles and forces observed and characterized since the 1930s. By redesigning the models of the proton, neutron, and electron, and developing a nuclear binding mechanism similar to covalent bonds in atoms, the book systematically develops models for more than 250 stable nuclei. It then uses the models to explain alpha and beta decay, and nuclear fission and nuclear fusion. — Publisher's description, Nuclear Alternative (2008) The argument The case against the standard picture
