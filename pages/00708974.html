Make Two 3D Vectors Parallel by Rotating Them Around Separate Axes August 13, 2022 James Smith Abstract To help fill the need for examples of introductory-level problems that have been solved via Geometric Algebra (GA), we show how to calculate the angle through which two unit vectors must be rotated in order to be parallel to each other. Among the ideas that we use are a transformation of the usual GA formula for rotations, and the use of GA products to eliminated variables in simultaneous equations. We will show the benefits of (1) examining an interactive GeoGebra construction before attempting a solution, and (2) considering a range of implications of given information. 1 1 Introduction As an example of the sort of problems that can be solved by rotating vectors until they are parallel to each other, consider the three spheres in Fig. 1: the lines from the spheres’ centers to the respective points of tangency are parallel to each other, because all three lines are perpendicular to the same plane.
