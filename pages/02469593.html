Anderson Minnesota Technolog January, 1953 Page number(s): 18-19, 46 A friendly, industrious lad is Leland Anderson, a double "E" student with great ability for sleuthing out little known facts, be they concerned with man or machine. Leland wants to make like Sherlock Holmes in the "EE" field, and if he doesn't change his mind and go on to grad school he will go to work as a research scientist when he graduates in the fall of '53 or Jan. '54. Music is a sideline for Leland but electronics is his main interest and he is a member of the AIEE and the IRE. Ten years ago, on the 7th of January, 1943, Dr. Nikola Tesla, died in New York City. He was perhaps one of the most controversial figures in the fields of science. To the younger generation of engineers, and the general public as well, Tesla is only a vague name, but his brilliant achievements have earned an undying tribute to his memory. Tesla arrived in America the summer of 1884, a recent graduate of engineering schools in Prague, Budapest and the Polytechnic Institute of Austria. He had heard of the then well-known Thomas A. Edison, and as he hoped, obtained a position as an aide to Mr. Edison in the designing of motors and generators.
