Ralph Sansbury Residence Larchmont, NY, United States Nationality USA Known for Electromagnetism , Electric Dipoles Inside Electrons and Atomic Nuclei Scientific career Fields Physicist Ralph Sansbury is an American physicist and independent researcher based in Larchmont, New York, whose work centres on a single hypothesis: that electric dipoles induced inside atomic nuclei and inside electrons underlie phenomena conventionally assigned to magnetism, gravitation and the finite speed of light. After graduating from the University of Chicago he taught at the University of Illinois and at the Polytechnic Institute of Brooklyn, and worked as a researcher at MIT's magnet laboratory. He has published in the Review of Scientific Instruments , the Journal of Classical Physics and the Optics Testing Digest , and holds patents relating to the applied side of his work, in nuclear fusion and ion separation. Education University of Chicago Research Nuclear electric dipoles Sansbury's central proposal is that an atomic nucleus is not an inert charge but contains a small orbital component — he estimates its mass at around 10 −55 kg — which can be displaced by an applied electric force to leave the nucleus polarised. Because ordinary matter's outer electrons screen static fields, he argues that polarisation survives only where the applied force is changing, which he takes to be the general case in rotating and orbiting bodies. His experimental contribution is a 1985 paper in the Review of Scientific Instruments reporting a force between a charged metal foil and a current-carrying conductor — an effect not predicted by standard electrodynamics, since a neutral current-carrying wire should exert no net electrostatic force. Claims of this general kind have a small literature; the best known, by Edwards and collaborators in 1976, was not confirmed by later searches.
