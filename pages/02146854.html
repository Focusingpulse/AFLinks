Prof. Joe Cummins and Dr. Mae-Wan Ho Two years ago we reported on recombinant vaccines against the human papilloma virus (HPV) infection and cervical cancer [1] ( Recombinant Cervical Cancer Vaccines , SiS 29). Clinical trials had been completed on two vaccine formulations, and these are being commercially released worldwide in government sponsored vaccination programmes that target women and girls (and even boys) as young as 9 years of age in a bid to prevent cervical and anogenital cancers [2]. This has aroused a great deal of controversy, which calls for a fuller discussion. Human papillomavirus According to the US government’s National Cancer Institute [3], human papillomaviruses (HPVs) are a group of more than 100 viruses. Certain types cause warts or papillomas that are benign. The HPVs that cause the common warts on hands and feet are different from those that cause growth in the throat or genetial area. Some types are associated with cancer, and are called “high risk” HPVs. Of the more than 100 types of HPVs, over 30 can be passed through sexual contact. Most HPV infections occur without any symptoms and go away without treatment over the course of a few years.
