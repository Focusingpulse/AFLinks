Ari Lehto Born ( 1942-09-16 ) September 16, 1942 (age 83) Residence Helsinki, Finland Nationality Finnish Known for Deriving the properties of matter from the Planck scale by period doubling Scientific career Fields Physicist ; nonlinear dynamics; materials science Institutions Helsinki University of Technology, Laboratory of Materials Science Ari Lehto (born 16 September 1942) is a Finnish physicist who has argued since the 1980s that the measured properties of matter can be derived from the Planck scale by period doubling — the universal route to chaos in nonlinear dynamical systems described by Mitchell Feigenbaum. His work is unusual in this collection for two reasons. It is quantitative and falsifiable in a direct way: it predicts specific numbers — particle rest energies, the elementary charge, planetary orbital radii — which either match measurement or do not. And a substantial part of it has appeared in ordinary peer-reviewed journals , including Astrophysics and Space Science in 1996 and Nonlinear Dynamics in 2008, rather than only in dissident venues. He worked at the Laboratory of Materials Science of the Helsinki University of Technology, now part of Aalto University. The idea The Planck scale is built from the natural constants h , c , G and ε 0 alone, which is what makes it attractive as a starting point: it is, in Lehto's words, "absolute and unadjustable". The obstacle is one of magnitude. The Planck energy is about 10 22 MeV against 0.511 MeV for the electron and 938 MeV for the proton, and the Planck length is 10 −35 m — too far from anything measurable for a direct connection to be obvious. Lehto's proposal is that the connection is made by a process that generates subharmonics rather than harmonics , stepping the Planck quantities down rather than up. Period doubling does exactly that: in a nonlinear dynamical system the period of oscillation successively doubles, which by E = h /τ means the energy successively halves , and correspondingly l = c
