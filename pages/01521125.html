Quantum Myths Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Roger J Anderton Date 21 February 2026 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Quantum Myths was a live session presented by Roger J Anderton on 21 February 2026 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The process that started creating myths in Relativity got extended to making myths in Quantum physics. Hosted by Roger Anderton. Summary A continuation of the previous fortnight's talk, carrying the argument from relativity into quantum theory. Anderton's thesis is that the methodological shift Einstein introduced in 1905 made the interpretive moves of quantum mechanics easier to take — and that both fields have since accumulated a layer of claims that go beyond what the experiments establish. What he means by a myth He opened by tightening the definition, which he felt had not been clear before. A myth is not a failed experiment, a wrong equation or a false prediction. It is metaphysical overreach: a methodological choice treated as an ontological conclusion. On this account the experimental results and the mathematics fitted to them are sound — he was explicit that he accepts that part of mainstream physics — and the fault lies in the layer of interpretation built on top. His illustration: clocks are observed to run at different rates. That the clocks are affected is physics; that time itself flows differently is interpretation. The relativity moves Four were listed. That only operationally defined entities are real, following from Einstein's definition of time as what clocks indicate and simultaneity by light-signal synchronisation. That no preferred frame can exist, when what follows is only that none is mathematically required — Lorentz's theory keeps one and is empirically equivalent.
