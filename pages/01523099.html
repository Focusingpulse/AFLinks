Hermann Fricke Born 16 December 1876 Goslar, Germany Died 1949 Residence Berlin-Schmargendorf, Germany Nationality German Known for Hydromechanical aether vortex theory; the "seawater theory" of the stars; the German Society for World-Aether Research Scientific career Fields Aether theory; gravitation; solar and stellar physics; meteorology Institutions Reich Patent Office ( Reichspatentamt ), Berlin Hermann Heinrich Karl Fricke (16 December 1876, Goslar – 1949) was a German physicist, mathematician and chemist who spent his working life as a civil servant in the Reich Patent Office in Berlin, and who became known in the first half of the twentieth century as an opponent of Einstein 's relativity and as the author of an independent hydromechanical aether vortex theory . From about 1935 he pursued his ideas through an organisation of his own, the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Weltätherforschung (German Society for World-Aether Research), which issued a stream of pamphlets and single-sheet circulars from his address in Berlin-Schmargendorf. His fullest statement is the 1939 book Weltätherforschung . Education Doctorate — institution and date not established Career Fricke was a member of the Reichspatentamt (Reich Patent Office) in Berlin, where he reached the rank of Oberregierungsrat , a senior civil-service grade. He was a patent official by profession throughout, not an academic physicist, and his scientific work was pursued outside the universities and against them. He lived at Berlin-Schmargendorf, which is the imprint address of all his society's publications. Work The aether vortex theory Fricke's aether is hydromechanical : a real fluid medium whose vortex motions are meant to produce gravitation, radiation and the structure of matter. It is a mechanical picture, offered in deliberate opposition to a physics its author regarded as having retreated into mathematics. He published on it from 1918 onward and was still defending it thirty years later. Close connections
