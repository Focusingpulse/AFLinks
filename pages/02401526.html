GC2026-SGG035 Features of the upper part of the inner core beneath Africa based on refracted P-wave data Vladimir M. Ovtchinnikov1 , Olga A. Usoltseva1 , Daria S. Oparina1 1 Institute of Dynamics of Geospheres of the Russian Academy of Sciences kriukova@mail.ru Knowledge of the specifics of the transition from the liquid outer core to the isotropic layer in the inner core and then to the anisotropic deeper central part is necessary for understanding geodynamic processes in the core, its mineralogical composition, the mechanisms of generation of the Earth's magnetic field, and the formation of an evolutionary model of the Earth's inner core. Modern seismological observations indicate that the velocities of P￾waves in the core propagating along polar paths (where the angle ξ between the Earth's rotation axis and the direction of the seismic ray at the deepest point is less than 35o ) are approximately 3% higher than the wave velocities propagating along equatorial paths [1]. The inner core is anisotropic [2], layered [3], and laterally heterogeneous [4]. However, the mechanisms for the formation of these characteristics are not yet fully understood. We analyzed seismograms of PKIKP and PKPbc waves with turning points in the inner and outer core, respectively, for more than 100 polar paths sampling the inner core beneath Africa. The database used consisted of measurements published in [5,6,7], as well as new ones obtained at the NRIK station and stations of the ASGSR network (ELT, ARDR, AKAR, and others).
