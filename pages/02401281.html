bmde2026-ecosystems020 Artificial Intelligence and Political Discourse in South Africa: A Systematic Literature Review Zamokuhle Mbandlwa1 , Ephias Ephias Ruhode2 1 Durban University of Technology 2 University of the West of Scotland zamokuhlem@dut.ac.za Background: Artificial intelligence (AI) systems increasingly mediate political communication globally through algorithmic curation, automated content production, and platform governance mechanisms In South Africa, where political discourse reflects high levels of social media penetration, linguistic diversity, persistent inequality, and an active electoral environment, AI-mediated systems have growing implications for democratic participation, misinformation dynamics, political mobilization, and public trust. Objective: This study systematically reviews empirical and analytical literature to examine whether and how AI influences political discourse in South Africa, identifying dominant mechanisms, actors, platforms, methodological approaches, and evidence gaps. Methods: A systematic literature review was conducted in accordance with PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Searches were performed across Scopus, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, Google Scholar, SSRN, and African Journals Online for publications between 2010 and 2026. Search strategies combined AI-related terms, political communication concepts, and South Africa–specific geographic identifiers.
