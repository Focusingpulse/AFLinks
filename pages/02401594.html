GC2026-SGG046 Fireball registration by infrasound and seismic monitoring stations Inna N. Sokolova1 , Vitaliy I. Dubrovin2 1 Geophysical Survey of the Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Institute of Geophysical Research NNC RK, Republic of Kazakhstan sokolovain@gsras.ru The International Monitoring System CTBTO (IMS) has been installed and is successfully operating last decades. There are seismic, infrasound, hydroacoustic, and radionuclide stations. The infrasound station network consists of 60 infrasound arrays installed across the Earth. Four of these stations are located in Russia: Dubna (IS43), Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky (IS44), Ussuriysk (IS45), Zalesovo (IS46), and one in western Kazakhstan, near the Russian border (Aktobe (IS31)). In addition, GS RAS infrasound arrays are in operation: Valaam VALR (Karelia), Barentsburg BRBB and Piramida PYR (on Spitsbergen), as well as the infrasound arrays of the NNC RK Kurchatov KURIS (Eastern Kazakhstan near the Semipalatinsk test site), Makanchi (Eastern Kazakhstan). The IDC and National Data Centers perform automatic detection of infrasound events and generate an infrasound event bulletin on regular base. However, discrimination of event nature is quite difficult problem. Most recorded infrasound events are quarry explosions, strong earthquakes, rocket launches, and others. However, many events are difficult to classify into any known class, including recordings of small fireballs, which are frequently recorded by infrasound stations and sometimes by seismic stations.
