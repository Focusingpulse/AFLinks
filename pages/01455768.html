Anomalous Zeeman Effect

· archived # 1,455,768
"How can I be normal when I m thinking about anomalous zeeman effect" - Wolfgang Pauli The Anomalous Zeeman Effect refers to the complex splitting of atomic spectral lines in a magnetic field when the simple Lorentz triplet (normal Zeeman effect) fails to match observation. Discovered in the late 19th century and systematically studied by Thomas Preston (1897) and Pieter Zeeman, it defied classical explanation until the advent of electron spin. The anomalous effect became a cornerstone in establishing the modern vector model of the atom and the spin–orbit interaction, and it remains essential for precision magnetometry, astrophysical magnetic field measurements (via Zeeman broadening in stellar spectra), and tests of quantum electrodynamics. Before spin was accepted, the anomalous Zeeman effect was quantum theory’s biggest unsolved puzzle, how one missing factor of 2 revolutionized atomic physics.
