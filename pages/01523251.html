John Hunter Residence United Kingdom Known for Gravity , Cosmology John M. Hunter is a British researcher in cosmology and gravitation who proposes that the observed expansion of the universe is better understood as a global rescaling of all lengths and physical constants, and that dark energy is an artefact of misreading the relationship between redshift and scale factor. Work The rescaling symmetry principle Hunter's central proposal, set out in " The Rescaling Symmetry Principle " (2008), is that every length in the universe and every physical constant changes continuously and simultaneously, at a rate governed by the number of length dimensions in the quantity concerned: d Q / Q = aH d t , with H half the Hubble constant. Lengths and the speed of light carry a = 1, Planck's constant 2, the gravitational constant 3, the permittivity of free space −3, while masses, the fine structure constant and the Hubble constant are unchanged. Because everything changes together, he argues, no local experiment can detect the change — timing light over a fixed distance, or lunar laser ranging for a drift in G , must both return null results. The cosmological redshift, on this account, arises from the changing value of Planck's constant rather than from expanding space. He reports that the resulting magnitude–redshift relation matches the Type Ia supernova data without invoking dark energy, and that a matter density of one quarter critical follows naturally, with an equation of state parameter of −1. Later statements His own summary of the position, given in the World Science Database record below, is that "dark energy is an illusion created due to the misunderstanding of the relationship between redshift and scale-factor", and that the model reproduces the WMAP5 value of Ω m = 0.249.
