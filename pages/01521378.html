Title Study of Gravity, Part 1 Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Mike Gamble Published 2011 No. of pages 114 Abstract I started this "Study of Gravity" back in 2007 more as a curiosity than research. Looking in textbooks all you find about gravity is the statement that it is an attraction between two bodies and varies as the mass and separation. I thought there must be more to it, so I started collecting data about the earth. In engineering you start with a ballpark number (WAG or SWAG) and work the problem from there. Being of the electronics type I was trained to think in terms of waves, harmonics and frequencies. The "wow moment" in the research came when I realized that the harmonic waveshapes were aligning very close to that of the measured data. After that things got real interesting! Overview Mike Gamble, an electronics engineer at Boeing in Seattle, presents the first part of a study of gravity undertaken from 2007 as a personal curiosity, delivered at the Space, Propulsion and Energy Sciences International Forum in 2011 with a Boeing public-release memo attached. His starting complaint is that textbooks say only that gravity is an attraction varying with mass and separation, and he set out to find more by collecting data about the Earth. His approach is that of an engineer beginning from a ballpark estimate, and — being "of the electronics type" — trained to think in terms of waves, harmonics and frequencies. The turning point came when he found that harmonic waveshapes aligned closely with the measured data. The study is laid out in four steps. First, document the existing and relevant data on the whole Earth system, including the core, mantle, crust, atmosphere and ionosphere, rather than only the surface acceleration commonly called gravity. Second, analyse that data in MATLAB — using curve fitting, complex arithmetic and Fourier transforms — to build a mathematical model of the total system.
