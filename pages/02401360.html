kimo2026-main161 Global governance in transition: global south agency and strategic adaptation in a transforming world order Muhammad Taimur F. Khan1 1 Institute of Strategic Studies Islamabad (ISSI), Pakistan muhammad.taimur.khan@issi.org.pk The contemporary system of global governance is undergoing profound transformation driven by power diffusion, geopolitical fragmentation, and increasing normative contestation. This study examines how these structural changes are reshaping patterns of agency and strategic adaptation among states traditionally positioned outside the core of the liberal international order. Drawing on neoclassical realism and Global International Relations approaches, the paper analyses the interaction between systemic pressures and domestic-level strategic responses in the evolving multipolar environment. Methodologically, the research employs qualitative document analysis and comparative case assessment, focusing on selected Global South actors and their engagement with emerging governance frameworks and major power initiatives. The findings demonstrate that Global South states are not merely reactive to great-power competition but are increasingly pursuing diversified alignment strategies and selective institutional participation to enhance autonomy and resilience. The analysis further shows that these practices contribute to incremental but meaningful reconfiguration of global governance norms and practices, particularly in areas of economic cooperation, diplomatic coordination, and strategic signalling.
