You can calculate it by determining the number of half-lives that have passed and dividing the total time by this number (T = t/n). For example, if 200g of a substance decays to 12.5g in 20 minutes, which is four half-lives, then the half-life is 5 minutes. Half-life is the time required for a quantity of a radioactive isotope to reduce to half of its initial value. Each radioactive element has a unique, constant half-life. Strontium-90: 29.1 years HOW TO COMPUTE HALF-LIFE SIMPLE METHOD: This method works when you know the initial and final amounts of the radioactive material. Determine the number of half-lives (n): Repeatedly divide the initial amount by two until you reach the final amount. Count how many times you had to divide by two. In summary, if you know how many half-live have occurred over a total time (t), you can find the half-life (T½) by dividing the total time by the number of half-lives (T½ = t/n). EXAMPLE 1: A sample of Thallium-201 decays from 200 grams to 12.5 grams in 20 minutes. Calculate it's half-life. Solution Number of half-lives: 200 g -> 100 g (1 half-life) 100 g -> 50 g (2 half-lives) 50 g -> 25 g (3 half-lives) 25 g -> 12.5 g (4 half-lives) So, n = 4.
