ISARD-2025-remote015 Adaptation of SMOGK deep convection detection system algorithms to high-orbit meteorological satellites "Arktika-M" Andrey E. Shishov1 , Gorlach A. Irina1 1 Hydrometeorological Research Center of the Russian Federation, Moscow, Russia shandruha@gmail.com Modern climate research indicates that recent decades have been the warmest in the history of meteorological observations across Northern Eurasia. This, in turn, has led to an increased frequency and intensity of atmospheric convection processes, resulting in the formation of single- and multi-cell clusters of hazardous cumulonimbus clouds and mesoscale convective systems (MCS). Powerful convective clusters and systems trigger extreme weather events—intense thunderstorms, heavy rainfall, large hail, destructive squalls, and tornadoes. The projected rise in convective extreme events underscores the urgent need to develop effective methods for monitoring deep convective clouds (DCC) using satellite data across the entire Russian Federation, including sparsely populated regions of Siberia and the Far East, where limited radar coverage and synoptic stations further reduce the quality and detail of atmospheric process monitoring. This study demonstrates the application of an interactive and automated Deep Convective Cloud Monitoring System (SMOGK) based on the analysis of individual DCC development cases in southern Russia in 2025.
