GTM2026-main027 Cashback loyalty programs as a price discrimination tool for firms acting on interrelated markets Daria I. Tabashnikova1 , Karina I. Tikhonova 1 National Research University Higher School of Economics, Moscow, Russia dtabashnikova@hse.ru Firms with significant market power often implement loyalty programs to retain customers or to increase profit. When a firm operates across multiple interrelated markets, it can offer limited bonuses that can be used only on one of those markets, thereby performing price discrimination. However, this mechanism is not the same as the second-degree price discrimination, as the customer’s decision here is limited to choosing which market to receive benefits from. Beyond that, the market imposes no entry fees and no restrictions on product quantity. This research analyzes interrelated market configurations that make it possible to increase profit by using the cashback mechanism.
