Frank Phillips

· archived # 1,459,506
Frank Phillips, oilman and founder of Phillips 66, built an empire across Oklahoma, his name emblazoned on hundreds of gas stations, yet he never learned to drive a car. For all his business acumen and wealth, there was something in him that belonged to an older, wilder world—a fascination with the outlaws and bad men of the Old West. Some of those very outlaws, like the notorious Henry Wells, found themselves oddly charmed by Frank Phillips. He welcomed them into his life not with suspicion, but with curiosity and respect. Every year, he hosted the “Cow Thieves and Outlaws Reunion” at his ranch, a gathering where men who had once been on opposite sides of the law mingled freely. Guns were checked at the gate, grudges were left behind, and for a few days, thieves and sheriffs drank, laughed, and shared stories under the same open sky. The rules were simple but unbreakable: no arrests on the ranch, no grudges allowed within the gates. Frank’s vision turned history’s rogues and lawmen into guests at the same table, creating a rare space where enemies became companions, and the harshness of the frontier softened into a human story of respect, fascination, and fleeting peace. It was a celebration of the West’s spirit, orchestrated by a man who commanded empires yet never needed to drive to feel the thrill of the frontier.
