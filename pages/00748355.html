Introduction The notion of an inner product and associated space is so rife in the literature that there is hardly any formal introduction. The inner product space tends to offer a useful terrain for achieving a large class of mathematical results, ranging from identities to inequalities. The result in this setting is often always the best possible. A typical instance is the Cauchy-Schwartz achieved in the setting of the Hilbert space [1]. In this paper we introduce the notion of the local product and the induced local product space. This space turns out to be a special type of a complex inner product space.
