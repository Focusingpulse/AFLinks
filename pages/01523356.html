Lucy Haye Residence Long Beach, CA, United States Known for Autodynamics Lucy Haye , Ph.D., is a representative of the Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics (SAA), based in Long Beach, California, and a co-author of papers on Autodynamics , the alternative relativistic mechanics developed by Ricardo Carezani . Biography Public sources identify Lucy Haye as SAA's representative, and her papers carry a Long Beach address in the same city where Carezani lived and worked. Her published work appears through the Society and its website, autodynamics.org; no independent biographical record has been located, and the wiki does not have a date or place of birth for her. Work Haye's contribution to the Autodynamics literature falls into two parts: expository work on the theory's foundations, and its applications to astrophysics. Foundations The Superfluous System is her restatement of the central Autodynamics objection to the Lorentz transformation without algebraic machinery. Beginning from a phenomenon at A and an observer at B , she argues that relativity already holds between the two, and that the third system Lorentz introduces — in which A is at rest — carries no physical information. Her technical claim is that the transformation then divides a constant distance by a factor that shrinks with velocity, so that a fixed separation appears to grow, and that this manufactured increase is the artificial energy the neutrino was later invented to remove. The paper works a numerical example taken from Feynman 's Lectures on Physics , and a footnote records that Carezani first framed the argument this way to explain it to the photographer Herman Leonard before a portrait session. Astrophysical applications Three papers apply Carezani's pico-graviton theory of gravitation to observed anomalies.
