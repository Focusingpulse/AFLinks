The standard account Pauli arrived at the rule empirically, as the condition needed to explain why electrons in an atom do not all collapse into the lowest orbit and why the periodic table has the shell structure it has. Its modern form is more general: the total wave function of a system of identical fermions particles of half-integer Spin such as the Electron , Proton and Neutron must be antisymmetric under exchange of any two of them, which makes the amplitude for two of them sharing a state vanish identically. Bosons, of integer spin, obey the opposite rule and may share states freely.
