Title Is the Differential Rotation of the Sun Caused by a Coriolis Graviton Engine? Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Thierry De Mees Keywords Differential rotation Sun, gravitons, inertial resistance, Coriolis gravity theory Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 2 Pages 105-106 Abstract Essential fundamentals of gravitomagnetism are found by applying the process of the reciprocal graviton-losses by particles that are defined here as trapped photons. The gravity field is found to be generated by a Coriolis effect, exerted by gravitons upon particles. Inertial resistance is generated by a Coriolis effect as well. In order to demonstrate the former case, we apply the graviton mechanics to the Sun. The amplitude of this effect is found to match the Sun's rotation frequency. Overview Is the Differential Rotation of the Sun Caused by a Coriolis Graviton Engine? is a two-page paper by Thierry De Mees of Edegem, Belgium, presented at the 17th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in June 2010 and published in the Proceedings of the NPA (vol. 7, pp. 105–106). It is the source of the relation used in his later Motion is Relative to the Universe but Inertia is not . The construction models a particle as trapped light circulating on an orbit, and gravity as the Coriolis effect of "gravitons" leaving one such orbit and crossing another. The Coriolis acceleration at the crossing is 2 c × ω ; setting that equal to the gravitational acceleration Gm/R² and counting the available intersections gives ν i = Gm i /2cR ij ² (equation 3) Inertia is given the same treatment: an accelerated trapped photon experiences a Coriolis reaction opposing the force, and De Mees notes that of the six possible orientations of the internal spin, four give the opposing acceleration and two "have a screwing shape… [and] don't undergo any Coriolis effect at all."
