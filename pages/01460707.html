MERLIN MAY HAVE BEEN A HISTORICAL FIGURE WHO LIVED HERE Dinas Emrys is a rocky, forest-clad hill rising above the Glaslyn Valley in Snowdonia, Wales. According to the 1200-year-old Historia Brittonum, attributed to the British monk Nennius, it is where, as a boy, the future British leader, Ambrosius, first proves his prophetic abilities to the British king Vortigern. And later, Ambrosius inherited a fortress on the hill. Ambrosius was a historical figure who lived at the end of the fifth century, the period when King Arthur is said to have lived, as mentioned in the writings of another British monk, Gildas, who wrote within living memory of that time. Significantly, Geofrey of Monmouth, who initiated the medieval tradition of Arthurian romance literature in the early 1300s, wrote about the same encounter between Ambrosius and Vortigern, but tells us that Ambrosius was also called Merlin, seemingly a title he inherited later in life when he became a bard, or royal advisor. The possibility that the character of Merlin was based on a historical figure is furthered by the fact that Emrys was an early Welsh rendering of the name Ambrosius, and Dinas Emrys means the Fortress of Ambrosius. Moreover, the foundations of an early post-Roman fortification still exist on the hill, which excavations in the last century dated to the late fifth or early sixth century, precisely the time when Ambrosius lived.
