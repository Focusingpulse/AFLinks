MolPhot2026-main040 Plasmon-enhanced luminescence of pyrillocarbocyanine dyes and singlet oxygen Niyazbek K. Ibrayev1 , Boris F. Minaev2 , Evgeniya P. Menshova1 1 Buketov Karaganda National Research University, Republic of Kazakhstan 2 Bohdan Khmelnytsky National University, Ukraine menshovayevgeniya@gmail.com Pyrylcarbocyanine (PyrC) dyes (Fig. 1a) are characterized by intense absorption in the near-infrared (NIR) region, high molar extinction coefficients, and tunable photophysical properties achieved through heteroatom substitution. These features make them attractive for fluorescence bioimaging [1], biomedical diagnostics [2], and photodynamic therapy (PDT) [3]. A comprehensive study of the photophysical properties of PyrC dyes (O-, S-, and Se-PyrC) in polyvinyl butyral (PVB) films, as well as their interaction with plasmonic nanostructures, was carried out. The absorption and fluorescence spectra are narrow, mirror-symmetric, and located in the NIR region (Fig. 1b). Replacement of the heteroatom (O → S → Se) results in a progressive bathochromic shift of the absorption and fluorescence bands due to a decrease in the HOMO–LUMO energy gap. Simultaneously, the fluorescence quantum yield decreases from 0.05 for O-PyrC to 0.005 for Se-PyrC, while the fluorescence lifetime decreases from 0.7 ns to 0.09 ns. This behavior is associated with an increase in the intersystem crossing (ISC) rate caused by the enhancement of spin–orbit coupling, reflected in the increasing matrix element ⟨S1 |HSO|T1 ⟩.
