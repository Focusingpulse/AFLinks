Title Gravitation in a Gaseous Ether Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Adolphe Martin Keywords Gravitation, Gaseous Ether Published 2005 No. of pages 9 Abstract Newtonian gravitation arises from the Clausius term added to the ideal gas equation to account for the space occupied by cosmons, fundamental particles of cosmonic gas. The gravitational force is explained as a pressure due to static pressure and cosmon concentration. Overview Adolphe Martin proposes that Newtonian gravitation arises from the finite size of the particles composing a universal gas — specifically from the Clausius excluded-volume correction to the ideal gas law. The paper appeared in the Apeiron volume Einstein and Poincaré: the physical vacuum , edited by Valeri Dvoeglazov. The gas pervades everything, including the space between and within elementary particles, and its particles are called cosmons. Their properties are stripped to a minimum: no moving parts, hence no internal energy and no rest mass, inertial or gravitational; zero spin, hence no forces or friction; a definite diameter, hence a volume impenetrable to other cosmons; and absolute void between them, with no fields at that level. Individual speeds range from zero to indefinitely large on a Maxwell distribution. Interactions occur only through encounters. Between them cosmons travel at constant velocity, and in an encounter they exchange the velocity component along the line of centres while each retains the normal component, with the midpoint of that line describing the same straight line throughout. Martin states that physics at this level is reduced to pure kinematics, that momentum and energy are not applicable there, and that mass, momentum and energy at the level of the gas are statistical effects of cosmon kinematics.
