GC2026-PERM005 New Magnetostratigraphic Age Constraints for the Final Stage of the Okhotsk-Chukotka Volcanogenic Belt, West Okhotsk Sector Ivan Lebedev1 , Varvara Dudanova1 , Ivan Fedukin1 , Kirill Sirotin1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences lebedev232@ya.ru The age of igneous rocks of the Okhotsk-Chukotka Volcanogenic Belt (OCVB) remains a subject of extensive debate to this day. The stratigraphic schemes used for compiling recent geological maps are based on paleofloristic data. However, in recent decades, modern precision isotope-geochronological methods have been increasingly applied, proving to be more reliable than classical methods. The most widely used is the U-Pb method on zircon, which is primarily applicable to felsic igneous rocks. Despite the potential application of the Ar/Ar method, intermediate and mafic volcanic rock varieties most often lack reliable age determinations. An alternative to isotope-geochronological methods could be information on the magnetic polarity of these rocks. A large part of the OCVB was formed during the Cretaceous Normal Superchron; for this part of the belt, the magnetostratigraphic method is, for obvious reasons, not applicable. However, its later stages extend beyond the Superchron. Given the known upper age constraint for the formation of the volcanic belt as ~76 Ma, reversely magnetized rocks with a high degree of confidence indicate their formation during the reverse chron C33r, which occurred 83.6–80 million years ago.
