kimo2026-main430 India and Technological Sovereignty: External Factors and Strategic Autonomy Interconnections Kirill Likhachev1,2 1 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia 2 Institute of China and Contemporary Asia RAS, Moscow, Russia likhachevkirill@gmail.com This paper explores the interconnections between technological sovereignty and strategic autonomy in India’s foreign and economic policy. It argues that India’s digital and industrial strategies cannot be understood solely through domestic developmental priorities; they are deeply shaped by external structural constraints, including export controls, geopolitical competition, and the concentration of critical technological nodes in Western economies. In an era of increasing technological fragmentation, access to advanced semiconductors, cloud infrastructure, AI standards, and digital platforms is embedded in asymmetric global networks that can be leveraged for political influence. Drawing on the concepts of embedded autonomy and weaponized interdependence theoretical frameworks, the paper conceptualizes India’s approach as one of managed interdependence rather than technological autarky. While engagement with Western technological ecosystems remains economically rational, concerns about overdependence and coercive vulnerability push India to diversify partnerships, promote selective localization, and invest in domestic digital public infrastructure.
