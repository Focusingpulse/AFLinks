MolPhot2026-main024 Effect of sulfur precursors on the properties of lead sulfide colloidal quantum dots Pavel B. Matiukhin1 , Maxim G. Spirin1 , Sergey B. Brichkin1 , Vladimir F. Razumov1,2 1 Federal Research Center of Problems of Chemical Physics and Medicinal Chemistry RAS, Russia 2 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (National Research University), Russia pavelm.1998@yandex.ru Lead Sulfide colloidal quantum dots (CQD) are widely used in various fields, including new-generation IR photodetectors [1,2]. PbS CQDs are synthesized via high-temperature colloidal synthesis in the presence of organometallic precursors and effective ligands. One of the main factors influencing the properties of CQDs is the nature of the initial precursors. Lead oxide, halides, acetate, or acetylacetonate are commonly used as metal sources, while bis(trimethylsilyl) sulfide (TMS)2 S is the preferred choice for sulfur. This reagent has a high reactivity, but it is a very expensive, toxic and flammable compound. Therefore, the search for other, more accessible and safer sulfur sources remains a pressing issue. This study explores how various sulfur precursors affect the synthesis of colloidal PbS quantum dots via a solution￾phase route. Two modifications of this method are considered: single injection and fractional injection of reagents. Thioacetamide (TAA) is shown to be the most versatile sulfur source, since in its presence it is possible to synthesize particles with a wide size range (with an average diameter from 2.5 to 11 nm).
