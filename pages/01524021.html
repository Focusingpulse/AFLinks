Title The Asymptotic Box Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Robert J Heaston Published 2003 Journal Journal of New Energy Volume 7 Number 3 No. of pages 8 Pages 32-37 Abstract An asymptote is a goal that can probably never be reached, and if reached, cannot be crossed. If crossed, there may be no return. There are at least six asymptotes that apply to our universe. A box has six sides. Consequently, we can use the metaphor that our universe is an asymptotic box. The six asymptotes of the natural world that apply to our universe are birth, death, the speed of light, absolute zero, gravitational collapse limit, and the superforce. There is also an asymptotic box of scientific reasoning. The paper will define the asymptotes and then discuss various consequences. Overview Robert Heaston's essay is a philosophical argument rather than a calculation — he says explicitly that "no mathematics or equations will be used." Its occasion was the May 2003 Scientific American cover feature announcing that parallel universes "probably real... should exist in four different ways," and his purpose is to argue the opposite case: that our universe is bounded by asymptotes we cannot pass, and that theories which cross them have left physics behind. He identifies six such bounds and arranges them as the faces of a box. Birth and death form the front and back; the speed of light and the gravitational collapse limit form the two sides; absolute zero is the floor and what he calls the superforce is the ceiling. The pairing is deliberate: birth and death are limits on time, light speed and collapse are limits on space, absolute zero and the superforce are limits on matter, so that the box's structure follows from the three basic units. Inside the box the laws of physics apply and there are no infinities.
