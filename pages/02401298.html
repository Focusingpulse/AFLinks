GC2026-STP005 Cosmophysical reasons of the present state climate on Earth Vjacheslav V. Ivanov1 1 St. Petersburg Branch of the Pushkov Institute of Terrestrial Magnetism, Ionosphere, and Radio Wave Propagation of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia sl_iva@mail.ru The lack of convincing evidence for the primacy of the anthropogenic hypothesis of global warming and the inability of the climate models used by the United Nations experts to reproduce the time-related changes in many climate characteristics casts doubt on their conclusions and highlights the need to consider cosmophysical factors in forecasting. An analysis of the dynamics of the coordinates of the North Pole has shown that, starting around 1954, their changes were only in the positive direction. The shift of the North and South magnetic poles, which has been actively observed since the early 20th century, played a crucial role in this transition. In the first case, the magnetic pole abruptly changed its direction of motion and began to move towards the geographic pole, which has moved more than 1,300 kilometers over the past 60 years. In the second case, it moved from Antarctica to the Southern Ocean. Since about 1968, there has been a “disruption” in the rhythms of the pole coordinates, and their smooth 25-30-year cycle has been replaced by more frequent 6-year cycles.
