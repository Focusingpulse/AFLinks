GTM2026-main047 Data Externalities and User Composition: The Unintended Consequences of Informed Consent in Digital Platforms Han Qiao1 , Ge Su1 , Sihang Cai1 , Shouyang Wang1,2 1 School of Economics and Management, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, China 2 Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences, China caisihang@ucas.ac.cn Platforms achieve value creation by collecting and utilizing user data. The existence of data externalities enables platforms to infer user characteristics based on existing data, leading to changes in the composition of users engaged in platform activities. As a result, the effectiveness of the informed consent policy, which centers on individual authorization, is called into question. To address this, this paper constructs a dynamic platform model that incorporates data externalities, integrates informed consent into users’ decisions regarding participation and data sharing, and analyzes the evolution of both platform behavior and user composition. The study yields the following findings: First, after the implementation of informed consent, the user composition of the platform expands from the dual structure of “participate and share, and non-participation” that existed before the policy to a tripartite structure that includes an intermediate group of “participate but not share”, thereby expanding users’ choice set.
