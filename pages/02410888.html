Log in I forgot my password Sympathetic Vibratory Physics &raquo; Tesla Electrical Transformer Page actions PDF Print Share Send link Tesla Electrical Transformer ( click to enlarge ) Tesla's Electrical Transformer Nikola Tesla Patent No. 593,138, which he filed on March 20, 1897, and was granted on November 2, 1897, outlines improvements in electrical transformers aimed at developing and utilizing electrical currents of high potential safely and efficiently. Tesla introduces a novel form of transformer or induction-coil system to transmit energy at higher potentials than previously practical. Key features include winding the primary and secondary coils in such a way that minimizes the risk of insulation damage and ensures safety during handling. Tesla's invention utilizes a flat spiral coil design, where the secondary coil is wound around the primary coil, with the terminal of highest potential being the most distant. By employing coils approximately one-quarter of the wave length of the electrical disturbance, Tesla maximizes the potential at the terminal while minimizing the risk of sparks or insulation failure. The secondary coil is electrically connected to the primary and to earth for safety purposes. The patent describes various embodiments of the invention, including coils wound in the form of a flat spiral or a spiral on a frustum of a cone. Additionally, it details the construction of transformers for both raising and lowering the potential of electrical currents, emphasizing safety measures such as grounding and isolation from the main apparatus. Tesla's improvements offer several advantages over traditional coils, including the ability to maintain very high potentials safely and efficiently, while minimizing the risk of insulation failure or electrical discharge.
