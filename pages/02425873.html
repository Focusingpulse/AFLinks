Our preferred region is approximately centered on (RA, Dec) = (40◦ , −15◦ ), extending ∼ 20◦ in all directions. Subject headings: Cassini; ephemerides; ranging 1. Introduction the same orbital plane. BB16a invigorated the community with a specific hypothesis: a Neptune-mass ( 10 − 15M⊕ ) planet in a distant ( a ∼ 700 AU), eccentric (e ∼ 0.6), and inclined (i ∼ 30◦ ) orbit is responsible for the orbital clustering seen among ESDOs. This planet would share the orbital plane of the ESDOs, but it would be apsidally anti-aligned. Although a range of orbits is feasible, the principal uncertainty is the planet’s location within its orbit, to which the longterm dynamics of BB16a are not sensitive. Almost immediately after the appearance of the BB16a results, Fienga et al. (2016) (hereafter F16) demonstrated that precise range measurements to the Cassini spacecraft from ground stations on Earth can rule out many orbital phases of Planet Nine , assuming the nominal mass and orbit reported by BB16a. F16 fit detailed ephemerides based on the INPOP model (Fienga et al. 2011, 2015), with its 150 planetary parameters and 150, 000 observations. Certain values of true anomaly, ν, (i.e. the orbital phase of Planet Nine ) result in significantly increased residuals compared to those from fits without an additional planet.
