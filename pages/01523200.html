James Arathoon Known for Critique of Special Relativity from dimensional analysis; competing models of the Michelson-Morley experiment; the Ritzian aether toy model; the fine-structure constant; the work of Alfred O'Rahilly Scientific career Fields Physics , Electromagnetism , History of science James Arathoon is a researcher and speaker in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society (CNPS) online seminar series, where he has given talks on the foundations of electromagnetism, the interpretation of the Michelson-Morley experiment, the nature of charge and mass, the fine-structure constant, and the history of science and medicine. His work is critical of Special Relativity and of the conceptual foundations that mainstream physics takes for granted, and he draws on the older critical electromagnetic tradition, particularly the writings of the Irish physicist and mathematician Alfred O'Rahilly. Biography Little biographical detail about Arathoon is available in public sources. A 1991 undergraduate thesis submitted for a B.Sc. in Geophysical Sciences at the University of Lancaster, Atmospheric Electricity Investigations: Measurement of Atmospheric Conductivity , reporting measurements made with a Gerdien condenser at a weather station near the university, was self-archived under his name on the Internet Archive in 2023. He has presented regularly in the CNPS livestream seminar series since 2023, hosted by Ian Cowan. Scientific contributions Incompatibilities with Special Relativity Arathoon argues that Special Relativity is not compatible with the ordinary theory of measurement and with dimensional analysis. On this view the relativistic treatment of length and time is not simply a novel but self-consistent account of measurement; rather, it conflicts with the requirements that any coherent system of physical units and dimensions must satisfy.
