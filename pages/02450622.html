I have to agree, I don't see any way it could be self-sustaining unless the inventor had some kind of trick, however, from an ecological standpoint, the idea of using air to run the majority of internal combustion engines would do wonders to reduce pollution and heal the atmosphere. But to compress that air in the first place would need energy that has to come from somewhere, a gravity wheel, a hydrogen fuel cell to drive an electric motor, a hamster in a cage..<g>, from that standpoint any of those could just drive the car direct. Rogers is saying his airmotor system will replenish its air charge at speeds over 20mph apparently using the inertia of the car. So perhaps an auxiliary power unit that was not required to run continuous but only runs when needed to restore the drained air charge from low speeds. Such a trickle charge type compressor system might be able to overcome the loss of so much pressure at low speeds. I don't think Rogers ever claimed it was free energy since he does admit that it will run down at low speeds but the importance of adapting such a system to reduce pollution makes it well worth checking into. ---LARRY SULLIVAN wrote: > > Finally some pictures, to bad the engine doesn't have a hope in hell of > being self sustaining.
