Overview J. P. Wesley argues that the Marinov motor demonstrates a force of induction acting where the magnetic field is zero, and that the Lorentz–Maxwell theory therefore fails. The paper appeared in Apeiron in 1998. His starting point is a proposed fundamental law for the force of induction on a charge: F = − q d A /d t / c , with A the usual vector potential computed from the current density. He shows that for an electromotive force around a fixed closed loop this reduces to Faraday's law, since the loop integral of ∂ A /∂ t is the rate of change of enclosed flux; and that when the loop moves instead, one recovers the flux rule EMF = −dΦ/d t . He then argues that the point law is more fundamental than the integral one. He cites Francisco Müller's 1987 experiments as showing that induction occurs locally and does not require an entire closed current loop, and observes that in the outer secondary winding of a toroidal transformer the magnetic field is zero while a current is nonetheless induced — so "the force of induction that drives electrons to produce a current cannot, thus, be, in fact, the zero B field itself."
