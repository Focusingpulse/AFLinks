Yee argues that naming units after Coulomb, Newton, Ampere and Tesla, while a fitting honour, has helped keep the electrical and mechanical domains of physics apart: a student meeting a named unit is less likely to ask what it actually measures. Most such units reduce to simpler SI terms the ampere to coulombs per second and he contends that one unit in particular, once simplified, bridges the gap between the two domains.
