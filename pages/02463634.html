It develops the Aether Physics Model (APM) 1 INTRODUCTION 1 Abstract As electronic devices shrink to the nanoscale, gate leakage and heat dissipation rise sharply because boundary layers that once behaved as classical insulators increasingly admit quantummechanical charge transfer. In parallel, silicon photonics seeks to move information with photons rather than electrons, yet silicon remains a poor on-chip light emitter and the integrated laser remains a central bottleneck. This paper frames both difficulties as one boundary problem: stable conversion separation fails when a junction cannot maintain a coherent admission state. Using the Aether Physics Model (APM) and Quantum Measurement Units (QMU), we represent boundary response by an integer event-admission law governed by a coherence window τg and a threshold landmark N0 . We propose that nanoscale “tunneling” corresponds to unintended event admission mediated by magnetic-charge linkage through Aether alignment (a magnetic bridge). This yields a direct falsifier: in a non-ferromagnetic nanogap junction operated at fixed electrostatic bias, the extracted window τg must shift systematically with applied magnetic field with an even-in-B leading-order dependence, while an odd-in-B term is permitted only when a chiral boundary is intentionally introduced. Finally, we show how the same gate language reframes the silicon-laser bottleneck as an emitter-gate design problem rather than solely a bulk-bandgap problem, aligning naturally with defect-engineered silicon emitters and cavity-enhanced emission.
