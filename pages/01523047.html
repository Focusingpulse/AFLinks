Title Gravitational Model of the Three Elements Theory: Short Version Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Frederic Lassiaille Keywords gravitational constant, General Relativity , Pioneer anomaly, dark matter Published 2011 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 8 No. of pages 6 Pages 341-345 Abstract The gravitational model of the three elements theory gives explanations for the dark matter mysteries, and the Pioneer anomaly. Concerning the earth flyby anomalies, the theoretical order of magnitude is the same as the experimental one. A very small modification of the perihelion advance of the planet orbits is calculated by this model. The disparity of the gravitational constant measurements might also be explained. Meanwhile, this gravitational model is perfectly compatible with restricted and general relativity, and is part of the three element theory, a unifying theory. Overview Frédéric Lassiaille presents the gravitational sector of what he calls the three elements theory, a unifying framework built from indivisible particles named "luminous points" which travel always at c and generate space-time deformations propagating outward at the same speed. Those deformations combine through a non-associative, non-linear "relativistic operator" applied once at each point of space and time, so that the local gravitational law depends on the distribution of energy throughout the universe along the past light cone. The consequence he emphasises is that Newton's law is recovered only in a limit: at long distances and for a constant, homogeneous energy distribution. At shorter distances there is a "first modification of Newton's law," and for a non-uniform distribution a further "second modification." He applies these to six problems — galactic rotation curves, the Pioneer anomaly, an additional anomaly he predicts during Pioneer 11's Saturn flyby, the Earth flyby anomalies, planetary perihelion advance, and the disagreement among laboratory measurements of G .
