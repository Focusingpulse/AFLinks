Topics In Modern Cosmology Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Harry Hamlin Ricker Date 2 November 2024 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Topics In Modern Cosmology was a live session presented by Harry Hamlin Ricker on 2 November 2024 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. This presentation will discuss issues in modern cosmology. The ideas of Dark matter, Dark Energy, The Cosmic Microwave background and other topics basic to modern cosmological models will be discussed and critiqued. Summary Harry Ricker continued his survey of standard cosmology with the two hypotheses that carry most of its weight, and closed on a conclusion about how the field works rather than about the physics. The session ran without a separate host, with Vladimir S Netchitailo and Nick Percival taking most of the discussion; Ricker's slides did not advance on the audience's screens for the first stretch, so part of the material had to be gone over twice. Dark matter follows from inflation Ricker's central point is one rarely put this plainly: dark matter is not primarily an astronomical inference but a requirement of a particle-physics programme. By the mid-1970s the standard model had been consolidated and its architects turned to cosmology; Guth's inflation followed, resolving the monopole problem, the matter–antimatter imbalance and the flatness problem. But inflation makes a definite prediction about the density parameter Ω, and it cannot work unless Ω = 1, whereas luminous matter gives a value far below that. Dark matter is what closes the gap — non-baryonic, smoothly distributed, and cold rather than hot, since hot dark matter would not let galaxies condense. The consequence Ricker drew is the important one. Showing that galaxy rotation curves can be explained without dark matter does not dislodge it, because dark matter is not held on the strength of rotation curves.
