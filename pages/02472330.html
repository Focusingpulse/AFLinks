New York Morning Telegraph March 6th, 1902 We knew all along that Nikola Tesla invented the wireless method of telegraphy, that he had it patented, and that "the transmission of messages was the most inconsequential part of the general scheme." What every one feared was that Mr. Tesla's overwhelming modesty-which amounts almost to a disease in his case-would prevent him from claiming his own. Tesla, as a matter of fact, invented everything which didn't grow. He has dragged one after another of Dame Nature's secrets from our reluctant mother and patented them. He has a blanket patent on all the wheels that go 'round and on myriads that should go 'round, 'and which will when he gets time to attend to the matter. But what boots invention if the inventor stands idly by and allows brigands like Marconi to carry off the children of his brain and sell them to slave buying capitalists: Nothing. Nothing in the world.
