Bridge From Nowhere: A Story of Space, Motion, and the Structure of Matter Book Author William Day Published 1989 Publisher House of Talos Publishers, East Lansing, Michigan Pages 288 ISBN 0935970010 Bridge From Nowhere: A Story of Space, Motion, and the Structure of Matter is a 1989 book by the chemist William Day . It is the founding statement of what he later called holistic physics : the claim that matter and motion are inseparable, that motion is a structural feature of matter rather than something matter does, and that the material world is built as a three-storey hierarchy of orbital systems — particles, atoms, gravitating systems — each obeying the same pattern at its own scale. The book is unusual in this literature for who wrote it. Day was not an outsider to peer review: a decade earlier he had published Genesis on Planet Earth , an origin-of-life book taken up by Yale University Press for its Bio-Origins series with a foreword by Lynn Margulis, and reviewed by such figures as Sidney W. Fox and Hyman Hartman. Bridge From Nowhere is what happened when he turned the same interest — how ordered structure arises out of a featureless beginning — from biology to physics. Its thesis is stated on the jacket: In this remarkable book the author challenges the traditional definition of motion. He contends that matter and motion are fundamentally inseparable. At each level of matter's hierarchy — particles, atoms, gravitational systems — motion is a structural feature; and as objects move in space, they behave as components conforming to a basic composition of kinematic systems. — Jacket copy, Bridge From Nowhere (1989) The argument Motion as structure The book's point of departure is a complaint about what physics chose to measure. Dynamics treats motion as something that happens to a body when a force is applied, and describes it with mass, momentum and energy. Day's contention is that this gets the order backwards: at every level of matter, motion is already there
