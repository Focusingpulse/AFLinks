And the First Large Motor Here to Use an Alternating Current is Installed by the Sentinel — Can be Operated at Any Hour of the Day or Night, and is Simple. There has just been installed in the Sentinel office's new three phase, induction electric motor for power for the presses of the Sentinel's establishment which is the first large motor of the kind in town. There is a one-horse power motor of the sort which operates cash carriers in F. E. Bacon's store. The use of this type of motor, which is a new invention of Nikola Tesla, is made possible by the new machinery at the electric light works. Rome was the first place in the country to have a compensating generator which allowed motors to be attached to and get power from electric light wires, with their alternating current, without interfering with the lights. Heretofore if a motor were started with attachment to the electric light wires, the lights would go down, because of the power diverted to the motor. With the compensating generator this does not happen. For power by the old system it was necessary to have a separate círcuít. The old motors, which are the ones now generally in use, used a direct current, or a current flowing always in the same direction. They were complicated affairs with comantators, moving contacts, brushes, etc, all of which were liable to accident and damage.
