Dayneko1 1 Irkutsk State University katdayneko@mail.ru Keywords: teaching English, Chinese learners Russian learners intercultural pedagogy, communicative language teaching, classroom interaction, feedback strategies This study examines how cultural context shapes English language instruction for Chinese and Russian young learners. In intercultural classrooms, teachers navigate between implicit cultural expectations and explicit pedagogical goals, particularly when learners' educational socialization and communicative styles differ substantially. Chinese young learners, socialized within high-collectivism, authority respecting environments, often demonstrate reluctance to make public errors, preference for structured tasks, and teacher-centered learning orientations. The research indicates that they exhibit a rational-logical, non-communicative style of language acquisition, favoring deductive approaches, visual scaffolding, and extensive drilling. Conversely, Russian learners, while also influenced by hierarchical traditions, typically show greater readiness for direct interaction, verbal experimentation, and individual expression, perceiving errors as natural learning steps.
