kimo2026-main711 Russia’s Charm Offensive through Investment in Bangladesh: A Case of the Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant Project Md Farid Hossain , Md Farid Hossain Ponencia invitada farid@juniv.edu Russia-Bangladesh relations date back to the 1971 Bangladesh War of Independence, when the Soviet Union strongly supported the country’s independence. In recent years, Russia has recalibrated its relations with Bangladesh through economic statecraft. Does Russian economic statecraft through investment generate soft power in Bangladesh? Russia aims to enhance its reputation in Bangladesh by utilizing economic statecraft, although its traditional soft power tools are not very effective in the country. Investment can be seen as a reflection of the soft power of the investing country when it has a positive effect on the host society and positively changes the minds of people of the host country towards the sending country. Strictly speaking, economic statecraft is not soft power; however, a sending country’s economic successes can enhance its soft power in the recipient country. The qualitative investigation reveals that the Russian-backed Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant (RNPP) mega project contributes significantly to Russia’s soft power projection through cultural exchanges, language promotion, and increased people-to-people contact between the two nations. This study offers a critical analysis of the relationship between economic statecraft and soft power, contributing to the broader literature on international relations and political economy.
