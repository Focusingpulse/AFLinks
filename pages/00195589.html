Why Billiard Balls, Soldier Crabs, and Quantum Computers Deserve the Same Research Funding On the Equivalence of Continuous-Variable Computational Paradigms Sergei Esipenko me@sergei.moe DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19020410 March 2026 Abstract We examine the foundational assumption shared by quantum computing, classical analog computing, billiard-ball computing, and biological computing (soldier crabs): that continuous physical variables can be controlled with su cient precision to perform useful computation. We introduce concrete error-correction schemes for billiard-ball systems (BBEC) and crab-based systems (CEC), demonstrate that their failure modes are structurally identical to those facing quantum error correction (QEC), and show that the reasons we immediately recognize BBEC and CEC as unworkable apply with equal force to QEC at scale. We present a formal framework for comparing precision requirements across paradigms, address the linearity objection by show￾ing that decoherence reintroduces eective chaos, and catalog 30 years of unful lled milestones in quantum computing.
