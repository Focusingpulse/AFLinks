SPAF-2026-main019 CDU: УДК 551.435.11 Quantitative assessment of erosion coastal cliff dynamics through the integration of tacheometric measurements and aerial photography Mark S. Mazilkin1 , Artyom A. Rybchenko1 , Anton A. Yuryev1 , Alyona V. Kadetova1 1 Institute of Earth Crust of the Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences oooazis148@gmail.com The coastal zone of large reservoirs is formed under conditions of artificially regulated water level regimes. Changes in the water level lead to the transformation of the geological environment and to the activation of exogenous geological processes, such as shore erosion. Shore erosion processes occur in areas composed of erodible rock formations and vary in intensity over time. They are intensified during high water levels and subside when water levels are lower [1]. The Irkutsk Reservoir, located in Irkutsk oblast, Russia, is characterized by a high dynamics and spatial diversity of the exogenic processes in the coastal zone, where wave impact is a key component of shoreline degradation [2]. So, for the geotechnical assessment of shoreline development, it is necessary not only to temporarily record the position of the erosion cliff, but also to quantitatively estimate the rates of retreat and the volumes of eroded material.
