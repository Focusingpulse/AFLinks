Weber Electrodynamics and Unipolar Induction Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Bob Gray Date 3 December 2022 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Weber Electrodynamics and Unipolar Induction was a live session presented by Bob Gray on 3 December 2022 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Join Bob Gray as he discusses the development of a mathematical model for a unipolar system to resolve paradox and clarify misconceptions. He will also be propsoing a fundamental experiment that can distinguish between Weber, Müller, and others. Summary Bob Gray on applying Weber electrodynamics to the unipolar generator, the third session in the run on unipolar induction after Harry Ricker 's two. Ian J Cowan hosted and Franklin co-hosted — the broadcast having to be restarted after Cowan spoke for some minutes without it running. Why Weber Gray's reason for taking up Wilhelm Weber's electrodynamics, roughly contemporary with Maxwell's, is that he finds Maxwell's foundation unclear. He quoted the first sentence of Einstein's 1905 paper — that Maxwell's electrodynamics as then understood leads, when applied to moving bodies, to asymmetries not inherent in the phenomena — and raised his own doubt about the Lorentz force, whose history he reads as an integration of Grassmann's force around a closed circuit, which would make it applicable only to closed circuits. He quoted Assis on Lorentz never having specified what the velocity is relative to, and on the modern reading — relative to an observer — having arrived only after 1905. He was explicit about the limits of the alternative. Weber's force equation rests on experiments of Ampère's and Weber's on classical systems, parts near one another and nothing moving at anything like light speed; within that domain it works very well, outside it is questionable. Extensions have been attempted by J. P. Wesley and Thomas E Phipps , but none is needed for unipolar induction.
