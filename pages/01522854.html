Eppur Si Muove Book Author Stefan Marinov Published 1977; 4th edition 2008 Publisher East-West Publishers (Graz, Austria) Pages 200 ISBN 978-9185917020 Eppur Si Muove (Italian for "and yet it moves," the phrase traditionally attributed to Galileo) is a book by the Bulgarian dissident physicist Stefan Marinov (1931–1997), first published in 1977 and reissued in a fourth edition in 2008. It gathers Marinov's experimental and theoretical case against Albert Einstein's special theory of relativity , centred on his claim to have measured the absolute velocity of the Earth through the aether by detecting a direction-dependence in the one-way speed of light . The book The core of Eppur Si Muove is Marinov's account of the experiments he devised to detect the Earth's motion relative to absolute space. Chief among them is his "coupled-shutters" (later "coupled-mirrors") method, in which two rotating shutters or mirrors mounted on a common shaft are used to compare the time of flight of light travelling in opposite directions along the same axis. Marinov reported that this arrangement revealed an anisotropy in the observed one-way velocity of light, from which he calculated an absolute velocity of the Earth of roughly 360 km/s in a definite direction — a result he presented as a direct measurement of motion through the aether and as a refutation of the relativistic postulate that the one-way speed of light is the same in every frame. Alongside the experiments, the book sets out Marinov's own "absolute space–time" theory: a reworking of relativistic mechanics and electrodynamics in which the world-invariant constancy of the two-way velocity of light is retained, but absolute simultaneity and a preferred rest frame — the aether — are restored. The fourth edition carries a preface by the Soviet physicist Andrei D. Sakharov and a detailed description of the apparatus, together with Marinov's replies to his critics.
