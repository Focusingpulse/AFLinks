Toby Grotz Born ( 1950-11-08 ) November 8, 1950 (age 75) Residence Prairie Village, KS, United States Nationality USA Known for New Energy , Project Tesla, wireless transmission of power, independent testing of the Tewari Space Power Generator Scientific career Fields Electrical Engineer , Inventor Institutions Wireless Engineering, Inc.; Institute for New Energy; International Tesla Society; Burns & McDonnell Toby Grotz is an American electrical engineer, energy-industry project manager and independent researcher best known for his work on Nikola Tesla 's wireless transmission of power, for his leadership of the International Tesla Society and the Institute for New Energy, and for his independent measurements of Paramahamsa Tewari 's Space Power Generator. He has spent more than four decades working simultaneously inside conventional power engineering and outside the mainstream, arguing that reports of over-unity and zero-point energy devices deserve careful measurement rather than reflexive dismissal. Biography Education B.S.E.E., University of Connecticut , 1973 Career Grotz received his B.S.E.E. from the University of Connecticut in 1973. His early career included exploration for oil, gas and geothermal resources using seismic and magneto-telluric methods, followed by work in the utility industry on coal, natural gas and nuclear power plants, on facilities up to 1,800 MW. He organized and chaired the 1984 Tesla Centennial Symposium — co-chaired with Dr. Elizabeth Rauscher — and the 1986 International Tesla Symposium, and was the first president of the International Tesla Society, a not-for-profit corporation formed as a result of the first symposium. He served on the steering committee that established the first International Symposium on New Energy, held in Denver in April 1993, and chaired the 1994 International Symposium on New Energy.
