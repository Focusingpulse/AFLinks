Global GM Crops Area Exaggerated Toggle navigation Home Article Index Search Contact Online Store Global GM Crops Area Exaggerated Ten years on, and the ‘growth’ in GM crops area is exposed to be more hype than substance as opposition heightens. Sam Burcher PR masquerading as fact The biotech industry’s mouthpiece, the International Service for the Acquisition of Agrobiotechnology Applications (ISAAA), has been exposed for grossly inflating the figures of GM crops grown globally. Its latest report [1] lists countries growing GM crops that do not grow them, or that have banned them. For example, Iran is down as having grown tens of thousands of hectares of commercial GM rice in 2006, despite the fact Iran has never approved or grown GM rice on any commercial scale. Bob Phelps of Gene Ethics Network criticizes the report for making these unsupported claims and ignoring the negative impacts of GM crops [2]: “The report emphasizes that 10.3 million farmers grew GM crops in 2006, but this is just 0.7 percent of farmers world-wide. And just 600 000 farmers grew 85 percent of all GM crops on industrial farms in North and South America. Small Third World farmers are misused as fodder in the ISAAA’s PR war.” India’s bid to ban all GM field trials The ISAAA launched the report in India, where the Supreme Court has recently banned any new GM crop trials until further notice [3].
