When Einstein Equivalence Principle is taught to physics students - it seems an easy concept to understand and intuitively must be right. But there are a lot of problems with the idea when one looks more deeply. Summary Roger J Anderton 's session on the equivalence principle, hosted by Ian J Cowan with Franklin Hu assisting. He described it at the outset as a rough outline of a subject too big for one session and one he is still not sure about in places. The argument His starting point is the standard route: two postulates give special relativity, the equivalence principle added to them gives general relativity. So whatever is wrong with special relativity is inherited, and Einstein 's own version of the principle — that in free fall the effect of gravity is abolished and general relativity reduces to special relativity — builds on it directly. His specific objection is that the principle deals only with attraction. He traced the contactless force from Newton's apple to Roger Boscovich, who reasoned that a force acting without contact cannot be attractive only, since particles sometimes repel, and drew the curve on which the force flips sign with distance. We are familiar with the attractive part of that curve and unfamiliar with the rest. Applied to a collapsing star, the reversal stops the collapse before a singularity forms — a "breathing" black hole rather than a singular one — and Anderton noted a paper observing that Einstein's 1905 notion of observer covariance had a precursor in Boscovich's eighteenth-century essay on space and time, which the mainstream ignores.
