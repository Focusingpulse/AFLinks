Saturday Morning Science Chats Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Bill Lucas , James Keene , Bob Gray and a new participant, Toby. The theme was set by Lucas — how to judge between competing theories — and it turned into a sustained cross-examination of his own. Distinti, and the case for polytheism Lucas had found a YouTube channel by Robert Distinti, an electrical engineer with a couple of hundred videos, and recommended it as a third party critical of everyone's theories rather than of any one of them. Hu played David de Hilster 's review of Distinti's latest. The point that stuck was Distinti's rule that a theory's ability does not prove its exclusivity: that a model gives good answers does not show no other model would give the same ones. De Hilster's illustration from his own experience was a dean telling him his thesis was ridiculous because Maxwell's equations are irrefutable — which, he said, misses that there may be another way to arrive at the same place. Distinti's remedy for what he calls polytheism — several theories fitting the same data — is to increase the sampling footprint until the candidates can be told apart, his example being two sine waves of different frequency that both pass through every measured point until one measures more finely. Lucas drew from it the moral he cared about: a theory is never finished. General relativity was put forward to explain gravity, and when the Pioneer trajectories and the spiral arms did not fit, the response was to add dark matter and dark energy rather than to falsify anything.
