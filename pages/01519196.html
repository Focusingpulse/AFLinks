A way in which the equations may be modified for the case when the mean flow of the medium is steady and uniform is proposed. The implication of this for the problem of the origin of planetary magnetic fields is discussed. Overview A Re-Examination of Maxwell's Electromagnetic Equations is a three-page paper by Jeremy Dunning-Davies of the Department of Physics, University of Hull, published in Progress in Physics vol. 3, 48–50 (October 2005). Its observation is that the two curl equations are derived on the assumption of a medium at rest. That assumption enters when the total time derivative is taken inside the flux integral: "if the medium is moving, then the surface S in the integral will be moving also, and the mere change of S in the field B will cause changes in the flux." Following Abraham and Becker's 1932 textbook, Dunning-Davies defines a derivative Ḃ whose flux across a moving surface equals the rate of change of the flux of B, and derives it by applying the divergence theorem to the volume swept out between the surface's positions at t − dt and t. The result is Ḃ = ∂ B /∂t + u (∇· B ) − ∇×( u × B ) which, expanding the triple product, becomes ∂ B /∂t + ( u ·∇) B + B (∇· u ) − ( B ·∇) u . For a steady, uniform mean flow this collapses to the Euler (material) derivative D B /Dt.
