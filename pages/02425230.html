Received 2014 July 3; in original form 2014 May 14 Key words: Magnetostratigraphy; Reversals: process, timescale, magnetostratigraphy. 1 I N T RO D U C T I O N The magnetic field of the Earth is primarily a dipolar field that exists in two antipodal polarity states, conventionally named as normal and reverse, that are equally likely, stable and alternates through geological time. Contrary to the Sun, where the magnetic field reverses regularly about every 11 yr, the pattern of geomagnetic field reversals on Earth is aperiodic, and the rate of reversals has varied over time (e.g. Lowrie & Kent 2004). The timing and dynamics of geomagnetic field reversals are debated and draw much interest (Leonhardt & Fabian 2007). A recent review of the 10 most detailed volcanic records of geomagnetic field transitions shows that the reversing field often bears a repetitive structure characterized by a precursory event, a polarity switch and a rebound (Valet et al. 2012).
