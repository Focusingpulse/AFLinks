By Von McNamara Special to The Herald Statesman Two famous inventors whose careers began in Yonkers — Edwin Armstrong and Charles Steinmetz — are being honored on commemorative stamps to be issued by the U. S. Postal Service this month. Edwin Howard Armstrong, the father of FM radio, built the world's first FM station as an experiment in Yonkers in 1933. Charles Proteus Steinmetz, whose electrical genius was second only to Thomas A. Edison, built the foundation for the career that was to make him famous while working in Yonkers for 34 years. The 20-cent stamps also honor inventors Nikola Tesla and Philo T. Farnsworth and are being issued as a se-tenant (different, but attached) block of four. Armstrong was born in New York City on Dec. 18, 1890 and his family moved to a house on Warburton Avenue just south of Odell Avenue in North Yonkers when he was 12. He attended School 6 on Ashburton and began his career as a serious inventor while he was a student at Yonkers High School. Armstrong enrolled at Columbia University and in World War I joined the U.
