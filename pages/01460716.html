Michael Dell

· archived # 1,460,716
His parents showed up unannounced. He panicked and hid everything in a friend's room. What he was hiding became worth $100 billion. A 19-year-old hiding computer parts from his parents built a $100 billion empire. Michael Dell was a pre-med student. His parents wanted him to become a doctor. They had no idea their son was running a business from his dorm room. When they showed up for a surprise visit, Dell rushed to hide his inventory of computer parts in a friend's room. Everyone said he was making a mistake. "Finish your degree first." "You can't compete with IBM." "The computer business is too risky." "You're throwing away a medical career." He didn't listen. Here's what Dell saw that everyone else missed: IBM and the big players were selling computers through retailers. The process was slow. Expensive. Customers couldn't get what they actually wanted. What if you could build computers to order and sell them directly to the customer? Cut out the middleman. Lower the prices. Give people exactly what they need. So Dell got to work. Room 2713 in Dobie dormitory. University of Texas at Austin. 1984. His entire operation? Three guys with screwdrivers sitting at six-foot tables. His startup capital? One thousand dollars. That's it. No investors. No fancy equipment. Just a dorm room and an idea. By the end of his freshman year, Dell was making between $50,000 and $80,000 per month. A month. As a 19-year-old. So he made a decision that terrified his parents. He dropped out. First
