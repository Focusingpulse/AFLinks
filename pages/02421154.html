It is shown how the former is compatible with the data while the latter has problems with the quantitative figures and the case of the immobilized projectile. Overview On the Mechanism of Railguns is a three-page paper by Andre K T Assis of the Department of Cosmic Rays and Chronology at the State University of Campinas, published in Galilean Electrodynamics volume 3, number 5 (September/October 1992), pages 93–95. It compares two accounts of what accelerates a railgun projectile. In a railgun, current from a source flows out along one fixed rail, across a conducting projectile bridging the rails, and back along the other; a ponderomotive force drives the projectile along the rails. Assis asks where that force comes from. The first candidate is Ampère's force law, the 1820s expression for the force between two current elements, which he gives as his equation (1). The second, advanced by Pappas and others, is that momentum is transferred by the reflection of electromagnetic waves from the projectile, with a backward-propagating radiation field carrying the recoil. Assis argues for the first on three grounds. Ampère's law satisfies Newton's third law "in the strongest form," so that a closed circuit cannot exert a net force on itself — consistent with the fact that no experiment has demonstrated such a bootstrap effect in metallic circuits.
