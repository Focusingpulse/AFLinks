kimo2026-main139 India and Technological Sovereignty: Domestic Factors and National Policy Prashant K. Singh1 1 Manohar Parrikar Institute for Defence Studies and Analyses, New Delhi, India prashant.idsa@gmail.com Technology sovereignty is integral to the vision of Viksit Bharat by 2047 (Developed India by 2047). The emphasis on technology sovereignty under the vision fundamentally sees technology no longer merely as a succession of consumer products. It is envisaged as a means to fundamentally re-shape society’s relations with the political￾economic system and to re-define the terms of engagement with international society. Possession of technologies and their production methods is viewed as an utmost national priority in order to counter their weaponisation by hostile actors, whether private or public. Therefore, the pursuit of technological self-reliance across domains is a key motivator behind India’s quest for technology sovereignty. Smart manufacturing, semiconductor capabilities, digital public infrastructure, data sovereignty and regulations, cyber security, open source adoption and defence self-reliance are some core aspects of this quest. Collaboration in production and development appears as a strong element in India’s ongoing diplomatic networking and international partnerships. This paper discusses the various initiatives undertaken by the government to ensure the country’s technology sovereignty, assesses their successes and limitations and strives to indicate a potential future trajectory for India’s quest for technology sovereignty.
