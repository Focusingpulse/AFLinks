Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF): A semi-crystalline thermoplastic polymer known for its excellent piezoelectric, pyroelectric, and ferroelectric properties. Piezoelectricity: PVDF generates an electric charge when subjected to mechanical stress or pressure. This phenomenon is reversible, meaning it can also deform mechanically under an applied electrical field. Flexible Film: PVDF can be processed into thin, lightweight, and flexible films, making it suitable for a variety of applications. Properties of PVDF Piezoelectric Coefficient (d33 and d31): PVDF exhibits relatively high piezoelectric coefficients compared to other polymers. The d33 coefficient (longitudinal mode) typically ranges from 20 to 30 pC/N, and the d31 coefficient (transverse mode) is slightly lower. Mechanical Properties: High flexibility and tensile strength. Durable and resistant to wear and mechanical fatigue. Thermal Properties: Thermal stability up to ~150°C. High pyroelectric sensitivity due to its temperature dependence.
