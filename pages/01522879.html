Title Evidence for the Anisotropy of the Speed of Light Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Bob Johnson Keywords speed of light , Michelson-Morley , earth Published 2011 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 8 No. of pages 6 Pages 293-298 Abstract The Beckmann model is considered in relation to both the Electric Universe perspective of a charged Earth and to the actual non-null results of the various Michelson-Morley-type experiments. It is demonstrated that the Beckmann model is consistent with both concepts. Furthermore, application of the Beckmann model to an orbiting charged Earth offers support to the interpretation of the non-null Michelson-Morley results as evidence not of an 'ether wind' but rather of an electromagnetically-induced directionally-dependent anisotropy of the speed of light. This hypothesis offers a new direction for research and possible re-analysis of existing data. Overview This conference paper attempts to join two dissident traditions that had not previously been combined: Petr Beckmann 's 1987 proposal that the speed of light is measured with respect to the locally dominant field rather than to the observer, and the Electric Universe contention that the Earth carries a significant net electric charge. The bridge between them is the historical claim, developed at length in the middle of the paper, that the Michelson–Morley experiment and almost all of its successors did not in fact produce null results. The synthesis proposed is that the small but non-zero "ether drift" velocities reported across a century of interferometry — typically 5 to 10 km/s rather than the 30 km/s expected from the Earth's orbital motion — are not evidence of a partially entrained ether at all, but of a genuine, directionally dependent anisotropy in the speed of light produced electromagnetically.
