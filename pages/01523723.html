Ralph G. Beil Born 1938 (age 87–88) Residence Marshall, TX, United States Nationality American Known for The Beil metric; a Finsler-geometry approach to unified field theory Scientific career Fields Mathematical physics Ralph G. Beil (born 1938) is an American mathematical physicist. Over some two decades he developed a programme that uses Finsler geometry to construct a unified field theory, deriving electromagnetism and other interactions from the geometry of the tangent bundle. The Beil metric , a Finsler metric he introduced for this purpose, is named after him and has been taken up by later workers in the field. Scientific work Beil's work belongs to the tradition, going back to Weyl, Kaluza and Klein, of seeking to unify the forces of physics within an enlarged geometry. His distinctive move was to base that geometry not on ordinary Riemannian spacetime but on Finsler geometry — a generalization in which the metric can depend on direction as well as position — and to build it on the tangent bundle of spacetime. In a sequence of papers beginning in the late 1980s he argued that a physical motivation for Finsler structure can be developed through gauge transformations on the tangent space, producing a correspondence in which geometric objects — metrics, connections and curvatures — play the roles of the physical gauge potentials, fields and energy-momentum. From this he recovered electrodynamics "from a metric," treated the classical charged particle as an extended geometric object, and extended the scheme toward the electroweak interaction with his work on "electroweak symmetry on the tangent bundle." The Beil metric introduced in this programme has since been studied by other geometers as a basis for unified field theories. Beil presented the approach at the 1995 Joint Summer Research Conference on Finsler Geometry, published in the American Mathematical Society volume Finsler Geometry (1996).
