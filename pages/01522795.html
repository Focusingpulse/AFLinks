Domina Eberle Spencer Born ( 1920-09-26 ) September 26, 1920 Died May 2022 (aged 101) Residence Roxbury Crossing, MA, United States Nationality USA Known for New Gaussian Electrodynamics , Theory of Holors, Field Theory Handbook Scientific career Fields Mathematics , Electrodynamics Domina Eberle Spencer (September 26, 1920 – May 2022) was an American mathematician, a longtime Professor of Mathematics at the University of Connecticut, and one of the most prolific figures in twentieth-century dissident physics. A child prodigy, she entered the Massachusetts Institute of Technology as a high-school student in a summer program and stayed to earn three degrees — an S.B. in Physics (1939), an S.M. in Mathematics (1940), and a Ph.D. in Mathematics (1942, at the age of 21) under the geometer Dirk Jan Struik. Over a career spanning more than seventy years she authored some 300 technical papers across mathematics, optics, illuminating engineering and the foundations of electrodynamics. Spencer is best known for her decades-long scientific partnership with the MIT electrical engineer Parry Moon (1898–1988) — whom she later married — and for the New Gaussian Electrodynamics she developed in her later years, a reformulation of electromagnetic theory built on Ampère's original force law that dispenses with Einstein's special relativity . She co-founded the Natural Philosophy Alliance (NPA) in 1993 and served as its long-standing president. She died in May 2022 at the age of 101.
