GC2026-STP010 The impact of particle precipitation on the middle atmosphere and ionosphere during the solar–geomagnetic disturbances of October 2003 Elina M. Kochneva1 , Irina A. Mironova1 , Galina A. Bazilevskaya2 , Andrei D. Mironov1 , Vladimir S. Makhmutov2 , Evgeny V. Rozanov1 1 St Petersburg University 2 Lebedev Physical Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences. P.N. Lebedev Physical Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences kochneva-elina@mail.ru In late October 2003, a sequence of exceptionally intense solar flares accompanied by coronal mass ejections was observed. These ejections triggered extremely strong geomagnetic storms, resulting in a significant enhancement of atmospheric ionization. This led to a pronounced increase in the concentrations of nitrogen and hydrogen radicals (NOx and HOx), which in turn contributed to substantial depletion of mesospheric ozone. The effects were particularly pronounced in the Northern Hemisphere, as polar winter conditions had already been established, preventing the photolytic destruction of nitrogen radicals. While the individual impacts of proton and electron precipitation on radical production and ozone depletion during this event have been extensively investigated, the present study focuses on a comprehensive assessment of both the separate and combined effects of protons and electrons. Using the RACOP model with detailed ion chemistry, we evaluate their influence on atmospheric ionization, ozone loss, and variations in electron density in the D-region of the ionosphere.
