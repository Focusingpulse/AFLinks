Kuznetsova1 , Elena V. Kolobkova2,3 1 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia 2 St. Petersburg State Technological Institute (Technical University) 3 National Research University ITMO i.ignatiev@spbu.ru Perovskite nanocrystals (NCs) are currently being actively investigated due to the broad prospects of their practical application in optoelectronics. NCs encapsulated in a glass matrix are highly resistant to external influences and high-power laser radiation, which is of particular interest to such systems. For many applications and for fundamental research, an ensemble of perovskite NCs with a narrow size distribution and, consequently, a narrow photoluminescence (PL) band is of interest. It is desirable that all NCs would have the same diameter, with an accuracy of a crystal lattice constant. The synthesis of nanocrystals in a glass matrix occurs at elevated temperatures (about 400 C) [1], which is inevitably accompanied by a statistical variation in the size of NCs. It should be emphasized that NCs in glass have a spherically symmetrical shape, which is determined by the thermodynamics of NC synthesis [2]. Therefore, the inhomogeneous broadening of the NC ensemble is mainly due to the variation in size, rather than in their shape.
