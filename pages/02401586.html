GC2026-SGG045 Machine Learning Detection of Failure Preparation in Rocks Samples Under Uniaxial Compression Andrei M. Golosov1 , Evgenii A. Sverdlov1 1 Far Eastern Federal University a-dune@mail.ru In recent years, there has been a rapid increase in interest in machine learning (ML) methods, where a program first "learns" from data with known class labels. Coefficients are carefully selected and tuned, enabling the program to classify new, unseen data with high accuracy [1]. Traditional approaches based on empirical correlations and deterministic models often have limited ability to account for high-dimensional input parameter spaces and complex nonlinear relationships between geological, geomechanical, and technological factors. ML methods overcome these limitations. ML algorithms enable automated feature extraction and construction of complex nonlinear models relating input and output parameters, significantly improving the accuracy of predicting rock fracture probability and timing [2]. However, limitations related to data quality and completeness, as well as result interpretation, must be considered. Thorough model validation and robustness assessment are critical. The "black box" problem in deep learning necessitates developing explainability methods to ensure reliable and trustworthy predictions. Overall, ML models represent a promising tool for enhancing rock fracture forecasting efficiency.
