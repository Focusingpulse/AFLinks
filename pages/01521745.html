Title The Law of Power Demands Exotic Particles Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Sadanand S Savarkar Published 2008 No. of pages 6 Abstract The Law of Power : is formally unexceptionable, but has an apparently bizarre consequence, requiring perpetual energy flows associated with the internal forces in an inertial equilibrium system - as revealed to Halley and termed the "Halley-power-flows" here - even when the force is not transparently overcoming any resistance and nor is the state of uniform motion of the body upon which it acts is being changed by the action of the force. Overview S. S. Savarkar, writing from Mumbai, takes an apparently trivial consequence of the definition of power and follows it to an unusual conclusion: that steady energy currents must circulate inside every stressed body in uniform motion, and that carrying them requires particles of a kind not yet known. The puzzle is dramatised through Edmond Halley, in the author's science play The Catherine Conspiracy . Halley rides in a coach pressing his feet against the footrest and reclining against the seat back. Since d W /d t = F · v , his feet do work on the coach continuously, and the seat back does work on him at the same rate, so a current of energy passes through his body. And because the velocity must be reckoned against some larger frame, the current is enormous: pressing with only 10 kgf, at the Earth's orbital speed of 30 km s −1 , the power is that of about 4000 horses; against the microwave background frame at some 300 km s −1 , about 40,000 horses, or 30 MW. He calls these the Halley-power-flows and insists they are not to be explained away. The alternative — denying physicality to energy, as Newtonian mechanics did — "militates against a large body of physical experience," and he notes that in Lorentz's theory such a flow is what secures the rotational equilibrium of a moving charged rod and accounts for the Trouton–Noble null result.
