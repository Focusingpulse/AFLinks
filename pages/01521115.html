Quantum Mechanics: Evolving Perspectives on the History of Science Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Eric S Reiter Date 29 January 2022 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Quantum Mechanics: Evolving Perspectives on the History of Science was a live session presented by Eric S Reiter on 29 January 2022 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. We will concentrate mostly on ideas that led to wave-particle duality. Early experiments found that matter and light each seemed to have wave and particle properties: a contradiction. Here you will see how our greatest physicists struggled over those ideas. Many physicists will admit that this wave-particle problem has not been resolved to this day. The double-slit test is a popular way to express the wave-particle problem, but a far better test is the beam-split coincidence test. The history of that test will be shown, starting from the thought experiment of Einstein in his definition of the photon, leading to modern actualizations. This critical history includes objections and alternatives that were rejected. From the original offprints, you can judge the assumptions that IMHO led us astray. After you see my other writings and videos of experiments and theory, you might understand how these historical components lead to the resolution of wave-particle duality. Summary Eric Reiter on the history of quantum mechanics, told as a sequence of specific assumptions he thinks were wrong, with David de Hilster hosting. It was the first in the society's "evolving perspectives on the history of science" series. Asked first how he came to it, Reiter said he wanted to see what was going on in the physical world and did not believe what he was told about the double slit — he knew at once something was wrong with it — so he went to the experiments rather than to people.
