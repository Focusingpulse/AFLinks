kimo2026-main097 Literature as Cultural Diplomacy: Language, Narratives and the Voice of the Global South Shruti . shruti@jayniti.org In an increasingly multipolar world, cultural and intellectual exchanges are emerging as important instruments of international engagement beyond traditional state diplomacy. Literature, as a carrier of language, memory, and social experience, plays a significant role in shaping cross-cultural understanding and amplifying voices from the Global South. This paper examines literature as a form of cultural diplomacy that contributes to dialogue among societies in a period marked by geopolitical tensions and competing narratives. It explores how literary traditions and vernacular languages reflect diverse historical experiences and provide alternative perspectives to dominant global narratives often shaped by Western discourse. By analysing the relationship between language, identity, and storytelling, the study argues that literature enables civil society actors, academic platforms, and cultural institutions to foster mutual understanding and soft connectivity across regions. The paper also highlights the relevance of literary engagement in strengthening people-to-people connections and promoting inclusive global conversations. It concludes that literature-based cultural diplomacy offers a meaningful pathway for the Global South to articulate its intellectual and cultural agency within the evolving international order.
