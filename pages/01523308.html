Laurent Hollo Born ( 1967-08-07 ) August 7, 1967 (age 59) Residence Carignan, QC, Canada Nationality French Canadian Known for dimensions of physical quantities Scientific career Fields Computer security analyst Laurent Hollo (born 7 August 1967) is a French-Canadian computer security analyst living in Carignan, Quebec, whose papers to the Natural Philosophy Alliance between 2010 and 2012 propose a standardised taxonomy of physical quantities based on their dimensions in space and time. Work Hollo's project is organisational rather than revisionary, and he is explicit that his formalism is "fully compatible with current knowledge". His starting observation is that the equations of different branches of physics share a recurring shape which the separate notational conventions of each branch conceal — so that the same role is played by quantities with unrelated symbols, while one name may cover quantities playing different roles. His scheme arranges every physical quantity on a matrix whose axes count powers of length and of time, so that neighbouring cells differ by one factor of a metre or a second. Quantities are then sorted into four domains — gravitic, thermic, electric and magnetic — alongside two special ones for space-time and for energy; into four groups according to whether they carry, promote or oppose the transfer of energy; and into generic roles — charge, current, flux, potential, field, resistivity, permittivity and so on — defined without reference to any particular domain. The organising axiom is a single generalised form of Ohm's law: Effect = Cause / Opposition . Because roles are defined generically, the scheme implies quantities that are unfamiliar or unnamed in one domain but standard in another, such as a gravitic permittivity or a current of heat; Hollo notes that most of these already exist under other names.
