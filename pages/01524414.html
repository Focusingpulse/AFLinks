Summary A panel on what the first James Webb images mean for the big bang, with George Coyne and Glenn Borchardt , both up at seven in the morning on the Canadian and American west coasts, and David de Hilster hosting. Harry Ricker , Ian J Cowan and Nick Percival joined from the green room. Coyne's brief Coyne opened with the reports. Before Webb the most distant galaxy known was one seen as it appeared about 420 million years after the supposed big bang; Webb has since found dozens dating to about 200 million years, and one report put a pair at under 100 million. The galaxies span a thousand light years and hold tens of millions of stars where a modern galaxy holds hundreds of billions, and astronomers put them at less than a hundred million years old and possibly as young as twenty million. One of the pairs is quoted at about a billion solar masses, which the team took as a hint that stars formed more rapidly in the early universe than believed. Coyne's objection was to the direction of the inference: rather than conclude that the universe is older than 13.787 billion years, astronomers prefer to revise how fast stars form.
