The answers to the question of the Correspondents' Club, "How can the influence of the press be increased?" received from a half hundred more or less well known citizens have attracted some attention and not a little wonder. Of the fifty asked only one man confessed himself unable to answer. The others settled the matter in a cheerful paragraph. There were the usual freak answers. Susan B. Anthony declared the only way consisted "in giving the right of suffrage to women;" Horace White, "by an honest effort to deserve it." The rest answered according to their vocabularies that "truth" was the sole test and the only solution. Some of the answers in this respect create feeling of surprise. Thomas A. Edison, for instance, said: "By publishing a fact now and then." When it is remembered that Mr. Edison has given out interviews in which certain alleged discoveries were revealed which were to revolutionize the world one is jarred slightly. During the Spanish war it was to surround the continent with an electric belt and shock any hidalgo to death who came trespassing. Nikola Tesla "wants a better system adopted by the papers for informing the world of scientific achievement."
