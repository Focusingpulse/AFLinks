Shiuji Inomata Born 1932 Japan Died April 17, 2001 ( 2001-04-17 ) Niigata, Japan Nationality Japanese Known for The N-machine free-energy device; the "consciousness–energy–matter" theory Scientific career Fields Physics; electrical engineering; "psychotronics" and consciousness studies Institutions MITI Electrotechnical Laboratory; Japan Psychotronics Institute Shiuji Inomata (1932 – 17 April 2001), also romanised Shuji Inomata , was a Japanese physicist and engineer, president of the Japan Psychotronics Institute , and a leading figure in the Japanese "new-paradigm science" and free-energy movements. He is known for his work on the N-machine — a homopolar "free-energy" generator — and for a speculative theory that sought to write consciousness into the equations of physics as a third term alongside energy and matter. He is listed in Jean de Climont 's Worldwide List of Dissident Scientists . Biography Inomata graduated in physics and engineering from the University of Electro-Communications in Tokyo and took a Ph.D. from the Tokyo Institute of Technology . He worked as a postdoctoral researcher at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, and spent much of his career as a government scientist in Japan's Ministry of International Trade and Industry (MITI), at its Electrotechnical Laboratory. From that position he became an organiser as much as a researcher. He founded and led the Japan Psychotronics Institute , through which in the 1990s he hosted several international symposia on "new-paradigm science," including one at Waseda University in Tokyo, and he launched a committee within MITI to investigate "new energy," which he associated with the traditional concept of ki . He died on 17 April 2001 in a hospital near his home in Niigata, of organ failure associated with liver cancer, at the age of sixty-eight.
