GC2026-STP049 Empirical model of substorm effects in subrelatvstic electron fluxes at geostationary orbit Maria A. Shukhtina1 , Victor A. Sergeev1 , Nikita A. Stepanov2 1 Saint-Petersburg University 2 Arctic and Antarctic Research Institute, Saint-Petersburg, Russia maria-shukht@yandex.ru An empirical model of subrelativistic (40-275 keV) electron (SE) fluxes at the geostationary orbit is proposed. The model suggests that the main factor, driving the SE fluxes, is injections, which are superimposed on existing background. GOES-13 and GOES-15 observations in 2017 are used for the model, which is further tested on data from 2012-2016. Results for both spacecraft, separated by 4 hours in MLT, are similar. The background fluxes demonstrate alike MLT variation, depending on electron energy. The effect of injections is described by linear filters, with the positive MPB index [1] increment as the input. The model reproduces the main observational features of SE fluxes: midnight-dawn maximum, evening minimum and energy dispersion, confirming the drift mechanism as the basic governing factor. Comparison with observations gives rather good correlations (~0.7) for lower energy channels (50 and 75 keV), whereas for higher energies the correlations decrease. It confirms that just the fluxes with energies below 100-200 keV are really injected. The best correlations are manifested for moderate geomagnetic activity, and even supersubstorms demonstrate reasonable results, some of them manifesting the Kennel-Petschek saturation.
