Modular Exponentiation Identities in Number Theory: Classification and Minimal Exponents Subhraneel Dutta Srihan Dutta Abstract This paper investigates two specific modular exponentiation identities involving fixed integers. First, we determine the set of non-negative integers m satisfying a N ≡ a m (mod N) for a fixed N > 1 and all integers a, deriving the minimum such m. Second, we analyze the minimum positive integer n such that a mn ≡ a n (mod x) holds for a fixed x > 1 and all integers a, m. We provide explicit formulas for these minimal exponents in terms of the prime factorization exponents and the Carmichael function λ(·). 1 Introduction Modular exponentiation is a cornerstone of elementary number theory and cryptography. While Fermat’s Little Theorem and Euler’s Theorem provide conditions for a ϕ(n) ≡ 1 (mod n) when gcd(a, n) = 1, general identities valid for all integers a (including those not coprime to the modulus) require more careful analysis of prime power divisors.
