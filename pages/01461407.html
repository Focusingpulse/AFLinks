But this carries us beyond the horizon of our musical world of twelve keys ; for in B# , the top of the tonic E , we have reached our twelfth fifth , and it here coalesces with C of the seventh octave , and closes the circle . This is the way that all notes become alternately altered, either by commas and sharps in the upward genesis of scales , or by commas and flats in the downward Genesis , by the alternate powers of 3 and 5 . In the upward Genesis in this illustration, notes by the power of 5 serve in three keys , and those by the power of 3 serve in four keys . In the minors it is just the inverse on this by the Law of Duality . But no note serves for more than either three or four keys , as the case may be. It is in the fifth octave , however, that the note which is the distinctive mark of the masculine and feminine modes is generated.
