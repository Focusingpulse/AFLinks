More US Soldiers Committed Suicide than Died in Combat In 2010, for the second year in a row, more US soldiers killed themselves (468) than died in combat (462). "If you think you know the one thing that causes people to commit suicide, please let us know," General Peter Chiarelli told the Army Times, "because we don't know what it is." Suicide is a tragic but predictable human reaction to being asked to kill—and watch your friends be killed—for a war based on lies. Perhaps being forced to bag the mangled flesh of fellow soldiers could be another reason why some are committing suicide. Body-bagging: ever heard the term? Soldiers in the Marine Corps' Mortuary Affairs unit at Camp Al-Taqaddum, Iraq, are given this job of collecting and cataloguing the bodies of dead Marines. They sift through the soldiers' remains and put them in body bags which they then place in metal boxes and, in turn, into refrigerated units.
