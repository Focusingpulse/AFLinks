Antirelativity part 2: Does the Sagnac experiment disprove special relativity? Summary The second of Roger J Anderton 's anti-relativity sessions, hosted by Franklin Hu and given over to a recorded lecture by Albrecht Giese of Hamburg, "The Sagnac effect disproves Einstein's relativity". Getting the video to play with sound took some time. Anderton's own position was stated at the outset and repeated throughout: he does not think the question can be settled, because special relativity is not a precisely defined theory and is vague about how it treats the experiment — but others think otherwise, and Giese is one of them. Giese's lecture Giese's framing is that the Sagnac experiment decides between two versions of relativity. Einstein's is built on a principle — the laws of physics are the same in every inertial frame — and requires contraction and dilation to be defined so that the principle holds. Lorentz's is built on known physical processes: fields contract in motion, which Heaviside derived from Maxwell's theory and which explains the Michelson–Morley null result; and internal oscillations at light speed slow in motion, suspected around 1900 and, on his account, confirmed for the electron by Dirac and Schrödinger. The two give the same results, but Lorentz's mathematics is dramatically simpler. On Sagnac he began by conceding the usual finding. Viewed from outside — an observer watching the rotating apparatus — everything conforms to Einstein, which is why most published analyses reach that conclusion.
