Title Quantum Physics and Classical Realism Read in full Link to paper Author(s) David McGraw Keywords Quantum Physics , Classical, Realism No. of pages 9 Abstract First of all, the correct analysis of classical realism is set forth. It is then shown how this eliminates the obvious problems derived from the thesis that various facts and features exist only when measured. This concern includes the claim that classical realism stands or falls with classical physics. Next, the concern that there is no actual reality of the quantum world prior to measurement is answered. Overview David McGraw argues that quantum physics does not overturn classical realism, provided classical realism is correctly analysed. The paper is philosophical throughout, containing no equations and appealing to no experiment. His first move is to relocate what realism claims. People think of it as the thesis that things exist apart from being observed, but McGraw holds this is "at most secondary and derivative". What is primary is that things exist "within themselves as having their own natural being" — existence in concrete fact as opposed to existence as an object of awareness. Independence from observation follows from that, rather than constituting it. On this analysis the measurement problem largely dissolves. Granting that facts about the quantum world exist only when measured, the processes involved — application of instruments, interaction with the background, the resulting outcome — are "wholly within the order of natural being", and no perceiving subject is required at any stage. McGraw makes the point vividly: the apparatus could in principle be assembled by a series of explosions in a junkyard, and a photon arriving at its screen would produce the same result with nobody present. He then argues that an observing subject cannot be the source of what it observes, because impressions must exist before they can be received.
