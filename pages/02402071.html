Monirul Islam1,2 1 Ural Federal University named after the First President of Russia B.N. Yeltsin 2 University of Dhaka, Bangladesh mdmonirul.islam@urfu.ru The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into manufacturing is accelerating globally, yet this technological adoption is susceptible to risks from domestic instability and geopolitical tensions. This study investigates the relationship between industrial robot installation and manufacturing output in five leading AI-using nations, including China, Japan, Germany, South Korea, and the United States, while accounting for the regulating effects of country-specific internal conflict and geopolitical risks. Utilizing monthly data from January 2011 to December 2024, we employ a novel partial multi-frequency quantile correlation (PMFQC) and copula density machine learning (CDML) techniques to address the heterogeneity and non-stationarity inherent in the dataset. Our findings reveal that industrial robot capacity positively influences manufacturing output predominantly over the long term and short term, with largely mixed effects observed in the medium run. Crucially, the benefits of automation are more significantly controlled by geopolitical risks than by internal conflict. We examine the robustness of the findings using the copula density machine learning (CDML) approach.
