It develops the Aether Physics Model (APM) 1 INTRODUCTION 1 Abstract The Galactic center hosts a population of long, magnetized nonthermal radio filaments confined to the inner few hundred parsecs, with characteristic lengths of several to tens of parsecs, strong linear polarization, and morphologies that appear only weakly correlated with the distribution of dense gas. In the Aether Physics Model (APM) and Quantum Measurement Units (QMU), space is composed of rotating Aether units with intrinsic magnetic structure (Au ) and torsion (curl). In a companion work, the Schwarzschild light–deflection ledger was recast in QMU as a relation between twice the gravitational length density and a scalar torsion–energy density curl2 Au , defining a relativistic potential in terms of Aether density gradients. In this paper we apply that torsion ledger to the Galactic-center mass distribution and the observed radial distribution and spacing of nonthermal filaments. We introduce a dimensionless torsion–gravity index ΞGC (rGC ) built from curl2 Au and the enclosed mass profile, and show that the observed confinement of bright filaments to rGC ≲ O(102 ) pc is naturally interpreted as the region where ΞGC exceeds a threshold for Aether-unit torsion locking. We also formulate a simple QMU winding condition which relates the number and approximate angular spacing of filaments to the integrated torsion around the Galactic center. All ledgers are written in QMU, with conventional observational quantities entering only through dimensionless ratios and catalogued profiles. Keywords: QMU; APM; Aether; Galactic center; nonther
