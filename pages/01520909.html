Optimization of Passive Orbit with the Use of Gravity Maneuver

Title Optimization of Passive Orbit with the Use of Gravity Maneuver Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Joseph J Smulsky Keywords Computing, Sun system, orbits, spasecraft, Gravity Maneuver Published 2008 Journal Cosmic Research Volume 46 Number 5 No. of pages 9 Pages 456?464 Abstract A high-precision method of calculating gravitational interactions is applied in order to determine optimal trajectories. A number of problems, necessary for determination of optimal parameters at a launch of a spacecraft and during its flyby near celestial bodies, are considered. The spacecraft trajectory was determined by numerical integration of the equations of passive motion of the spacecraft and of the equations of motion for planets, the Sun, and the Moon. The optimal trajectory of the spacecraft approaching the Sun is determined by fitting its initial conditions. Overview This paper is out of the ordinary for this collection: it is conventional celestial mechanics, published in Cosmic Research — the English edition of Kosmicheskie Issledovaniya — by a researcher at the Institute of the Earth's Cryosphere of the Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences. It challenges no established theory. It applies Newtonian gravitation, competently, to a practical problem in mission design. The problem is how to put a spacecraft into a close orbit around the Sun as cheaply as possible. The author's proposal is a wholly passive flight: booster engines impart the departure velocity near the Earth, and every subsequent correction is made by planetary gravity alone, with no cruise engines carried at all. Dispensing with them reduces the launch mass and so the cost — but it imposes a demanding condition, which the paper states plainly: "Only in the case when a realized trajectory coincides with that calculated in advance, one can abandon the cruise engines."
