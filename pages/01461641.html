The triplet B, D, F, has been called the imperfect triad , because in it the two diatonic semitones , B-C and E-F, and the two minor thirds which they constitute, come together in this so-called imperfect fifth . But instead of deserving any name indicating imperfection, this most interesting triad is the Diatonic germ of the chromatic chord , and of the chromatic system of chords. Place this triad to precede the tonic chord of the key of C major, and there are two semitonic progressions . Place it to precede the tonic chord of the key of F# major, and there are three semitonic progressions . Again, if we place it to precede the tonic chord of the key of A minor, there are two semitonic progressions ; but make it precede the tonic chord of E? minor, and there are three semitonic progressions .
