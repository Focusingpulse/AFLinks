GC2026-STP075 Investigation of the dependence of the quality of forecasting space weather factors on the position and length of data sets using the SIBYL system Mark V. Semenov1 , Irina N. Myagkova2 , Roman D. Vladimirov2 , Vladimir R. Shirokiy2 , Sergey A. Dolenko2 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University 2 Skobeltsyn Institute of Nuclear Physics, Lomonosov Moscow State University (SINP MSU), Russia marksemenov979@gmail.com Space weather affects the functioning of a large number of systems, such as space equipment, power lines, radar systems, etc. Therefore, its forecasting has a high level of relevance. This study considers the prediction of the geomagnetic Dst index, of the hourly flux, and of the daily fluence of relativistic electrons with energies E>2 MeV in geostationary orbit. Machine learning (ML) methods can effectively solve the problem of space weather forecasting, and they are widely used in this field. It was previously noted that the prediction errors of such models depend on the position and size of the training and test datasets. This property is primarily explained by the changing phases of the solar cycle, and it makes it difficult to compare the effectiveness of various forecasting methods. This study presents the results of using the batch mode implemented by the authors for the SIBYL forecasting system they created earlier. The name SIBYL originates from the Russian language abbreviation for an Intelligent, Easy-to-Train Adaptive Laboratory Interrelation Research System.
