Published 2010 No. of pages 5 Abstract In a recent article, a single tired light mechanism, based in the interaction between electromagnetic waves, has been proposed for explaining both redshifts: cosmological (without expansion of the universe) and intrinsic. A second paper specifies that said interaction would be the scattering. This article is to reinforce and clarify the whole idea. Overview Cosmological and Intrinsic Redshifts is a five-page paper by José Francisco García Juliá of Valencia, dated 19 November 2010 and revised the following month. It is the third in a short series, consolidating two earlier notes. (The wiki's infobox left the author field empty; the paper carries his name and address.) Its proposal is a single tired-light mechanism accounting for two things at once: the cosmological redshift, in a static universe with no expansion, and the "intrinsic" redshift attributed by Arp and others to quasars in excess of their distance. The mechanism is scattering of light by other electromagnetic radiation. García Juliá argues that since every body emits and absorbs thermal radiation in equal measure, intergalactic space must contain a thermal radiation field, and that the works of Eddington, Regener and Nernst on the temperature of interstellar space put it at 2.7 K — so the microwave background is that equilibrium field rather than a relic of the Big Bang. Light crossing intergalactic space then "opens a linear path (without any change of direction) through the microwaves of the CMBR scattering them", losing energy exponentially. The analogy offered is the radiation length of fast electrons, E = E 0 e −x/X 0 , with the photon "acting like a particle of 'effective mass' hν/c²" scattering microwaves as an electron scatters virtual photons.
