Or will aliens find the same ones? was a live session presented by David de Hilster on 5 November 2022 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Would intelligent aliens on other worlds find the same physics and cosmological constants? Or are they arbitrary? Join this fascinating discussion live with other like-minded critical thinkers from the CNPS and around the world! Summary A panel discussion led by David de Hilster on whether the fundamental constants are arbitrary, framed by a question he kept returning to: at an intergalactic science conference, which of our results could we present without embarrassment, and which would we hide? His starting point was the Wikipedia list of fundamental physical constants, and in particular its own admission that there is no single correct way of enumerating them and that which are taken as fundamental and which as derived is a matter of arbitrary choice. He was cheerful about where his own embarrassment would lie: the gravitational constant, the speed of light and Planck's constant he could imagine anyone finding; the Yukawa couplings for quarks and leptons, the two parameters of the Higgs potential, the four of the quark mixing matrix and the three gauge coupling constants he would keep under the table. He recommended Alexander Unzicker 's The Higgs Fake and an interview on Unzicker's channel with a Nobel laureate who claimed to have reduced the constants to four, and who grew visibly uncomfortable as the questions became basic.
