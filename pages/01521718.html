The Great Confusion: Wave or Particle? Book Author John-Erik Persson Language English Subject Ether theory; the wave and particle models; criticism of special relativity and of the Copenhagen interpretation Published 2011 Publisher Lulu Enterprises Media type Paperback Pages 48 ISBN 978-1471010804 The Great Confusion: Wave or Particle? is a 48-page book of 2011 by the Swedish electrical engineer John-Erik Persson , self-published through Lulu. It is a short statement of the position Persson has argued across some fifty papers recorded on this wiki: that the wave–particle problem is not a discovery about nature but an avoidable confusion, produced by applying each of the two models to the wrong thing. Its central move is an inversion of the usual assignment. Light is treated as waves only, and the ether as particles only. Persson holds that once each model is applied where it belongs, no duality is required, and a series of results normally taken as evidence for relativity and for quantum indeterminacy can be reinterpreted mechanically. Publisher's description The following is the description issued with the book, and was formerly the whole of this page's text. This book presents a solution to a great enigma in modern physics, namely the wave or particle confusion. Light is here described by the wave model only and ether by the particle model only. It is here explained how one planet can move without retardation from the ether, but nevertheless can the ether push two bodies towards each other. An explanation is also given to the fact that the sum of two light waves can be zero without violating the rule of energy conservation. Many of Einstein's and Bohr's ideas are refuted, but Newton's, Maxwell's and Planck's ideas are accepted. Many errors in the interpretations of fundamental phenomena are revealed.
