Saturday Science Chat sponsored by the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Dennis Allen , Bob and James Keene , beginning on gyroscopes and ending on what is actually in a particle accelerator beam. Gyroscopes Bob had raised a Buckminster Fuller piece explaining why a gyroscope pushed one way moves at right angles, and Allen added two experimentalists who report that classical mechanics does not give the right numbers for gyroscopic motion — one in Kyrgyzstan working with a precision gyroscope and a stopwatch and no grant, and an engineer, now dead, some of whose experimental material is still online. Allen expects it to disappear in due course, remarking drily that the modern way is not to refute people but to take down their videos after they die. The room agreed on what a gyroscope does — hold its axis in space, which is why spacecraft use them — and disagreed about why. Allen's account: in free space with no fields, striking the axis produces transient behaviour and the axis settles fixed again, though not necessarily parallel to the old one. Hu offered a mechanical picture from a rolling bicycle wheel, which resists being pushed over because it can only roll straight while upright. Keene's first answer — that the translation of angular momentum into linear momentum opposes the gradient of the gravitational potential — he immediately called unsatisfying, and said it needed more thought. That produced Hu's question of the session: where is the energy of a bullet in flight?
