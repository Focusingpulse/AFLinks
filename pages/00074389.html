Removing the two gauge freedoms of that surface, the freedom to relabel its points and the freedom to rescale its metric, by the Faddeev–Popov method produces a nilpotent BRST charge QB, and its cohomology H(QB) is the physical spectrum of the string. The condition Q2 B = 0 fixes the number of spacetime dimensions at twenty-six for the bosonic string and ten for the superstring. At one loop the surface is a torus, and its modular invariance chooses the allowed spectrum, the GSO projection of the superstring, and the charge lattices the theory can carry. The same degeneration limit gives unitarity, since the amplitude factorizes on physical intermediate states. When that intermediate state is a massless one emitted into the vacuum, its coefficient is a tadpole, and requiring the tadpole to vanish is what picks out an allowed background. For the unoriented open string this condition, equivalently the cancellation of gauge anomalies, forces the gauge group SO(32). These results, and the tree and loop amplitudes built from them, make up the perturbative theory. Perturbation theory is limited to weak coupling, and reaching strong coupling relies on a set of non-perturbative probes that supersymmetry protects.
