MolPhot2026-main025 Increased optical coherence storage time using dark excitons detected by photon echo Roman S. Nazarov1 , Yuri V. Kapitonov1 1 St Petersburg University st062833@student.spbu.ru One of the key challenges in optical information processing is increasing the coherence lifetime [1]. We demonstrate a spin-dependent three-pulse stimulated photon echo (SPE) protocol that enables the transfer of optical coherence of excitons into long-lived dark states in a single InGaAs/GaAs quantum well under the application of a weak transverse magnetic field. In this study, using time-resolved four-wave mixing at T ≈ 1.5 K, we demonstrate the transition from an exponential decay of the SPE amplitude in the absence of a field to a long-lived regime and oscillations under the application of a field in the Voigt geometry. In the absence of a field, the bright exciton lifetime was T₁ᵇ ≈ 15 ± 1 ps; In the optimal weak field (B ≈ 0.25 T), the maximum long-lived response corresponds to a dark exciton lifetime of T₁ᵈ ≈ 170 ± 30 ps, ​which is an order of magnitude greater than T₁ᵇ. The protocol is universal and scalable to systems with even higher T₁ᵈ, such as quantum dots. This work was performed using equipment at the St. Petersburg State University Nanophotonics Resource Center.
