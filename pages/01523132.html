We provide experimental observations of Milky Way Galaxy and Andromeda Galaxy and explain them in frames of WUM; concentrate on observations of Milky Way Centre by NASA and Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration and analyze them in frames of WUM; discuss experimental results for Fermi Bubbles and propose explanation of them by WUM. Summary The fourth of Vladimir S Netchitailo 's eight-part series, applying the World-Universe Model to the centre of our own galaxy and to the observations made there by NASA and the Event Horizon Telescope. Ian J Cowan hosted. Answering the objection Netchitailo opened with a section composed in response to the previous session's questions, and led with a concession Nick Percival had suggested he make at the outset: the reason many physicists feel dark matter does not exist is that they take the construct to have been created erroneously from a false analysis of rotation curves in the outer parts of spirals — and in his own model there is no need for dark matter to explain rotation curves at all. He recorded the exchange approvingly, including Harry Ricker 's remark that the word dark is the canary in the coal mine, screaming that something is fundamentally wrong with modern cosmology, and Percival's that the model is empirically driven rather than mathematical manipulation. Cowan's question from the previous week — whether dark matter is conceived as analogous in its constitution to ordinary matter — he called the perfect one, and the talk is largely an answer to it.
