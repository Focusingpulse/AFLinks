GC2026-SGG057 Deep fluid systems of the lithosphere based on seismic and geochemical data Mikhail V. Rodkin1,2 1 Institute of Oil and Gas Problems of the Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Institute of Earthquake Prediction Theory and Mathematical Geophysics, Russian Academy of Sciences rodkin@mitp.ru The deep fluid flow regime is important for understanding seismicity and the processes of oil and ore genesis. A more complete picture of the fluid regime can be obtained using a combination of seismicity and geochemical data, particularly the ME trace element composition of various fluid types. Let's consider the results obtained using these two types of data. According to seismic data [1], earthquakes in the depth range of approximately 20 to 80-90 km are characterized by shallower earthquake depths based on the seismic moment solution than based on the hypocenter position. That is, in this depth range, the seismic process tends to develop upward, which can be explained by the presence of low-density fluid at the source, with a tendency to break upward along the resulting fault system. Conversely, for shallower earthquakes and for events deeper than 90-100 km, the sources predominantly develop downward; this can be attributed to the increase in temperature with depth. Similar evidence is provided by the trend in changing depths of weak earthquakes in the vicinity of a strong earthquake [2].
