Fast Radio Bursts from the Collapse of Strange Star Crusts 1 2 Yue Zhang1, Jin-Jun Geng1,2, and Yong-Feng Huang1,2 School of Astronomy and Space Science, Nanjing University, Nanjing 210023, Peopleʼs Republic of China Key Laboratory of Modern Astronomy and Astrophysics (Nanjing University), Ministry of Education, Peopleʼs Republic of China Received 2018 January 16; revised 2018 March 23; accepted 2018 March 28; published 2018 May 10 Abstract Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are transient radio sources at cosmological distances. No counterparts in other bands have been observed for non-repeating FRBs. Here we suggest the collapse of strange star (SS) crusts as a possible origin for FRBs. SSs, which are composed of almost equal numbers of u, d, and s quarks, may be encapsulated by a thin crust of normal hadronic matter. When a SS accretes matter from its environment, the crust becomes heavier and heavier. It may ﬁnally collapse, leading to the release of a large amount of magnetic energy and plenty of electron/ positron pairs on a very short timescale. Electron/positron pairs in the polar cap region of the SS can be accelerated to relativistic velocities, streaming along the magnetic ﬁeld lines to form a thin shell. FRBs are produced by coherent emission from these electrons when the shell is expanding. Basic characteristics of observed FRBs can be explained in our model.
