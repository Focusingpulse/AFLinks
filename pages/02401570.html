GC2026-PERM032 Comparative evolution and statistics of the components of the total geomagnetic energy for three models based on direct measurements Sergey Starchenko1 , Svetlana Yakovleva1 1 Pushkov Institute of Terrestrial Magnetism, Ionosphere and Radiowave Propagation of the Russian Academy of Sciences sstarchenko@mail.ru For our study, we used the most popular models of geomagnetic field evolution that are freely available. The longest-term model is BIGMUDIh.1 [1] for 1400-1900, followed by GUFM1 [2] for 1590-1990, and the shortest, but at the same time the most reliable, thanks to the use of satellite data, is COV_OBS.x2 [3] for 1840-2020. Using the corresponding Gaussian coefficients from these models, we obtain the integral energy components as a function of time. Integration is made from the mantle/core boundary to a sphere located several thousand kilometers from the Earth's surface to guarantee sufficient accuracy. We denote E as the total energy, E1 as its dipole component, E2 as its quadrupole component, etc. We used a spherical harmonic expansion up to the 10th degree for all models. A comparison of the evolution of E for the GUFM1 model showed good agreement with the evolution of E for the BIGMUDIh.1 model only since the early 19th century, as well as for the COV_OBS.x2 model since the early 20th century. For the pair of BIGMUDIh.1 and COV_OBS.x2 models, only a small range of agreement is observed at the beginning of the 20th century. The evolution of the dipole component E1 is mutually consistent for all models only in the range of mid-18th century to the ear
