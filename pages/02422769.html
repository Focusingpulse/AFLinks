👤 Rudolf Steiner · 📅 17 September 1912 · archived # 2,422,769
, Basel Translated by Conrad Mainzer Lecture Three [ 1 ] In the last lecture we pointed out the significance of the fact that the Gospel of St. Mark begins by introducing the grand figure of John the Baptist, who is contrasted in a marked manner with that of Christ Jesus Himself. If we allow Mark's Gospel to influence us in all its simplicity, we receive a significant impression of John the Baptist; but only when we consider the Baptist against the background of spiritual science does he appear, so to speak, in his full greatness.
