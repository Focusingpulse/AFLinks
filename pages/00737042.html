Riemann sums of sin x and cos x Pawel Piskorz∗ Abstract Riemann integrals of the trigonometric functions sin x and cos x have been computed directly from the appropriate Riemann sums. Introduction Let us remind the two fundamental theorems of integral calculus. Let F(x) be the area under the plot of f(x) between the line x = a, x coordinate line and the Ox axis. By looking at the Figure 1. we can see that lim ∆x→0 F(x + ∆x) − F(x) = lim ∆x→0 f(x + ∆x)∆x (1) Dividing the above equation by ∆x we arrive at F ′ (x) = lim ∆x→0 F(x + ∆x) − F(x) ∆x = f(x) (2) F ′ (x) is the derivative of F(x). F(x) is being called the primary function of F ′ (x) = f(x). The first theorem of integral calculus states that the area derivative F ′ (x) is equal to the function f(x) under the plot of which the area F(x) is being computed.
