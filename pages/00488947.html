Pi’s Irrationality: Geometry and Logic Timothy W. Jones March 22, 2026 Abstract We give a proof of π’s irrationality that references principles of set the￾ory and cardinality within the context of basic geometric properties of a circle. Introduction There have been many proofs of the irrationality of π [2, 4, 7]. The first is attributed to Lambert. It’s long and complicated. In 1947 Niven gave an entirely different shockingly short half a page proof [9, 10]. Still his proof made various unacknowledged (hence obscure) references to the techniques of Hermite in his transcendence of e proof [8]; difficult. In both proofs the natural connection of π to the circle is quite remote. The proof here makes this connection. It is geometric in nature. Other geometric proofs of note are Sondow’s proof of the irrationality of e [11] and Hardy’s of the square root of five [5]. These might be thought of as curiosities, not destined for standard analysis textbooks. But, I suggest, π’s origins in geometry might make a geometric proof of its irrationality more natural and attractive (classy) to students and mathematicians. Of course all these words are premised on the proof being correct. It uses an atypical argument.
