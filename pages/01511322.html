Hendershot was working on something completely different. He wanted to solve a "navigation" problem and make improvements to the so called "induction compass". He also wanted to make a motor for his son's toy airplane. You can study the "induction compass" material as background. That device, invented by M.M. Titterington -- patent #1,770,245 (and some others) circa 1930 was used by Charles Lindbergh when he crossed the Atlantic. The wind during flight is used to SPIN a shaft and generate a small amount of current to move a needle left or right depending upon your compass heading. This tells the pilot which way to go. This generator used the earth's magnetic field to create power (from a bit of wind). Hendershot figured out how to do this w/o wind.
