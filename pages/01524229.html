Title The Structure of the Visible Universe Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Arnold G Gulko Keywords Big Bang , Structure, Universe Published 2008 No. of pages 3 Abstract In issue #62 of Infinite Energy magazine (2005) in an article entitled "Two Competing Cosmological Theories" (pages 31-39) this writer established that the Big Bang theory did not fit the facts whereas his 25 year old Universe Cycle theory did. At the start of that article the respective theories were summarized, and at the end of the article it was pointed out that the characteristics of the gamma-ray bursts now being received correlated remarkably well with the overall structure of the universe. To briefly summarize the Universe Cycle theory, the cosmos initially contained an infinite space filled with widely spaced apart supermassive black holes, and our visible universe came into being when these holes exploded in gamma-ray bursts of enormous power. Overview The Structure of the Visible Universe is a three-page paper by Arnold G Gulko , dated 2008. It follows an earlier article, "Two Competing Cosmological Theories," published in Infinite Energy issue 62 (2005), pages 31–39, and develops one argument from it. The framework is what Gulko calls the Universe Cycle theory, which he dates to about 1980. On this account the cosmos initially contained an infinite space filled with widely spaced supermassive black holes left over from previous universes. These exploded one at a time as gamma-ray bursts "of enormous power," starting in our region of space, with the zone of explosions expanding outward to form everything we now observe. He offers two general objections to the Big Bang. A single explosion is "inconsistent with everything we know about explosions," whereas a sequence of separate events naturally produces widely spaced galaxies with little matter between them.
