Log in I forgot my password Sympathetic Vibratory Physics &raquo; Eugene Burkins Page actions PDF Print Share Send link Eugene Burkins In 1900, a brilliant young inventor named Eugene Burkins filed a patent for an automatic machine gun that met military performance standards. It was a remarkable innovation—one that could’ve reshaped firearm technology. But Eugene Burkins was a Black man living in Jim Crow America. Despite his invention’s promise, doors remained closed. Funding, recognition, opportunity—denied, simply because of who he was. Born in New Orleans in 1877, Burkins moved to Chicago seeking a better life. When his invention failed to gain traction, he had no choice but to set aside his dreams and run a restaurant to survive. His genius was shelved—not by failure, but by racism. He later moved to Detroit, where he died in 1929, penniless and forgotten by the very system his ideas could have strengthened. His story isn’t just about a lost invention. It’s about how many great minds have been silenced, erased from the pages of history because of prejudice. Today, as we strive to uncover and honor the forgotten builders of our world, we say Eugene Burkins’ name with respect. He wasn’t just an inventor—he was a symbol of brilliance denied, and resilience unbroken. History may forget, but we remember.
