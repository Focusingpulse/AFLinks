1. Church Leadership and Structure Catholicism: The head of the Catholic Church is the Pope, who is considered infallible in matters of faith and morals when speaking ex cathedra (officially). The Catholic Church has a strict hierarchy: the Pope, cardinals, bishops, and priests. Orthodoxy: In Orthodoxy, there is no single head for all churches. Each Orthodox Church (e.g., Russian, Greek) has its own patriarch or archbishop, and the churches are independent of each other. Patriarchs are considered "first among equals" but do not have the same authority as the Pope. 2. Theology and Dogma Catholicism: An important dogma is the teaching of the "filioque," which states that the Holy Spirit proceeds from both the Father and the Son. This was one of the reasons for the Great Schism (1054). Catholics also believe in the doctrine of purgatory and that the Pope can declare dogmas. Orthodoxy: Orthodoxy teaches that the Holy Spirit proceeds only from the Father. It does not have a doctrine of purgatory. Orthodoxy emphasizes the mysterious nature of faith and is less dogmatically structured. 3. Worship and Liturgy Catholicism: Catholic masses are more standardized and were conducted in Latin for centuries (now often in local languages). The liturgy focuses heavily on the Eucharist. Orthodoxy: Orthodox Christians also recognize seven sacraments, but the approach to them is less juridical than in Catholicism.
