John M. Bockris Born ( 1923-01-05 ) January 5, 1923 Residence Bryan, TX, United States Nationality Australian / USA Known for Chemistry , Electrochemistry , Cold Fusion , New Energy , Paranormal , Anomalies Scientific career Fields Professor of Electrochemistry John O'Malley Bockris, an Australian electrochemist, coined the term "hydrogen economy" in the 1970s. Bockris' hydrogen economy describes a system in which hydrogen would be used to transport energy from renewable sources over large distances and store it in large amounts. In 1962, Bockris first proposed a plan to supply US cities with solar-derived energy via hydrogen. In 1971, he published the first article on hydrogen in a referred journal. Bockris published Energy: The Solar-Hydrogen Alternative in 1975, one of the first detailed description of what a solar-hydrogen economy would look like. Mainstream career Bockris (1923–2013) was one of the significant figures of twentieth-century electrochemistry, and the great majority of his work is entirely conventional. Born in Johannesburg and raised in England, he took his doctorate at Imperial College London, where he ran a research group in the late 1940s — Martin Fleischmann was among the graduate students who joined its discussions. He moved to the University of Pennsylvania in 1953, then to the Flinders University of South Australia, and to Texas A&M in 1978, where he held a Distinguished Professorship until his retirement in 1997. His textbook Modern Electrochemistry , written with A. K. N. Reddy and first published in 1970, became one of the discipline's standard works and remains in print. He published several hundred papers across five decades on electrode kinetics, the structure of the electrical double layer, electrocatalysis, corrosion and fuel cells, and supervised a very large number of doctoral students.
