495 Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 24, No. 3, pp. 495–512, 2010 0892-3310/10 HISTORICAL PERSPECTIVE The 1907 Psychokinetic Experiments of Professor Filippo Bottazzi antonio giuditta Department of Biological Sciences, University “Federico II” Via Mezzocannone 8, Naples 80134 Italy giuditta@unina.it Reprint requests to giuditta@unina.it Abstract—If we define the Thomas Prejudice (Doubting Thomas) as the habit of trusting only your own sensory capacities, Professor Filippo Bottazzi was imbued with it when rumors of the wondrous psychokinetic (PK) performanc￾es of Eusapia Palladino reached his ear at the turn of the last century. Aiming to find out for himself, he persuaded distinguished professors at the Royal University of Naples to join him in a series of sessions held with Eusapia at the Institute of Physiology he directed. The outstanding data obtained in eight such sessions were masterfully described in a book Bottazzi published one century ago (1909). The main interest of these observations regards the instru￾mental recording of PK movements Eusapia learned to perform. Most notably, she succeeded in simultaneously pressing two electric keys, one with her natu￾ral arm, the other with her anomalous limb. These and related observations convinced Bottazzi that PK events required Eusapia to extend and operate an anomalous limb endowed with sensory and motor features comparable with those of her natural arms. Such anomalous limb was controlled by the same brain/mind that modulated her natural arms.
