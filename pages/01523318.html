Leslie C. Case Born ( 1930-09-11 ) September 11, 1930 Tulsa, Oklahoma, United States Died July 15, 2010 (2010-07-15) (aged 79) Newfields, New Hampshire, United States Residence Newfields, NH, United States Nationality USA Known for Cold Fusion Scientific career Fields Chemical engineering Leslie C. Case (September 11, 1930 – July 15, 2010) was an American chemical engineer, inventor, and independent researcher who, in the later part of his career, became known within the Cold Fusion community for a gas-phase process he called "catalytic fusion." Biography Case was born in Tulsa, Oklahoma, on September 11, 1930. He spent his career as a chemical engineer and inventor, holding patents in the field of polymer chemistry; his work on polyether polyols was the subject of the reported patent-office case Case v. CPC International, Inc. (U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, 1984). Case had a broad range of interests over his life, including gemstone cutting, the recovery of shale oil, the growing of grapes and production of wine in Australia, and alternative energy. He died at his home in Newfields, New Hampshire, on July 15, 2010, at the age of 79. Education Several degrees, including a Doctorate of Science — Massachusetts Institute of Technology Work In his later years Case turned to low-energy nuclear reaction research, which he preferred to describe as "catalytic fusion" rather than "cold fusion." His approach used a sealed container holding a precious-metal catalyst supported on activated carbon together with deuterium gas. In demonstrations he loaded activated carbon, including coconut-shell carbon, with roughly 0.1 to 0.5 percent by weight of a precious metal, reporting that palladium at about 0.4 percent worked best. When the sealed cell was pressurized with deuterium (on the order of a few atmospheres) and heated to roughly 200 °C, Case reported excess heat and the accumulation of helium-4, which he interpreted as a signature of deuterium-deuterium fusion.
