Title Explanation of Dynamical Biefeld-Brown Effect from the Standpoint of ZPF Field Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Takaaki Musha Keywords Electromagnetic propulsion, zero-point field, electrogravitics, artificial gravity, high-voltage capacitors, Biefeld- Brown effect Published 2008 No. of pages 6 Abstract The research group of the HONDA R&D Institute observed a weight reduction by applying alternating electric field to a capacitor. This phenomenon, which is called the “dynamical Biefeld-Brown effect”, cannot be explained within the framework of conventional physics. From the standpoint of ZPF field, the author tries to explain this phenomenon as an interaction between the vacuum electromagnetic zero-point field and the high potential electric field. By theoretical analysis, it is considered that the interaction of zero-point vacuum fluctuations with high potential electric field can induce a greater momentum for the dielectric material, which would produce sufficient artificial gravity to propel space vehicles. Overview Takaaki Musha proposes that the Biefeld–Brown effect — the thrust or weight change reported for high-voltage capacitors with dielectric fillings, first announced by Thomas Townsend Brown in 1956 — arises from an interaction between the applied field and the electromagnetic zero-point field of the vacuum. The specific target is a 1996 experiment by a research group at the Honda R&D Institute, which reported that a 62 g dielectric-glass capacitor immersed in insulating oil lost weight when energised, and lost roughly 2.8 times more weight under 8 kV AC pulses than under 18 kV DC. Musha's route runs through the ZPF inertia programme of Haisch, Rueda and Puthoff, in which a particle's inertial mass arises from its interaction with vacuum fluctuations. If the effective high-frequency cutoff of the ZPF spectrum is shifted by an applied field, the argument goes, the inertial mass shifts with it. He models the dielectric's electrons as driven dipoles
