Vera Hutchins

· archived # 1,468,813
Oklahoma, Summer 1934** By the summer of 1934, life in Cimarron County had become almost unimaginable. Dust storms swept across the plains so often and with such force that keeping children in a schoolhouse was becoming dangerous. Fine dust slipped through every crack in the walls, covering desks, books, and lungs alike. Cases of dust pneumonia were rising, and several local schools had already been forced to close. According to the story, one teacher refused to let the storms end her students' education. Her name was Vera Hutchins. With no safe classroom available, she turned to the only place she knew could protect her students—a storm cellar on a nearby farm, twelve feet below the ground. Each morning, Vera unlocked the cellar doors, and her twelve students climbed down the wooden steps into their unusual classroom. Once everyone was inside, she closed the heavy doors above them, shutting out the choking dust that filled the Oklahoma sky. The cellar slowly became a real school. A map hung on one wall. A blackboard rested against old wooden shelves. Every morning, Vera carried a crate of books downstairs and brought them back up at the end of the day. Kerosene lamps placed in the corners filled the room with a warm glow as the children studied reading, arithmetic, geography, and history while dust storms raged overhead.
