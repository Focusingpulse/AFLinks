Title Introduction to Error Analysis & Interpretation Author(s) Leslee A Kulba Keywords Error analysis, uncertainty, systematic error, significant figures Published 2010 Journal Electric Spacecraft Journal Volume 48 Pages 32-34 Introduction to Error Analysis & Interpretation is a three-page article by Leslee A Kulba , published in 2010 in issue 48 of the Electric Spacecraft Journal , the journal's final double issue. It is the tenth and last part of the handbook on perpetual motion and its testing that Kulba, the journal's technical editor, contributed to that issue, and it closes both the practical sequence and the journal itself. The text of the article has not been recoverable, and this page describes its subject and its place in the series rather than its argument. The subject Two kinds of error Random errors scatter repeated readings around a value; they are reduced by averaging, and the scatter itself estimates them. Systematic errors displace every reading in the same direction; averaging does nothing to them, they leave no trace in the scatter, and they are found only by changing something about the method — a different instrument, a different technique, a different laboratory. A result can therefore be highly precise and entirely wrong, and the whole of the preceding series is concerned with sources of the second kind: probe loading, average-responding meters, thermoelectric offsets, mismatch, apparent power read as real. Propagating uncertainty An uncertainty on a measured quantity implies an uncertainty on anything computed from it. For a sum or difference the absolute uncertainties combine in quadrature; for a product or quotient the relative ones do. Only the first of these is peculiar to this subject, and it is peculiar in a way that matters a great deal. An over-unity claim is a claim about a difference : output minus input.
