kimo2026-main160 Alignment without Alliance: Explaining Russo–Chinese Cooperation through Asymmetric Threat Perceptions Dayea Kim1 1 St. Petersburg State University, Russia kimdayea71@gmail.com Section: Issues of International security Keywords: alliance, alignment, threat perception, Korean Peninsular, security coordination This paper examines the mechanisms of Russo–Chinese cooperation in the absence of a formal military alliance, focusing on the role of asymmetric threat perceptions. Despite growing strategic coordination, Russia and China have deliberately avoided alliance commitments, particularly in the context of regional security challenges such as the Korean Peninsula. The study argues that this pattern can be explained through threat asymmetry: while both states perceive the United States as the primary external threat, the intensity, scope, and immediacy of this perception differ between Moscow and Beijing. Drawing on the concept of threat asymmetry, the paper demonstrates how divergent security priorities generate coordination rather than alliance formation. Using qualitative analysis of diplomatic practices, security discourses, and policy coordination in Northeast Asia, the study shows that asymmetric threat perceptions allow Russia and China to align selectively on security issues while preserving strategic autonomy. This form of alignment enables joint responses to U.S.-led pressure without binding institutional obligations.
