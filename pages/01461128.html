Orgonomy

· archived # 1,461,128
Orgonomy is a field of study developed by Austrian psychoanalyst Wilhelm Reich in the 1930s. It is based on the concept of "orgone," which Reich described as a universal life force or cosmic energy that permeates all living and non-living matter. He believed this energy was fundamental to physical, emotional, and even cosmic processes. Orgonomy encompasses Reich's theories and practices related to this energy, blending elements of psychoanalysis, biology, physics, and natural science. At its core, orgonomy explores how orgone energy functions in nature and the human body. Reich claimed it could be observed and measured—visually, thermally, and with tools like electroscopes—and that it played a key role in health and disease. He developed techniques like "medical orgone therapy" to treat emotional and physical conditions by addressing blockages of this energy, often linked to what he called "muscular armoring" (chronic tension in the body reflecting repressed emotions). He also invented devices like the "orgone accumulator," a box meant to concentrate orgone energy for therapeutic use. The field extends beyond medicine into areas like sociology, meteorology (e.g., his "cloudbuster" for weather modification), and cosmology, aiming to explain natural phenomena through orgone’s interactions.
