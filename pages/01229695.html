`` FROM THEISM TO INFORMATION: AN EXPLORATION OF REALITY'S FUNDAMENTAL OPERATING SYSTEM Driven by Dean A. Kulik July 2025 Part I: Deconstructing the Transcendent Ruler: The Immanent God of Process The intellectual journey that begins with the deconstruction of established religious frameworks often leads to profound questions about the fundamental nature of reality. The intuition that any divine principle or controlling entity must itself be subject to the rules of the system it governs represents a sophisticated philosophical critique. It moves beyond the idea of an external, arbitrary monarch and toward the concept of an immanent, lawful operating system. This line of inquiry finds a powerful and articulate counterpart in the 20th-century philosophical movement known as Process Theology, which offers a model of divinity that is not a ruler over the system, but an integral and evolving participant within it. The Classical Theistic Model and its Limitations To appreciate the radical shift proposed by Process Theology, one must first understand the model it seeks to replace: classical theism. This is the dominant conception of God in much of Western philosophy and theology, characterized by a set of attributes often referred to as the "omni" properties. In this view, God is understood to be omnipotent (all￾powerful), omniscient (all-knowing), and omnibenevolent (all-good). Crucially, this God is also described as immutable (incapable of change), impassible (unaffected by the world), and transcendent (existing wholly outside of time and space).1 This model posits a deity who is a unilateral creator, bringing the universe into existence ex nihilo (from nothing) and governing it through coercive power.1 God stands apart from creation, a perfect and static being whose relationship with the world is fundamentally one-way.
