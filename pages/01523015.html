Glenn Borchardt Born ( 1942-07-28 ) July 28, 1942 (age 84) Residence Berkeley, CA, United States Nationality USA Known for Relativity , Ether , Aether , Gravity , Neomechanics , Cosmology , Infinity , Infinite Universe Theory , Scientific Philosophy , Univironmental determinism , Scientific Worldview , Universal Cycle Theory Scientific career Fields Author , Philosopher , Scientist Glenn Borchardt (born 28 July 1942) is an American geologist, scientific philosopher and Director of the Progressive Science Institute in Berkeley, California. After a career of more than fifty years in earth science — in soil mineralogy, soil tectonics and seismic hazard assessment, producing over 480 scientific reports — he became known for a systematic philosophical critique of modern physics and cosmology. His programme rests on a single move: the replacement of the unprovable assumption of finity with the equally unprovable assumption of infinity . From it he derives neomechanics (classical mechanics adapted to an infinite universe), univironmental determinism (the doctrine that what happens to any portion of the universe depends equally on the infinite matter in motion inside and outside it), and Infinite Universe Theory (the cosmological alternative to the Big Bang). He set out the foundations in The Ten Assumptions of Science (2004) and The Scientific Worldview (2007), and the applications with Stephen J Puetz in Universal Cycle Theory (2011) and alone in Infinite Universe Theory (2017). Unusually among the critics documented on this wiki, Borchardt's objection to relativity is not mathematical but ontological. He argues that Einstein committed a single philosophical error — the objectification of motion , treating motion as though it were a thing rather than something that matter does — and that this error, analogous to the old caloric theory of heat, has held physics back for over a century.
