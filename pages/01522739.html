David Rosignoli Born 1972 (age 53–54) Residence Chalfont, PA, United States Known for Electromagnetism , Anomalies , Magnetic Propulsion , New Energy David Rosignoli (born 1972) is an American electrical engineer, a licensed professional engineer with more than fourteen years' practice as of 2011, and an experimentalist with an interest in anomalous and unconventional science and technology. He is Director of the Research Advisory Group for the Tesla Science Foundation, a member of the NTIC, and assistant organiser of the Tesla Conference. Work Rosignoli's contribution catalogued here is experimental rather than theoretical, which distinguishes it from most of this collection. Experimental Results in Measuring Atmospheric Electricity (2011), written with Michael R Treat and presented at the 18th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference, reports a measurement of the current drawable from the fair-weather atmospheric electric field. A metallised foil balloon lofted a 300-foot carbon-fibre tether, and a Keithley picoammeter isolated behind a remotely operated contactor recorded 0.8 μA at 275 feet — consistent with the roughly 1 μA that C. L. Stong had reported at 300 feet in Scientific American in 1974. The paper is careful about method: the tether was kept off the ground on a varnished platform, the meter ran from a battery through an inverter, and readings were taken with the experimenters clear of the apparatus. Its practical findings — that an uninsulated carbon-fibre tether performs as well as the insulated wire earlier workers used, and that the splayed five-micron filaments of an uncoated tow make an efficient, very light collector — are of use to anyone repeating the measurement.
