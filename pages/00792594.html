The absolute encryption algorithm consists in communicating a truly random physical band of randomness to its correspondent beforehand and encrypting the original message with this stochastic pattern by adding or superimposing it on the message, in the possession of both parties. These solutions exist but remain out of reach of the general public because they are expensive and require rigorous and heavy logistics of physical transport at the same time as a physical device of generation of randomness. This algorithm is optimal because the algorithm is chance. It cannot be recreated, by definition. The second-best solution consists in generating this true randomness (or True number generator TRNG in opposition to the pseudo-randomness) remotely on a computer, a means allowing a priori only the pseudo-random.
