The Fine-Structure Constant Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker James Arathoon Date 1 February 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording The Fine-Structure Constant was a live session presented by James Arathoon on 1 February 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. A discussion of the fundamental nature of the physical constant alpha that quantifies the strength of the electromagnetic interaction between elementary charged particles. Hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary James Arathoon began by disclaiming expertise. What prompted the talk was exasperation: the popular treatments of the fine-structure constant oscillate between presenting it as an unexplainable mystery and offering numerology, and he wanted to do some calculations instead — even wrong ones — to see where they lead. Why it matters α = e 2 /4πε 0 ℏ c is the only dimensionless number that can be made from these constants of nature, all units cancelling. Its inverse is close to but not exactly 137. Arathoon stressed something usually left out: the constant now sits at the heart of the SI system. Where once only the speed of light was fixed by definition, the Planck constant and the elementary charge are fixed too, so the metre follows from c , the kilogram from h and the ampere from e — and a measurement of α ties the system together. The permeability of free space, formerly defined as exactly 4π × 10 −7 , is now a measured quantity that happens to sit within the error bounds of that value. The open questions he set out: whether α approximates a notional irrational number as π does; whether it can be computed by an algorithm independent of experiment; and whether it is constant through the history of the universe. Dimensionless numbers A digression on measurement theory, following his earlier session on dimensional analysis. A measurement is itself a ratio, carrying units because one term is a standard.
