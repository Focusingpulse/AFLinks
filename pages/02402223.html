Favorsky, Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Irkutsk, Russia 2 Angarsk State Technical University user.contacts@mail.ru Heating in combustion-based furnaces is a widespread process in the petrochemical and oil refining industries, as it allows substances to be heated to temperatures higher than those achievable, for example, through heat exchangers. Industrial tubular furnaces are used for this purpose [1]. The key characteristic of a tubular furnace is its temperature regime, defined as the law (trajectory) of the heated stream's temperature change along the length of the furnace. The ability to implement various heating profiles is the primary advantage of continuous-type heating furnaces, as it allows for rapid adjustment of heating modes when the characteristics of the feed stream or fuel change. The temperature regime significantly determines the quality of the main stream heating and the furnace's technical and economic performance indicators. Researchers primarily rely on these two key indicators when studying and evaluating temperature heating trajectories.
