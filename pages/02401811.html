GTM2026-main030 Opinion dynamics of large language models: an Argument-based communication theory perspective Ivan V. Kozitsin1 , Arseniy Khlytchiev2 1 V.A. Trapeznikov Institute of Control Problems of the Russian Academy of Sciences. 2 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (State University) kozitsin.ivan@mail.ru This paper examines the behavior of large language models (LLMs) through the lens of argument‑based communication theory (ACT), which conceptualizes social communication as a sequential exchange of arguments, where arguments serve as fundamental building blocks of opinion formation. According to ACT, each argument carries a valence—a directional signal indicating whether it supports or opposes a given object (such as a person, law, or statement)—with supporting arguments termed as pro and opposing ones as con, thereby situating opinions along a continuous one-dimensional spectrum. An agent’s overall opinion then emerges as a linear aggregation of relevant arguments, weighted by their respective valences. Our empirical findings largely corroborate the core tenets of ACT: LLMs exhibit a tendency to synthesize numerical opinions via linear mapping of arguments and to systematically declines in communication those arguments that contradict their current opinion (this tendency is slightly more pronounced when gpt-4.1 is inputted to imagine that it interacts with human).
