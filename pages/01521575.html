The Electrical Experimenter Former name Modern Electrics ISO 4 abbreviation Find out here Discipline Electrical experiment; radio; popular science and invention Peer-reviewed No (magazine)No (magazine) Language English Edited by Hugo Gernsback Publication details Publisher Experimenter Publishing (United States) Publication history 1913–1920 under this title; continued as Science and Invention to 1931 Frequency Monthly The Electrical Experimenter was an American monthly magazine of electrical experiment and popular science, founded in May 1913 by Hugo Gernsback as the successor to his Modern Electrics . It ran under this title until July 1920, when it broadened its scope and was renamed Science and Invention , continuing until August 1931. It is of standing here as the principal popular venue for Nikola Tesla in his later years. Between August 1917 and July 1919 Tesla wrote five articles for the magazine, among them the serialisation of his autobiography My Inventions , and he appeared on the cover of the February 1919 issue with his "wireless light". Gernsback was an open admirer: on Tesla's death he commissioned a death mask and published photographs of it. Gernsback edited the magazine until March 1929, when the Experimenter Publishing company he ran with his brother Sidney was forced into bankruptcy. He is better remembered now as a founder of magazine science fiction, but the Experimenter was a technical publication first, aimed at readers who built apparatus. Its run is out of copyright and freely available; the issues are a primary source for the state of amateur electrical and radio experiment in the decade either side of the First World War. Holdings in the Aether Physics archive An offline research archive assembled by John Reed and surveyed in September 2026 holds 54 issues , spanning 1913 to 1920 — three issues from the first year, then seven for 1914, twelve for 1915, fourteen for 1916, eight for 1917, five for 1918, four for 1919 and one for 1920.
