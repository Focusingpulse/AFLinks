It is necessary, however, to insulate the winding much more carefully, and, having wound the primary on the iron core, insert it bodily in an ebonite tube, over which wind the secondary; then immerse the whole in a non-conducting vessel of boiled linseed oil. The iron core should be of soft iron wire, making a bundle of about 3/4 in. diameter and 8in. long, and wound with two layers of No. 14 guttapercha-covered wire. The secondary may consist of about thirty layers of No. 26 silk-covered wire. Full particulars and illustrations are given in S. R. Bottone's book, "Electrical Instrument Making." Arthur E. R.
