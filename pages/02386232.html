Deuterium substitution effects both thermodynamics (bond strengths) and kinetics (reaction rates). In most reactions, including those in biochemical pathways, deuterium slows the rate of reaction dramatically. Reaction rates are often slowed by 20-30% by deuterium substitution, and sometimes by more than ten times. The source of this effect is the different force constants of the deuterium or hydrogen bond to the other element. Introductory physical chemistry books generally discuss this. At any rate the effect of different bond strengths and altered reaction rates on biological systems can be easily imagined especially since proton (or deutron if poisoned by D2O) transfer is intimately involved in biological processes.
