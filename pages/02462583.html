Sympathetic Vibratory Physics

· archived # 2,462,583
"A plasma beam just became an operational antenna. Researchers at North Carolina State University just demonstrated that a laser-ionized shaft of air (LIPF) can transmit radio signals via capacitive coupling without physical contact, wires, or metal. It delivered a 150% boost in signal strength compared to unguided air. For decades, conventional telecommunications insisted on rigid metal hardware. But plasma itself is an active, tunable conduit. When energy is structured correctly, the field is the antenna. From laboratory laser filaments to the plasma dynamics governing planetary and biological systems, nature doesn't need circuit boards to transmit coherent frequencies. Hardware-free communication isn't just the future of technology it's the underlying architecture we're finally catching up to." The experiment is real, and coupling is a promising subject for us to explore. Your comparison with sympathetic association is useful as a starting analogy: one system excites another through an intervening relationship, without necessarily touching it. The post stretches some of the findings, however: - The plasma replaces the metal radiating element. The apparatus still uses a laser, an RF generator, and a metal ring that feeds the plasma capacitively. - The reported 150% increase is real: received signal strength rose to 2.5 times the baseline with the laser blocked. This was a 30 MHz test with the receiver approximately 5 cm away, in the near field—not a demonstrated long-distance communication system. - Planetary and biological communication are extrapolations beyond what this experiment established. [Office of Research and Innovation] Capacitive coupling means that a changing electric field in one system drives an electrical response in another. A continuous conducting connection between them is unnecessary. Here, the ring’s field drives charges in the plasma, allowing the filament to function as an antenna. The plasma is ionized matter, so “the field is the antenna”
