Overview This is the October–November 2000 double issue of New Energy News (Volume 7, Numbers 5–6, ISSN 1075-0045), the newsletter of the Institute for New Energy, edited by Patrick G Bailey with substantial contributions from Hal Fox . It is not a scientific paper but a compilation: conference reports, letters, book reviews, abstracts of experimental work, press clippings and commercial announcements, distributed by email in plain ASCII and, from this issue onward, also as a PDF. The editor's note opening the issue announces exactly that change, and offers to mail an Adobe Acrobat CD-ROM to subscribers who cannot download one. Its value as a document is archival rather than argumentative. It captures the cold fusion and free-energy community at a specific moment — eleven years after the Fleischmann–Pons announcement, at the point where several strands (hydrino chemistry, charge clusters, transmutation, over-unity generators) were being promoted simultaneously to investors and to a sceptical scientific establishment. What makes the issue unusually interesting is that it does not present a unified front: the most substantial contributions in it are internal criticism, and the sharpest sceptic of the claims reported is a cold fusion advocate. The Mills controversy The issue opens with a letter from Donald Kelly of Space Energy Journal arguing that Randell Mills' BlackLight Power results deserve credence — two laboratories reporting output at multiples of input, a utility investing a million dollars — and that the U.S. Patent Office may be "in violation of their own Rules of Practice" in stonewalling five Mills patents.
