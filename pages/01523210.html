Hofmann is an American historian of science, Professor of Liberal Studies at California State University, Fullerton . He is the author of André-Marie Ampère: Enlightenment and Electrodynamics (Cambridge University Press, 1995), for a long time the only full-length biography of Ampère in English and still the standard one. He appears on this wiki because Ampère's force law between current elements is among the most argued-over subjects here, and Hofmann is the historian most often relied on for the circumstances in which that law was produced. André-Marie Ampère: Enlightenment and Electrodynamics Published in the Cambridge Science Biographies series edited by David Knight, the book sets Ampère's scientific work against the whole of his life: a man with no formal schooling who read his way through his father's library, whose father was guillotined in the Terror, and who held simultaneously to the scientific optimism of the Enlightenment and to a Catholic faith he found difficult. Hofmann treats the mathematics, the chemistry and the electrodynamics together rather than extracting the physics from the life, and gives close attention to how Ampère actually reasoned from experiment to force law. "The providential timing of scientific discovery" (2026) Hofmann returned to the subject for the 250th anniversary of Ampère's birth, in "The providential timing of scientific discovery: André-Marie Ampère in 1820", Comptes Rendus — Mécanique 354 , 671–678 (2026), doi:10.5802/crmeca.374 . The paper asks why Ampère was able to respond to Ørsted's 1820 discovery as quickly and completely as he did, and answers it biographically.
