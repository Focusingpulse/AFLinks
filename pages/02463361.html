Bridging Mathematical Tools and Physical Reality: A Case for Dimensional Analysis in Modern Physics Abstract While calculus has proven invaluable as a computational tool in physics, its dominance as the primary framework for describing physical reality may inadvertently obscure deeper physical understanding. This paper presents dimensional analysis, particularly as developed in the Aether Physics Model (APM), as a complementary approach that offers direct insight into physical mechanisms while maintaining mathematical rigor. We demonstrate how this approach has led to significant insights into electron binding energies, the photoelectric effect, and general relativity while suggesting a path toward unifying quantum and classical descriptions of nature. Introduction Modern physics relies heavily on calculus-based descriptions of physical phenomena, which, while mathematically robust, often obscure the underlying physical mechanisms (Visser, 2018). Consider the wave function "collapse" in quantum mechanics - a mathematical construct that has led to decades of debate about its physical meaning. Such interpretational difficulties suggest that we benefit from additional approaches that maintain closer connections to physical reality. The Role of Mathematical Tools in Physics Mathematics serves two distinct functions in physics: 1. Computational tools for predicting measurements 2. Frameworks for understanding physical reality While calculus excels at the first function, providing powerful computational methods for complex problems, there may be better options for the second. Calculus's success in generating accurate predictions can lead us to mistake mathematical abstractions for physical reality.
