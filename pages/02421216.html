Vladimir Kadyshevsky Born ( 1937-05-05 ) May 5, 1937 Moscow, Soviet Union Died September 24, 2014 (2014-09-24) (aged 77) Residence Moscow Region, Russia Nationality Russian Alma mater Lomonosov Moscow State University Known for The Kadyshevsky equation; quantum field theory with a fundamental mass Scientific career Fields Theoretical physics; quantum field theory; elementary particle physics Institutions Joint Institute for Nuclear Research, Dubna; Lomonosov Moscow State University Vladimir Georgievich Kadyshevsky (5 May 1937 – 24 September 2014) was a Russian theoretical physicist, an Academician of the Russian Academy of Sciences, and Director of the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research at Dubna from 1992 to 2005. He worked on quantum field theory and elementary particle theory, and is remembered for two things in particular: the three-dimensional relativistic scattering equation that carries his name, and a long programme to modify quantum field theory by introducing a fundamental mass — an upper limit to the mass spectrum, standing to the mass scale as the velocity of light stands to velocity. He appears on this wiki as an editor of The Lorentz Group, CPT and Neutrinos (2001) and as the author of " A New High Energy Scale ", the paper in which he set out the fundamental-mass programme to the Frontiers of Fundamental Physics conference at Olympia in 1993. Biography Kadyshevsky was born in Moscow on 5 May 1937. From 1946 to 1954 he attended the Suvorov Military School in Sverdlovsk, graduating with highest distinction, and entered the Physics Department of Lomonosov Moscow State University.
