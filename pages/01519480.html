Classical vs Quantum: Revisiting Fundamental Phenomena through a New Classical Approach Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Date 27 September 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Classical vs Quantum: Revisiting Fundamental Phenomena through a New Classical Approach was a live session on 27 September 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. This seminar revisits phenomena typically viewed as quantum—the Compton effect, Planck’s radiation law, and the photoelectric effect—through a new classical framework. We show that experimental results can be reproduced without standard quantum postulates. The Compton effect is derived via classical Doppler scattering and confirmed with FDTD simulations. The Planck spectrum emerges from electron velocity distributions and classical field–electron interactions, linked through a velocity–wavelength relation. The photoelectric effect is reinterpreted as classical energy transfer, consistent with threshold behavior. These results invite a re-examination of the boundary between classical and quantum physics. With Halim Boutayeb hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary Halim Boutayeb , a full professor of electrical engineering at the Université du Québec en Outaouais and a specialist in antennas and computational electromagnetism, returned to argue that two of the pillars of modern physics rest on the same misunderstanding, and that both can be dismantled with nothing more exotic than Maxwell's equations solved on a computer. What counts as classical He opened with a question about vocabulary. Ampère and Weber's electrodynamics was classical until Maxwell replaced it, at which point Maxwell became the new classical. Why then, after a hundred and twenty years, are special relativity and quantum theory still not classical?
