Newspapers' Finish Is in Sight if Tesla's Dream Comes True New York Morning Telegraph March 10th, 1904 Page number(s): 1 Once More the Greatest Electrical Inventor in the World ('Rah,'Rah,'Rah!) Has Brought Forth Something That Is Going to Do Something — Maybe. Nikola Tesla, the hibernating wizard of electrology, has again left his cave, at Wardenclyffe, Long Island, and appeared with a world-beating idea. In brief, it is a telegraphic instrument dependable upon no wire more concrete than the currents of the earth and sea. It will be nothing but a small box, which, when placed upon the ground, will become a receiving and transmitting station. Its first demonstration will be, according to some of Mr. Tesla's admirers, the destruction of all newspapers. Mr. Tesla is pitiless. How the Ideas Comes to Him. These beautiful ideas of Mr. Tesla's all come to him while he is lying on his back in the salt water. Neptune is so proud of his guest's preference for salt water as Summer environment that he feeds him with the accumulated wisdom of all the men who have gone down to the sea in ships. Newspapers, on the contrary, have no tutelary god to aid them in their approaching struggle for existence.
