Title Study of Gravity, Part 2 Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Mike Gamble Published 2011 No. of pages 132 Abstract I started this "Study of Gravity" back in 2007 more as a curiosity than research. Looking in textbooks all you find about gravity is the statement that it is an attraction between two bodies and varies as the mass and separation. I thought there must be more to it, so I started collecting data about the earth. In engineering you start with a ballpark number (WAG or SWAG) and work the problem from there. Being of the electronics type I was trained to think in terms of waves, harmonics and frequencies. The "wow moment" in the research came when I realized that the harmonic waveshapes were aligning very close to that of the measured data. After that things got real interesting! Overview Study of Gravity, Part 2 is a 132-slide presentation by Mike Gamble , an electronics engineer at the Boeing Company in Seattle, given at the Space, Propulsion and Energy Sciences International Forum in March 2011. It continues the Part 1 presented at the same forum in 2009. Its origin is candidly described: "I started this 'Study of Gravity' back in 2007 more as a curiosity than research… Being of the electronics type I was trained to think in terms of waves, harmonics and frequencies. The 'wow moment' in the research came when I realized that the harmonic waveshapes were aligning very close to that of the measured data." Part 1 arrived at a "four wave harmonic dipole accelerating field model" of gravity: a standing-wave field composed of a fundamental at 7.07 Hz and harmonics at 14.14, 21.21 and 28.28 Hz, sourced at dipole feedpoints on the polar axis separated by λ/8. Part 2 recasts that model in electromagnetic terms.
