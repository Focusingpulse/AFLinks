Herbert Smitherman

· archived # 1,459,797
Next time you brush your teeth or pour a cup of coffee, remember Dr. Herbert Smitherman. Born in 1937 in Birmingham, Alabama, Smitherman grew up in segregated schools but graduated top of his class. He went on to earn a BS and MS in Chemistry at Tuskegee University, taught at Texas Southern University, served in the U.S. Army, and completed a Ph.D. in physical organic chemistry at Howard University. In 1966, he became Procter & Gamble’s first African American hire with a doctorate. There, he didn’t just blend formulas—he invented and improved products that millions still use today: Crest toothpaste, Safeguard soap, Bounce fabric softener, Folgers Coffee, Crush soda, and more. Smitherman held dozens of patents, with one toothpaste formula cited in 31 other patents, influencing generations of chemists. He wasn’t just a scientist—he was a pioneer breaking racial barriers in STEM. He helped recruit minority graduates, founded the National Organization for Black Chemists and Chemical Engineers, and was known as the “Jackie Robinson of Procter & Gamble.” His impact extended beyond the lab. Smitherman’s leadership empowered other Black scientists, engineers, and inventors to claim their space in corporate America. His family mirrored his drive: all six children were college-educated, with careers in medicine, public service, and leadership.
