The standard account Hydrogen's visible spectrum was fitted empirically by Balmer in 1885 and generalised by Rydberg; the Rydberg constant is now among the most precisely known quantities in physics. Bohr's 1913 model recovered those lines by quantising angular momentum, giving a ground-state binding energy of 13.6 eV and a characteristic radius (the Bohr radius) of about 5.29 10 11 m. Schrödinger's 1926 wave mechanics replaced the orbits with stationary states and reproduced the same energies; the Dirac Equation of 1928 added spin and fine structure.
