Title The Quantum Mysteries Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Richard Oldani Keywords quantum mechanics , Quantum Physics , Quantum Theory Published 2008 No. of pages 5 Abstract The allure of quantum mechanics lies in its mysteries and the exotic possibilities they introduce, such as worm holes, parallel Universes, and teleportation. It is shown here that these are illusions perpetuated by the self-serving ends of establishment physicists. The mystique disappears when experiments are described in strict detail with classical fields and straightforward causal arguments. Quantum physics is shown to be accessible to everyone, not just mathematicians. It is shown that quantum variables do not commute because the initial state of a quantum system is indeterminate. Overview The Quantum Mysteries is a five-page paper by Richard Oldani , presented at the 15th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in April 2008. Its thesis is that the standard puzzles of quantum mechanics are artefacts of a mathematical formalism maintained for professional advantage, and that each dissolves when the experiments are described with classical fields and ordinary causal reasoning. The opening sections are sociological. Oldani argues that physicists, being mathematicians, "behave in many respects like priests"; that the formalism functions as "canon law" accepted on faith; that access to the priesthood is limited and changes made by consensus; and that the mysteries serve to engage popular imagination with wormholes, parallel universes and teleportation, thereby securing funding. He then takes three mysteries in turn. The first is that interference fringes build up even when light is so faint that photons arrive one at a time. Oldani's answer is that photons "do not retain their identity throughout transmission".
