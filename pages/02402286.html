MolPhot2026-main043 Low-threshold polariton lasing in a 2D perovskite microcavity Ioannis Paschos1,2 , Aleksei O. Murzin3 , Emmanouil Mavrotsoupakis4,2 , Cui Zhen1,2 , Eleni C. P. Macropulos4 , Apostolos Pantousas5 , Constantinos C. Stoumpos4,3 , Yury Kapitonov3 , Pavlos G. Savvidis1,2,6 1 Westlake University, China 2 Westlake Institute for Advanced Study, China 3 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia 4 University of Crete, Greece 5 European Synchrotron Radiation Facility, France 6 IESL-FORTH, Greece aleksei.murzin.science@mail.ru Two-dimensional hybrid halide perovskites are promising materials for polaritonic devices due to their large exciton binding energies, high oscillator strengths, and strong light-matter interaction. These properties make them attractive for the development of compact coherent light sources operating in the strong exciton-photon coupling regime. In this work, we study low-threshold polariton lasing in an optical microcavity filled with crystalline sheets of the Ruddlesden–Popper two-dimensional perovskite BA2 MA2 Pb3 I 10. The microcavity was formed by high-temperature vacuum bonding of two dielectric mirrors based on 10.5 pairs of Ta2 O5 /SiO2 layers. Ti/Cu pads were deposited at the mirror edges, defining a cavity length of about 6.8 μm. The precursor solution was then introduced into the pre-fabricated cavity by capillary filling, followed by slow cooling, which resulted in the growth of perovskite microcrystals directly inside the cavity.
