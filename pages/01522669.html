It acts at right angles to the body's velocity and to the rotation axis, and in textbook mechanics it takes the form 2 m v , where m is the mass, the angular velocity of the frame, and v the body's velocity within it. The expression was written down by the French engineer Gaspard-Gustave de Coriolis in an 1835 paper on the energetics of rotating machinery such as water wheels, where he called it the compound centrifugal force .
