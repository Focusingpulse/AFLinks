The Cowardice of “Going Along to Get Along” One of the greatest failures in human society is the refusal to ask hard questions. Instead of standing firm in the search for truth, too many go along to get along. This cowardice is not harmless — it is the very soil in which corruption, ignorance, and deception thrive. Take the case of John Worrell Keely. For more than a century, the lazy refrain has been repeated: “Keely was a fraud.” People echo the accusation as though repetition makes it fact. Yet how many of those who parrot this judgment have examined the records, read his notes, or studied the context of his discoveries? Very few. They believe the hype, never testing it for themselves, never daring to ask whether the smear campaign was convenient for those who feared his work. This is the failure of society: the unwillingness to do one’s own research, the eagerness to accept easy explanations, and the fear of being different. Progress has never come from those who conformed. It has always come from those who were willing to question, to doubt the official story, and to pursue truth even when mocked or opposed. Until we overcome this habit of blind agreement — this cowardice masquerading as common sense — we will remain bound by illusions. Society advances only when individuals find the courage to think for themselves.
