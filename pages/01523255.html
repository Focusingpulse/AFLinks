John Linus OSullivan Born 1938 (age 87–88) Tralee, Ireland Residence Fairfield, CT, United States Nationality Irish / American Known for A wave-based model of the electromagnetic field, mass, light and gravity Scientific career Fields Cosmology (independent researcher) John Linus O'Sullivan (born 1938) is an Irish-American independent researcher in cosmology and the foundations of physics. Born in Tralee, Ireland, he took up the study of cosmology after retiring from a career in business, and developed a wave-based model in which the electromagnetic field is held to have a duality — much as light has a wave–particle duality — which he presents as a new way of thinking about the structure of the universe. His ideas are his own and are not part of mainstream physics. Biography O'Sullivan was born in 1938 in Tralee, County Kerry, Ireland, and has lived in the United States since 1960, settling in Fairfield, Connecticut. After a career in the business world he turned in his retirement to the study of cosmology and physics, working as an independent researcher and publishing his conclusions through the Natural Philosophy community. Cosmological model O'Sullivan's central idea is that the electromagnetic field is dual in nature, paralleling the wave–particle duality of the photon. From this starting point he builds an account in which space and matter arise from standing-wave energy: mass, light and gravity are treated as different aspects of the same underlying electromagnetic waves rather than as separate ingredients. In his 2013 paper " Space and Time " he set out the relationship between space and time in these terms, and in " Electromagnetic Wave Energy and Time " (2015) he developed the theme further, distinguishing a finite energy that carries time — mass-energy linked to light and gravity through "open-ended standing waves" — from an infinite energy that has no time, and arguing that time exists only as the transfer of finite energy.
