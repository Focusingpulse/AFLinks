Edison and Ford

· archived # 1,458,834
On the evening of December 9, 1914, Thomas Edison stood in West Orange, New Jersey and watched his factories burn. The explosion started in the film inspection building. Nitrate stock, the most flammable material in the place. Ten buildings went. The fire threw flames a hundred feet into the air, jumped five city blocks, and smoldered for weeks afterward. More than two hundred fifty people ran out of those buildings. Edison stayed until midnight. He was sixty-seven years old. A reporter found him at the scene that night and asked him what he was going to do. Edison told him that although he was over sixty-seven, he would start all over again tomorrow. He did. Fifteen hundred men were put on the cleanup. Within days his people were working out of temporary space nearby. By New Year's Day, three weeks after the fire, the buildings were partially back and his employees were back in them. He did not let anyone go. This is a story about what two men did for each other over thirty-five years, and about the fact that the best of it happened when both of them were old. The table in Brooklyn They met in August of 1896, at the annual meeting of the Association of Edison Illuminating Companies, held at the Oriental Hotel at Manhattan Beach in Brooklyn. Forty-four men from eighteen companies. Edison sat at the head of the table. Henry Ford was thirty-three and nobody.
