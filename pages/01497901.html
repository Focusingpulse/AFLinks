Volume 228, number 1 PHYSICS LETTERS B 7 September 1989 CONVENTIONAL SOURCES OF FAST NEUTRONS IN "COLD FUSION" EXPERIMENTS M. CRIBIER, M. SPIRO IRF-DPhPE, CEN Saclay, F-91191 Gifsur- Yvette Cedex, France and J. FAVIER LAPP, Chemin de Bellevue, BP 909, F- 74019 Annecy-le- Vieux Cedex, France Received 19 May 1989 In "cold fusion" experiments with heavy water a source of neutrons is the dissociation of deuterium induced by alpha particles emitted by natural occurring radioisotopes. We evaluate the rate of fast neutron emission as a function of the concentration of U, Th, Rn in contact with deuterium and discuss the possibility that the neutrons claimed to have been observed in "cold fusion" experiments could be due to this conventional source. During electrolysis, a large amount of radon might be desorbed from the porous electrodes into the heavy water, thus faking the kind of observed signals in "cold fusion" experi￾ments. The alpha particle energy for reaction (1) at threshold is 6.6 MeV in the laboratory system or 2.2 MeV total energy in the center of mass. The neutrons have a spectrum extending up to 3.1 MeV, with a large fraction above 2 MeV. The cross section for this re￾action was measured by Schutz in 1938 to be 1 mb at 8 MeV [ 1 ]; it has more recently been measured to be 0.3 b at 19 MeV [ 2 ]. The energy dependence of this cross section is dominated near threshold by Li* in￾termediate states [3]. Therefore we would expect a cross section of about 100 mb for an alpha particle energy of 8 MeV in the laboratory system.
