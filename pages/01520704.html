Title New Energy News (February 2001) Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Patrick G Bailey Keywords New Energy , Cold Fusion, Zero Point Energy, climate change, patent policy Published 2001 Journal New Energy News Volume 8 Number 2 No. of pages 25 Abstract Overview This is the February 2001 issue of New Energy News (Volume 8, Number 2, ISSN 1075-0045), the monthly newsletter of the Institute for New Energy, edited by Patrick G Bailey with material contributed by Hal Fox , Thomas Valone, Toby Grotz and others. It is a news bulletin rather than a research paper, organised into standing sections — Abstracts, Book Review, Articles, Letters and Meetings — and it announces the switch to distribution in both plain ASCII and PDF, with an Adobe Acrobat CD-ROM offered by post for $2 to readers without Internet access. The issue is unusual in the New Energy News run for how much of it is given over to mainstream climate science. Roughly half the pages reprint the Shanghai draft Summary for Policymakers of the IPCC Third Assessment Report, together with BBC, Washington Post , New Scientist and Munich Re coverage of it. The remainder carries the newsletter's characteristic content: energy statistics, a critique of U.S. energy policy, a Hal Fox editorial on patent-office treatment of paradigm-shifting inventions, a cold-fusion patent defeat, and a call for papers for a Space Physics conference in India. The editorial through-line — stated most directly in Fox's piece — is that the institutions that certify what counts as a workable invention are structurally hostile to discoveries that fall outside the accepted model, and that this, rather than any failure of the evidence, is why new-energy technologies remain uncommercialised.
