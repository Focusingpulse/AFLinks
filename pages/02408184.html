THE PLOUGH ‘Old farm tools and machinery in pictures Foreword by BERNARD PRICE Hand to the Plough Old Farm Tools and Machinery in Pictures The day he was demobbed from the Second World War Douglas Lawson took a photograph of a man ploughing a sunlit field in Surrey. This picture was the start of an intensive hobby which lasted throughout Douglas’s illustrious professional photographic career. This book, consisting of 150 of the best of his black and white photographs, traces the development of farm machinery from the 1880’s to the 1940’s. It shows clearly the increasing sophistication of agricultural machines, and the growing reliance of farmers on them, as cheap, plentiful labour vanished. Here, beautifully photographed, are ploughs, harrows, cultivators, seed drills, stone and root crushers, pumps, water carts, stationary engines, fert- ilizer spreaders, harvesters, steam engines, embryo tractors, waggons and carts for all seasons. The captions have been written by Henry Jackson, owner of the Tilford Agricultural Museum, where many of the photographs were taken. They explain clearly to the layman how each machine worked and how it fitted into the farming year. This is an evocative collection by a master photographer. Douglas Lawson was Chief scientific photographer to Beecham Pharmaceuticals. He retired in 1980 and died the same year.
