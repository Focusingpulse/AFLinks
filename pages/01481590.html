To understand the thinking of the people of that era, it's important not to apply the modern divisions of religion, magic, medicine, and astrology to it. An amulet was perceived not simply as an image of a deity or a passive symbol, but as a functioning ritual object—a kind of seal that combined the stone's material, image, divine name, magical signs, and astrological correspondences. The human body was understood as part of the cosmic order: according to the principle of melothesis, certain planets, signs, and decans were associated with specific body parts and organs. Therefore, invoking Khnoubis could mean influencing the area of ​​the human microcosm to which his celestial power corresponded. Moreover, Greco-Egyptian magical thinking was often not strictly dualistic, but syncretic: various divine powers could be combined in a single amulet for the desired protective or healing effect. People often project modern categories onto ancient symbols because it is easier than entering the complex religious worldview of the period. Syncretism developed through centuries of cultural contact, when Egyptian, Greek, Roman, Jewish, astrological, and philosophical ideas were compared and recombined according to their functions. A magical amulet could unite the image of one deity with the names and signs of other powers without treating them as identical.
