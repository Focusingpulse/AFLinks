GTM2026-main024 Dynamic Information Design for Emergency Replenishment under Disaster Response Qiuhong Zhao1 1 Beihang University, China qhzhao@buaa.edu.cn In disaster response, intelligent technologies are reshaping government decision-making on emergency replenishment. Yet how improved information translates into better replenishment outcomes remains unclear in dynamic settings, and how governments should strategically design information disclosure over time. We study government information disclosure as a coordination mechanism in this context and develop a dynamic Bayesian persuasion model in which the government observes AI-generated demand signals and strategically designs disclosure policies to influence a supplier’s replenishment decisions over time. The government acts as an information intermediary with commitment power, while the supplier chooses replenishment quantities by trading off shortage losses against redundancy costs. A key feature of the model is history-dependent belief updating: inventory carried over from earlier periods dilutes the impact of newly disclosed information, creating an intertemporal trade-off in disclosure design. We first characterize the supplier’s optimal replenishment policy and the government’s optimal disclosure strategy in a single-period benchmark, identifying when full disclosure, no disclosure, or intermediate disclosure is optimal.
