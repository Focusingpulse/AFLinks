Cohesion Figures

· archived # 1,456,376
Do you know what is it Cohesion Figures? These were discovered by a certain Di Wright, and are analogous to the figures produced on dry plates by electrical action (see Ex. No. I). Take a metal plate and lay on it a sheet of glass, placing in the middle a drop of liquid. This may be a solution of a salt of some metal, or a dilute acid or alkali. One of the poles of a small Ruhm-korff coil is connected with the metal plate, and a wire attached to the other pole is caused to dip into the drop of liquid. Set the coil in action, and curious figures are produced and spread themselves over the plate. The positive figures are the most beautiful (fig. 5I), but a variety can be formed, and, as in the, reversal of the pole alters the figure. Te shape of the figure is thought to be determined by the cohesion existing in the particles composing the drop of liquid, and also between the liquid and the surface of the glass. In the light of Experiment I, the figures have an additional interest, and the student is recommended to study them.
