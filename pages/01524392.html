Ginzburg (1916–2009), the Soviet physicist and 2003 Nobel laureate, who is a different person entirely. Vladimir B. Ginzburg (born 6 June 1935 in Moscow) is a Russian-American mechanical engineer, inventor and independent theorist based in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. In his professional career he became a leading authority on steel rolling technology, the author of standard reference works on flat rolling and the holder of more than sixty U.S. and foreign patents. In parallel he pursued, over some three decades, a revival and reconstruction of the aether and vortex tradition in physics, developing in turn his Spiral Field Theory, the relativistic torus and helix as the "prime elements of nature", the toryx and helyx, the 3-Dimensional Spiral String Theory (3D-SST) and finally the Universal Spacetime Theory (UST). This work, published in a series of books through his own Helicola Press and in papers in Speculations in Science and Technology , the Journal of New Energy and the Proceedings of the NPA , places him outside the mainstream programmes of the Standard Model and conventional string theory, which he regards as having taken a wrong turn away from the geometric, vortical picture of matter. Biography Education M.S. in mechanical engineering, 1961 Ph.D. in technical sciences, 1968 Career Ginzburg was born in Moscow, Russia, on 6 June 1935.
