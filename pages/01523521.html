Physics, 1964) Known for Twin-paradox analysis; critiques of special relativity 's treatment of time Scientific career Fields Foundations of physics ( Twin Paradox , Relativity ), Software entrepreneurship Website http://twinparadox.net/ Nick Percival (born 30 September 1942) is an American independent physics researcher and software entrepreneur, best known for his work criticizing and re-interpreting Einstein's special theory of relativity's treatment of time — in particular the Twin Paradox . He entered Harvard University in 1959 at the age of 17 and graduated in 1964 with a degree in physics; that launched a focus on the Twin Paradox spanning more than fifty years, from which he developed a distinctive view of the relationship between special relativity and aether (preferred-frame) theory. Scientific work Percival argues that special relativity's account of time contains a fundamental flaw. Echoing the physicist Herbert Dingle 's well-known objection — that special relativity "unavoidably requires that clock A works more slowly than clock B and clock B more slowly than clock A" — he contends that relativistic time dilation must be a merely "observed" effect rather than a real physical one, since a genuinely reciprocal physical slowing would be a logical impossibility. On his view, the physically correct account is given by a preferred-frame (aether) theory , in which clock rates and other process rates depend on absolute velocity, while special relativity can still yield correct numbers for certain limited phenomena even though the physics it implies is wrong. Percival initially debated many individual professors who held the standard view of how the net proper-time difference accumulates in a Twin Paradox scenario.
