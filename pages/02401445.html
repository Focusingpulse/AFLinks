GC2026-SGG021 Available High-precision Temperature Sensors for Deep Mountain Rock Valentin Gravirov1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences vvg@ifz.ru Temperature measurement is one of the key tasks in various fields of science and technology. Temperature, as a physical quantity, plays an important role in many natural processes. In recent decades, there has been significant progress in the field of thermometry, which is due to the development of new technologies and materials that improve the accuracy and reliability of measurements. Let's analyze the current precision technologies for measuring the temperature of rocks. Among the most common types of temperature-sensitive elements, thermocouples provide the most accessible and straightforward method for obtaining data. The principle of thermocouples is based on the thermoelectric effect [1]. The accuracy of the best thermocouples can reach 0.001°C. However, thermocouples have significant drawbacks, such as non￾linear temperature characteristics, sensitivity to interference, the need for cold junction compensation, and a limited lifespan. Distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) technology is an innovative method for measuring temperature based on analyzing changes in the characteristics of an optical fiber under external temperature conditions [2]. Unlike traditional point sensors, DAS allows for continuous temperature monitoring along the entire fiber by creating a large number of virtual thermometers, providing high-density spatio-temporal data.
