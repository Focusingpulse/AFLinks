Food and Chemical Toxicology 47 (2009) 353–360 Contents lists available at ScienceDirect Food and Chemical Toxicology journal homepage: www.elsevier.com/locate/foodchemtox Endogenous small RNAs in grain: Semi-quantiﬁcation and sequence homology to human and animal genes Sergey I. Lindbergh Blvd., Mail Code O3F, St. Louis, MO 63167, USA a r t i c l e i n f o Article history: Received 8 July 2008 Accepted 14 November 2008 Keywords: RNAi Small RNA siRNA miRNA Crops History of safe consumption a b s t r a c t Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) are effector molecules of RNA interference (RNAi), a highly conserved RNA-based gene suppression mechanism in plants, mammals and other eukaryotes. Endogenous RNAi-based gene suppression has been harnessed naturally and through conventional breeding to achieve desired plant phenotypes. The present study demonstrates that endogenous small RNAs, such as siRNAs and miRNAs, are abundant in soybean seeds, corn kernels, and rice grain, plant tissues that are traditionally used for food and feed. Numerous endogenous plant small RNAs were found to have perfect complementarity to human genes as well as those of other mammals. The abundance of endogenous small RNA molecules in grain from safely consumed food and feed crops such as soybean, corn, and rice and the homology of a number of these dietary small RNAs to human and animal genomes and transcriptomes establishes a history of safe consumption for dietary small RNAs.
