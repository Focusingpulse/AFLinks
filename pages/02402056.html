GTM2026-main034 Modeling the behavior of LLM agents in a Repeating Prisoner's Dilemma Game: tolerance to defection and the Memory Window effect Dina A. Rusinova1 , Ivan V. Kozitsin2,1 1 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (State University) 2 V.A. Trapeznikov Institute of Control Problems of the Russian Academy of Sciences. Ponencia invitada rusinova.da@phystech.edu Our work presents the results of an analysis of the Iterated Prisoner’s Dilemma in which the players are Large Language Models (LLMs) embodying individuals with predefined age and gender characteristics (hereafter, LLM agents). The cooperative behavior of LLM agents is examined within the framework of Markov strategies, where a player’s action in the next round is conditioned on the opponent’s previous action and characterized by two parameters: mutual cooperation (the probability of cooperating in response to cooperation) and tolerance to defection (the probability of cooperating in response to the opponent’s defection). We find no evidence of an age effect on the probability of cooperation. However, we identify systematic gender￾related differences. LLMs impersonating male individuals exhibit consistently low, near-zero levels of cooperation, whereas LLMs impersonating female individuals demonstrate a substantially higher propensity to cooperate. Moreover, for the “female” LLM agents, tolerance to defection depends significantly on the memory window size m, defined as the number of previous rounds included in the prompt.
