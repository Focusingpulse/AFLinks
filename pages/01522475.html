Astronomy is the observational study of objects beyond the Earth. On this wiki it is the discipline that supplies most of the evidence in dispute: nearly every argument about cosmology , redshift and gravitation catalogued here rests on an astronomical measurement, and the disagreements are usually about what those measurements mean rather than about the numbers themselves. Category: Astronomy holds over 260 items. What is disputed Astronomical observation is indirect. Almost nothing beyond the Solar System can be sampled, weighed or repeated; distances, masses and ages are all inferred through a chain of assumptions, and the criticism collected here generally attacks a link in that chain rather than the observation at its end. Distance — the redshift –distance relation underpins the extragalactic distance scale. Halton Arp 's intrinsic redshift work argues from physically associated objects with discordant redshifts that the relation cannot be read as a simple distance indicator. Age and evolution — the Hubble Deep Field and its successors are argued to show galaxies more mature at high redshift than the standard chronology permits. Mass — galactic rotation curves are explained in the standard model by dark matter ; the alternatives catalogued here modify gravitation instead. Compact objects — the interpretation of black holes and of the objects identified as them is contested. Light propagation — whether deflection near the Sun occurs in curved spacetime or in the solar plasma, a case argued by Edward Henry Dowdye . Solar System work A distinct and more testable strand concerns the Solar System, where measurement is direct enough to constrain theory sharply: planetary radar ranging, the Allais effect during solar eclipses, the Pioneer anomaly, lunar laser ranging, and satellite orbital dynamics.
