Overview Lunar laser ranging, using the retroreflectors left by Apollo 11 and later missions, shows the Moon receding from the Earth at about 3.8 cm per year. The orthodox explanation is tidal: the Earth's tidal bulge leads the Moon, torques it forward, and transfers angular momentum from the Earth's rotation to the lunar orbit. Carezani and Haye argue instead that most of the recession is the same effect that produces the extra gravitational tug on the Pioneer spacecraft, and that only a small residue is tidal. The argument The Moon as a third body The paper's framing is that the Earth–Moon–Sun system is a three-body problem with no closed solution, and that Pioneer 10/11, Galileo and Ulysses are similar: each "falls" towards the Sun–Earth direction at the anomalous 8 . 5 × 1 0 − 8 cm/s 2 reported by NASA. The Moon, on this reading, is a third body falling in the solar direction too — except that the Earth retains it, so that the fall shows up not as an acceleration towards the Sun but as a steadily increasing Earth–Moon distance.
