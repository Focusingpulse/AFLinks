Saturday Morning Science Chats Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Ian Cohen, Bob Gray , Dennis Allen , Harry Ricker and Jim, given over entirely to unipolar induction — the Faraday disc. An email storm had been running all week on the subject, with messages arriving several times an hour, and this session was the attempt to settle it against the experimental record. The question When a cylindrical magnet is rotated about its own magnetic axis, does its field rotate with it? Hu says no: put a compass beside the magnet, spin the magnet about its axis, and the compass will not move; nothing about the space around it changes. Field lines, on his account, are a mathematical device with no physical realization, so there is nothing there to be traced from one angular position to another. He predicted that iron filings on a paper over a spinning magnet would sit perfectly still. Ian says yes, and gave an analogy worth keeping: a member under stress viewed in polarized light shows stress lines; rotate the member and the lines rotate with it. To hold that the lines of force stay fixed while their source turns is, he said, to claim the stress lines would remain static in some absolute frame, which he thinks absurd. He also noted the history — Faraday first introduced the idea of the lines not following the magnet, to explain the homopolar result by having the magnet cut its own lines of force, and changed his mind some twenty years later. Gray declined both. Working from Weber electrodynamics, there is no magnetic field at all; a magnet may be replaced by a closed current loop, and rotating the loop speeds the electrons and the protons alike, so the net effect is nothing.
