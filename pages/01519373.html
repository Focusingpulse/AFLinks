Aspden in the late 50's is examined, and a new field system based on this law is investigated. Maxwell's equations are not affected by this, but the Lorentz force law is modified, and the existence of a new type of radiation is considered. Overview This six-page paper in the Hadronic Journal examines a modification of the Lorentz force law proposed by Harold Aspden in 1959, develops it into a field theory, and speculates that it implies a new kind of radiation. Dennis P Allen is careful about what he is and is not claiming: "Maxwell's equations stand as they are. The only change is that the formula for the force on a charged particle in a magnetic field contains an additional new term." Aspden's extra term For two point charges, with r the separation vector from the source q ′ (velocity v ′) to the test charge q (velocity v ), the usual magnetic force is q v × B with B the Biot–Savart field of q ′. Aspden adds G = −(μ 0 /4π) qq ′ ( v ′· r ) v / r 3 a force directed along the test particle's own velocity . Allen notes that this term integrates to zero for closed circuital currents, which is why the classical experiments of Ampère, Biot and Savart, on which Maxwell relied, could not have detected it. He also records that Aspden's later, 1969 revision of the law multiplied the term by a mass ratio, and that this version "had as a corollary that entropy could be reversed, something this author cannot accept." The scalar field Allen writes G = q φ( r ) v with φ( r ) = −(μ 0 /4π) q ′ ( v ′· r )/ r 3 and treats φ as a field in its own right.
