John G. Hartnett Born ( 1952-03-24 ) March 24, 1952 (age 74) Manjimup, Western Australia Residence Australia Nationality Australian Alma mater University of Western Australia Known for Cryogenic sapphire oscillators, Carmelian cosmology, cosmology without dark matter or dark energy, Relativity , Redshift Scientific career Fields Physics , Frequency standards and metrology, Cosmology Institutions University of Western Australia; University of Adelaide John Gideon Hartnett (born 24 March 1952) is an Australian physicist known in two very different capacities: as an experimentalist who helped build some of the most stable clocks in the world , and as a cosmologist who argues that the universe requires neither dark matter nor dark energy — and who rejects the Big Bang outright. Like Pierre-Marie Robitaille , Hartnett is a case of dissent issuing from technical accomplishment rather than from outside the discipline. His work on ultra-stable cryogenic oscillators is mainstream precision metrology of a high order, commercialised and sold to national standards laboratories. His cosmology is not accepted at all. Education and career Hartnett was born in Manjimup, Western Australia. He took both his B.Sc. (Hons I) and his PhD with distinction at the School of Physics, University of Western Australia, and in 2005 became an ARC QEII Post-Doctoral Research Fellow. He worked for many years in the Frequency Standards and Metrology research group at UWA, latterly as a research professor, and subsequently at the University of Adelaide. He has published more than eighty papers in refereed journals and holds two patents. Frequency standards and metrology Hartnett's experimental speciality is the cryogenic sapphire oscillator — a frequency standard in which a pure sapphire resonator, cooled with liquid helium, supports an exceptionally stable microwave "whispering gallery" mode.
