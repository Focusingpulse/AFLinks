Conceptual Design of a Near-Future Laser ICF Driver “Pulsar” By Ye Junchi (China) finnleykepler@gmail.com Abstract The use of Fusion as the energy source of a rocket propulsion system has great potential to significantly reduce travel times to the outer planets . Research into this has been focused on more far-future and exotic fusion [3] drives , while recent research efforts into near future fusion drives focus mainly on Zeta-Pinch or direct fusion [9] . However, Inertial-confinement fusion (ICF) could also be a viable option . This paper is a proposal of a [10−12] [3] conceptual fusion drive - “Pulsar”. Studies include the selection of fuel options (D-T and D-3He), concept, thermal-mechanical properties, theoretical performance, and applications compared to other options. A combination of Deuterium-Tritium (D-T) fuel, reflected fiber pulse laser system, spin-aligned frozen fusion pellets, and a Tantalum-Hafnium Carbide (TaHfC) heat shield shows great promise for a viable near-future ICF propulsion system. “Keywords” D-T, Conceptual design, Near-future, Laser confinement, Inertial Confinement Fusion, Fusion Driver 1 1. Introduction The study of fusion energy for the means of propulsion started ever since the first years of fusion research. Most proposed concepts show great promise for a manned mission to the stars (given we solve cryosleep), requiring great technological advancements and an unreasonable amount of funding to make it feasible . However, only a [9] handful of concepts are designed for the use of interplanetary travel , specifically for a manned mission to the [3, 10, 12] outer planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto). Most of such concepts are designed to use Zeta-Pinch fusion or direct fusion with the use of microwave heating. Zeta-Pinch fusion has undesirable neutron flux and plasma instabilities that limits its power output , and dedicating a significant mass fraction of the spacecraft for [11] thermal management.
