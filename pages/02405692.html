We do not refer primarily here to the current of mystical and introspective alchemy (nei tan') which was as powerful no doubt as that of the West, but to the practical preparation of elixirs and the attempts to obtain the transmutation of the metals (wai tan2). It is this tradition which naturally most interests the historian of science, and the more one studies it the more one comes to feel that in spite of the usual bewildering terminology it was on the whole less systematically allegorical and more practically empirical than its counterpart in Europe.
