Title A Theoretical Model of the Structure of Space Based on the Speed of Light Read in full Link to paper Author(s) D DeWayne Birkhofer Keywords speed of light , space, wave-particle duality, Quantum Theory , light speed Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 5 Pages 31-36 Abstract Present mainstream physics has abandoned the concept of a carrier of light waves. This paper's intent is to restore the concept of a carrier by utilizing light speed as the foundation for defining the structure of such a carrier, which was commonly referred to in the 19th century as aether. By using established empirical and experimental data, it is possible to put forward an extremely powerful 3-D structure over which angular impulses (aka light, aka electromagnetism) can move in a wave distribution at a speed of ~300000 kilometers per second ( c ). Once the structure has been defined, proper understanding of the aether's dynamics makes it possible to move beyond the speed of light alone and encompass concepts of gravity, gravitational lensing, and origination of mass. Based on the structure of the medium as presented here, the model strongly supports a return to a mechanics-based physics in rejecting wave-particle duality while absorbing quantum theory. Overview D. DeWayne Birkhofer, a physician writing from Minnesota, proposes to restore the aether by working backwards from the one number he takes to be securely known: the speed of light. If light is a wave, something must carry it; if something carries it, that carrier has a structure; and the structure can be deduced from the requirement that it transmit a disturbance at 3 × 10¹⁰ cm/s. The construction proceeds in steps. Energy is motion. Total kinetic energy divides into linear and rotational parts. Since sound does not travel in vacuum, the linear part vanishes there, leaving only rotation — so light must be an angular impulse, I ω, transmitted from one spinning body to the next.
