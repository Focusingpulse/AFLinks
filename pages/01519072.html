Title A Classical Quantum Theory Read in full Link to paper (Internet Archive) Author(s) Bruce Harvey Keywords Quantum Mechanics , Magnetic Flux, Hydrogen, Quantized Energy Published 2008 No. of pages 5 Read the full paper here (archived copy — the original link is no longer available) Abstract This work shows that if the concept of the quantization of magnetic flux is included, a combination of electromagnetism and orbital mechanics predicts the quantized energy levels of the hydrogen atom. Overview Bruce Harvey argues that classical electromagnetism combined with orbital mechanics predicts the quantised energy levels of hydrogen, provided one further ingredient is admitted: the quantisation of magnetic flux. He sets the historical scene — Thomson's electron, Rutherford's nuclear atom, radioactivity apparently breaking Newtonian determinism, Lorentz's unfinished programme of an electromagnetic origin for mass — and identifies the obstacle to a classical atom. An orbiting electron forms a current loop and, by Maxwell's laws, should be radiating vast amounts of energy. The missing link, on his account, is that the moving electron generates two distinct magnetic fields: one surrounding it, with flux lines circling its direction of motion and travelling with it, and one sitting stationary within the current loop formed by its orbit. Magnetic flux, he notes, has been discovered to behave as if quantised — short lengths of tinned copper wire cooled to superconducting temperatures trap flux in integer multiples of a value now called the quantum fluxoid. The calculation follows. The current in the orbital loop is I = e ν, with ν the orbital frequency, and classical physics gives the energy in a loop of flux Φ threaded by a current as ½Φ I . Setting Φ = n Φ 0 and taking Φ 0 = 2.07 × 10 −15 Wb — the measured superconducting flux quantum, h /2 e — Harvey finds that the energy content of the quantum fluxoid is exactly half the kinetic energy of the electron on Bohr's figures.
