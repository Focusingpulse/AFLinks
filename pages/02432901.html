Observation influences subtle energies at a distance. The "Double Slit Experiment" is the classic experiment that really turned the world upside down in the world of physics. For a more technical explanation, see this The Feynman Double Slit Richard Feynman, The Character of Physical Law (MIT, 1965), Chapter 6 Richard P. Feynman, Robert B. Leighton and Matthew Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics (Addison-Wesley, 1963), Vol III, Chapter 1 (A simplified explanation is disussed in a video linked to below) It proves that the very act of observing something not only can but actually DOES influence the properties of things at a distance. Electrons beaming through two slits without an observer makes the electrons act like waves and as soon as these probability waves are OBSERVED, the electrons act like particles...the opposite state of a wave. Basically meaning that what we focus on collapses a probability into actually being what we perceive. It is an ancient concept that philosophically has been pondered for ages.
