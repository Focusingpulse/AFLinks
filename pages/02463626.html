It develops the Aether Physics Model (APM) 2 APM/QMU PRIMITIVES USED IN THIS PAPER 1 Abstract The double-slit experiment is typically presented as evidence that microscopic objects are described by a complex wave whose amplitude superposes [Feynman et al., 1965]. In the Aether Physics Model (APM), the same observed fringe buildup is explained by separating (i) transport in space, (ii) boundary transfer at apertures, and (iii) discrete gate events in matter [Thomson III, 2026c,b]. Light transport is treated as a conserved delivery content ligt distributed over expanding photon fronts, while a slit-mask is modeled as a geometry-dependent transfer operator T acting on the incident delivery density lint (light intensity) [Thomson III, 2026a, Thomson, 2025]. At the detection screen, photoelectric events occur only when a resonance gate G is open, and the spatial event rate follows the gated effective intensity linteff [Thomson III, 2026b]. Interference fringes therefore arise as a pre-existing standing-wave modulation in lint(ν; r) produced by the two transfer channels, while the discreteness of detection is attributed to gate events. The model yields a direct QMU prediction for fringe visibility in terms of the coherence-window parameter τg∗ = τg Fq , namely that interference requires path-difference ∆ℓ ≲ λC τg∗ [Thomson III, 2026b]. A key operational refinement is that the observed visibility rolloff length is predicted to be the smaller of a transport coherence length Lc and a detector gate coherence length Lg = λC τg∗ : Lrolloff ≈ min(Lc , Lg ) [Mandel and Wolf, 1995, Thomson III, 2026b].
