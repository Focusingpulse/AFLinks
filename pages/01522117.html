Title Update on the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Ian J Cowan Keywords Special Relativity , Electrodynamics, Electromagnetic wave propagation, Relativity and gravitation Published 2003 Journal Galilean Electrodynamics Volume 14 Number 6 No. of pages 10 Pages 103-110 Abstract This paper considers experiments up to the present that have been concerned in independent ways with electrodynamic measurements. If electromagnetic energy generated on the Earth is considered to be approximately carried by its co-ordinate frame in its solar orbit, this, together with a reanalysis of classical electromagnetic theory retaining the small, but non-zero, term involving the electric conductivity of free space, leads to a satisfactory explanation of observed phenomena, and to a necessary reassessment of relativistic and cosmological physical theories developed during the last century. PACS: 03.30.+p, 04.40.Nr, 41.20.Jb, 95.30.Sf Also (revised and updated) in Proceedings of the VIII International Scientific Conference, Space, Time, Gravitation, Baltic State Technical University, pp. 143-195, Saint-Petersburg, Russia, 2005. Overview Published in Galilean Electrodynamics in 2003 and given in revised form at the St Petersburg conference on Space, Time and Gravitation in August 2004, this is Ian Cowan's fullest statement of an alternative to special relativity built on two propositions. The first is empirical: that the classic interferometric and electrical experiments were null only with respect to the earth's orbital speed, and that where modern equipment has been sensitive enough it has shown residuals at the scale of the earth's rotational speed of a few hundred metres per second. The second is theoretical: that Maxwell's conductivity term σ 0 , routinely set to zero for free space, is small but non-zero, so that electromagnetic waves dissipate as they propagate.
