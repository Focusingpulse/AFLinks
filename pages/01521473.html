Tests of the Lorentz Ether Theory by Stephan Gift Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Date 13 September 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Tests of the Lorentz Ether Theory by Stephan Gift was a live session on 13 September 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The Lorentz Ether Theory (LET) is subjected to five tests: 1925 Michelson-Gale experiment, 2003 Wang experiment, GPS Clock operation, GPS Clock synchronization, and application of the Correspondence principle. Hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary Among critics of special relativity, Lorentz's ether theory is often held out as the theory that should have won: same transformations, same predictions, but with a real medium and a real preferred frame behind them. Stephan Gift , returning to the society after several sessions on the Selleri transformations, here argued that this refuge does not exist. He put Lorentz's theory through five tests and reported that it fails all five. Host Ian J Cowan introduced the session by tracing the transformations back past Lorentz to Voigt, Larmor and FitzGerald, and noting that Lorentz — unlike Einstein — treated contraction as a real physical effect on the electron, not an appearance. The theory on trial Gift took his statement of the theory from the second edition of Lorentz's The Theory of Electrons (1916; first edition 1909). Lorentz built on three postulates: length contraction, clock retardation, and Einstein clock synchronisation — synchronising distant clocks by assuming a constant light speed for the signal that travels between them. From these he obtained the Lorentz transformations, with the rest frame S understood as the ether. Mainstream opinion holds the resulting theory to be operationally indistinguishable from special relativity, so it inherits the transverse Doppler effect, relativistic beaming, Compton scattering and the rest.
