A Testable "Spacetime Chaperone" Mechanism from Hierarchical Mirror Compactification Authors: Zhi Cheng email: gzchengzhi@hotmail.com Abstract The recent detection of a 400 GeV photon arriving approximately 0.4 days after the prompt emission of GRB 221009A by the Purple Mountain Observatory (PMO) team presents a significant challenge to standard astrophysical models. While a conventional explanation invoking cascade radiation from pair-produced electrons deflected by intergalactic magnetic fields has been proposed, this mechanism predicts no associated neutrino signal. Here, we present an alternative explanation within the framework of hierarchical mirror compactification of spacetime. In this framework, a dynamical "spacetime chaperone" field, arising from the modulus associated with the neutrino interaction layer (Layer V, 𝐸 ∼ 107 − 108 GeV), induces an energy-dependent modification of the photon dispersion relation. We demonstrate that a delayed 400 GeV photon is a natural consequence of this mechanism, yielding a required quantum gravity scale of ΛQG ≈ 2.8 × 108 GeV, in remarkable agreement with the independent energy scale of Layer V. We provide a detailed comparative analysis with the cascade model and propose a decisive experimental test: the presence or absence of delayed high-energy neutrinos in coincidence with the delayed photon. This test, feasible with current IceCube data, renders our model sharply falsifiable and distinct from conventional astrophysical interpretations. 1.
