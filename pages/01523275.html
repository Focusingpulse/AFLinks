Joseph Levy Born 1936 Residence Saint-Germain-lès-Corbeil, France Nationality French Known for Aether theory; the distinction between measured and real values in relativity Scientific career Fields Physicist Joseph Levy (born 1936) is a French physicist who came to the foundations of relativity from experimental work in magnetic resonance. He argues that measurement in physics does not report reality directly — that rods, clocks and masses in absolute motion are themselves altered, so that what an experiment yields depends on the absolute speed of the frame it is made in — and that a preferred frame, an aether , must be restored to physics if the apparent values are to be translated back into real ones. He is co-editor, with Michael C Duffy and Volodymyr V Krasnoholovets , of the three-volume series Ether Space-Time and Cosmology , and has written two books of his own on the subject. Biography Education Graduate studies in physics — Université Paris 6 Diplôme d'études approfondies in theoretical physics — Université Paris 6 Ph.D. in physics , with honours — Interaction spin-réseau et tenseur spectroscopique de l'électron , researched at the Centre de mécanique ondulatoire appliquée (CNRS) Diploma in radioisotope work — Institut national des sciences et techniques nucléaires, Saclay Career Levy did his military service at the Centre d'études nucléaires of Fontenay-aux-Roses, where he was put in charge of work on electron spin resonance, and published a number of papers in peer-reviewed journals on that and related questions in quantum physics. His interest in relativity was longstanding but critical, and from 1990 he began attending the international conferences devoted to the subject, among them Physical Interpretations of Relativity Theory (PIRT), sponsored by the British Society for the Philosophy of Science, where he has lectured several times.
