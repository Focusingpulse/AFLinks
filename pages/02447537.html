The reason that a person would not "gravitate" towards the rim is that gravity seems to appear as an acceleration FIELD in "free" space. Inertia is a force acting on/within mass but not, apparently, on/within "free" space (no mass - no inertia?). In fact now that I think about it maybe there is a solution to the Einstein windowless lift "experiment": An occupant of the accelerating lift, without the help of technological devices, could not decide whether he weighed 70 KG due to the effects of gravity or inertia. But, gravity is an acceleration FEILD with an intensity that decreases with distance from the mass giving rise to it. All we need is a sensitive gravitometer to detect the change in field strenght between the floor of the lift and the ceiling. If no deviation between floor and ceiling can be detected then we are experiencing acceleration. If we detect a change in force (weight) between floor and ceiling then we are experiencing the effects of a gravitational feild. The point here is, I think, that a gravitational field is capable of permeating space whereas inertia, apparently, can not. Hmmmmmm. Bill. Previous message: Jerry W.
