A Geometry-Only Tension Probe for Reverse Exploration of Single-Block SHA-256 &nbsp; &nbsp; Exact one-step reverse algebra, a zero-score theorem for true schedule words, and empirical evidence of a nontrivial hot/cold field Driven by Dean Kulik April 8, 2026 &nbsp; Proof boundary This paper proves exact one-step reverse closure conditioned on W[t], proves a zero-score theorem for the true word under the exported geometry bundle, and reports empirical hot/cold behavior. It does not claim a proof of SHA-256 inversion. &nbsp; Abstract This paper formalizes and checks the mathematics behind a one-round reverse probe for single-block SHA-256. The probe is explicitly separated into two logically distinct parts: (i) a geometry-only hot/cold score that uses admissible side data only, and (ii) a hidden verifier that knows the true schedule word and exists only for evaluation. The reverse algebra is exact once the next state x[t+1] and the candidate schedule word W[t] are given. We prove an exact one-step reverse closure theorem under that condition, prove a zero-score theorem for the true word under the exported side-observable bundle, and show why these statements do not amount to a proof of SHA-256 inversion. Empirically, the companion notebook shows a strong gradient: for the demo message b"abc", the true W[63] = 0x12b1edeb receives score 0 while sampled false guesses score substantially higher on average; across six test messages the true word ranked first among 5,000 sampled candidates in each trial. At the same time, low-scoring false positives remain, so the present construction is best interpreted as a tension field rather than a full reverse oracle. 1. A lock picker does not know the correct pin setting in advance; instead, they apply tension and read which candidate settings bind cleanly and which ones fight the mechanism.
