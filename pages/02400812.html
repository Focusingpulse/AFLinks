GC2024-STP079 Spatial-energy dependencies of maximum electron fluxes of the outer radiation belt during geomagnetic activity Danil S. Gruzdov1,2 , Vladimir V. Kalegaev1,2 , Natalia A. Vlasova1 , Alexandra R. Ivanova1,2 , Demekhov G. Andrey3 , Bazilevskaya A. Galina4,1 , Tatyana A. Yahnina3 1 Skobeltsyn Institute of Nuclear Physics, Lomonosov Moscow State University, Russia 2 Lomonosov Moscow State University 3 Polar Geophysical Institute of the Kola Scientific Center of the Russian Academy of Sciences 4 Lebedev Physical Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences. P.N. Lebedev Physical Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences gruzdov.ds20@physics.msu.ru The Earth’s outer radiation belt (EORB) is highly dynamical domain in the magnetosphere. The most significant changes of the outer radiation belt are influenced by solar wind and accompanied by geomagnetic disturbances. During such events strong variations of the energetic electron fluxes by several orders of magnitude can be observed. The outer radiation belt spatial parameters like EORB maximum or its high latitude boundary are also changed. Dynamical changes of trapped (in the orbit of the Van Allen Probes A spacecraft) and quasi-trapped (in the low polar orbit of the Meteor M2 satellite) EORB electron fluxes were studied at different phases of geomagnetic storms of different intensities. The temporal and spatial profiles of electron fluxes in different energetic ranges (>0.1, >0.7 and >2 MeV) were reconstructed from satellites measurements obtained during several radiation belt crossings.
