Richard Lawrence Garwin

· archived # 1,461,817
There are physicists, and then there are physicists. There are engineers, and then there are engineers. There are government advisors, and then there are government advisors. And then there’s Dick Garwin. [Ashutosh Jogalekar] Richard Lawrence "Dick" Garwin (1928–2025) was a polymath American physicist often described as "the most influential scientist you've never heard of". At 21, he earned a PhD under Enrico Fermi. Just two years later, Garwin designed the first working hydrogen bomb 'Ivy_Mike' the device that opened the darkest chapter of the nuclear age. But what made Garwin extraordinary wasn’t just brilliance it was restraint. For decades he quietly advised US presidents from Truman to Obama, known as the scientist you called when you needed the truth, even if you didn’t like it. He helped shoot down billion-dollar bad ideas, pushed for arms control, and spent his life trying to reduce the dangers of the power he helped unleash. His work also touched everything from MRI and GPS to cryptography and space systems. A rare kind of scientist: technically unstoppable, morally serious.
