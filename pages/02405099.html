Memoirs of a French martyr Let's now turn to written sources, namely to see how the "Moscow fire" is described by the French, who were in the Russian capital at that time. Here, for example, what Lieutenant Charles Artois of Napoleon's army wrote in his diaries. That day, the dim sun illuminated Moscow with golden light. Suddenly, a second sun appeared just above the true sun, it was so bright, it blinded my eyes. and burned the face of Paul Berger, who was relaxing on the balcony. Our house and roof started smoking so we had to flush them with water. In other estates, that were closer to the "false sun", fires broke out... A week later, after the second sun appeared, Paul wrote that all the officers and men began to lose hair, that the men and horses were sick and weak, so the administration decided to leave Moscow and it was received with great relief. Pavlos even described the retreat as very strange. From his archive it shows that the French soldiers suffered not only from Russian glaciers and rebel raids, but first and foremost from some strange disease that they had infected in Moscow. People couldn’t eat, covered in ulcers and sores, leading to hundreds of deaths every day, and horses were weak and collapsed. Paul Artois himself returned to France disabled, resigned and soon died of the "Russian transmission" at the age of thirty-two. According to the Moscow publication "Russians and Napoleon Bonaparte" (1814), the French lost more than thirty thousand people during their forty-day stay in Moscow, that is as much as in Borodino.
