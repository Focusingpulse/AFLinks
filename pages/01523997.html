Tao Lin Born July 2, 1983 Residence United States Nationality American Known for Journalism on alternative cosmologies; critique of the Big Bang model; advocacy of dissident science and self-healing Scientific career Fields Cosmology , science writing, natural health Tao Lin (Chinese: 林韜; born July 2, 1983) is a Taiwanese-American novelist, poet, essayist and artist who has taken a strong public interest in dissident science. Since 2018 his work has shifted from fiction toward nonfiction concerned with natural health, environmental toxins, and the sociology of scientific orthodoxy, and in 2022 he published a widely read journalistic survey of non-Big-Bang cosmologies. He is not a professional physicist; his contribution to the dissident community is as a writer who brings suppressed and marginalized scientific models to a large mainstream literary audience. Biography Lin was born on July 2, 1983 in Alexandria, Virginia, to a Taiwanese-American family, and grew up in suburban Orlando, Florida. He began publishing poetry and fiction in 2006 and founded the independent press Muumuu House in 2008. His novels include Eeeee Eee Eeee (2007), Shoplifting from American Apparel (2009), Richard Yates (2010) and Taipei (2013), the last published by Vintage. His 2018 nonfiction book Trip: Psychedelics, Alienation, and Change documents his recovery from pharmaceutical drug dependence and his engagement with the ideas of Terence McKenna. His fourth novel, Leave Society (2021), follows a novelist researching pharmaceuticals, environmental toxins, vaccines, diet and alternative models of society. Lin is also a visual artist, known for a long-running series of hand-drawn mandalas, which he has exhibited and sold online. He has lectured on his writing and art at Vassar College, the Kansas City Art Institute, the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill and other universities and museums.
