Re-evaluating Nuclear Binding Energy & Mass Defect: A Proton-Electron Composite Model Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Edo Kaal Date 15 August 2026 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Re-evaluating Nuclear Binding Energy & Mass Defect: A Proton-Electron Composite Model was a live session presented by Edo Kaal on 15 August 2026 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The Structured Atom Model (SAM) does not consider the neutron to be a fundamental particle on the same level as the proton and the electron, but a proton-electron composite, which means a very closely bound system of two particles. This has consequences for the determination of the mass-defect and the equivalence of binding-energy and mass-defect. Summary A technical session of the Saturday series, hosted by Ian Cowan, in which Edo Kaal set out the case that the neutron is not a fundamental particle but a closely bound proton–electron composite, and that treating it as fundamental hides a fixed error of 0.782 MeV in the standard accounting of nuclear binding energy. Kaal presented without video; his co-author Andreas Otte joined for the discussion. Kaal opened by introducing the team behind the Dutch foundation he set up to develop the Structured Atom Model : himself; James Sorensen , who wrote the modelling and visualisation code that Kaal called an invaluable research tool; Otte, his co-writer and co-explorer; and Jan Emming, whose facility with language carries much of the paper and book writing. Their book is The Nature of the Atom , available in print and as an e-book; Kaal stressed that the foundation is a charity and that margins were kept to the minimum the publisher would accept. The session narrowed away from the model's usual subject, the geometric build-up of nuclei, to the smallest systems: hydrogen, deuterium and the free neutron. The discrepancy In the Structured Atom Model the neutron is a proton–electron pair, or PEP.
