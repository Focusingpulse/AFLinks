A NEW CLASS OF PRIME NUMBERS BASED ON TWO DISTINCT REGULAR ALTERNATING GAP SEQUENCES AND THEIR PREDICTIVE PROPERTIES. Christoper Muoki Mututu mututuchristoper@gmail.com November 20, 2025 Abstract. Prime numbers have fascinated mathematicians for centuries due to their inherent unpredictability and fundamental role in number theory. Despite extensive research into their distribution and patterns, primes continue to surprise and challenge scholars. The Riemann Hypothesis, one of the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics, is a prime example of this unpredictability. This paper introduces a newly discovered class of prime numbers which under two distinct alternating gap sequences – 2,4,2 and 12,14,12 - predict the occurrence of six additional primes, either forward or in reverse, a phenomenon previously unknown in the study of prime numbers. This work offers not only a novel approach to prime generation but also introduces the idea that primes themselves can act as building blocks for other primes, leading to new methods of understanding prime distribution and its profound implications for both theoretical and applied mathematics. 1.Introduction The search for regularities in prime numbers has led to several well-known results, such as the Prime Number Theorem and conjectures like the twin prime conjecture. However, few discoveries have revealed such precise, reproducible patterns in the gaps between primes. In this work, we present a class of primes exhibiting two alternating gap sequences — 2,4,2 and 12,14,12 — that allow for the prediction of six additional primes from certain “seed” primes. These primes are extremely rare: among approximately one billion primes analyzed, only 66 ending in 9 and 61 ending in 1 exhibited the dual-gap behavior. The remarkable property of these prime numbers is that, knowing the specific conditions under which the alternating patterns occur, one can easily predict an additional six prime numbers.
