Platonic Supremacy and Unorthodox Takes on Reality Tariq Khan Omaha, NE USA A short speculative philosophical essay presenting some unique or unorthodox descriptions of the nature of reality. A few example interpretations of reality are discussed, extending arguments from scientists including Julian Barbour, Donald D. Hoffman, and Roger Penrose involving the nature of time, consciousness, and fundamental reality. The idea of Platonic Supremacy is proposed where our reality coexists in a single universe with Platonic ideals. A universe without time is considered as well as one where Platonic ideals actually drive all conscious actions - instead of free will - as they interact with our minds. ‘The wand chooses the wizard. Unlike in a Block Universe theory, objects in time cannot be equated with trajectories in a block. And unlike in presentist theories, there is not just one real now. We have the impression of living in a world with a past because certain structures in the now we inhabit can be interpreted as traces of previous nows that have preceded our own.
