The Essential Electric Field Disturbance Nature of Electromagnetic Field and a Classical Unified Explanation of the Stern-Gerlach Experiment Abstract Classical electromagnetism separates electric fields and magnetic fields into two fundamental physical fields. To interpret the Stern-Gerlach experiment, quantum mechanics introduces ad hoc hypotheses such as intrinsic electron spin, wave function collapse, and spatial quantization, leading to a fundamental disconnect between classical and quantum physics. Conventional theories fail to reveal the physical origin of matter waves and merely regard them as intrinsic inherent properties of microscopic particles. This paper proposes a novel underlying physical mechanism: the magnetic field has no independent physical entity. It is essentially an equivalent disturbed electric field formed by the polar oscillation of charged particles due to asymmetric polarization of electron clouds in the cosmic background disturbed electric field. Macroscopic static magnetic fields are the spatio-temporal statistical average of microscopic high-frequency electric field disturbances. This study thoroughly reverses the causal relationship established in traditional quantum mechanics: matter waves do not induce electric field disturbances; instead, the cosmic background electric field disturbances trigger asymmetric distribution of atomic electron clouds, generate electric dipole polarity, and drive continuous oscillation of polar particles under electric field force, thereby producing matter wave phenomena. Based on Maxwell’s equations, this paper constructs a classical electric dipole model for atoms with odd electrons. Without adopting any quantum ad hoc assumptions, the proposed pure classical electric field dynamics completely explains all characteristics of the Stern-Gerlach experiment, including double-peak splitting, intermediate zero-probability regions, and cascaded uniform field splitting.
