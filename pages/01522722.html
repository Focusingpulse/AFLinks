David Calder Hardy Born ( 1929-04-15 ) April 15, 1929 Te Kuiti, King Country, New Zealand Residence New Zealand Nationality New Zealander Known for cosmology , big bang , infinite eternal universe Scientific career Fields Photographer , Scientist David Calder Hardy (born 15 April 1929) is a New Zealand photographer, milking-machine and pump engineer, and independent cosmological theorist. He is best known as the author of Genesis Continuous , a self-published model of an eternal, infinite universe that he advanced as an alternative to the big bang theory. Biography Hardy was born on 15 April 1929 at the Te Kuiti Hospital annex in the King Country region of New Zealand. An only child raised on a dairy farm, he described his formal schooling as limited, interrupted by illness and by farm work; he nonetheless recalled excelling in agriculture, practical science and elementary geometry at secondary school. When his father was injured in an accident, Hardy left school to run the family farm. In 1946 he joined the Air Training Corps and was accepted into the Royal New Zealand Air Force, reporting to Wigram Air Force Base near Christchurch, but he was among a large portion of the intake released shortly afterward as servicemen returned from overseas following the Second World War. He later worked as a photographer, running his own photographic business, and as an engineer of milking machines and pumps. Among his engineering work, he designed a conversion kit giving a positive-displacement pump a variable flow rate, which was recorded in the patent journal. Hardy married Ann Compton on 7 February 1955 at the registry office in Tauranga, New Zealand; the couple later divorced. Scientific contributions Hardy's central work is Genesis Continuous , a cosmological model he first set out in 1973 and expanded in a more detailed 1995 edition.
