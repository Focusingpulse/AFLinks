Classical and Relativistic Derivation of the Sagnac Effect

· archived # 1,522,633
Title Classical and Relativistic Derivation of the Sagnac Effect Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Wolfgang Engelhardt Published 2013 Abstract Both the classical and the relativistic composition law for velocities are applied to re-calculate the Sagnac Effect. The ensuing formulae for the fringe shift are found to differ already in first order of v/c. Whilst the classical formula is validated by interferometric measurements and verified by the GPS-system, this is not the case for the relativistic result. Overview Wolfgang Engelhardt, retired from the Max-Planck-Institut für Plasmaphysik at Garching, published this short 2013 paper arguing that special relativity, correctly applied, predicts no Sagnac effect — and that since the effect is measured and is used in GPS, the theory's basic postulate is at stake. The argument is set out in two parallel derivations. Section 2 treats the rotating interferometer classically. Adapting the Galilean transformation to a circular path by writing d s ′ = d s − v ₀d t with d t ′ = d t , he obtains the composition law v φ ′ = v φ − v ₀, and hence travel times L /( c ∓ v ₀) for the two beams and the standard fringe shift ΔZ = 4 A · Ω / cλ ₀. He notes that Michelson and Gale verified this in 1925 by varying the enclosed area, and reads the result as showing "an 'ether wind' must exist due to the rotation of the earth". Section 3 repeats the exercise with the Lorentz transformation. Writing the differential form d t ′ = γ(d t − v ₀d s / c ²) and d s ′ = γ(d s − v ₀d t ), and dividing, he obtains the relativistic composition law v φ ′ = ( v φ − v ₀)/(1 − v φ v ₀/ c ²) which for v φ = c gives v φ ′ = c . He concludes that both beams travel at c in the rotating frame, so Δt′ = L / c − L / c = 0, and that "the SRT correctly applied to a rotating Sagnac Interferometer does not predict the Sagnac Effect."
