Northrop and the YB-35: While working at Northrop Corporation during WWII, Marquardt discovered that waste heat from engine cooling systems on the YB-35 "Flying Wing" could be harnessed for thrust, sparking his deep interest in the ramjet principle. Founding Marquardt Aircraft (1944): Roy started his company with just $1,000, initially operating out of a grocery stall in West Los Angeles. Ramjet Innovation: His team hand-hammered their first 20-inch ramjet engine over a curbstone; it was delivered to the Navy in 1945. Aviation Milestones: Marquardt's engines powered the first crewed flight using only ramjets on a Lockheed P-80 Shooting Star in 1948. The Marquardt 20-inch diameter ramjet, often designated as the C-20 or RJ30-MA-6, was a pioneering American supersonic engine developed in the mid-1940s by Roy Marquardt. Primarily used for testing and early missile development, these engines powered test flights on P-51 Mustangs and F-80 Shooting Stars, producing roughly 1,320 lbs of thrust, at 580 mph. Key Details of the 20" Marquardt Ramjet:Initial Design: The first 20-inch model was created in 1945 for the U.S. Navy, with the casing famously hand-hammered over a curbstone. Designation: Known as the C20-0.85, it was an athodyd-type ramjet designed for subsonic and low-supersonic flight.
