Overview This is Section 11 — headed "Illegal Statics" — of an unpublished book Yuri N. Keilman wrote in 2000. The wiki abstract is his own note on it: "Electron, Proton, Neutron solutions were obtained in violation of my 'Extended Electrodynamics'. In 2008 was published a much better version of the theory." The section opens candidly: "Sometimes we want to jump over the troubles." His Extended Electrodynamics imposes a boundary condition at the interface with vacuum, requiring both μ² and its derivative across the boundary to vanish. He proposes to violate it, and identifies two ways. A soft violation keeps μ² continuous but lets its derivative jump, so the governing equation fails at a single surface with a finite left-hand side — a defect confined to a set of measure zero, which does not affect volume integrals. A hard violation lets μ² itself jump, so the derivative becomes infinite and the equation's left-hand side is infinite rather than zero. He chooses the hard violation, on the grounds that "it is the same order like the use of a point charge but even better because the integral of energy will not turn infinite." The advantage claimed is decisive: "we will get a static model for any particle with any charge, spin, mass, and magnetic moment." He then rewrites Gauss's theorem for a vector with a jump across an internal surface, finds that the resulting surface terms are proportional to certain scalars that often vanish or integrate to zero by symmetry, and computes the global quantities. The consequence he flags is the important one. Evaluating the integrals with α = 1 and k 0 = 1, he obtains A = ∫ T 00 d V and B = ∫ T 11 d V
