PRIMER An emerging clock mechanism in a hydrozoan jellyfish Ezio Rosato * Division of Genetics and Genome Biology, School of Biological and Biomedical Sciences, College of Life Sciences, University of Leicester, Leicester, United Kingdom * er6@leicester.ac.uk The timed release of gametes is an important feature of marine organisms, and in hydrozoan jellyfish is usually controlled by light. A recent study in PLOS Biology reveals an emerging endogenous clock controlling rhythmic egg release in a novel hydrozoan species. For many marine species that fertilize externally, eggs, and sperm must find each other in the open ocean, while avoiding inter-species mixing. Light-dark cycles provide an obvious synchronizer, being a common cue which all individuals can align to [1]. Moreover, synchronization allows species to occupy distinct temporal niches within the same area (like reserving a table at the restaurant), reducing the risk of inter-crossing. A timing mechanism that relies on environmental cues resembles an hourglass. The stimulus sets the time (turns the hourglass), and at the end of the count-down a new stimulus is required to initiate another cycle.
