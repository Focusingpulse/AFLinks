On the calculation of both power and moment required of motors to drive a robot arm in a controllable manner Aloys J. Sipers* , Joh. J. Sauren July 14, 2025 Abstract In this letter we use complex numbers to calculate both the power and the mechanical moment required of robot motors to drive a robot arm subjected to loads on joints and on multiple parts. 1 Expressing mechanical power with complex numbers We define a robot arm as a construction consisting of two parts, X1 and X2 , see Figure 1. Part X1 is directed from joint 1 to joint 2, part X2 is directed from joint 2 to joint 3. Joint 1 is at rest, joint 2 and joint 3 are freely displaceable. The required powers of the robot motors, located at joint 1 and joint 2, respectively, depend on the forces F2 and F3 excerted at joints 2 and 3. The mechanical moments M1 and M2 drive and rotate the robot parts X1 and X2 . Figure 1: One motor is located at joint 1, a second motor is located at joint 2. The gripper is located at joint 3. The robot parts X1 and X2 are mechanically at equilibrium.
