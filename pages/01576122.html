FCD-RB — Part XXVII: Dipolar Anchoring, Velocity-Dependent Braking, and the Leptonic Origin of Light Ricardo J. Miralles — independent researcher, Valencia, Spain FCD-RB Working Paper — August 2026 Abstract This paper develops two complementary hypotheses within the FCD-RB framework. The first is that the mirror brane should not be represented on the visible brane as an additional gravitating mass in the conventional sense. Its observable influence is instead proposed to arise from the dynamical response of the trans-brane dipolar anchor and from screening in the Einstein-Rosen (ER) bridge connecting the two branes. The hypothesis has two components: a velocity￾dependent braking of anchor displacement, and a topological fixation of isolated dipoles that contributes structural rigidity to a galactic halo. When extended toward high velocities, the same braking is proposed to produce an asymptotic limiting velocity identified with c, with increasing resistance interpreted as apparent inertia rather than as an intrinsic growth of mass. The process is postulated to be thermodynamically indifferent: it is a geometrical redistribution of the state of the bond, not an exothermic or endothermic conversion. The second hypothesis replaces an earlier description of light by a leptonic-vibration picture. Light is proposed to be an organized excitation transmitted through the ER channel that also supports the electronic resonance at the minority-mass end of the quark dipole. It can therefore couple to dipolar anchors along its route. Two non-exclusive redshift channels follow: cumulative anchor coupling and a geometric rescaling associated with the effective inter-brane separation Re f f . The latter is now given a concrete local waveguide form, with cutoff ωc= π c / Re f f and a path transfer function. This is a candidate mechanism, not a data-validated redshift law.
