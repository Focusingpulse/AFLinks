Water is a changed texture of light. Overview A note on the source. This entry is a PowerPoint file rather than a paper, and it is a short one. Extracting its text yields about 570 distinct words — slide titles, one four-column table, and a page of speaker notes — much of it repeated across the deck. There is no derivation, no reference list and no figure caption that survives extraction. The summary below therefore reconstructs the argument from the numbers, which turn out to be enough: the table can be reverse-engineered exactly, and once it is, the whole scheme is visible. The claim. Matter emanates from ether, and water is "a changed texture of light". Concretely: the 13.6 eV Rydberg photon is the building block of both the proton and the oxide ion, so a water molecule is an assembly of "millions of photons" clustered about seeds of ether. The seed ether "is what scientists and electricians measure as electric charge, Q ", and the number of such seeds is what chemistry calls the oxidation state. Fernandes offers electrolysis of water as experimental proof that the ether exists, and the Schwarzschild radius as the size an electron takes when it collapses back into ether — evolution into matter, involution into ether, with mass conserved throughout. The argument The Rydberg photon The starting observation is a numerical coincidence. The first ionization energy of oxygen is 13.618 eV; the figure quoted for hydrogen is 13.6057 eV. Since both sit at the Rydberg energy, Fernandes takes it as "safe to assume that the Rydberg photon comprises both the proton and oxide ions respectively".
