Are they really "holograms?" W. Beaty 1997 Here's why I call these images "holograms" In conventional "white light" or "Benton Rainbow" holograms, each point on the photographed object generates a particular interference pattern on the film. This interference pattern is the basic "pixel" of the hologram. However, the entire "swatch" of interference fringes on the film plane might be several mm wide and a fraction of a mm tall. It's similar to a small slice of the bullseye pattern in a Gabor zoneplate lens. The many points on the object become little horizontal swatches of interference fringes on the film.
