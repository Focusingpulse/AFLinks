GTM2026-main028 Non-Cooperative Task Scheduling Game with Linear Externalities for Volunteer Computing Natalia Kukushkina1 1 Institute of Applied Mathematical Research of the Karelian Scientific Center of the Russian Academy of Sciences kukushkina@krc.karelia.ru In the literature, a wide range of mathematical models have been derived from the KP model [1] with parallel links of different capacities. In addition to the classical model, negative externalities allow to model congestion effects [2], while positive externalities can reflect information learning benefits, cost sharing between the users, or collective action [3]. This work addresses a task scheduling model with positive externalities where the players contribute to the collective progress of solving a computational problem in volunteer computing. Different task subsets have different values for the project and different computational complexities. Consider a computational system S = S(N, p, e) with the set of tasks N and the set of externalities e. N is divided into disjoint subsets N1 , ..., Nn (N = {1, 2, ..., n}); for each subset Ni , pi is the rate of obtaining useful results (e.g., the hits of virtual drug screening), 0 < pi ≤ 1. Coefficient eik > 0, where i, k ∈ N, i ≠ k, expresses the contribution of the load on task subset Nk (e.g., information exchange) into the performance of computations on Ni .
