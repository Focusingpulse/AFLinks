At the same time, Nikola Tesla, 75-year-old inventor, vigorously attacked the Volstead law. Declaring that an increase in crime has been a by-product of prohibition, Willard said: "There are those who hold that before we can have an entirely satisfactory condition, some modification must be made of our prohibition law and I am inclined to be sympathetic with that thought. "It should be possible to so amend the law that not only would the saloon be forever abolished, but the control of the liquor traffic so established as to remove the most serious objections which exist today, and that too in such a manner as to really promote temperance, and I doubt very much if the existing law has done that." Senator Glass, a life-long abstainer, asserted that the issue of repeal should be resubmitted to the States for determination through State conventions rather than by the State Legislatures.
