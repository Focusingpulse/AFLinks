Alfred O’Rahilly by James Arathoon Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Date 29 March 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Alfred O’Rahilly by James Arathoon was a live session on 29 March 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The work of Irish academic Alfred O'Rahilly, who published his monumental Electromagnetic Theory: A Critical Examination of Fundamentals (formerly titled: Electromagnetics) in 1938. Hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary James Arathoon offered this as a preliminary sketch rather than a finished piece of research: he and Ian J Cowan hope to work through Alfred O'Rahilly 's archives at University College Cork, and Cowan had already spent a morning on the papers held at Blackrock College, which turned out to be political rather than mathematical. Much of what survives is gone anyway — a fire at Blackrock in 1984 destroyed most of O'Rahilly's personal letters and unpublished work. Cowan's video repeatedly broke the session up until he turned his camera off, which Franklin Hu diagnosed for him from the other side. The life O'Rahilly was born in County Kerry in 1884 and educated in the Jesuit system, entering as a novice in 1901 and studying to ordination — until he fell in love with his first cousin, wrote her letters that caused a stir, left, and married her. He was interned on Bere Island in 1921 for what he had written, having joined Sinn Féin in its wholly peaceful phase; he was consulted on the 1922 constitution and on the later one; he rose to the presidency of University College Cork; and after his wife's death he was finally ordained in 1955, becoming a monsignor. He died in 1969. Arathoon set him against the collapse and slow recovery of the Irish population — from a peak of about eight million before the famine to a low of 4.2 million within O'Rahilly's lifetime — and against the extraordinary run of Irish physicists: Hamilton, MacCullagh, Stokes, Kelvin, FitzGerald, Larmor.
