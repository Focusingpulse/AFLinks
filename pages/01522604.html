Title Change of Characteristics of the Light Source at its Movement Relatively Receiver Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Joseph J Smulsky Keywords light source, movement, relatively, receiver, laws of electromagnetism, Doppler effect, aberration Published 2009 No. of pages 6 Abstract The influence of light source on the receiver as electromagnetic interaction is considered. The mechanical influence of the moving charged body on motionless one is defined by the experimental laws of electrodynamics. These laws determine the changes of parameters of the light source which moves relatively the receiver. The laws of change of light frequency and its direction of the moving source are derived from the laws of electromagnetism. At small velocity of source movement they coincide with classical results: the Doppler effect and phenomenon of aberration. The interaction of the source and receiver depends only on their velocity of movement relatively each other. There is no world medium, the relative movement to which would influence on the characteristics of light source. Overview Joseph J. Smulsky, of the Institute of Earth's Cryosphere at the Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences, argues that the changes in a light source's observed frequency and direction when it moves relative to a receiver follow directly from the experimental laws of electromagnetism, with no need for a propagation model, a world medium, or the theory of relativity. This is the longer, six-page 2009 version of work he also presented to the Natural Philosophy Alliance in 2010; the abstracts are identical, and the companion page Change of Characteristics of a Light Source with its Movement Relative to a Receiver covers the shorter version. His framing is that light is simply electromagnetic action of one body on another, no different in kind from the action of a transmitting aerial on a receiving one.
