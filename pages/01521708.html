Title The Geometry of Quantum Mechanics Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Richard Oldani Keywords quantum mechanics , geometry, Quantum Theory Published 2008 No. of pages 4 Abstract It is shown that the strange mathematics of quantum mechanics can be accounted for if it describes the interaction of three vector fields; nucleus, electron, and photon. A state vector is formed as the combination of two of the three vector fields. This yields an infi-nite number of possible solutions, the probability amplitudes. The remaining vector field, or operator, is then applied to the state vec-tor to obtain an infinite number of possible values for the physical variable, the eigenvalues. Combining the vector fields in a different order yields two distinct, but mathematically equivalent solutions, matrix mechanics and wave mechanics. Overview The Geometry of Quantum Mechanics is a four-page paper by Richard Oldani , presented at the 15th conference of the Natural Philosophy Alliance in 2008. It is a companion to his " Energy-Time Uncertainty " and shares its central premise, that the photon is created during excitation and is therefore present before emission begins. The proposal is that the formalism of quantum mechanics has the structure it does because atomic emission is a three-body problem — nucleus, electron and photon — rather than the two-body problem it is conventionally treated as. Oldani observes that the classical three-body problem cannot be solved by combining its three constituent two-body problems, but argues that in quantum mechanics the difficulty evaporates: since the position of the nucleus relative to the laboratory and the orientations of electron and photon relative to it can be disregarded, the analysis reduces to "spatial and temporal difference measurements among the three field sources," and the usual method of combining pairs then works.
