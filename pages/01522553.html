Brian Ahern Nationality American Alma mater Massachusetts Institute of Technology Known for Cold Fusion , Nanomagnetism, Ball Lightning Scientific career Fields Materials science , Physics Brian S. Ahern is an American materials scientist and physicist known for his research in Low Energy Nuclear Reactions (LENR), also called Cold Fusion , and for a theory connecting nanomagnetism and energy localization to anomalous excess-heat effects. He holds a PhD in materials science from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and was co-inventor on one of the earliest patents related to cold fusion. Biography He spent roughly 17 years as a senior scientist in research and development for the United States Air Force, working at the Rome Laboratory at Hanscom Air Force Base. Over the course of his career he was credited as an inventor on more than two dozen patents. He is associated with Hydroelectron Ventures Inc. Education Ph.D. in materials science — Massachusetts Institute of Technology His academic interests there included BCS theory and superconductivity. Scientific contributions Ahern's work focuses on the behavior of nanoscale particles and their possible role in producing anomalous heat. He has argued that magnetism exhibits amplified effects in the approximately 3 to 12 nanometer size range, and he has proposed that this "amplified nanomagnetism," together with energy localization and anharmonic collective oscillations, could account for the excess energy reported in cold fusion and LENR experiments. His nanofusion concept involves dispersions of nanosize particles containing roughly 1,000 to 3,000 atoms, loaded with hydrogen or deuterium and stimulated by electrical pulses. In 2012 he prepared an in-depth report on nano-scale cold fusion for the Electric Power Research Institute (EPRI).
