For several weeks workmen have been setting poles and stringing steel cables on the Erie Canal at Black Rock, near Buffalo, preparatory to testing the value of electricity for towing canal boats in place of the historic mule. Richard Lamb, who devised the motor that is to be used, has been giving much time and attention to the work, and there is now no doubt that it will be ready by September 24, the date stipulated for the test. It is provided in the contract between Mr. Lamb and the Niagara General Electric Company, upon whose order the trial section has been erected, that the boats must be moved at a six-mile-an-hour rate as a condition to the making of a final contract for further equipment of the canal with the cableways. No trouble is expected in reaching and maintaining the speed required, but there is some question in the minds of those who have given careful attention to canal navigation whether an ordinary canalboat can be propelled at a six-mile rate through a canal, whether by cable way or other device, without serious damage to the banks by the wash from the boat. Mr. Lamb says that in his opinion the models of the boats will gradually be changed to meet the new conditions, and that probably canal boats with “spoon-shaped” bows and metal bottoms will be used eventually.
