GC2026-STP061 Integral characteristics of the geomagnetic field as a tool for investigating solar–terrestrial relationships Denis A. Ukolov1 , Nikolai N. Semakov1 1 Novosibirsk National Research State University d.ukolov@g.nsu.ru In this study, the first integral parameter is defined as the calculated coordinates of the pole of the equivalent radial dipole. The second integral characteristic is the local magnetic constant, whose value does not depend on the orientation of the equivalent dipole [1]. The equivalent magnetic dipole is understood as a dipole located near the center of the Earth and producing the magnetic field observed at a given point. The equivalent dipole is characterized by the magnitude of its magnetic moment, the distance from the dipole center to the observation point, and its orientation relative to the Earth's rotation axis [2, 3]. The advantage of analyzing the behavior of these integral parameters, as compared with analyzing individual elements of the geomagnetic field, lies in the transition from a more complex and sometimes contradictory triple dependence of the measured elements to a simpler double dependence of the integral characteristics. The calculated coordinates of the pole of the equivalent dipole do not depend on the magnitude of its magnetic moment. In turn, the local magnetic constant does not depend on the orientation of the equivalent dipole and is determined solely by the magnitude of the magnetic moment and the distance from the dipole center to the observation point [1].
