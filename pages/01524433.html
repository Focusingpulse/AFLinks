What to Keep in Physics & Cosmology Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker David de Hilster Date 19 November 2022 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording What to Keep in Physics & Cosmology was a live session presented by David de Hilster on 19 November 2022 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Big bang, relativity, dark matter, dark energy, the neutrino, black holes, the higgs boson, quarks, strings... What to keep? What to throw away? Join in this fascinating discussion of what is important in physics and cosmology today. Summary A panel discussion led by David de Hilster around a single slide: a list of about a dozen areas of physics, cosmology and mathematics that members of the society dispute, and the question of which of them, if any, should be kept. The session also announced the new fortnightly arrangement — general topics with de Hilster on one Saturday, more technical sessions hosted by Ian J Cowan and Franklin on the next — and the first Lifetime Achievement Award, to Nick Percival , a fortnight off. The framing De Hilster's diagnosis had two parts. The first was Einstein setting a bad precedent: rather than observing and then modelling, he began by legislating what the universe must do — nothing faster than light, the same physics in every frame, a constant light speed — from which the strange consequences follow, and physics has been made to agree with them ever since. The second was cultural, and he drew it deliberately wider than science. The twentieth century trained everyone to accept anything put in front of them. Duchamp's urinal entered an art show; Cage wrote a piece in which the players sit silent; and in physics the going phrase became that the universe is stranger than we can understand.
