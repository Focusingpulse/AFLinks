The Reality of Gravity: Why “Curved Spacetime” Falls Flat Introduction One of the most persistent concepts taught in modern physics is that “gravity is not a force but the curvature of spacetime.” This slogan is repeated in classrooms, documentaries, and popular science books until it is accepted as self-evident truth. Yet on close inspection, it is a philosophical metaphor masquerading as physical mechanism. It explains how to calculate motions within a geometric model but not why masses interact, exchange energy, or sustain structure. By contrast, Sympathetic Vibratory Physics (SVP), as articulated by John Worrell Keely and later expanded through the Vibration Series, treats gravity as a vibratory phenomenon arising from real flows in a real medium (ether as quantum particles, fields, and forces). Rather than abstracting matter into coordinate grids, SVP models mass, energy, and force as harmonics of a universal sympathetic field — a living continuum of vibration and oscillation. This review critiques the “curved spacetime” narrative and outlines why SVP’s vibratory model is both more physically intelligible and experimentally promising. 1. Geometry Is Not Mechanism Einstein’s general relativity (GR) replaced Newton’s “force of attraction” with the idea that mass-energy tells spacetime how to curve, and curved spacetime tells matter how to move.
