Double Helicoidal Fields and the Geometric Origin of Electron Rails (FCD-RB XXXI) Ricardo J. Miralles — independent researcher, Valencia, Spain FCD-RBv4 Working Paper — August 2026 Compatibility notice. This paper follows Part XXX, where the electron was separated into a Y-gate surface mode and a delocalized orbital mode. It supplies a proposed geometrical anchoring mechanism for the latter: radiative, opposite-helicity A-end fields whose superposition fixes transverse electron rails. It also extends the earlier single-atom discussion to the azimuthal morphology of real atomic orbitals. As throughout the FCD-RB series, the papers form a time series and are not always corrected retroactively when new solutions to earlier problems emerge; when in doubt, the newest formulation governs (Newton over Galileo). The present text closes the leading-order mechanism gap between orbital delocalization and a fixed anchoring pattern, but it does not derive the rail radius, the polar orbital structure, or the B-end frequency from first principles. Abstract Part XXX established the distinction between the electron's surface mode in the Y-gate throat and its delocalized orbital mode. The present paper proposes that the orbital mode is geometrically anchored by a double helicoidal electromagnetic field emitted by the two protonic A-ends of the triplet structure. The A-ends carry opposite helicities, share the periodic phase supplied through the B-end, and begin at opposite azimuthal angles. In the elementary circular-polarization approximation their superposition is not a rotating transverse vector: it is a linearly polarized, fixed two-rail structure with antipodal antinodes. The B-end determines the propagation period through the geometry of the synchronized distal protonic centers of mass, while the A-end separation determines the field amplitude.
