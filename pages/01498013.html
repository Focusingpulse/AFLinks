This depends on the fact that binding energies of external atomic electrons are in the eV range. On the other hand, in nuclear transformations, the energy quantities that can be absorbed or released are of the order of mega-electronVolts (MeV) for every couple of nuclei involved in the process. As a consequence, for every given amount of energy obtained, the mass to be transformed by a nuclear process is about a millionth of that necessary for a combustion. It is a general rule, valid for all stable compounds, that the mass for a compound is lower than the total mass of all constituents.
