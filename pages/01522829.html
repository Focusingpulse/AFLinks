Edwin Hubble Born ( 1889-11-20 ) November 20, 1889 Marshfield, Missouri, United States Died September 28, 1953 (1953-09-28) (aged 63) San Marino, California, United States Nationality USA Known for Galaxies beyond the Milky Way, Redshift–distance relation , Hubble sequence Scientific career Fields Astronomer Edwin Powell Hubble (November 20, 1889 – September 28, 1953) was an American astronomer who established that the spiral nebulae are galaxies far beyond the Milky Way, and who discovered in 1929 the linear relation between a galaxy's redshift and its distance now known as Hubble's law . His name is attached to the expanding universe, and his relation is universally cited as its observational proof. This is one of the more striking ironies in the history of cosmology, because Hubble himself never accepted that interpretation , warned repeatedly against it, and in later life inclined toward a static, endless universe in which redshift arises from some unrecognised principle of nature. His caution is regularly invoked by the researchers catalogued on this wiki. Early life and education Hubble was born in Marshfield, Missouri, in 1889. He studied mathematics and astronomy at the University of Chicago and then went to Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar, where he read law at his father's urging. He passed the bar and practised briefly in Louisville, Kentucky, before abandoning law for astronomy — returning to Chicago for his doctorate and joining the staff of Mount Wilson Observatory. Galaxies beyond the Milky Way Using the 100-inch telescope at Mount Wilson, then the largest in the world, Hubble identified Cepheid variable stars in the Andromeda Nebula in 1924. Because the period–luminosity relation of Cepheids allows their distance to be calculated, he could show that Andromeda lies far outside the Milky Way.
