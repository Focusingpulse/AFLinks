Jeff Baugher presenting, hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary Jeff Baugher returned to give an update on the framework he calls SIPNAI — the calculus, philosophy and notation of axiomatic homogeneous infinitesimals — and on the result underneath it, which he calls the fundamental theorem of measurement. He noted at the outset that none of the work involves a language model. Homogeneous infinitesimals The premise reverses the usual order. Conventionally a line is taken as given and divided indefinitely; here the infinitesimal elements come first and are summed to make the line. The elements are homogeneous : length is built from elements of length, area from elements of area, volume from elements of volume, and one kind cannot be assembled from another. Dividing an area requires dividing by elements of area. Baugher identified this as a form of Eudoxus's theory of proportions, and said he can find no instance of it being tried in four hundred years. The theorem states the summing rule in set notation, with two further clauses. Where the elements are equal in magnitude the result is Euclidean geometry; where their magnitudes vary, the result is non-Euclidean geometry.
