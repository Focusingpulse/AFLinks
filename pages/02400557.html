Introduction: The Mechanics of Computational Primitives The conventional abstraction of computational systems relies heavily on a procedural ontology: algorithms are conceptualized as sequences of instructions operating upon stored values, where data serves as a passive substrate manipulated by active logic. However, when highly constrained systems—ranging from cryptographic hash functions and integer relation algorithms to the self-attention mechanisms of large language models—are evaluated mechanically rather than symbolically, a strictly structural paradigm emerges. Within this paradigm, variables are not arbitrarily assigned labels, but are generated coordinates in a transformation; values are not stored entities, but the stable attractors (residues) of ongoing processes. This report provides an exhaustive extraction and formalization of these underlying architectural primitives. By analyzing systems through the lens of constraint boundaries, decoupling barriers, and algebraic parity, this analysis isolates the fundamental mechanics of computation.
