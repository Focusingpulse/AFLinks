We obtained responses to 608 true–false questions from 52 self￾reported abductees and compared their responses to those of 75 non-ab￾ductee controls and to 26 simulators whom we asked to respond “as if” they had been abducted. The entire question set, as well as a subset of 65 ques￾tions identifi ed by discriminant analysis, diff erentiated among self-reported abductees, controls, and simulators. This result helps to defi ne a state of mind that we call the UFO Abduction Syndrome. Introduction A nationwide survey led to the conclusion that perhaps two percent of Americans had experienced what the survey sponsors called the “UFO Abduction Syndrome” (Hopkins, Jacobs, & Westrum, 1992).
