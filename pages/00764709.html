STATISTICS OF L1 DISTANCES IN THE FINITE SQUARE LATTICE RICHARD J. MATHAR Abstract. The L1 distance between two points in a square lattice is the sum of horizontal and vertical absolute differences of the Cartesian coordinates and — as in graph theory – also the minimum number of edges to walk to reach one point from the other. The manuscript contains a Java program that computes in a finite square grid of fixed shape the number of point pairs as a function of that distance. 1. Notations We consider the statistics of distances in a finite M × M square lattice, where distances between points at coordinates (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) are measured in the L1 norm [3]: Definition 1. (Distance) (1) d ≡ |x2 − x1| + |y2 − y1|. The Cartesian coordinates are 0-based in this manuscript, as usual in program￾ming: (2) 0 ≤ x, y < M. Example 1. The example of Figure 1 shows for the off-center reference coordinate (x, y) = (4, 3) one point with distance 0 (the point itself ), 4 points with distance 1, 8 points with distance 2,. . . , 3 points with distance 10 and 1 point with distance 11. Definition 2. (Number of points with distance d) NM(x, y, d) is the number of points in the M × M square lattice with distance d to the reference point (x, y). The points with a common distance d build a square lattice tilted by 45◦ relative to the host lattice; its lattice constant is larger by a factor √ 2. They fall basically into four sets to the NE, NW, SW and SE directions. Definition 3. (Marginal statistics) (3) N (u) M (d) ≡ M X−1 x=0 M X−1 y=0 NM(x, y, d) is the number of unordered pairs of points with distance d in the finite square lattice. If the case d = 0 is excluded, it is the statistics of distinct point’s distances. Date: June 13, 2023. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 51F10; Secondary 05-04. 1 2 RICHARD J.
