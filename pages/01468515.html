The Story Everyone Believes Inequality is worse than ever. The 1% hoard wealth while everyone else falls behind. The American Dream is dead. Capitalism failed. This narrative drives policy debates, campus activism, and election campaigns. It feels like settled science. But economist Hans Rosling discovered something that destroys this entire story. And it starts with a camel. From Camel World to Dromedary World In 1950, global income distribution looked like a camel with two humps. One small hump of wealthy people in the West. One massive hump of poor people in developing nations. By 2020, the world transformed into a dromedary with one hump. Most of humanity moved into the middle. The gap between nations collapsed. This is the opposite of growing inequality. The world became dramatically more equal. And almost nobody knows it happened. Market Reforms Lifted Billions Out of Poverty Between countries, inequality plummeted. Since 1980, when China and India embraced market reforms, inequality between nations fell sharply. In 1820, 94% of humanity lived in extreme poverty. By 1980, it was 42%. By 2015, just 10%. The biggest winners were the global poor. Markets didn't increase inequality globally. They reduced it faster than any system in history. But here comes the trick that fooled everyone. Within some countries, inequality measurements increased in recent years.
