Laya

· archived # 1,460,412
Mind , Soul , Spirit . The remaining three terms are all from verse 9 of Stanza I: "But where was the Dangma when the Alaya of the universe was in Paramartha and the great wheel was Anupadaka?" The word 'laya, like parinispanna, is one of the characteristic technical terms of the Yog'c'ra school of Buddhism. And similarly, the standard Sanskrit dictionaries do not record its meaning as a Buddhist technical term, because the Yog'c'ra sourcebooks were not yet published when these dictionaries were compiled. This has led some to question whether the term in the Stanzas should be alaya or 'laya, the former being taken as a-laya, or "non-dissolution." However, Blavatsky's comments on pages 48-49 of The Secret Doctrine, I, as well as in The Theosophical Glossary, "The name belongs to the Tibetan system of the contemplative Mahayana School," leave no doubt that 'laya is meant. Blavatsky defines 'laya as "Soul as the basis of all," "Anima Mundi," the "Soul of the World," the "Over-Soul" of Emerson, the "Universal Soul." As can be seen from the Buddhist texts now available, 'laya is short for 'laya', which can be defined literally as the "storehouse consciousness." This is the eighth and highest consciousness posited by the Yog'c'ra school, where it is indeed understood to be the universal consciousness, or "soul," as the basis of all.
