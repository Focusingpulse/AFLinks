Energy Loss by Electrons in an Absorber

· archived # 1,519,928
Title Energy Loss by Electrons in an Absorber Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Autodynamics , inelastic scattering, Bethe-Heitler theory, Neutrino, beta rays, heavy nuclei Published Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics (unpublished) Website https://www.autodynamics.org/energy-loss-by-electrons-in-absorber/ "Energy Loss by Electrons in Absorber" is an unpublished paper by Ricardo L Carezani applying the Autodynamics equations to a discrepancy in the pre-war scattering literature: measurements of the energy lost by fast electrons traversing absorbers of heavy nuclei that came out well above the Bethe–Heitler prediction. It is the one Autodynamics paper that engages a quantitative anomaly in a regime where the neutrino had already been experimentally excluded. Abstract For many years a considerable number of experimenters have reported that the energy loss by electrons traversing absorbers of heavy nuclei is larger than the energy calculated from the well-established Bethe-Heitler theory considering together energy loss by collision and radiation. This is particularly true when only the radiation is considered. The phenomenon is far from an explanation accounting for the electrons stopped by the absorber. To account for the excess of energy loss, many authors suggested the emission of neutrinos, but as was overwhelming proved experimentally by Buechner and van de Graaff there was no energy carried away by neutrinos or other penetrating radiation. For the first time, this phenomenon is explained by a general theory. Overview Carezani's historical framing is the strongest part of the paper. Between roughly 1915 and 1956, cloud-chamber experiments on beta rays between 0.5 and 17 MeV repeatedly found inelastic scattering in heavy elements several times the Bethe–Heitler value — factors of 2 to 5 in krypton and xenon, 10 in argon, 30 in lead, 6 in mercury.
