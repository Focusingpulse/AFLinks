"Some Polish man," suggested Ken Daise, assistant manager of Longacre Copy Center, 80 West 40th Street. Nikola Tesla, who won the Nobel Prize for his work in electricity. "Never heard of him," said Brij M. Bali, who runs the Bali Newsstand across the way. Tesla may be obscure, but the Croatian-born American Nobel Prize winner, who died in 1943, now has his own corner -- the northeast one at 40th Street and Avenue of the Americas, across from his office. The Nikola Tesla Corner is to be dedicated on Saturday in a ceremony at the Consolidated Edison Energy Museum at 145 East 14th Street. (Tesla was a pioneer in electricity.) He joins a long list of people blessed with a piece of immortality through roadway namings, a tradition that has shed glory on such figures as Ceferino Viera (a boulevard in Brooklyn), Ernie Ottuso (a square in the Bronx) and Rabbi Israel Mowshowitz (a way in Queens). The tradition, however, has also shed a measure of political ridicule on those responsible for the street naming. And now there are hints of a backlash, at least around midtown. Community Board 5 turned activist on the subject after agreeing in 1991 to honor Tesla, who was sponsored by the Croatian New Yorker Club, which was very active at the time.
