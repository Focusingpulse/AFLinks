Manfred Geilhaupt Residence Niederrhein, Germany Nationality German Manfred Geilhaupt is a German physicist associated with the Hochschule Niederrhein, the University of Applied Sciences at Krefeld and Mönchengladbach. He works on the origin of the physical constants, and in particular on the proposition that the rest mass of the electron, its charge and the fine structure constant can be derived rather than measured, by combining general relativity with the principles of thermodynamics. Approach Geilhaupt describes his programme as a "GR+TD" one — general relativity supplemented by thermodynamics, and specifically by the second law. He has argued that applying the second law to the action of an elementary mass-system at rest, such as a free electron, implies a mass-like uncertainty relation, and that the electron's rest mass follows from the Sommerfeld fine structure constant within such a scheme. He has extended the same treatment to the muon and the tau, under the heading of a "hidden thermodynamics" of the charged leptons, and has published a calculation of the fine structure constant from these principles. A recurring theme is the set of large dimensionless numbers relating atomic to cosmological scales, in the tradition of Eddington and of Dirac's large numbers hypothesis. His two conference contributions recorded here address the two halves of that theme: one the relations among the measured constants themselves, the other the numerical span between the electron and the universe. Work recorded here " The Set of Physical Constants of Nature " (2008) sets out an input set of seven quantities — Planck's constant, the speed of light, the Rydberg constant, the permeability of free space, Boltzmann's constant, Avogadro's number, the fine structure constant — together with a quantity he calls the quantum number N , of order 10²².
