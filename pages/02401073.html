ISARD-2025-remote010 Reconstruction of atmospheric aerosol parameters using the Optical Depth Sensor (ODS) Vladimir S. Khorkin1 , Anna A. Fedorova1 , Yury S. Dobrolenskij1 , Ilya A. Dzyuban1 , Nikita A. Vyazovetskij1 , Andrey Y. Titov1 , Oleg I. Korablev1 1 Space Research Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow, Russia vs_khorkin@mail.ru The ODS (Optical Depth Sensor) instrument was developed at the Space Research Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences for the ExoMars-2022 mission. This instrument was part of the lander's meteorological suite and was designed for long-term observation of the state of the Martian atmosphere and the aerosol suspended in it. The ODS instrument is determined the average daily value of the aerosol optical depth and the parameters of the distribution of aerosol particles by size. In the case of the Martian atmosphere, the aerosol is important for the planet's climate - it alters the radiation balance by scattering solar radiation, and atmospheric aerosol particles are condensation nuclei that affect the water and CO2 ice cycles in the atmosphere. When aerosols settle on the planet's surface, they change its albedo, with the greatest effect in the polar regions of Mars [Haberle R., et al, 2017]. Long-term monitoring of the state of the Martian atmosphere and its dynamics is therefore a relevant and important task. Currently, after the cancellation of the ExoMars 2022 mission, the ODS instrument is being used to carry out measurements in the Earth's atmosphere.
