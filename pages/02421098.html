Title Experimental Design Author(s) Leslee A Kulba Keywords Experimental design, controls, closed-loop test, replication Published 2010 Journal Electric Spacecraft Journal Volume 48 Pages 18-20 Experimental Design is a three-page article by Leslee A Kulba , published in 2010 in issue 48 of the Electric Spacecraft Journal , the journal's final double issue. It is the fifth part of the ten-part handbook on perpetual motion and its testing that Kulba, the journal's technical editor, contributed to that issue, and it begins the practical half: having described the machines and the principles, the remaining six articles are about how to find out. The text of the article has not been recoverable, and this page describes its subject and its place in the series rather than its argument. The subject The ordinary requirements An experiment is a question put in a form that can be answered wrongly. The requirements are the familiar ones and they are not negotiable by subject matter: state in advance what result would count as confirming and what would count as refuting; change one variable at a time; run a control — the same apparatus with the effect deliberately disabled — and check that it gives the null result; record the conditions, not only the readings; and have someone else repeat it with their own equipment. The last is the one that matters most and is skipped most often. A result that only appears in one shed, on one bench, with one person present, is not yet a result. Independent replication is what distinguishes a discovery from a fault in a particular set-up, and it is unforgiving precisely because the apparatus that produced the anomaly does not travel with it. What the design has to exclude For the claims surveyed in the first two articles, the design problem is specific: every path by which energy could enter the device has to be identified and either measured or eliminated.
