Saturday Morning Science Chats Summary Bob Gray presented on the internal consistency of Maxwell's equations, hosted by Franklin Hu , with Bill Lucas , Harry Ricker , James Keene , Cornelis Verhey , Carl and, later, David de Hilster . Gray's claim Gray had been working from Frederick David Tombe 's account of Maxwell's original equations, drawn from the 1861 and 1864 papers — twenty equations, presented in vector notation as eight. His question is not whether the equations agree with experiment but whether they are mathematically consistent among themselves: can one show, for a given equation, that the left-hand side genuinely equals the right? To ask that he had to add assumptions, which he listed and offered for challenge: vacuum permittivity and permeability, one constitutive relation Tombe omits, point charges as an adequate approximation to their positions, the charge density as a sum of delta functions, the current density as charge density times a velocity, and expressions for the scalar and vector potentials for discrete charges. Everything then stands in fundamental quantities and the check can be made. His result: it fails. Working out the divergence of E with those substitutions yields, on the right, a term containing the accelerations of the charges — the vector potential involves the current density, hence velocity, hence a time derivative giving acceleration — and there is nothing corresponding on the left. So the two sides cannot be equal in general.
