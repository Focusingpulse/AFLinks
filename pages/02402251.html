MolPhot2026-main008 Laser synthesis of bismuth tungstate as an efficient photocatalyst for CO2 utilization Еlena Fakhrutdinova1 , Matvey Gerasimov1 , Valery Svetlichnyi1 1 National Research Tomsk State University Fakhrutdinovaed@gmail.com Using solar energy to convert CO2 into renewable fuels or high-value-added chemicals (CH4 , CH3 OH, HCHO, HCOOH, C2 H5 OH, C2 H4 ) is an attractive strategy for simultaneously addressing energy and environmental challenges. Despite advances in photocatalytic CO2 reduction technologies, the search for active, stable, and selective photocatalysts for conversion into useful products such as methanol and ethanol remains a pressing issue. Currently, photocatalysts based on bismuth tungstate (Bi2 WO6 ) are attracting attention due to their low cost, favorable band gap energy (~2.7 eV), appropriate energy band positions for CO2 photoreduction, and their layered perovskite-like structure, which facilitates more efficient separation of photogenerated charge carriers [1]. In this study, we synthesized bismuth tungstate for the first time using pulsed laser ablation. Metallic Bi and W targets in distilled water were irradiated with focused Nd:YAG laser radiation (1064 nm, 150 mJ, 7 ns, 20 Hz), followed by mixing the colloidal solutions, drying, and calcination (BWO series). To initiate interaction between Bi and W at the colloidal solution stage, we also irradiated the mixture with focused laser radiation (BWO-hv series).
