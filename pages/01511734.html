I have been finding recurring mentions of "hydraulic air pumps" in the ever-expanding literature on self-fueling air cars, which has prompted me to go back again to Bill Truitt's design and question the one part of it I never could figure out. Other inventors are found making big claims for this kind of air compressor whatever it is. One even states that when using hydraulic power to compress air, there is an inherent overunity situation. Anybody know why? Something about force multiplication or what? My guess on Truitt's pumps is that they comprised a small hydraulic pump and an air piston in the same unit. The air piston would be reciprocated by hydraulic fluid on the non-air side instead of a piston rod. I can see a big advantage in doing this.
