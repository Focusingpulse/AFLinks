Desk Top Drug Discovery Toggle navigation Home Article Index Search Contact Online Store Desk Top Drug Discovery How a radical idea that molecules intercommunicate at long distances can speed up drug discovery and cut costs Dr. Mae-Wan Ho Increasing costs of drug development “While biotech offers significant promise in treating entire categories of disease for which no medicine previously existed, it comes at a significant cost,” said Tufts Centre for the Study of Drug Development (CSDD) director of Economic Analysis Josepha DiMasi [1]. CSDD estimates that on average, it take US $1.2 billion to develop a new biotechnology product, and reflects the costs of drugs that fail in testing and the length of time required to bring a new biopharmaceutical to market. A new biotech product took 97.7 months on average to wend its way through clinical development and regulatory review, about 8 percent longer than for pharmaceuticals. But it is the process of discovering drugs in the first place that has proved increasingly difficult and costly. The head of science and technology at Eli Lilly, Steven Paul, warned that the cost of producing a successful drug could top $2 billion by 2010 unless the pharmaceutical industry can identify new and better ways to improve efficiency and effectiveness of drug discovery and clinical trials [2]. Success rates to market varies from 12 to 33 percent [3], and total R& D costs are increasing at an annual rate of 7.4 percent above the general price inflation [4].
