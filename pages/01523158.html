Ingvar Astrand Born ( 1939-11-10 ) November 10, 1939 (age 86) Residence Södertälje, Sweden Nationality Swedish Known for Entropy , Cosmology , Unification Scientific career Fields Architect , Inventor , Independent Researcher Sven Ingvar Astrand (born November 10, 1939) is a Swedish inventor and independent researcher based in Södertälje , Sweden. Trained in architecture, he has worked professionally as an inventor and is the author of a proposed unified theory of physics founded on a claimed radiation entropy law. Biography Astrand studied architecture at the Royal Institute of Technology (KTH) in Stockholm beginning in 1970 but did not complete the program. In 1975 he won a Swedish national inventors' contest, and he has since worked as an inventor operating his own business. He holds seven patents and has filed approximately one hundred patent applications. Astrand presented his proposed Unified Theory of Physics at several international conferences: Natural Philosophy Alliance (NPA) at the University of Connecticut in Storrs, 2000 TH2002 at UNESCO in Paris, 2002 (poster introduction) Baltic State Technical University in St. Petersburg, Russia, 2002 Kazan State University, Russia, 2003 (plenary lecture) NPA and AAAS at Denver, Colorado, 2004 (a lecture on a proposed carbon dioxide sink) Scientific contributions Astrand proposes a unified framework for physics based on wave displacement, expressed by the relation Δλ = k·s, in which the elongation of wavelength is held to be proportional to the distance travelled. On this basis he argues that Planck's constant (h) and the Hubble constant (H) describe the same underlying phenomenon rather than two distinct constants. He interprets the cosmological redshift as an entropy effect in which heat radiation causes wavelengths to lengthen as they propagate, rather than as evidence of an expanding universe.
