Gilbert BARTHOLOMEW -- Engine -- Burns its own carbon monoxide -- Popular Science, Oct 1932 & USP 1904775 rexresearch Gilbert BARTHOLOMEW Engine Popular Science ( October 1932 ) Engine Burns Its Carbon Monoxide Elimination of deadly carbon monoxide from exhaust gas is claimed for a new gasoline engine of radical design. Half of the cylinders and pistons in the new engine are larger than the other half; and the number of spark plugs, valves and intake and exhaust manifold openings is half that of an ordinary engine having the same number of cylinders. The inventor, G.A. Bartholomew, of Cleveland Ohio, uses a principle similar to that of compound steam engines. Only the small cylinders have spark plugs for ignition. When the explosion in the small cylinder is almost over, the hot gases produced pass through a valve into the adjacent large cylinder, and are mixed with fresh air. Here they continue burning, using up all carbon monoxide, and producing additional power with a reduction in fuel consumption. US1904775 Engine [ PDF ]
