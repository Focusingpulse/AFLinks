Recently he has compiled from his two earlier volumes of poems, “The Winter Hour and Other Poems,” published in 1892, and “ Songs of Liberty and Other Poems ,” published in 1897, a third book which is entitled simply, “Poems by Robert Underwood Johnson.” It includes substantially all the serious verse he has written during the past twenty years. About two-thirds of the volume are to be found in the two issues above mentioned, the remaining third including verse that has not appeared in book form, but which has appeared for the most part in the leading magazines. These include the “Italian Rhapsody,” from the Atlantic Monthly for March of this year; “Dewey at Manila,” “An English Mother,” “The Flower of Fame,” “Love, the Conquerer, Came to Me,” “The Dread Before Great Joy,” “The Welcome of Our Tears,” the “Song of Remembrance” and “The Tryst.” The volume is full of lyrics and singable songs, illustrating even more completely than the earlier volumes Mr. Johnson’s delicate fancy, his mastery of technique and fine scholarship.
