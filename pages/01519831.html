Title Electromagnetic Propulsion of Matter in Violation of Newton's 3rd Law Read in full Link to paper Author(s) John R Warfield Keywords Electromagnetic Propulsion, matter, Newton's Third Law, magnetic field Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 9 Pages 603-611 Abstract The intention of this article to hypothesize a theory of electromagnetic propulsion of matter, using a pulsed electric current, located within a wire conductor, that induces a magnetic field, which then interacts with its own current to propel that conductor [Mass or matter] without a propellant. This function is a violation of Newton's third law. So in this scenario F x ma in one direction does not equal F x ma in the opposite direction. Overview John Warfield proposes that a suitably designed electromagnetic device can accelerate itself without expelling anything, and that this constitutes a genuine violation of Newton's third law. The paper builds the case through four examples and then applies the result to a modified rail gun for use in space. The first example is hypothetical. A magnetic flux compression generator in space emits a powerful magnetic pulse lasting ten nanoseconds. A hundred nanoseconds later the pulse reaches a distant current-carrying conductor and exerts a force or torque on it. By then, Warfield observes, "the field is no longer in continuity with the producer" — and the reaction on the generator points along one fixed direction, opposite the emitted pulse, whereas the force at the distant conductor can point in many directions depending on that conductor's orientation. The two are therefore not collinear and generally not equal. The remaining examples concern the homopolar machine, which Faraday invented and which Warfield treats in unusual detail. He works through the generator, the motor, and the simplified "co-rotating" version in which a magnet is stuck to a screw hanging from a battery terminal.
