Dr. Mae-Wan Ho Ban on conversion of land to biofuel production United Nations Special Rapporteur on the right to food, Jean Ziegler, has called for a 5-year moratorium on biofuel production. This recommendation was contained in his interim report [2] submitted to the UN General Assembly, which met in October 2007. He stressed that rushing to turn food crops &mdash; maize, wheat, sugar, palm oil &mdash; into fuel for cars, without first examining the impact on global hunger, would be a recipe for disaster. Among the potential impacts identified are increasing food prices, increasing competition over land and forests, forced evictions, impacts on employment and conditions of work, and increasing prices and scarcity of water. According to Ziegler, a five-year moratorium on biofuel production would provide time for technologies to be devised and regulatory structures to be put in place to protect against negative environmental, social and human rights impacts. It would also allow measures to be put in place to ensure that biofuel production can have positive impacts and respect the right to adequate food. The 232 kg of corn needed to make 50 litres of bioethanol would enable a child to live for a year, Ziegler pointed out [3].
