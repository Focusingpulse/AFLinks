Lars Wåhlin Born Lars Erik Wåhlin 31 August 1929 Malmö, Sweden Died 25 June 2015 Louisville, Colorado, United States Residence Rollinsville, CO, United States Nationality Swedish / USA Alma mater Nobel Institute for Physics, Stockholm Known for The Colutron velocity-filter isotope separator and ion source; Cosmology , Gravitation , Time , Relativity , Quantum Theory Scientific career Fields Ion-beam physics, atmospheric electricity, Cosmology Institutions CSIR, Pretoria; University of Colorado Boulder; Colutron Research Corporation Lars Erik Wåhlin (31 August 1929 – 25 June 2015) was a Swedish-American physicist and instrument designer who worked in Boulder, Colorado. He is best known in laboratory physics as the designer of the Colutron , a compact velocity-filter isotope separator and low-energy-spread ion source that has been in use in atomic- and surface-physics laboratories since the mid-1960s, and as the founder of Colutron Research Corporation, which manufactured it. Under that company's name he also pursued two lines of his own research: an electrochemical account of how the atmosphere becomes charged, published in the mainstream literature and in the monograph Atmospheric Electrostatics (1986), and a non-expanding, Machian cosmology set out in The Collapsing Universe (1985) and The Deadbeat Universe (1997). Biography Wåhlin was born in Malmö on 31 August 1929 and grew up in Stockholm, on the island of Stora Essingen. He served in the Swedish Navy and took his doctorate in physics at the Nobel Institute for Physics in Stockholm. In Sweden he learned the technique of electromagnetic isotope separation, and around 1957 he took a post in Pretoria building a separator for the laboratories of the South African Council for Scientific and Industrial Research.
