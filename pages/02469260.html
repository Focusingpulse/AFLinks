Krivit LENR s Demonstrate Reproducible Net Excess Energy Production Lasting Days to Weeks In the past four decades, excess heat and the production of nuclear products have been demonstrated many times in low-energy nuclear reaction ( LENR ) research. There is still no simple and foolproof experimental recipe that will allow a broad cross-section of scientists to repeat these results. However, scientists who have carefully followed the developing trends in the field have been able to reliably reproduce excess heat (energy) production in LENR s. Our new peer-reviewed study, published in the European Journal of Applied Physics, summarizes sustained, macroscopic thermal outputs across multiple gas-phase LENR platforms and shows how engineered nanoscale materials contribute to these results. LENR researchers have repeatedly demonstrated net excess-energy production lasting days to weeks. Unlike nuclear fusion research, where net energy is typically discussed only at the physics level, LENR net energy measurements account for full, system-wide energy input. The use of engineered nanoscale materials has improved mechanical stability, surface uniformity, and experimental repeatability compared with earlier bulk-material approaches. We believe these findings represent meaningful progress in LENR research toward the development of a possible new source of abundant, carbon-free energy.
