Ralph E. Juergens Born ( 1924-05-06 ) May 6, 1924 Died November 2, 1979 (1979-11-02) (aged 55) Residence Arizona, United States Nationality USA Known for The electric Sun hypothesis; documenting the Velikovsky affair Scientific career Fields Civil engineering; technical editing Ralph E. Juergens (6 May 1924 – 2 November 1979) was an American civil engineer and technical editor, remembered for two things: his part in documenting the scientific establishment's treatment of Immanuel Velikovsky , and his proposal that the Sun is powered electrically rather than by nuclear fusion. The second is the more consequential. In 1972 he argued that the Sun is not a self-contained thermonuclear furnace but the focus of a cosmic electric discharge, drawing its radiant energy from the galaxy around it. Astrophysics does not accept the idea, but it became the founding statement of the electric Sun model and, through that, of the electric universe literature; Wallace Thornhill has written that the electric universe model "follows the brilliant work of Ralph Juergens". Velikovsky himself never accepted it. The thermonuclear account of the Sun seemed sound to him, and he declined to follow the man who had done more than almost anyone to defend him. Biography Education B.S. — Case Western Reserve University, Cleveland. He trained as a civil engineer Career Juergens worked as an editor in the publications division of McGraw-Hill. By de Grazia 's account he gave up that work in 1960 and moved from the Midwest to Hightstown, New Jersey, near Princeton, in order to take up Velikovsky's work; he later lived in Arizona. He was Associate Editor of Pensée and a Senior Editor of Kronos , the two journals in which most of his writing appeared. The Velikovsky affair Velikovsky suggested to the writer Alfred de Grazia that Juergens serve as science editor for a book they were planning on Velikovsky's treatment by scientists, with the historian of science Livio Stecchini writing the historical part.
