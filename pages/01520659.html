Title Modern Experiments Related to Weber's Electrodynamics Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Andre K T Assis Keywords Weber, Electrodynamics, Experiments Published 1989 Pages 8-22 Abstract We present Weber's force law and the classical results that follow from it. We discuss the historical controversy surrounding Ampere's law of force between current elements versus Grassmann-Biot-Savart's law. Then we make a review of modern experiments related to this topic and to the electric field generated by a steady and stationary neutral current. Finally we analyze some theoretical aspects of Weber's law as its extension through retarded potentials to include electromagnetic radiation, and its relation to alternative interpretations of experiments devised to show the mass radiation with velocity. Overview This is a review paper, presented to a 1989 conference in Perugia on the foundations of mathematics and physics, and written by a physicist at Campinas who has since become the leading modern scholar of Weber 's electrodynamics . It has three parts: a compact derivation of the classical results that follow from Weber's 1846 force law; a survey of the experimental controversy between Ampère 's force law between current elements and the Grassmann–Biot–Savart law; and a shorter discussion of the electric field predicted around a steady, electrically neutral current. The paper's character is worth stating at the outset: it is largely a bibliographic and expository review of other people's experiments, chiefly Graneau 's, rather than a presentation of new results, and it is scrupulous about giving references and about noting where opponents have replied.
