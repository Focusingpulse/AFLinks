Title The Forbidden Ether Read in full Link to paper Author(s) John-Erik Persson Keywords Ether , special relativity theory , starlight aberration, Sagnac effect Published 2009 No. of pages 7 Abstract The two main pillars in the foundation for the special relativity theory are given alternative interpretations. Starlight aberration, as well as Michelson-Morley-s experiments, is thereby demonstrated to be wrong and useless. A new interpretation is also given to the Sagnac effect, and this produces evidence for entrained ether. The existence of ether is found to be very helpful when we want to describe different phenomena in physics. Overview This is the earliest and fullest of three papers by John-Erik Persson in this collection developing the same case for an entrained ether, and it sets out the physical picture that the later two assume. Its argument is that the two experimental pillars of special relativity — stellar aberration and the Michelson–Morley result — admit alternative readings under which each becomes "wrong and useless," and that the Sagnac effect, properly interpreted, positively supports entrainment. Persson proposes an ether of subatomic, neutrino-like particles with very small but non-zero mass, moving omnidirectionally at high speed. Their invisibility explains why the medium has never been detected, their penetrating character explains why it passes through large masses, and their mass is needed because the ether must transmit forces. Static conditions in the particle flow account for gravity and for the ether wind; dynamic vibrations account for light. On this view light is a collective behaviour of the particles rather than a thing in itself, and since all our knowledge of light comes from watching matter respond to it, "the quantization of energy relations in matter can therefore not with certainty be extended to exist in light."
