As the polarization of photons is easily controllable and resistant against atmospheric turbulences, it has been successfully used in a variety of different long-distance quantum experiments (1–4). However, polarization of photons resides in a twodimensional state space, restricting the complexity of entangled states both for certain quantum communication tasks and for fundamental tests. In contrast with polarization, the orbital angular momentum (OAM) modes of photons have an unbounded state space. Photons carrying OAM have a twisted wave front with a phase that varies from 0 to 2πℓ in the azimuthal direction. Here, ℓ is an integer which stands for the topological charge, and ℓ · Z is the OAM of the photon. Such states can carry larger amount of information per photon. It also allows more complex types of nonclassical correlations, such as entanglement of large quantum numbers (5) or high-dimensional entanglement (6–10).
