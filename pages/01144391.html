Three centuries ago LEIBNIZ analyzed these systems, such as the binary positional system for calculating (thus being a precursor of computer science), and was aware of the limitations of human languages, ambiguous and ambiguous, so he proposed the need for a "Characteristica universalis" common and integrative of all languages, which was analogous to the accuracy and intuitiveness of positional number systems. He sensed the difficulty of achieving it, but at the same time its ease of learning and use, as well as its communicative utility. At his death, after several failed attempts -and therefore the erroneous belief that it was not possible- this article explains that yes, it is possible such "Characteristica" and how it has been achieved. It was between 1984 and 1996 I analyzing the intrinsic characteristics of human thought and its languages on the basis of an innovative interdisciplinary methodology, integrating linguistics (semiology and semantics), psychology, neurology, mathematics and computer science. The possibility of defining concepts exactly, even the most virtual ones such as human psychic (i.e. mental) faculties, also makes it possible to bring order to the current ambiguities of psychology and pedagogy.
