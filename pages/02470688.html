Our generators will soon be complete, and the waters of Niagara Falls will supply me with a billion horsepower in the form of electricity. My tower at Wardenclyffe, L. I., will receive as much of this power as is needed. Only one one-hundredth part will be necessary to reach Mars. Whether we get an answer or not depends on who is there. More than likely the first answer from our neighbor will be: “Well — well — at last. We have been calling you the last ten thousand years.” Astounding as this prediction is — a prediction that is also a bona fide promise — it becomes even more astonishing when translated into plain terms capable of human comprehension. For it means that we are on the eve of sending communications into space for a distance of 4,800,000,000 miles. In other words, no planet within the solar universe will be outside the range of communication promised. It will be as simple as telephoning from one part of a city to another after the preliminary attuning of instruments on the two planets and the study and interpretation of each other’s codes and languages are done.
