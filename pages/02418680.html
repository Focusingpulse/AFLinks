Damping and driving are caused by two additional forces acting on the pendulum: The damping force and the driving force . Damping force An undamped pendulum can be realized only virtually as here in the Pendulum Lab. In reality dissipation of energy leading to damping is unavoidable. Usually dissipation is included in the equation of motion by adding a viscous damping term which is a damping constant times the velocity. Thus, the equation of motion of the damped pendulum reads (1) d 2 / dt 2 + d / dt + 0 2 sin = 0, where is the damping constant. Such a force occurs, for example, when a sphere is dragged through a viscous medium (a fluid or a gas). For a laminar flow (i.e., a flow without eddies) the dragging force is given by Stoke's law F damping = -6 Rv , where is the viscosity of the medium, R is the radius of the sphere, and v is its velocity relative to the medium.
