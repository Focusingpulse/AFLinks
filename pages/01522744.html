David V. Connell Born ( 1927-11-01 ) November 1, 1927 Residence Portimao, Algarve, Portugal Nationality English Known for Relativity , Gravity Scientific career Fields Physics; instrument development; systems analysis David V. Connell (born 1 November 1927) is an English physicist and former development engineer and systems analyst who, after retiring to Portugal, returned to physics and became a critic of both relativistic and gravitational theory. In a series of papers presented to the Natural Philosophy Alliance between 2009 and 2013 he argued that the changes to mass, length and frequency usually attributed to motion and to gravitation follow instead from the conservation of energy, and that gravity itself is electrostatic in origin rather than a property of mass. Biography Education BSc in Physics, University of Leeds, 1958 (as a mature student) Connell worked as a development engineer on surveying instruments, on seismometers, and on the isotope gamma-ray analysis of light elements in slurry. He moved into computing, becoming chief programmer at Elizabeth Arden in 1968 and, by 1980, a senior analyst and consultant on critical-path analysis systems in New Jersey. He retired to Portugal in 1990 — "better weather" — and resumed his interest in physics in 2003, publishing articles on his own website from 2007 and conference papers from 2009. He continued to take part in the alliance's discussions into 2015. Scientific positions Connell's papers develop a single line of argument in which energy, rather than motion or gravitation, is the source of relativistic change. Relativity from conservation of energy. He holds that neither speed nor a gravitational field has any mechanism for altering the mass of a body, but that raising or accelerating one requires energy, which by mass–energy equivalence appears as added mass in the body's own frame.
