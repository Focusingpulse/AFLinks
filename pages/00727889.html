Bertrand-Chebyshev Theorem and the Sum of Primes by YUNG ZHAO Tianjin University Tianjin, 300072 China E-mail: yzhao@tju.edu.cn * Abstract It is deduce from Bertrand-Chebyshev theorem that every even integer 2n greater than four can be written as the sum of two primes. Thus, Goldbach conjecture is true. Keywords Bertrand-Chebyshev theorem, Goldbach Conjecture. MSC2020 11P32, 11A41 1 Introduction One of the most famous problems in mathematics is Goldbach conjecture. The best progress occurred in 1973, when Jingrun Chen proved that every sufficiently large even number is the sum of a prime and a number with at most two prime factors [1]. Unfortunately, there is a key limitations inherent in standard sieve methods. The conjecture remains unsolved and very significant new ideas are required for the proof. In fact, the solution lies in Bertrand-Chebyshev theorem. 2 The Sum of Primes Bertrand-Chebyshev theorem For all integers n ≥ 2, there is always at least one prime P such that n < P < 2n For all integers n≥ 3, we construct the following interval: (n, 2n) In this interval, there is at least one prime, and let Pn denote the prime. We construct the following interval: (0, 2n − Pn + 2) *Copyright@2023 by Yung Zhao 1 Let P ′ n denote the largest prime in this interval. Then, by Bertrand-Chebyshev theorem, (2n − Pn + 2) 2 < P ′ n < (2n − Pn + 2) namely, P ′ n ≤ (2n − Pn) There are two possibilities: (1) P ′ n = (2n − Pn) (2) P ′ n < (2n − Pn) If it is case (1), then 2n = Pn + P ′ n It indicates that, for any integer n ≥ 3, even integer 2n can be written as the sum of two primes.
