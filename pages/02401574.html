GC2026-STP062 Ground-based recording of VLF-radiation from high-voltage power lines at mid-latitudes of Northeast Asia during the geomagnetic storm of February 3, 2022 Rustam R. Karimov1 1 Y.G. Shafer Institute of Cosmophysical Research and Aeronomy of the Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences karimovrim@gmail.com The geomagnetic storm of February 3, 2022 is of interest to solar-terrestrial physics due to its specific features: with moderate index values (Kp=5, Dst ~ -80 nT), the solar wind parameters created conditions for significant magnetospheric deformations. A prolonged southward orientation of the Bz component (down to -18 nT for ~2 hours), sharp changes in the azimuthal By component, and a dynamic pressure increase led to the auroral oval shifting to mid-latitudes. This paper presents the results of ground-based VLF observations during this storm at two sites in Yakutia. Recording of the electric component of VLF radiation (up to 20 kHz) was carried out at the IKFIA SB RAS radiophysical test site (Oibenkel, 62.93° N, 129.75° E) and at the "Maimaga" optical test site (61.98° N, 129.89° E). The distance between the sites is 130 km. At the Oibenkel site, magnetic field recording was carried out using a Metronix ADU-07e magnetometer, and VLF radiation in the range up to 10 kHz was recorded using a magnetic loop oriented in the east-west direction. Data analysis showed that in the electric component spectra at both sites during the 12th hour UT, narrow-band radiation was observed at several harmonics in the range of 1.5–6 kHz.
