Harry Ricker will be talking about Electromagnetic Induction, a more general look at that topic, which includes Unipolar Induction as a subtopic. Can 100 years of confusion be resolved using scientific method? Summary Harry Ricker 's second talk on electromagnetic induction, subtitled "can 100 years of confusion be resolved using scientific method?" — his answer being that in this case the method does not appear to be working. Ian J Cowan hosted, in the first of these technical sessions to use the society's new streaming setup, and Franklin co-hosted through some difficulty with Ricker's bandwidth. His thesis was that the trouble is too many contradictory opinions rather than too little evidence, and that the same name is attached to two different things. He opened, as a plug for the society's own concerns, with Thomas Jefferson's principles of scientific inquiry: freedom of the scientific mind, independence of conclusions from party, government, religion, peer review and the press, and reliance on observation and experiment. The history Faraday discovered induction while others were looking for it, and found what they missed because the effect in his iron ring was transient — earlier experimenters put magnets into coils and, by the time they had connected everything, had nothing to see. Ricker flagged the transience as something that would matter later. Faraday's own account was the flux-cutting rule: a wire moved across the pole of a magnet cuts lines of magnetic force, and that produces the current.
