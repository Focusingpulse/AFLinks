It is well known that in radio broadcasting, the electro-magnetic waves are dissipated in all directions, so that a receiving set at any considerable distance receives only an infinitesimal fraction of the power discharged into the ether. Even the system of “pointing” now used by some wireless stations, which concentrates and directs the rays by a device comparable to a searchlight reflector, spreads the energy over several degrees of a circle, and so wastes nearly all of it. When Nicola Tesla, the famous electrical scientist, comes forward with a similar prophecy, he commands a respectful hearing. Tesla is admittedly a dreamer, an unusual combination of scientific mind and imagination, but he has succeeded in making many of his wildest dreams come true. And now Tesla says, in a newspaper interview, that within a few years airplanes will dispense with gasoline tanks and heavy engines and use small electric motors, getting their power through the ether, as they need it, from distant stations.
