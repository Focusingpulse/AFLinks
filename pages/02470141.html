LAWRENCE CANAL CONTRACTORS AT WORK. Nikola Tesla Makes a Remarkable Prophecy in Connection With the Enterprise — Says New York Will Be Benefited. The New York Herald gives the following concerning the work on the St. Lawrence Canal, the object of which is to tap the river of the same for electrical power. The village of Massena, St. Lawrence County, N. Y., once noted as a summer resort, and largely visited on account of its sulphur springs and pure air, is the seat of one of the largest proposed pieces of engineering work this country has seen for some years, being exceeded only by the Chicago Drainage Canal. The village is located on the Grasse River, about three miles south of the St. Lawrence, of which the Grasse River is a tributary. The country intervening between these rivers is comparatively low and flat and furnishes pasture for a large number of dairy farms, for which this section of New York State is noted. The enterprise consists of diverting a portion of the water of the St. Lawrence River just above the Long Sault Rapids and conveying the same a distance of three and one-tenth miles through a canal to Massena, at which point it has a fall of forty-five feet into the Grasse River.
