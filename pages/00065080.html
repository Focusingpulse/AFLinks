Introduction It has long been believed that throughout the cosmic expansion, the baryon and lepton numbers are conserved and the mass of fermions remains constant. However, scrutinizing carefully from a global perspective, we may find that it is practically impossible for both of them to be true at the same time. Unless the pressure of fermionic matter is exactly zero (which is clearly not the case) or there is an improbable mechanism structurally assures that the decrease of their total energy due to the slightly positive pressure (since ρ ∝ a −3(1+w) ) is rightly compensated via interactions with other types of energy, the evolution of the mass density of fermionic matter in a dynamic universe cannot be exactly proportional to the inverse cube of the cosmic scale factor. In short, the current cosmology rests on two incompatible premises.
