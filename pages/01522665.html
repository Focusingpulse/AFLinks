Title Constraints on Gravitational Shielding Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Michele Caputo Keywords Gravitational Shielding, extinction coefficient, Gravity, equivalence principle Published 2006 Journal Journal of Astrophysics and Astronomy Constraints on Gravitational Shielding is a short review paper by the geophysicist Michele Caputo , published under the title "On new limits of the coefficient of gravitation shielding" in the Journal of Astrophysics and Astronomy , vol. 27, no. 4, pp. 439–441 (2006; sometimes cited as 2007). It re-examines the experimental and observational upper limits on the hypothetical phenomenon of gravitational shielding and updates the state of the art. Abstract (Summary — see the published article for the full abstract.) New limits on the coefficient of gravitation shielding have become available in the literature. Caputo reviews and discusses these values in order to update the state of the art, observing that the most recently published limits are not necessarily the smallest — and that the smallest limits are the ones of interest when planning new experimental research or evaluating possible theoretical gravitational-shielding effects. The work builds on the extinction (shielding) coefficient introduced by Quirino Majorana and on Caputo's own earlier bound (λ < 6×10 −16 , 1962). Overview On New Limits of the Coefficient of Gravitation Shielding is a three-page research note by Michele Caputo of the Physics Department at the University of Rome La Sapienza and the Department of Geology and Geophysics at Texas A&M, published in the Journal of Astrophysics and Astronomy 27(4), 439–441, in 2006. It is a review rather than a new measurement, and its purpose is narrow and practical. Gravitational shielding is the hypothesis, going back to Bottlinger in 1912, that a ray of gravity is attenuated on passing through matter: g = g 0 exp(−λ∫δ dl) with λ the shielding coefficient and δ the density along the path.
