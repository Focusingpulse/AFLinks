This "spooky action at a distance," as Einstein called it, suggests faster-than-light correlations, but measuring its speed is complex. Experiments, like those conducted in 2013 by the University of Vienna, have tested entanglement by observing how quickly correlations appear between distant entangled particles. These experiments show that any "communication" between entangled particles occurs at least 10,000 times faster than light, with some estimates suggesting near-instantaneous effects, limited only by experimental precision. However, this speed doesn't imply actual information transfer, as quantum mechanics forbids faster-than-light communication due to the randomness of measurement outcomes. The challenge for human understanding lies in the non-intuitive nature of quantum mechanics—entanglement defies classical notions of locality and causality.
