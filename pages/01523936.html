Stanley Jeffers Residence North York, Ontario, Canada Nationality English Canadian Alma mater Imperial College, University of London Known for Founding organiser of the Vigier Symposia; experimental tests of the B(3) field; critical assessment of consciousness-anomaly claims Scientific career Fields Experimental physics, optics, foundations of quantum mechanics Institutions Department of Physics and Astronomy, York University, Toronto Stanley Jeffers is an English-Canadian experimental physicist, Associate Professor (now retired) in the Department of Physics and Astronomy at York University in Toronto. He is best known in this field not for a theory of his own but for having built the principal international forum in which heterodox interpretations of quantum mechanics are argued: he was the executive local organiser of the Vigier Symposia , and co-editor of the volumes that came out of them. His position is an unusual one among the figures documented on this wiki, and worth stating plainly. Jeffers is a conventionally credentialed experimentalist who has taken heterodox claims seriously enough to organise conferences around them, edit their proceedings, and in several cases test them in the laboratory — while frequently reporting results that do not support the claim in question. He is at once a builder of the dissenting community's institutions and one of its more exacting critics. Education and career Jeffers took his B.Sc. in Physics with Special Honours (A.R.C.S.) at Imperial College, University of London, in 1963, and his Ph.D. (D.I.C.) in opto-electronics at the same institution in 1968. He spent his academic career in the Department of Physics and Astronomy at York University in Toronto, where he taught undergraduate courses including The Nature and Growth of Scientific Thought .
