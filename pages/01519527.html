Title Compressed Orbits and the Secret Behind Einstein's Mass-Energy Equivalence Equation Read in full Link to paper Author(s) David Tombe Published 2017 Journal General Science Journal No. of pages 4 Abstract On the astronomical scale, the potential energy in a closed orbit is due to the gravitational force of attraction. These are uncompressed orbits. The gravitational force is opposed by a centrifugal force acting internally from within the system. This article will now consider the nature of the potential energy in a system of multiple orbits that are pressing against each other with centrifugal force while striving to dilate. Centrifugal potential energy will be shown to lie at the root of the famous equation E = mc 2 Overview Compressed Orbits and the Secret Behind Einstein's Mass-Energy Equivalence Equation is a four-page paper by David Tombe , dated 25 January 2017 and posted to the General Science Journal . It proposes that rest energy is the stored centrifugal potential energy of internal circulation, and that E = mc² follows from it. Section I sets out what Tombe calls an uncompressed orbit, using a comet as the example. Gravity converts potential to kinetic energy on the inward leg; the transverse part of that kinetic energy corresponds to a centrifugal force following an inverse-cube law, which "increases at a greater rate than gravity which obeys an inverse square law", so "the two different inverse power laws create a reversal threshold" at perihelion. Section II introduces the contrast. "Centrifugal force is induced between any two bodies in mutual transverse motion", and in a many-body system an orbit can be "forcibly closed, not by an internal attractive force, but rather by the external centrifugal pressure generated by neighbouring orbits that are striving to dilate."
