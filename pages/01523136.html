Title Ideal Universal medium Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Nainan K Varghese Keywords universal medium, distance, aether , real Published 2013 Abstract No actions can be performed at a distance through empty space. An all-encompassing medium is essential to facilitate (apparent) physical actions between material bodies at a distance. Aether, used in aether-theories and other imaginary entities currently used to explain various physical phenomena are too vague and fails to describe many physical actions of matter bodies, logically. By realising a real entity that fills the entire space as an all-encompassing universal medium, the formless space can be replaced with a structured real entity. An ideal universal medium should be a real entity with positive existence and with all properties currently assigned to space or to other imaginary entities like; different types of aether, imaginary particles or various types of fields. Overview Nainan K. Varghese argues that action at a distance through empty space is untenable, and sets out the properties an all-encompassing medium would need in order to replace it. The paper is a specification rather than a derivation: it lists requirements, and contains no equations, no numerical predictions and no proposed experiments. The opening position is that "action at a distance through empty space is the worst assumption in physics", and that the substitutes physics has adopted — fields, force-carrying particles, the space-time continuum, vibrating strings — are undefined entities differing from the discarded aether only in name. Each is associated with one phenomenon and has no structure of its own. A genuine unified theory, Varghese argues, must therefore begin by constructing a single medium from which all of them follow. The requirements he assembles for such a medium are these. It must be infinite, isotropic, homogeneous, self-sustaining and static, providing an absolute reference for all motion. It must be made of matter
