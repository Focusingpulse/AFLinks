Title Model of the Electron Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Philipp M Kanarev Keywords Planck's constant, quantum mechanics , structure of the electron, radiation Published 2000 Journal Apeiron Volume 7 Number 3-4 No. of pages 10 Pages 184-194 Abstract The interpretation of some of theoretical foundations of physics will be changed. Planck's constant is known to be one of such foundations, which serves as a basis of quantum mechanics. Let us consider how a refinement of interpretation of the physical essence of this constant allows to make a theoretical penetration into the depth of electromagnetic structure of the electron and to connect this structure with the results of the experiment. Difficulties encountered in explaining the radiation of the theoretical black body were overcome in December 1900 when Max Planck supposed that energy, p E, in EM form is not emitted continuously, but discrete amounts Planck named quanta; that is: Ep = hn, where n - frequency of EM radiation; h - a universal constant later called Planck's constant. Overview Published in Apeiron in 2000, this paper by Philipp Kanarev of the Kuban State Agrarian University proposes a ring model of the electron , derived from a reinterpretation of what Planck's constant physically is. Kanarev's starting point is that h does not have the dimensions of action in any illuminating sense but the dimensions of angular momentum, and that this has been under-exploited. He quotes the remark that "if Planck had determined his constant as a quantum of angular momentum modern physics would have been quite different." Combining E = h ν, E = mc ² and c = λν gives h = m λ²ν, and Kanarev observes that m λ² has the form of a moment of inertia. Adding the assumption that the radius of the rotating structure equals its wavelength, r = λ, turns this into h = mr ²ν — the angular momentum of a rotating ring. From that he computes the electron's parameters: an energy of 5.11×10⁵ eV, a rotation frequency of 1.236×10²⁰ s
