Nagel Residence Washington, DC, United States Nationality USA Alma mater University of Notre Dame; University of Maryland Known for Advocacy and assessment of LENR as a research field and prospective energy technology; chairman of ICCF-14 Scientific career Fields Radiation physics; X-ray spectroscopy; materials science; microelectromechanical systems; low energy nuclear reactions David J. Nagel is an American physicist and engineer, Research Professor in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering of the School of Engineering and Applied Science at The George Washington University. He spent the greater part of his career at the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory , where he rose from research physicist to Superintendent of the Condensed Matter and Radiation Sciences Division, while serving concurrently as a naval officer, retiring as a Captain in the United States Naval Reserve. He appears on this wiki as one of the most senior and conventionally credentialled figures associated with low energy nuclear reactions (LENR), a field he has followed as scientist, reviewer and manager since the day it began. His distinctive contribution is less experimental than assessive : a long series of status reviews, roadmaps and policy arguments setting out what the field has established, what it has not, and what governments, journals, societies and companies would have to do for it to progress. He prefers the expansion lattice-enabled nuclear reactions to the more usual "low energy", on the grounds that it puts the solid rather than the input energy at the centre of the phenomenon. Education B.S. in Engineering Science, University of Notre Dame, 1960 ( magna cum laude ) M.S. in Physics, University of Maryland, 1969 Ph.D. in Engineering Materials, University of Maryland, 1977 Career Naval service In 1960 Nagel received a regular commission in the U.S. Navy after ranking first in his NROTC class.
