Title A Compensating Term for the "Side Force Component" Term in the Barometric Equation Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Donald G Carpenter Keywords atmosphere, atmospheric particle motion, barometric Published 2000 Journal Apeiron Volume 7 No. of pages 5 Pages 143-147 Abstract The derivation of the barometric equation concerns central forces in three dimensions. It has recently been shown that the sum of the incremental volume's side force components in the direction of the center of gravity (the (−2 p / z )(Δ x )(Δ y )(Δ z ) force) must be included in the Cartesian derivation. That results in the side force component term (−2 p / z ) being added to the differential equation, or d p /d z = − NmMG / z 2 + (2 p / z ). Meteorological data does not, though, conform unambiguously to the corrected barometric equation. This implies that an approximately compensating term might exist. Such a term results from consideration of the central force due to atmospheric particle random motion perpendicular to the radial from the center of gravity in a central force field (the +(2 f p /3 z )(Δ x )(Δ y )(Δ z ) force, where " f " represents the degrees of freedom). The more-complete barometric equation is: d p /d z = − NmMG / z 2 + [2( f −3)/3]( p / z ). Overview A Compensating Term for the "Side Force Component" Term in the Barometric Equation is a five-page paper by Donald G Carpenter , published in Apeiron in 2000. It is the second of a pair; the first, An Inconsistency in Sir Isaac Newton's Derivation of the Barometric Equation (Apeiron, 1999), argued that the standard hydrostatic relation is incomplete. Carpenter's starting point is Newton's construction in the Principia : imaginary cylinders of small rectangular cross-section erected radially from the surface of a planet, covering it completely, with the fluid outside them "held up archwise" and contributing nothing to the pressure inside.
