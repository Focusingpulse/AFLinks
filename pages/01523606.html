Title Neutrino: Origin Read in full Read in full Author(s) Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Keywords Neutrino, Radium E, Ellis and Wooster, Wolfgang Pauli, beta decay, Autodynamics Published Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Website https://www.autodynamics.org/neutrino-origin/ "Neutrino: Origin" is the Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics 's account of how the neutrino came to be proposed. It is the historical foundation on which the rest of the Autodynamics case against the particle rests, and the same narrative is reproduced in A New Experiment With RaE . Overview The argument is that the neutrino was not discovered, or predicted from theory, but introduced to reconcile a calorimetric measurement with a calculation made using the special relativity kinetic-energy equation — and that it is the equation, not the energy balance, that is at fault. The argument Ellis and Wooster, 1927 C. D. Ellis and W. A. Wooster placed the purest available sample of Radium E — the beta emitter 210 Bi, decaying to polonium with a five-day half-life — in a calorimeter and measured the heat produced. Since the number of atoms was known, the heat gave the energy released per disintegration directly: 0.36 MeV, which the SAA account notes is "exactly equivalent to the energy of one electron." The account stresses what the two experimenters were and were not doing. "Ellis and Wooster were not interested in confirming or refuting special relativity. They did not use Einstein's equations in their calculations. They were only interested in discovering the total amount of energy generated in the experiment. Once the experiment was performed, they moved on to other research." Pauli, 1930 Other groups then measured the velocity of the emitted electrons with a mass spectrograph and converted velocity to energy using the relativistic formula.
