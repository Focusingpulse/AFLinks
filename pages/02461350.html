Maybe in reply to: Hans von Lieven: "Adams motor: Bedini version" Next in thread: Carrigan, Ken: "RE: Adams motor: Bedini version" Robert, et all, There is a limit to the maximum repeatable switching time that a microswitch can switch at. It has to do with the spring constant if it is spring loaded, or reed switch with rebounding properties, or other physical properties.. which will regulate how fast it can switch and rebound. Given 2400rpm, or 40 rps and a 10 percent duty cycle of switching (maybe shorter?) will yield 2.5 milli sec switching speed at best. If a microswitch has 10mS rating on switch over to recovery time, then the duty cycle will suffer until it reaches 50%. That would be called 'floating' cause the switch will not open (or close) when it was suppose to. Weird things then could happen. Suggest a faster switch be used that has rating of 1uS time so that absolutes can be obtained.
