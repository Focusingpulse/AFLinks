Required Conditions for Successful Lattice Confinement Fusion in D/ Pd Electrolysis Experiments Jozsef Garai Independent Scholar Szob, Hungary jozsefgarai29@gmail.com Fleischmann and Pons [1989, 1990] reported electrochemically induced excess heat in palladium–deuterium system. No new chemical product has been detected in the experiments. The observed quantity of heat could not have been produced by any known chemical reaction. It was speculated that the reaction must be nuclear. In the past three decades many hundreds of successful experiments, producing electrochemically induced excess heat in the palladium–deuterium system, have been reported [Storms, 2007; Storms & Grimshaw, 2010]. Experiments along with the excess heat, also detected 4He with qualitative correlation to the measured excess heat [Bush et al. 1991; Gozzi et al., 1998; Arata & Zhang, 1999/a, b; Miles et al., 2003; 10]. The collected 4He had the correct magnitude for the deuterium fusion reaction: D + D ➞ 4He (73.7 keV) + (23.8 MeV). (1) The production of 4He was confirmed by many independent experiments [Liaw et al., 1991; Chien et al., 1992; Miles et al., 1996], and the measured quantity of 4He in many cases exceeded the 4He content of the air. Thus, possible contamination by air can be excluded. The many hundreds of experiments reporting excess heat, which cannot be explained by chemical reaction, the measured 4He fission product commensurate with deuterium fusion, demonstrates that fusion can occur at low temperature and pressure.
