By ROBERT J. SCHADEWALD Will Mische never expected to find himself in a dispute with electrical engineers when he began reading about Nikola Tesla a year ago. Mische had never even heard of Tesla until then. Mische isn't even a scientist, though he has a high interest in science and has read a lot of technical literature. Mische, who has a master's degree in sociology, works at the Anoka office of the Minnesota Department of Employment Services. Now, after reading about Tesla's work, he also heads the People's Power Project, a group that hopes to show that electric power can be sent through the ground without transmission lines. The People's Power Movement has built a huge coil and antenna system on a farm near Belgrade, Minn. About 700 miles away, near Timmins, Ontario, a Canadian group is building a similar system. Bottles serve as capacitors for power system The devices are based on the theories of Tesla, an electrical engineer who was prominent around the turn of the century.
