Konstantin Meyl Born 29 August 1952 Lemgo, Germany Residence Villingen-Schwenningen, Germany Nationality German Alma mater Technical University of Munich, University of Stuttgart Known for New Energy , Electrodynamics , Expanding Earth Scientific career Fields Professor of Electrical Engineering Thesis Dreidimensionale nichtlineare Berechnung von Wirbelstromkupplungen (1984) Website http://www.meyl.eu Konstantin Meyl (born 29 August 1952 in Lemgo) is a German electrical engineer who taught power electronics and drive technology at Furtwangen University in the Black Forest from 1986 until his retirement in 2018. His formal training and his early published work are in the three-dimensional computation of eddy currents, a conventional and well-regarded corner of electrical machine design. He is known outside that field for a body of work he began in 1990, in which he argues that Maxwell's equations are incomplete. To the eddy current — the current vortex that forms in conductors — he adds a dual object he calls the potential vortex , which he holds to form in dielectrics and to propagate as a longitudinal scalar wave . From this he derives claims about wireless energy transmission after Nikola Tesla , about the neutrino as a carrier of usable energy, about cell and DNA communication, and about the growth of the Earth. The work is self-published through his own company and is not accepted in physics or electrical engineering; Furtwangen University has formally distanced itself from it, and detailed technical rebuttals have appeared in the engineering literature. This article sets out both the claims and the objections. Biography Meyl took his Abitur in 1972 and served his military conscription in radar technology before beginning electrical engineering at the Technical University of Munich in 1973.
