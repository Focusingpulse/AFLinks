King (born 1949) is an American engineer and author, and one of the most persistent advocates of the idea that the zero-point energy of the vacuum could be tapped as a practical power source . He has written four books on the subject and has presented at energy and Tesla conferences for over forty years. His work is a synthesis rather than an experimental programme: he takes results reported by others — Ken Shoulders 's charge clusters, anomalies in water electrolysis, ball-lightning phenomena — and argues that a single mechanism underlies them, namely that certain self-organising vortex structures in plasma can cohere the ordinarily random vacuum fluctuations and so draw energy from them. Education University of Pennsylvania — BS in electrical engineering University of Pennsylvania — MS in systems engineering University of Pennsylvania — completed the coursework for a PhD in systems engineering; the degree was not taken In 1978 he proposed to his engineering faculty a doctoral thesis on the proposition that energy could be tapped from space. He took leave from the university in 1979 to join the Eyring Research Institute in Provo, Utah, and did not return to complete the degree. Career King encountered the zero-point energy in the mid-1970s.
