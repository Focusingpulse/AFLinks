GC2026-SGG023 Static and Dynamic Elastic Moduli of Tas-Yuryakh Reservoir Rocks: Experimental Determination and Comparative Analysis Svyatoslav O. Barkov1 1 Institute for Problems of Mechanics named after A.Yu. Ishlinsky of the Russian Academy of Sciences barkov.sviat@yandex.ru To build geomechanical models of hydrocarbon reservoirs, select optimal drilling parameters, and ensure safe well operation, key input data include the static elastic moduli of rocks. These are determined from laboratory uniaxial and triaxial compression tests on core specimens. At the same time, well logging measurements provide compressional (P) and shear (S) ultrasonic wave velocities, from which dynamic elastic moduli are derived. Within the framework of linear elasticity for a homogeneous isotropic medium, the dynamic and static elastic moduli are identical [1]. However, for porous media such as reservoir rocks, dynamic elastic moduli depend strongly on the type and magnitude of the applied stress, the fracture density, and fluid saturation, and typically exceed the corresponding static values [2]. For weakly cemented, highly porous, and fractured rocks at low confining pressures, the discrepancy may reach a factor of 4 to 5 or more [3]. Therefore, establishing an empirical relationship between static moduli (from laboratory tests) and dynamic moduli (from well logging data) is highly challenging. It should also be noted that no single universal relationship between static and dynamic moduli exists for rocks of different origins [4].
