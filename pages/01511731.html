Copyright works such that if you don't give any kind of statement what others may or may not do with your work, they basically may do nothing with your work, but what is considered "fair use", which is about limited to quoting. So, if you want others to be able to do more than that, you have to say so one way or another. So, if you want your work to be "open source", you have to say so, and you have to say what you mean by that. Now I understand you don't feel any desire to dig into the laws and waste a lot of time into writing a "licence" in order to express what you want to allow. However, over at Creative Commons they have a couple of standard licenses you could consider using: Licenses - Creative Commons This might become very important, if for example, for one reason or another, Aaron would be forced to pull the plug from this website.
