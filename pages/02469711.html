Last week a New York electrician accidentally made himself a conductor for a current of electricity with an estimated strength of 2,000 volts, or 300 more volts than is developed by the machines at Sing Sing which electrocute condemned murderers. As will be seen by the account printed of the accident printed on our seventh page to-day, the electrician was thrown into convulsions by the shock, and for an entire day his life hung in the balance. But when he recovered he pronounced himself as feeling better than he did before the accident. He is experiencing a feeling of rejuvenation, like that which follows a long rest, and has lost a stubborn case of rheumatism which was the cause of much pain and suffering. While the victim of the electrical accident was slowly recovering his equilibrium, the section on general medicine of the New York Academy of Medicine was discussing “The Theory and Treatment of Rheumatism,” and expressing opinions as to the cause of the disease. This discussion revealed the fact that the medical profession is inclining to the belief that rheumatism is caused by bacteria in the blood.
