Decker: "Inertial Drives" Next in thread: Kenneth Carrigan: "Re: Inertial Drives" >The point here is that Aspdens experiment where he found that a mass >brought to rotation always took the same amount of power if brought from >a DEAD STOP....however, if slowed down ALMOST to a stop and brought back >up to speed, it only took about 30% of the energy required from a DEAD >stop. Nothing new or exciting here.... Sure, rest is zero velocity (v) and zero acceleration (a). To get this mass rotating at a contant velocity v1 the energy required is (.5m *v1^2). However, if the mass has some rotation, v0, then the energy required is (.5m*(v1^2 - v0^2). The same would be true of a car at rest at a stop light, when the light changes, a car next to you still has some velocity (energy) and can easily accellerate (add energy) to build up to a higher velocity quicker than the stopped car.
