The Farce of Physics Book Author Bryan G Wallace Language English Subject Criticism of special relativity ; sociology of physics; the velocity of light Published 1993 Publisher Self-published; distributed over the internet Media type Online text The Farce of Physics is a book by Bryan G Wallace (1933–1997), copyright 1993, arguing that "modern theoretical physics has become to a large degree, little more than an elaborate farce." It is part memoir, part polemic and part sociology of science: an account of Wallace's roughly fifty-year career as a physicist working outside the academic mainstream, organised around his contention that Einstein's second postulate — the constancy of the velocity of light — is contradicted by interplanetary radar data and is the load-bearing assumption of everything built on it. It was never issued by a publisher. Wallace circulated it over the early internet, and it remains freely readable in full; see Reading the book below. The central argument Wallace states the thesis of the book most compactly in Chapter 4, prompted by a reader's question in Marilyn vos Savant's newspaper column about what single discovery would overturn modern science: Einstein's special relativity theory with his second postulate that the speed of light in space is constant is the linchpin that holds the whole range of modern physics theories together. Shatter this postulate, and modern physics becomes an elaborate farce! His evidence for shattering it is the one he had pressed since 1969: that published interplanetary radar data, in particular the 1961 radar contact with Venus, fit a classical c + v relative velocity better than a constant c . The book's wider claim is that the physics community's failure to engage with this is not an accident but a symptom — of funding structures, of peer review, and of what he regards as physics having taken on the character of a faith. The title is a deliberate echo of Fritjof Capra's The Tao of Physics , which Wallace quotes
