John Worrell Keely's ' Airship ' In the late 19th century, Philadelphia-based inventor John Ernst Worrell Keely (1837-1898) ventured into using sound resonance to manipulate what he identified as the radiant energy of the universe. This initiative led him to develop theories and experiments that significantly departed from the scientific standards of his era, introducing notions that many contemporaries regarded as groundbreaking. His endeavors are detailed in Chapter Three of "A Breakthrough to New Free Energy Sources" by Dan A. Davidson, a seminal document emphasizing Keely's scientific pursuits and his flair for innovation. Keely's theoretical approach suggested that the universal ether—a medium then believed to fill all space and transmit forces—could be harnessed through sound resonance to achieve levitation and propulsion. This theory resulted in the creation of various devices, most notably the Keely Motor, which he asserted could influence gravitational forces. The sophistication of Keely's designs and the complexity of his demonstrations drew the attention of numerous respected scientists and intellectuals of the time, who were often left deeply impressed by the potential implications of his work for energy generation and mechanical propulsion. In 1896, Keely demonstrated an airship equipped with finely tuned resonation plates and vibratory mechanisms, which he showed could ascend and float by harnessing what he termed the 'etheric polar current.' This technology enabled the airship to overcome traditional gravitational boundaries and achieve speeds of up to 500 miles per hour. This demonstration not only showcased his technology's ability to control gravitational effects but also reflected his broader aim of tapping into invisible natural forces through harmonic resonance, a concept that finds parallels in certain modern theoretical physics discussions involving quantum fields and energy states.
