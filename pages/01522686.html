Title Cosmology-Galactic Renewal Read in full Link to paper Author(s) James B Wright Keywords Cosmology, galaxies Published 2009 No. of pages 8 Abstract The Observable Universe is seen as a tiny sample of an infinite ocean of galaxies, a self-evident existence that is engaged in a constant re-cycling of its galactic ashes and debris into new galaxies at an efficiency of 100%. The motor that drives this re-cycling is gravity. Galactic clusters are built into massive arrays and, in time, build the central galaxies, the Seyferts, out of which are born the Quasars, the precursors of new galaxies, a la Halton Arp. Overview James Wright proposes a cosmology of endless galactic recycling. The observable universe is a small sample of an infinite and eternal ocean of galaxies, in which the ashes of dead galaxies are returned to circulation and rebuilt into new ones at an efficiency of 100%, with gravity as the motor. The cycle he describes runs as follows. Gravity draws galaxies into clusters, which grow by accretion and collision until a dominant central galaxy emerges — invariably, he says, a spiral with an active nucleus, and the most dominant of these are Seyferts. When a critical mass and density are reached in the central region, the Seyfert's core undergoes a transformation producing quasar precursor masses, which are ejected in opposite directions along the poles, perhaps by magnetic repulsion. Following Halton Arp, the ejected quasars grow fuzzy as they travel and evolve into ordinary galaxies, which in time are drawn back into clusters and the cycle repeats. The starting point is philosophical rather than observational. Wright takes Existence as "a self-evident fact, as an axiomatic concept that cannot be refuted," and argues that "if we would rule out magic, we must conclude that existence exists everywhere," so the ocean of galaxies extends in every direction to infinity and has neither beginning nor end.
