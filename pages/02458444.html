EDITORIAL Theme and Variations: The Life and Work of Ian Stevenson EMILY WILLIAMS KELLY AND CARLOS S. ALVARADO Shortly after Ian Stevenson died on February 8, 2007, Henry Bauer asked us to guest-edit an issue of this Journal to be devoted to him – our mentor, colleague, and friend. Most readers of the Journal will probably agree that no one deserves such an honor more than Ian. He was a founding member of the Society for Scientific Exploration, but more importantly he exemplified the kind of scientist that the SSE was founded to encourage. For example, in 1957 he challenged a central tenet of psychoanalysis, then the predominant ideology in psychiatry, by publishing a paper in the American Journal of Psychiatry entitled ‘‘Is the Human Personality More Plastic in Infancy and Childhood?’’ He later said that, after this paper came out, a colleague in psychiatry asked him whether he could walk the streets unarmed (Stevenson, 1990, p. 8). We are not at all certain that Ian himself would have welcomed the suggestion that an issue of this Journal be devoted to him.
