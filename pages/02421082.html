Daniel H. Deutsch Born ( 1922-08-29 ) August 29, 1922 Died November 16, 1997 (1997-11-16) (aged 75) Residence Pasadena, California, United States Nationality American Known for Planck's constant as a quantum of angular momentum; electromechanical models of elementary particles; local quantum reality Scientific career Fields Foundations of quantum physics, particle structure Daniel H. Deutsch (29 August 1922 – 16 November 1997) was an American physicist based in Pasadena, California, who over the last decade of his life developed an alternative account of quantum physics built on two claims: that Planck's constant is a quantum of angular momentum rather than of action, and that elementary particles are physical structures made of rotating massless charges rather than featureless points. His work was published in Physics Essays and Galilean Electrodynamics , and presented at meetings of the Natural Philosophy Alliance . It is unusual among the material collected here in being a fully constructive programme: rather than arguing chiefly that the standard account is wrong, Deutsch set out to build concrete mechanical models of the photon, electron, proton, neutron and neutrino, and to derive their properties from geometry and motion. Biography Deutsch lived and worked in Pasadena, California. He remained active to the end of his life, reading papers at the Natural Philosophy Alliance meeting in San Luis Obispo in July 1997 and at a meeting in Cologne in late August of the same year, only months before his death on 16 November 1997 at the age of 75. John Chappell , writing in the Alliance's newsletter shortly afterwards, recorded: Dr. Daniel H. Deutsch of Pasadena, Calif. died in mid-November, just prior to the completion of this newsletter, aged 75. He read carefully-reasoned papers at the San Luis Obispo meeting last July, and then also at the Cologne meeting in late August. He also published in major dissident journals. Cheerful and generous, Dan will be sorely missed.
