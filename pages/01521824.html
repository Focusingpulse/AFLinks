The Photoelectric and Compton Effects Without Photons Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Date 15 April 2023 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording The Photoelectric and Compton Effects Without Photons was a live session on 15 April 2023 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The speaker will perform original derivations, supported by physics' most famous experiments and also supported by his own original experiments. An inner meaning of constants e and h will be explored Summary Eric S Reiter on how the photoelectric and Compton effects can be derived without photons, and on the experiments he has run since 2001 to test the alternative. Ian J Cowan hosted. The session started late — the standing link led to the previous fortnight's ended broadcast, and Cowan had to post a working one in the comments. Reiter worked on a pen tablet rather than slides, drawing the arguments as he went. The problem His statement of the difficulty is Einstein's own definition of the photon, which has two halves. Send single emissions at a beam splitter with a detector on each side, and each one goes one way or the other — a click here or a click there. Recombine the paths into an interferometer and the accumulated clicks build an interference pattern, so each one must have gone both ways. The contradiction is in the definition, and a century of attempts to live with it has produced what he called acts of desperation: many worlds, pilot waves, simulation arguments, superdeterminism. The theory His starting point is the hydrogen spectrum. The Balmer, Rydberg and Bohr expressions give a frequency as a difference of two terms — which is to say a beat. Multiplying through by Planck's constant to make it an energy statement is the later move; Balmer wrote it as frequencies, and Schrödinger's first paper explained charge in terms of beats and difference tones.
