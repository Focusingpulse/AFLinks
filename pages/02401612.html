GC2026-PERM042 A 1.1 Myr record of dust-transporting winds in the Afghan-Tajik basin: Insights from the anisotropy of magnetic susceptibility of loess-paleosol deposits Ekaterina P. Kulakova1 , Ramona Schneider2 , Thomas Stevens2 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Uppsala University, Sweden liverpool.town.uk@gmail.com Rock magnetic methods applied to loess-paleosol sequences can play a crucial role in studying dust transport dynamics and the evolution of atmospheric circulation. In particular, the anisotropy of magnetic susceptibility (AMS) measures the magnetic fabric, i.e., the orientation of magnetic particles within a sample, which can provide insights into the direction of paleowinds that align magnetic grains at the moment of their deposition from the atmosphere. Based on AMS studies of two key sections of the Khovaling loess plateau in Southern Tajikistan, covering a geological interval of approximately 1.1 Myr, we performed the reconstruction of dust-transporting paleowind directions for the Afghan-Tajik basin. We conducted a detailed study of the magnetic fabric of two loess-paleosol sections in Southern Tajikistan – Kuldara and Khonako-II. Kuldara is located on the left bank of the wide Obi-Mazar river valley, with a maximum elevation of 1400 m a.s.l. The studied section has a thickness of 84 m and contains 11 loess-paleosols couplets, corresponding to ~920 kyr [1].
