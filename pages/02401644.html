GC2026-PERM050 Identifying the geomagnetic jerk period using spectral estimation based on Eskdalemuir Observatory data Svetlana Riabova1,2 1 Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia 2 Sadovsky Institute of Geosphere Dynamics of Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia riabovasa@mail.ru It was previously established [1, 2] that jerks (or secular accelerations) occur approximately every 3–4 years. This means that the complete cycle of the phenomenon (taking into account polarity reversals) should have a period in the range of 6–8 years. This study utilized instrumental observations from Eskdalemuir Observatory, the UK's national environment observatory. Geomagnetic monitoring data collected over 110 years, specifically from January 1, 1913, to December 31, 2023, was analyzed. Data on diurnal variations in the global Wolf number, available on the website of the Center for Solar Influence Data Analysis (Belgium), were also used. Spectral analysis was used to obtain information on the frequency composition of the digital series under study. Analysis of the spectrum of geomagnetic variations confirms the presence of a peak at 6.4–6.5 years, which is absent from the spectrum of Wolf number variations, i.e., it cannot be caused by magnetospheric or ionospheric sources, and its origin is most likely caused by movements in the Earth's conductive liquid core.
