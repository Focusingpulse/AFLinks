That not only sounds like Hamel but also like Searl claiming that in his initial observations with electric motors that rotating metal would cause electrons to be 'thrown' to the outside rim and with sufficient velocity, a very high voltage would be developed which would soone provide lift and upward acceleration. It almost correlates with the homopolar motor as current flows from the inside to the outside with both the inside and outside serving as 'terminals' for this rotating body....as with DePalma's 'N' machine. Except this is high current as opposed to high voltage, but the principle is similar. rotating metal produces the high voltage, rotating magnets produces low voltage, high current....kind of like a mechanical stepup or step down transformer depending on the form of electrical energy desired.
