MATRIX THEORETIC AND COMBINATORIAL STRUCTURES IN ADDITION CHAINS THEOPHILUS AGAMA Abstract. An addition chain of length h that leads to a number n is a sequence of positive integers s0 = 1, s1 = 2, . . . , sh = n such that si = sj + sk (i > j ≥ k) for each 1 ≤ i ≤ h. We introduce a matrix-theoretic framework for studying the properties of arbitrary addition chains that lead to a target integer n ≥ 2 by associating each chain and the sequence of dominant and lower weight summands that calculate each term in the chain an adjacency matrix that encodes the internal geometry. In this linear-algebraic and matrix theoretic framework, we investigate how rank, rank profiles, singular values, eigenvalues, matrix norms, predecessor depths, and track-interaction terms encode the combinatorial and arithmetic structure of addition chains.
