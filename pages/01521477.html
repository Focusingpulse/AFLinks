Overview The Action of an Electrostatic Potential on the Electron Mass is a nine-page paper by V F Mikhailov of Almaty, Kazakhstan, published in Annales de la Fondation Louis de Broglie volume 24 (1999), pages 161–169. It reports an experiment testing a prediction of André Assis's Weber-electrodynamic approach to unifying electromagnetism and gravitation. Assis holds that a charged particle placed inside a uniformly charged spherical shell has its inertial mass altered in proportion to the shell's potential, so that a particle of charge q inside a shell at Coulomb potential U has effective mass m = m ₀ − m w , with the "Weber mass" m w = qU /3 c ². For an electron this gives m w / m ₀ = eU /3 m ₀ c ², which at U = 3 kV amounts to about 2 × 10⁻³ — small, but within experimental reach. Mikhailov's method is ingenious in its simplicity. He places a neon glow-lamp RC relaxation oscillator inside a glass sphere of 5 cm radius coated with an indium–gallium alloy, and measures its oscillation frequency as the sphere is charged. The reasoning is that the oscillation period depends on the lamp's differential resistance R i , which in turn depends on the mass of the charge carriers: taking T = ξ R i and R i = ζ m , the fractional period shift equals the fractional mass shift, Δ T / T ₀ = m w / m ₀. Each flash of the lamp drives a timer counting a group of N = 1000 pulses.
