Brian O'Leary Born ( 1940-01-27 ) January 27, 1940 Boston, Massachusetts, United States Died July 28, 2011 (2011-07-28) (aged 71) Vilcabamba, Ecuador Nationality American Known for NASA scientist-astronaut; later advocacy of new-energy and consciousness research Scientific career Fields Astronomy , energy policy, new-energy research Brian Todd O'Leary (27 January 1940 – 28 July 2011) was an American astronomer, author and NASA scientist-astronaut who, in the second half of his life, became a leading advocate of "new energy" and consciousness research. Trained as a planetary scientist, he was selected as an astronaut for a projected crewed mission to Mars, held faculty posts at Cornell and Princeton, and advised on national energy policy, before turning to the investigation of unconventional energy sources and the frontiers of science — the work for which he is catalogued here. Biography Education 1961 – B.A. in physics, Williams College 1964 – M.A. in astronomy, Georgetown University 1967 – Ph.D. in astronomy, University of California, Berkeley Astronomer and scientist-astronaut O'Leary carried out his doctoral research on the planet Mars, publishing papers on its atmosphere and surface while a graduate student at Berkeley. In August 1967 NASA selected him as a scientist-astronaut. He was the first astronaut chosen specifically for a potential crewed mission to Mars — then still in NASA's plans as a follow-on to Apollo, projected for the 1980s — and the only planetary scientist in the astronaut corps during the Apollo program. When the Mars program was cancelled in 1968, he resigned from the astronaut corps for lack of any prospect of spaceflight, never having flown. Academic and policy career O'Leary was assistant professor of astronomy at Cornell University from 1969 to 1971, working alongside Carl Sagan, and a consultant at the NASA Ames Research Center. Through the 1970s he taught technology assessment and energy policy at the University of California, Berkeley
