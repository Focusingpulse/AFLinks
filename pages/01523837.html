Saturday Morning Science Chats with Franklin Hu Summary Bob Gray presented an update on his classical electrodynamics research, hosted by Franklin Hu , with Bill Lucas , Ian Cohen, James Keene and Alistair Riddoch taking part. Gray: from Weber to a generalized Maxwell system Gray is looking for the simplest and most complete route to classical electrodynamics. His starting point is Weber electrodynamics, which is conventionally presented without fields at all: everything follows from a single potential energy function. He has taken that function and defined fields and potentials from it, arriving at four equations that look much like Maxwell's but carry three extra terms. Those terms come from the time derivative of the charge density, which appears in Weber's approach and, for reasons he cannot see, not in Maxwell's; set them to zero and the two systems coincide. He treats this as evidence that Maxwell's system comes up short. What he wants and cannot yet get is the expression for the electrodynamic field that he was taught comes from special relativity. He would rather not import relativity into Weber to obtain it, and he reported a correction to his own understanding: Heaviside derived that same expression from Maxwell's equations in 1888, seventeen years before the relativity paper, so it does not depend on relativity at all. Lucas's The Universal Force derives it too, from six empirical laws of electrodynamics — more assumptions than Weber's one potential function, which is why Gray keeps looking.
