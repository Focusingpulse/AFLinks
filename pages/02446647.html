Homeowners tap the income in their homes

· archived # 2,446,647
09/27/14 - Homeowners tap the income in their homes Sites like Airbnb turn ordinary people into landlords, altering economics of real estate. Got a room above the garage? A furnished basement? A bedroom that a grown child has vacated? You may have an extra source of income right under your roof. As more Americans find their spending power squeezed, they're flocking to an Internet-powered twist on an old money-making idea: taking in lodgers. Tapping middlemen on the Web, would-be hosts can link up with renters much faster and more easily than before, and both sides can size each other up through online reviews and email. The level of commitment is also very different than the old days. Rather than worry about having renters around the house for months or years at a time, this new breed of landlord often hosts guests for just a night or two at a time�giving homeowners much more flexibility. All of which means that people who never thought about renting out their homes before are beginning to warm to the idea�and the change could potentially shift the economics of owning a house for many Americans. "It is becoming more and more prevalent for people to turn their homes into mixed-use properties. If you have a room over the garage or other unused real estate, you definitely can turn it into a source of revenue," says Brian Ashcraft, operations director for tax preparer Liberty Tax Service. The financial benefits add up. Let's say a home buyer wants to purchase a 2,000-square-foot home but only can afford 1,900 square feet. He or she could buy a 2,400-square-foot property, which includes a 400-square-foot room over the garage. The mortgage payment would be about $2,300 a month, compared with $2,150 for the 1,900-square-foot property, according to Mr. Ashcraft.
