Title On Cosmic Electricity Author(s) Martin Kruskal, Ralph E Juergens , C. E. R. Bruce, Eric W Crew Published 1973 Journal Pensée Volume 3 Number 3 Pages 42 On Cosmic Electricity appeared in Pensée Volume 3, Number 3 (Fall 1973) — the issue titled Immanuel Velikovsky Reconsidered V — at page 42. It is not a paper by a single hand. It is a published exchange between four contributors: the Princeton mathematician Martin Kruskal , writing as a critic, and Ralph E Juergens , C. E. R. Bruce and Eric W Crew replying. It is the second of two such exchanges, the first being On Celestial Mechanics in Pensée Volume 3, Number 1 (Winter 1973), Immanuel Velikovsky Reconsidered III , with the same four names. Background Both exchanges grew out of Juergens 's paper "Reconciling Celestial Mechanics and Velikovskian Catastrophism", published in Pensée Volume 2, Number 3 (Fall 1972). That paper set out the proposal Juergens is now chiefly remembered for: that the Sun and stars are powered electrically, by a discharge drawing on a galactic potential difference, rather than by thermonuclear fusion in the core. It became a founding document of what is now called the electric universe account, and of the electric Sun model in particular. The electrical reading of astronomy that Juergens drew on was older than his paper. C. E. R. Bruce , of the Electrical Research Association in Britain, had argued from 1941 that a jet ejected from the Sun had the character of a lightning discharge, and extended that interpretation to stars and galaxies. Crew , a British electrical engineer and a Fellow of the Royal Astronomical Society, took up Bruce's ideas and developed them; his own "Lightning in Astronomy" appeared in Nature in December 1974.
