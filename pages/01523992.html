Title TB — The Hall Effect, Part II Read in full Link to paper (Internet Archive) Author(s) Johann Marinsek Keywords Hall effect, electron holes, electrical conduction, crystal structure Published 2008 No. of pages 4 Read the full paper here (archived copy — the original link is no longer available) Abstract The second of two papers arguing that the theory of moving charge carriers cannot explain the electrical conduction of the Hall effect . Part II attacks the concept of the positively charged "hole" as a category mistake, and proposes instead that the sign of the Hall coefficient is governed by crystal structure , within a model in which conduction is the propagation of a state of charge distribution rather than a flow of charges. The argument against holes Marinsek's target is the standard account in which conduction in a solid is carried by electrons and by holes — vacancies in a filled band treated as particles of effective positive charge. His objection is not quantitative but conceptual, and he states it as a category mistake : "a successive occurrence of different holes is not identical with the movement of one hole." A hole, on his account, is simply the previous location of an electron; a location cannot move unless the atom moves, which the theory does not claim. He works this through the "row of seats" analogy used in textbook expositions and in the Wikipedia article on the electron hole, in which people shift one seat along and the empty seat appears to travel the other way. The analogy fails, he argues, on the same point: seat #4 does not move towards the edge of the row, and all that occurs is a succession of empty seats. From this he draws the stronger claim, which is where the ontological objection lies. Treating the absence of an electron as a particle amounts to a transubstantiation of an empty place into a positron — a creation ex nihilo .
