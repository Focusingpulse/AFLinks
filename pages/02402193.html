GTM2026-main054 Bayesian Nash Equilibrium in a Dynamic Recommendation Game with a Strategic User Alexey Vazhenin1 , Denis Fedyanin1,2,3,4 1 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (State University) 2 National Research University Higher School of Economics, Moscow, Russia 3 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia 4 V.A. Trapeznikov Institute of Control Problems of the Russian Academy of Sciences. vazhenin.aa@phystech.edu We consider a dynamic game between a recommendation platform and a strategic user under incomplete information. At each period t = 1, . . . , T , the platform and the user choose actions simultaneously: the platform selects a recommendation rt ∈ [0, 1], and the user decides whether to click (at = 1) or skip (at = 0). Both observe the realized actions at the end of the period and update their beliefs. The user has a private preference type θ ∈ [0, 1] drawn from a common prior distribution F . The platform does not observe θ but maintains a belief πt (θ) updated via Bayes’ rule after each period. The user is forward-looking and maximizes discounted expected utility: E[ Σ δU t−1 uU (rt , θ) ] , uU (r, θ) = −(r − θ)2. The platform’s payoff is: u P (r, a, θ) = a [αR(r) + (1 − α)uU (r, θ)] − c(r), where α ∈ [0, 1] captures the platform’s commercial orientation, R(r) is advertising revenue, and c(r) is the cost of promoting content r. Specific objectives: • Establishing existence of BNE. • Deriving conditions under which the user engages in explorative clicking—clicking on suboptimal content to influence the platform’s beliefs and secure better future
