Princess Lwoff-Parlaghy Princess Lwoff-Parlaghy, who for the last few years has been living at the Plaza, surrounded by an entourage of servants that included three butlers, maids, waiters, a private physician and special representative, it was learned yesterday, has left the Plaza and is living with a friend in an apartment in Riverside Drive. The Princess, who is an artist of no mean talent, has left the greater part of the pictures she has painted, together with her valuable bronzes, tapestries, potteries and ecclesiastical hangings, in the possession of the management of the Plaza. There seems to be a question of the non-payment of a bill to the amount of about $12,000. It was admitted by the management of the Plaza that the pictures and other valuables of the Princess had been appraised as worth more than $100,000. She has devoted much of her time to painting since she was a young girl, and among the pictures now in possession of the Plaza is one she painted of the Kaiser, seated in a Catherine de Medici chair. In fact, the Princess once asserted to a Herald reporter that it was after a talk with the German Emperor that she decided to come to the United States and paint the portraits of prominent men.
