Simhony solves the problem of gravity in an amazingly simple way - presented by Mark Dorazio. Summary Mark Creekwater Dorazio 's account of Menahem Simhony 's explanation of gravity, and the companion to his presentation on Ernest Sternglass a fortnight earlier. Ian J Cowan hosted. Dorazio was unable to get in for the first hour — he had never registered as a member and the generic link led to the previous week's session — so most of the recording is the improvisation that filled the gap. The improvised first hour Cowan called on James Keene , who described the study he regards as the turning point of his work. Ten years ago he set out to disprove binary mechanics by testing its most outlandish prediction: that surface temperature matters to gravitational attraction. He downloaded lunar laser ranging data from a French observatory, wrote software to read the files, and correlated distance against lunar phase — phase serving as a proxy for the temperature of the near face. The Moon came out closer to the Earth at and near full moon, by some thousands of kilometres, and the attempt to falsify failed. His argument for why this excludes the standard accounts is the one worth recording. Through all phases half the Moon's surface is illuminated; only the distribution changes, so the volume-averaged temperature, and hence the energy content and the mass equivalent, stays the same. Neither Newtonian gravity nor general relativity has anything to vary.
