GC2026-STP063 Energy dependence of energetic electron precipitation rates near the foot of electron isotropy boundary V. A. Sergeev1 , A. Dibaeva1 , N. A. Stepanov2 1 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia 2 Arctic and Antarctic Research Institute, Saint-Petersburg, Russia victor40sergeev@gmail.com Intense isotropic precipitation of energetic electrons is actively explored as one of space weather hazard. Its equatorial boundary (IBe) helps to probe equatorial magnetotail in the case if the precipitation in the IBe foot region is formed by field-line curvature scattering (FLCS) in the tail current sheet. To distinguish FLCS from scattering produced by different kinds of waves, we suggest to explore the relationship of loss cone flux ratio (LCFR) magnitudes from neighbouring energy channels in the IBe foot region. We illustrate the methodolgy by presenting a unique quiet time event in which the NOAA-18 polar spacecraft skimmed along the IBe foot and observed highly irregular variations of the precipitated flux which, nevertheless, obey a very ordered energy dependence of precipitation rates with higher LCFR observed at higher energy. Similar LCFR energy dependence was observed during meridional crossings of other POES spacecraft which crossed the IBe, as well as in several skimming passes found during other periods suggesting that it may have an universal appearance.
