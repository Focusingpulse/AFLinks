caption Wikipedia Dispute : wikipedia:Dark energy This Natural Philosophy wiki page disputes content found on Wikipedia page wikipedia:Dark energy Dark energy is the name given, in mainstream cosmology, to whatever is causing the expansion of the universe to accelerate. It is not observed. It is inferred: type Ia supernovae appear fainter than the standard model predicts for their redshifts, and within the expanding-universe geometry that shortfall is read as acceleration, which in turn requires a term in the field equations to drive it. On the standard accounting dark energy supplies about 68% of the total mass-energy of the universe — making the single largest constituent of reality a quantity that has never been detected, isolated, or produced, and whose value cannot be calculated from any accepted theory. The literature collected on this wiki almost uniformly rejects it. The objections fall into two broad families, and they are independent of one another. The first is conceptual: that "dark energy" commits a category error by treating energy — a bookkeeping quantity, a measure of the motion of matter — as a substance that can fill space and push. The second is observational: that the acceleration itself is an artefact of how redshift and supernova brightness are interpreted, so that there is no effect requiring a cause. Either objection alone is sufficient to dissolve the entity; researchers here hold both. This article states the mainstream case as its proponents make it, then the objections found in the papers archived here, then the alternatives those researchers propose. The observational problem The case should be stated accurately before it is criticised. In 1998 two independent teams measuring distant type Ia supernovae — treated as standard candles, since their intrinsic brightness is held to be uniform — found them dimmer than expected. Within the standard Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker cosmology, dimmer means further away than the redshift implies
