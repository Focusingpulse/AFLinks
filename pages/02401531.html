GTM2026-main015 Hybrid Competition under Fragmented Demand and Limited Consumer Choice Ruslan A. Bogatyrev1 , Marina S. Sandomirskaia1 1 National Research University Higher School of Economics sandomirskaya_ms@mail.ru Competition in many markets is neither fully global nor purely local. Firms may operate in a common market and yet face markedly different competitive environments across consumers. Some buyers compare several close substitutes, while others effectively face only one or two relevant options. As a result, firms compete intensely for some transactions and hardly at all for others. This view is consistent with a large literature emphasizing the role of search frictions, limited attention, and intermediation in shaping market outcomes (Varian, 1980; Gabaix and Laibson, 2006; Ellison and Ellison, 2009; Dinerstein et al., 2018). Standard models abstract from this structure. Canonical models of monopolistic competition impose symmetric interaction between firms and consumers, while spatial models generate local competition through a highly structured notion of distance. This paper studies price competition in such a hybrid environment. The economy consists of a unit mass of consumer equally distributed across multiple regions, each with a continuum of firms (potential product varieties). A fraction of varieties is realized through entry, while some firms remain inactive, that is, varieties remain unpresented.
