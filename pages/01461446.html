Progress and Poverty (1879) – Historical Summary Overview Progress and Poverty (1879), authored by Henry George, is a foundational work in political economy that investigates the paradox: Why does poverty persist—and even deepen—amid increasing wealth and technological progress? The work presents a comprehensive inquiry into the causes of industrial depressions, the distribution of wealth, and proposes a radical yet systematic remedy centered on land and economic structure. Historical Context Henry George wrote Progress and Poverty following direct observation of extreme contrasts between wealth and poverty in rapidly developing urban environments, particularly in New York and San Francisco. He observed that: Technological advancement and industrial growth were accelerating Wealth was increasing dramatically Yet poverty, unemployment, and social distress were simultaneously intensifying This contradiction became the central problem the book seeks to resolve. The Central Problem George identifies a recurring global pattern: Increased production does not eliminate poverty Industrial progress correlates with: Economic instability Labor displacement Concentration of wealth Cyclical depressions He further observes: The most advanced societies—those with the greatest wealth and technological development—also exhibit the most severe poverty and inequality.
