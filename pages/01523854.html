Saturday Science Chats lead by Franklin Hu sponsored by the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society. Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with James Keene and Bob Gray , most of it a sustained and good-tempered argument between Hu and Keene about what counts as an explanation. Deriving the constants Keene's claim for binary mechanics is that it derives what are called the fundamental constants — the speed of light, Planck's constant, the electron mass and others — from first principles, and that nobody else does. His first principles he described as minimal: the full quantisation of space, time and energy, quantisation being already accepted for charge. The distinction he insisted on, and which Hu eventually accepted as a clean statement of where they differ, is between deriving a constant and showing interrelationships among constants. The literature is full of formulae relating the fine structure constant, the charge, the speed of light and the rest to one another, and Keene has no quarrel with that work — it is simply, as he put it, a different cage in the zoo. His rule is that unexplained data may not be explained by reference to other unexplained data. He noted, as support rather than opposition, that the standard model concedes it cannot derive any of these constants from its own principles. Hu's counter, pressed several times, was that binary mechanics grounds out the same way one level down: it takes fundamental quantities of length, time and mass, and cannot say where those come from. Keene agreed without embarrassment — he has not attempted to, and there is an empty slot for anyone who wants to.
