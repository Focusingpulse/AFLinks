Peter Glück Nationality Romanian Known for Commentary on cold fusion and LENR Scientific career Fields New Energy Institutions Infinite Energy magazine (Scientific Advisory Board); Ego Out blog Peter Glück (Péter Gluck) was a Romanian chemical engineer based in Cluj who became one of the most prolific and outspoken commentators on cold fusion and low-energy nuclear reactions (LENR). Through his long-running blog "Ego Out" and his contributions to Infinite Energy magazine, he chronicled and critiqued the field for more than three decades, arguing that cold fusion represented a genuine but badly mismanaged path to a new energy source. He died on 23 March 2021 at the age of 83. Biography Glück was a chemical engineer by training and profession, with a career spent in the Romanian chemical industry, working out of Cluj. He often invoked his hands-on plant experience in his writing, describing himself as a practical "technologist" rather than an academic theorist. He followed the cold fusion field from close to its beginnings after the 1989 Fleischmann–Pons announcement, and remained an active participant in the online LENR community until shortly before his death, on 23 March 2021 — the anniversary of the Fleischmann–Pons announcement — at the age of 83. Work Glück's principal platform was his blog Ego Out , where he published near-daily commentary on the history, politics and technical direction of cold fusion and LENR. He also wrote numerous articles for Infinite Energy magazine and, according to the magazine, served on its Scientific Advisory Board from 1995 until his death. His outlook combined dissident optimism with sharp internal criticism of the field. He argued that classic palladium–deuterium and nickel–hydrogen cold fusion research had reached a dead end, and distinguished it from what he called "LENR+" — the higher-output, commercially oriented systems he associated with Andrea Rossi's E-Cat and with Defkalion — which he viewed as the field's real future.
