Nicolas Leonard Sadi Carnot

· archived # 1,461,011
Nicolas Leonard Sadi Carnot (1 June 1796 - 24 August 1832) was a French military engineer who, in his 1824 "Reflections on the Motive Power of Fire", gave the first successful theoretical account of heat engines , now known as the Carnot cycle , thereby laying the foundations of the second law of thermodynamics . He is often described as the "Father of thermodynamics ", being responsible for such concepts as Carnot efficiency , Carnot theorem , Carnot heat engine , and others. Wikipedia, Nicolas Leonard Sadi Carnot In 1824, a young French military engineer named Sadi Carnot published a modest 118-page book, selling it for three francs along the banks of the Seine. He died of cholera a few years later, largely unnoticed. From that modest work emerged thermodynamics - one of the deepest frameworks in physics. It stands as one of the rare examples in the history of science where an engineer made a profound, fundamental contribution to physical theory, rather than theory guiding engineering. Sometimes, understanding nature begins not with equations, but with the question: How efficient can a machine ever be?
