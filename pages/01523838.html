Saturday Morning Science Chats Summary A small chat over the Christmas week — Franklin Hu and Ian Cohen for most of it, joined by James Keene and, on a poor line, Harry Ricker . It was given over almost entirely to Ian's report of attending a public lecture on the 1919 eclipse expeditions, and to what he learned there. Ian at Astronomy Ireland Ian had gone to a lecture by Professor Peter Coles of Maynooth, given to Astronomy Ireland, on the centenary of the Eddington–Dyson eclipse measurement. He asked from the floor why no terrestrial experiment had been contemplated, and then put his own position: that general relativity as such — the space-time account — has not been verified, only certain peripheral anomalies. Coles replied that if one neglects all the evidence then of course there is no evidence, and the audience applauded. Ian's own description of the moment was fair to both sides: the speaker had said all the right things about science being open to question, was not hostile, and was courteous afterwards in the pub, but was plainly reluctant to engage, and the applause was for putting down a crank at the back. He pressed two further points privately. On the standard rejoinder that GPS confirms general relativity daily, he answered that what GPS shows is the variation of atomic clock rates with gravitational potential, which is not general relativity as such; and he said that GPS contradicts special relativity, since the speed of light there is not independent of the observer's motion.
