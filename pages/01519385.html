Ball Lightning & Tesla's Electric Fireballs

👤 Nikola Tesla · · archived # 1,519,385
Ball Lightning & Tesla's Electric Fireballs Book Author Leland I Anderson Published 1997 Publisher Tesla Book Company Pages 31 Ball Lightning & Tesla's Electric Fireballs is a short monograph by Leland I. Anderson , the electrical engineer, archivist and Tesla historian who founded the Tesla Society and edited the great part of the primary-source Tesla literature available in English. Running to 31 pages, it surveys the reported phenomenon of ball lightning, sets out the principal historical proposals for how it forms, and connects that literature to Tesla 's own reports of producing electric fireballs with his apparatus at Colorado Springs. Library cataloguing records the monograph as a 1997 imprint of Twenty First Century Books of Breckenridge, Colorado (LCCN 99491546); it carries no ISBN, having been issued as a small-press pamphlet rather than a trade book. The monograph also prints, for the first time, Chapter 34 of John J. O'Neill's 1944 Tesla biography Prodigal Genius , a chapter titled "Tesla Tries to Prevent World War II" that was cut from the published book. Summary Anderson's purpose in the monograph is documentary rather than theoretical. He assembles what the historical record actually contains: the long run of published observations of luminous spheres seen during thunderstorms, the small number of nineteenth- and early twentieth-century investigators who attempted a physical account of them, and Tesla's Colorado Springs material describing fireballs generated inside and around the streamers of his coils. The book is written for readers approaching the subject from the Tesla side rather than from atmospheric physics, and its value on this wiki is that it places Tesla's fireball observations in the context of the wider ball-lightning literature instead of treating them as an isolated curiosity. The publisher's description presents the subject as one that has "mystified people for hundreds of years" but that can now be produced at will, with researchers
