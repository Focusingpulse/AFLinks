Geneva Times September 4th, 1901 Page number(s): 6 The Question Asked by Norman Allison. A Caprice on the Wireless Telegraphy of Nikola Tesla. The trouble with Nikola Tesla seems to be that he has not got enough room. Marconi potters about telegraphy from Dover to Calais across the Channel without wires. Tesla comes out with the patents of his plan of wireless telegraphy across the Atlantic all cut and dried. Tesla is a man of imagination; you can’t pin him down to a measly continent; the earth is not big enough. He wants to hitch on to the sun and stars and the outer darkness. For my part I am quite ready to believe in any project he may have in his coat tail pocket for driving ships across the Atlantic in a few hours by means of concentrated bottled and liquefied moonbeams. I am prepared for beam-ships. But the Atlantic Ocean makes Tesla tired. He wants elbow room. This is why I am a little astonished at his modest propositions to talk with Mars. Why not ring up Neptune and the Polar Star right away! Mars seems such a half way sort of job. Let me say at once that I do not at all approve of gossiping with outside planets, flighty comets, or fixed stars. For one thing, it shows a spirit of distraction. Humanity is always wanting to get somewhere else. “Here” has always been an undesirable locality; “elsewhere” is just heaven — until you get stuck up there.
