Title Experimental Evidence Favoring Brightson's Nucleon Cluster Model Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Robert W Bass Keywords Brightson, Nucleon Cluster Model, Isotopes, Atomic structure Published 1997 Journal Journal of New Energy No. of pages 4 Abstract Brightsen's Nucleon Cluster Model (NCM) predicts that a relatively low-energy photon can stimulate a nuclear transmutation under certain specified conditions. Examination of an experiment by Lin & Bockris demonstrates that the transmutation of mercury-201 to gold-197 induced by a mere exothermic chemical reaction (burning gunpowder) is an actual concrete example of the novel process predicted by the NCM. Overview Experimental Evidence Favoring Brightsen's Nucleon Cluster Model is a four-page note by Robert W Bass of Innoventech, Inc., published simultaneously in the February 1997 issues of the Journal of New Energy and Infinite Energy . It argues that a published chemistry experiment already contains an example of a nuclear process predicted by Brightsen's Nucleon Cluster Model. The model itself is described briefly. From the systematics of (Z,A) and (Z,N) plots, Brightsen deduced "that the nuclei of all beta-stable nuclides are comprised of just 3 basic building blocks: deuterons, tritons, and helium-3 nuclei", written NP, NPN and PNP. Unlike shell models, the NCM "postulates that aggregates of any or all of the 3 fundamental clusters are present in the nucleus with their own separate quantized properties, such as energy levels, spin, magnetic moment", so that each isotope has many "isodynes" — distinct modes of existence that Brightsen holds to be "ontologically real population distributions rather than… merely fictitious probabilistic QM calculations". Bass is emphatic about the model's evidential standing.
