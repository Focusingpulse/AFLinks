Stanley A. Meyer Born ( 1940-08-24 ) August 24, 1940 Columbus, Ohio, United States Died March 20, 1998 (1998-03-20) (aged 57) Grove City, Ohio, United States Residence Grove City, OH, United States Nationality USA Known for The Water Fuel Cell; New Energy Scientific career Fields Independent Researcher , Inventor Stanley Allen Meyer (24 August 1940 – 20 March 1998) was an American independent inventor from Grove City, Ohio, known for the Water Fuel Cell — a device he said could split water into hydrogen and oxygen using far less electrical energy than conventional electrolysis requires, so that a vehicle could run on water alone. He fitted the apparatus to a dune buggy, demonstrated it publicly from 1986 onwards, and held a series of United States patents on the components. Meyer's claim was that the cell dissociated water not by brute-force current but by voltage — a high-voltage pulse train applied to a resonant cavity, drawing negligible current. He called the sequence the Hydrogen Fracturing Process . If it worked as described, the energy released by burning the resulting gas would exceed the energy used to produce it, which is why the claim attracted both wide attention and the flat rejection of the physics community. In 1996 an Ohio court, hearing a suit brought by two investors, found that the cell was performing ordinary electrolysis and ruled that Meyer had committed "gross and egregious fraud". He died two years later, at 57, of a ruptured cerebral aneurysm; his reported dying words — "they poisoned me" — have kept the circumstances in dispute among his supporters ever since, though the coroner's finding and a three-month police investigation found no evidence of foul play. Biography Stanley Meyer was born in Columbus, Ohio, on 24 August 1940, one of twin boys; his brother Stephen Meyer , an engineer, worked with him for much of his life and carried on the work after his death. The family lived on the East Side of Columbus before moving to Grandview Height
