GC2026-STP043 A New Method for Estimating Reynolds Numbers in Plasma Flows Based on Data from the Parker Solar Probe Mission Ilia A. Dukanov1,2 , Egor V. Yushkov1,3 , Dmitry D. Sokoloff1,2 , Peter G. Frick4 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow, Russia 2 Pushkov Institute of Terrestrial Magnetism, Ionosphere and Radiowave Propagation of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow, Russia 3 Space Research Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow, Russia 4 Institute of Continuum Mechanics of the Ural Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences idukanov@icloud.com The magnetic and kinetic Reynolds numbers (Rm, Re) are parameters of physical processes occurring in magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) systems [1]. They characterise the relationship between dissipation and nonlinear flow effects determining the development of the turbulent cascade, the length of the inertial interval of the turbulent spectrum and the dissipation rate. The task of reconstructing Reynolds numbers from spectral or correlation characteristics of the process is recognised as a classic problem in astrophysics and, in particular, solar wind physics. Nevertheless, for solar wind plasma, the question of estimating Reynolds numbers appears to be quite problematic because, on the one hand, there are many works in which these numbers have already been analysed, see, for example, review [2], but on the other hand, the spread of existing estimates covers many orders of magnitude, and it raises questions about the methods used for estimation.
