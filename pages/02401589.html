GC2026-STP065 Spectral characteristics of electron precipitation from the Earth's outer radiation belt caused by different scattering mechanisms Alexandra R. Ivanova1,2 , Andrey G. Demekhov3 , Vladimir V. Kalegaev1,2 , Tatiana A. Yahnina3 1 Skobeltsyn Institute of Nuclear Physics Lomonosov Moscow State University (SINP MSU), Russia 2 Faculty of Physics, Moscow State University 3 Polar Geophysical Institute of the Kola Scientific Center of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Apatity, Russia ivanova.ar20@physics.msu.ru We study statistical properties of relativistic and subrelativistic electron precipitation from the Earth’s outer radiation belt based on the data of the NOAA POES and MetOp satellites. Two different magnetic storms were considered: a weak prolonged storm including the HILDCAA event on October 11-16, 2017, and a strong magnetic storm with isolated substorm activity on March 23-30, 2023. Precipitation caused by resonant interaction with whistler mode waves and EMIC waves, and by scattering on the curved tailward magnetic field lines was considered. We approximate the integral energy spectrum of the precipitating electron flux by a power law function and show that such an approximation is good in most cases. Thus, we study the index of this power law as a measure of the rigidity of the integral energy spectrum. The scattering efficiency for electrons of different energies was estimated using the ratio of the trapped and precipitating electron differential fluxes. Energy channels E1 (>40 keV), E2 (>130 keV), and E3 (>287 keV) of the MEPED instrument were considered,
