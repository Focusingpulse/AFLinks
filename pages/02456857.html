This finding has major implications for understanding environmental threats to many species. Researchers exposed the eggs of a common species of frog to various levels of acidity and UV-B radiation, the most harmful form of ultraviolet light. By themselves, even the most acidic water or the most severe UV-B radiation did not kill many embryos. But when the two environmental assaults were combined, as many as half of the eggs failed to hatch. Ecologists should look for similar interactions in nature as they try to explain why many populations of amphibians around the world are dying out, the study's authors say. Stresses such as habitat loss, a thinning ozone layer, acid rain, pesticides, disease, and global climate change may combine to wreak more havoc on frogs, toads and salamanders than any one factor could by itself.
