Claude Poher Poher speaking at the National Press Club, Washington DC, 2007 Born ( 1936-02-19 ) February 19, 1936 (age 90) Brittany, France Nationality French Known for The "universons" theory of quantum gravitation; founder of GEPAN Scientific career Fields Astrophysics; space research; aeronautics Institutions Centre National d'Études Spatiales (CNES) Claude Poher (born 19 February 1936) is a French astrophysicist and space researcher, known within the dissident literature for the "universons" theory — a corpuscular, "pushing" model of gravitation in the Le Sage tradition — and known publicly as the founder of GEPAN , the French space agency's unit for the scientific study of unidentified aerospace phenomena. He is listed in Jean de Climont 's Worldwide List of Dissident Scientists . Career Poher was born in Brittany and holds degrees in space-research engineering and electronics together with a doctorate in astrophysics. He joined the Centre National d'Études Spatiales (CNES) in the 1960s and spent some three decades there in space research and aeronautics, rising to direct the Rockets and Probes division and, by 1976, the division of Scientific Systems and Projects . He contributed instruments and experiments to NASA's lunar programme and to planetary exploration missions. In May 1977 he established and directed GEPAN ( Groupe d'Études des Phénomènes Aérospatiaux Non-identifiés ) in Toulouse — the first official, government-funded scientific body charged with investigating unidentified aerospace phenomena. He began with a secretary and built the group to about ten scientists and engineers. He took a sabbatical on 30 December 1978 and ceased official work on the subject, though he has continued to follow it privately. The unit itself survived under later names and remains part of CNES.
