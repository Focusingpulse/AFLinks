Title Declining Speed and Frequency in Waves Crossing the Cosmic Space Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Mario Ludovico Keywords Frequency, space, speed of light , electromagnetic waves Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 3 Pages 343-345 Abstract Upon the basic assumption that the universe's physical space consists in an undetectable continuous and incompressible fluid, it is found that the propagation, through such a medium, of transverse waves - like gravitational standing waves or electromagnetic waves - entails loss in the wave frequency and propagation speed as the distance from the waves' source increases. It is a conclusion that derives from the hypothesis that the fluid cosmic space (which is here dubbed "the plenum" and thought of as destitute of mass) is kept cohesive by its own kinetic viscosity , in place of an impossible dynamic viscosity . Thus, the hypothesis leads to question the postulated constancy of the speed of light. Overview Mario Ludovico's three-page paper, presented at the 19th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference in July 2012, extends a fluid model of space he had set out in earlier work. Physical space is taken to be a continuous, incompressible, undetectable fluid — "the plenum", opposed to the true void — and, crucially, one "destitute of mass". Because the plenum has no mass, Ludovico argues, it can have no dynamic viscosity μ, whose dimensions involve mass. He therefore builds the model on kinematic viscosity γ = μ/ρ, with dimensions L²T⁻¹, and posits a corresponding "kinetic tension" τ = γ d v /d r acting between adjacent layers in laminar motion. Gravitational sources are ring vortices in this fluid. Around a vortex whose core is a "void doughnut" of cross-sectional radius r ₀ at distance D from the centre, the plenum's speed is v = Vr ₀/( r − D ), so that d v /d r = − Vr ₀/( r − D )².
