Exotic Research Report ISO 4 abbreviation Find out here Discipline New energy , suppressed technology, alternative health, conspiracy research Peer-reviewed NoNo Language English Edited by Steven R Elswick Publication details Publisher Exotic Research (United States) Publication history 1996–1999 Frequency Quarterly Links [ Back issues Journal homepage] Exotic Research Report was a quarterly magazine published between 1996 and 1999 by Exotic Research , the venture Steven R Elswick founded after leaving the International Tesla Society in 1996. It ran alongside the Exotic Research Conferences of 1998–2001, and preceded Elswick's later magazine ExtraOrdinary Technology , which began in 2003. History Elswick had been an officer and director of the International Tesla Society from 1984, editing its symposium proceedings and its periodicals Tesla and Extraordinary Science . He left in 1996 to form Exotic Research, and the first Exotic Research Report is dated January/February/March 1996; the magazine was run from a post-office box in Stanfield, Arizona. Eleven issues are archived: four in Volume 1 (1996), four in Volume 2 (1998) and three in Volume 3 (1999). No issue dated 1997 appears in the archive. The magazine gave way to the conference series and, after a gap, to ExtraOrdinary Technology . The publisher's column in the last archived issue, for the third quarter of 1999, spoke of "changes... and more changes as we fight against overwhelming odds". Character Exotic Research Report had a noticeably wider and more political range than the magazine that succeeded it. Alongside the technical material — plasma research, Paul Pantone's GEET fuel processor, radiant energy devices, cold fusion — the early issues carry a great deal of conspiracy and alternative-health writing : Gulf War Syndrome and alleged germ warfare, HAARP as environmental warfare, mind control, ECHELON surveillance, colloidal silver, and the "dumbing down of America".
