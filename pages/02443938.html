US patent office reveals number of secret patents

· archived # 2,443,938
07/18/14 - US patent office reveals number of secret patents In the year to the end of September 2008, a total of 68 new US inventions were made secret - but only 22 of them, against 53 last year, came from what the US Patent And Trademark Office (USPTO) calls "John Does" - private US citizens who invent stuff in the garages and sheds. You know, minor league economic contributors like Google founders Sergey Brin and Larry Page. In 15 NATO countries, plus Australia and New Zealand, inventors can see their ideas disappear in a cloud of government secrecy - oftentimes never to be seen again in any useful (for which read 'exploitable') timeframe. That's because if spooks or the military services deem an invention a threat to national security, its publication is suppressed until such a time as the threat is deemed to have lapsed. This article explains how US and UK officials make the secrecy choice. As we pointed out last year, with 5002 patents under wraps, the US has suppressed ideas from the equivalent of five Thomas Edisons, who famously had over a thousand patents to his name. Although 68 new secrecy orders were applied in 2008, some 47 older ones were rescinded - so a total of 5023 secrecy orders are still in effect. Still, at least the USPTO has the good grace - and open government ethos - to publish the figures: the other countries operating secrecy order schemes do not. All you'll see, now and again, is a patent with a bafflingly old filing date emerging with any one week's new crop of patents.
