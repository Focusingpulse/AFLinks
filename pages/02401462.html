GC2026-SGG024 Application of the Nakamura Method in Seismic Microzonation for Critical Facility Siting Daniil A. Sidoryk1 , Arkady G. Aronov1 1 Geophysical Monitoring Center National Academy of Sciences of Belarus, Republic of Belarus sidorik@cgm.by The HVSR (Horizontal-to-Vertical Spectral Ratio) method, also known as the Nakamura method, is widely used in the modern seismic microzonation practice to estimate the fundamental resonant frequency of microsites, as only one three-component station is needed for this purpose. In the Republic of Belarus the seismic microzonation is used at a construction site to determine the degree of seismic impact on the MSK-64 intensity scale depending on the soil conditions. Therefore, the potential adaptation of the relatively inexpensive Nakamura method to existing seismic zoning practices is being considered. Seismic records obtained during seismic zoning work at a critical facility site in 2009 were used as the initial data. At that time, the seismic microzonation involved the calculation of the intensity increments due to the seismic rigidity changes, as well as fieldworks aimed at recording explosions and microseisms, the resulting data being processed using standard methods with an assessment of the ratio of maximum amplitudes. Based on the initial three-component records, the Nakamura spectra were calculated for 80 points within the study area using the MicroReq software package [1, 2].
