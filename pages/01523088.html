Munera Born ( 1943-10-16 ) October 16, 1943 (age 82) Residence Bogotá, D.C., Colombia Nationality Colombian Alma mater University of Surrey; Universidad Nacional, Bogotá; University of California, Berkeley Known for The stationary Michelson–Morley interferometer at Bogotá; the reinstatement of absolute space; neo-Cartesian mechanics and Le Sagian gravity Scientific career Fields Physics , nuclear engineering, foundations of mechanics Institutions Centro Internacional de Física, Bogotá Héctor Augusto Munera Orozco (born 16 October 1943) is a Colombian physicist at the Centro Internacional de Física in Bogotá, and one of the very few researchers documented on this wiki who has built and run a Michelson–Morley interferometer of his own rather than reanalysing the historical ones. Between 2003 and 2005 he operated a stationary interferometer close to the equator, and reported that after subtracting environmental effects the residual fringe shifts remain periodic and correlate with the Earth's motion — a result he holds to be contrary to the predictions of special relativity and to point toward the reinstatement of absolute space . Education and career Munera graduated as a chemical engineer in Medellín, and took an MSc in Radiation Studies at the University of Surrey, England, and an MSc in Operational Research at the Universidad Nacional in Bogotá. Eight years at the Institute for Nuclear Affairs , Bogotá, in experimental nuclear physics and radioisotope applications PhD in Nuclear Engineering , University of California, Berkeley Postdoctoral work at ETH Zürich — the institution where Einstein studied At Berkeley he was, in his own phrase, "caught by the swirl of the nuclear risk controversy", and spent some fifteen years working instead on the foundations of decision making .
