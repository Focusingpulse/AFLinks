08/09/13 - Red light at night � sleeper�s delight Now US scientists say red light at night signals significant health benefits, compared with other colours. The latest animal study, by US neuroscientists at Ohio State University, builds on a growing body of research on the health hazards of light at night, and suggests that the colour of the light makes a big difference. In research on hamsters, the scientists found that blue light � the kind emitted by electronics and energy-efficient light bulbs � had the worst effects on the rodents� moods, followed closely by white light. Hamsters exposed to red light at night had significantly less depression-like symptoms and changes in the brain linked to depression. The only rodents that fared better than those exposed to red light were those who spent the night in total darkness. The findings may have relevance for humans, particularly those whose work on night shifts makes them susceptible to mood disorders, says Dr Randy Nelson, co-author of the study and professor of neuroscience and psychology at The Ohio State University, in a university press release. "Our findings suggest that if we could use red light when appropriate for night-shift workers, it may not have some of the negative effects on their health that white light does," Dr Nelson says. Scientists point out that before the industrial revolution, and the advent of artificial lighting, the sun was the major source of lighting, and people spent their evenings in (relative) darkness, lightened only by the moon and the stars. They maximised their sun exposure by rising early, working outside, and going to bed when the sun went down. The invention of fluorescent lighting, TV, computers, mobile phones and tablets has dramatically changed patterns of behaviour by increasing exposure to blue light.
