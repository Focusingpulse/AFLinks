Inside the Neutron Calculation of the mass and β-decay-curve of the Neutron Abstract In this work, the classical neutron model of proton and core electron was used in combination with the findings of projection theory. The latter means that on the one hand - analogous to the proton - it is a cubic neutron and on the other hand the interior of the neutron lies below the spatial resolution of our projective world and the laws of our space-time world only apply here to a limited extent, so that the objections raised by established physics against this model do not apply. According to our findings, the electron does not reside in the entire cubic interior, but in a potential sphere (r = 6.355181 10-16 m) around the positron. The potential space is statistically occupied by the electron by random jumps with a jump duration of tmin . From the statistical frequency of the individual potential energies, the average mass of the neutron could be calculated as 1.674927328 . 10-27 kg. From the above-mentioned potential jumps, the resulting changing potential energies and masses at constant elementary particle density result in a change in the size of the neutron in the rhythm of the jumps (vibration model). The focus of this work was on the creation of a decay curve for the β-decay of the neutron, i.e. on the calculation of the frequency of the individual kinetic energy contributions of the decay product electron. The cause of neutron decay is the electron jumps from the potential space to the surface of the neutron, which lead to harmonic oscillations of the neutron shell. The electron on the surface is ejected from the neutron by the outwardly directed counter-amplitude. As the oscillation energy resulting from the released potential energy is evenly distributed over all spatial directions, only 1/6 of the oscillation or potential energy is transferred to the electron in the event of a targeted jump in one spatial direction.
