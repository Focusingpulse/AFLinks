An Ideal Inelastic Collision Model using Center of Mass Frames Shows Conservation of Kinetic Energy

· archived # 1,519,337
Title An Ideal Inelastic Collision Model using Center of Mass Frames Shows Conservation of Kinetic Energy Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Edward Henry Dowdye , Shahin Ghazanshahi Keywords mass, conservation of energy Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 4 Pages 137-140 Abstract A frame transfer model introduced here shows that the kinetic energy is totally conserved and accounted for in the ideal inelastic collision as well as in the elastic collision. The kinetic energy transfer between colliding masses in the ideal inelastic collision case is found to be totally consistent with the law of conservation of energy which states that energy can be neither created nor annihilated. In all inelastic collisions, the two colliding masses move jointly at precisely their center-of-mass velocity, a velocity which is unchanging in a closed system of unbound masses. For this reason, a properly formulated energy transfer model is chosen to be one that goes from the initial frame of the moving mass to that of the center-of-mass frame. Overview Edward H. Dowdye Jr., of Pure Classical Physics Research, and Shahin Ghazanshahi, professor of electrical engineering at California State University Fullerton, argue that kinetic energy is conserved in a perfectly inelastic collision when the energy transfer is referred to the centre-of-mass frame rather than the laboratory frame. Their starting observation is correct and is the basis of the argument. When a moving mass strikes a stationary mass that is free to move, and the two join, they proceed together at exactly the centre-of-mass velocity V CM , which is unchanged for a closed system of unbound masses. This, they note, is confirmed by countless experimental observations. From it they draw a conclusion about the energy.
