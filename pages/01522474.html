Arthur Zinzen Born ( 1893-09-29 ) September 29, 1893 Düsseldorf, Germany Died January 4, 1965 (1965-01-04) (aged 71) Berlin, Germany Nationality German Known for The Zinzen diagram; late work on the nature of inertia and of the fundamental physical quantities Scientific career Fields Engineering (combustion and steam power); standardization Institutions Technical University of Berlin; German Standards Committee (DNA) Arthur Zinzen (29 September 1893 – 4 January 1965) was a German engineer, standards expert and university teacher. In mainstream engineering he is remembered for the Zinzen diagram , a phase diagram for the melting of fuel ashes, and for his long leadership of the German Standards Committee. Late in his career he turned to foundational questions in physics and its philosophy, publishing in 1962 on the nature of inertia and on the classification of the fundamental physical quantities — the work for which he is catalogued here. Biography Education 1921 – Diploma in engineering, Technical University of Munich 1927 – Dr.-Ing. (doctorate), Technical University of Berlin 1943 – Habilitation, Technical University of Berlin Career After completing his studies Zinzen worked in industry: at Wumag in Görlitz (1921–1923), at AEG in Berlin from 1923, at Klöckner-Humboldt-Deutz in Cologne-Kalk as scientific assistant to the general director, and from 1934 at Rheinmetall-Borsig in Berlin. From March 1946 he taught at the Technical University of Berlin as a Privatdozent , and from January 1953 until the winter semester of 1963–64 he was an associate professor of steam boilers and combustion systems there. In parallel he became a leading figure in German industrial standardization: he was secretary to the presidency of the German Standards Committee (Deutscher Normenausschuss, DNA) in 1948, its business manager in 1950, and its director from 1952 to 1963, holding numerous posts in the International Organization for Standardization (ISO).
