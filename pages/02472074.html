And it was nearly or quite as long ago that he produced a beautiful effulgence in a sealed glass tube by electrical means, yet without connecting the tube with a wire. These and other wonderful achievements of that eminent investigator have been the outgrowth of his study of "high frequency" and "high potential" currents. In fact, his most brilliant and original experiments for several years I have been conducted in this, his favorite, department of research, and he has contributed greatly to the general stock of knowledge on this subject. Every schoolboy, nowadays, knows the difference between a direct and an alternating current; and the practical electrician, if not the schoolboy, is aware that an alternating current may have oscillations of almost any frequency. The waves may number only twenty-five to the second, as in the great Niagara dynamos, or they may occur several thousand times as frequently.
