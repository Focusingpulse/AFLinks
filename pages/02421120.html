Jean-Louis Naudin Residence France Nationality French Known for JLN Labs; the Lifter experiments; open replication of alternative-science claims Scientific career Fields Experimental replication; electrokinetic propulsion Jean-Louis Naudin is a French experimenter who, through his laboratory website JLN Labs ( jnaudin.free.fr ), carried out and documented what is probably the largest single body of replication work in alternative science. Where most of the literature catalogued on this wiki consists of theory, Naudin's contribution is almost entirely practical: he built other people's devices, measured them, and published the results — including the negative ones. Method What distinguishes Naudin's work from most amateur replication is its openness . For each project he published construction drawings, parts lists, circuit diagrams, oscilloscope traces, photographs and video, in enough detail that a reader with a workbench could repeat the experiment and get the same numbers. He also maintained the practice of publishing when a device failed to perform as claimed , which is uncommon in this field and is the reason his measurements were taken seriously by people who did not share his interpretations. That openness had a consequence he may not have intended but which is to his credit: by putting a device into hundreds of hands with reproducible instructions, he made it possible for a claim to be settled rather than merely argued about. The Lifter is the clearest case. The Lifter experiments Naudin's best-known work is the Lifter — a lightweight triangular frame carrying an asymmetric capacitor, a thin wire electrode above a wider foil skirt, energised at tens of kilovolts. It rises silently, with no moving parts, lifting its own weight and a payload on electrical power alone.
