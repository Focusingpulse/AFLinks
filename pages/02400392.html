A Full-Stack Object-Graph Method with a SHA-256 Seam-Gate Case Study Runtime Decompilation and Reflective Compilation in the Nexus Framework Driven By Dean A. Kulik May 2026 Abstract This paper formalizes a working method that emerged from the Nexus Framework research program: runtime decompilation. The method begins with the observation that a local object is not equivalent to its local definition. A software class is not merely its source file; it is the resolved dependency field containing imports, interfaces, dependency-injection registrations, constructor paths, lifetimes, threads, recursion, side effects, exception paths, logs, and unimplemented stubs. Likewise, a mathematical or physical object is not only its visible instance; it is the resolved object graph that makes the instance executable. The method is stated as a compile/decompile loop. A trace is decompiled into a mold, the mold is recompiled into a new trace, and the two traces are compared by invariant preservation. The exhaust of the comparison is not discarded; it becomes the next object of analysis. In this frame, open problems are not treated as vague unknowns. They are typed unresolved stubs, unoccupiable seams, or dead branches. The distinction matters: an unresolved stub has a signature and a path to implementation, while a dead branch is excluded. The paper applies the method to the SHA-256 message schedule. The prior seam-gate analysis identified 𝐾 = {16,17} as the universal mixed seam in the clean echo lattice.
