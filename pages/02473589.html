Goldbach's Conjecture with Twin Primes Only - Potential Proof Author: James DeCoste Date: October 2, 2026 Contact: jbdecoste@eastlink.ca Abstract Following a structural breakthrough in proving the eternal persistence of twin primes, this paper presents a unified, geometric proof of Harvey Dubner's variation of Goldbach's Conjecture. This variation asserts that every even integer greater than 4,208 can be expressed as the sum of two twin primes. By mapping even integers to a symmetrical, triangular mirror grid, we show that the addition of numbers converts into a physical intersection along a linear center axis. We demonstrate that numbers divisible by 6 act as a structural shield, yielding a regular 2x combination advantage over non-divisible numbers. Using an inductive contradiction modeled after Euclid, and backed by the infinite pool of twin primes established in my prior work, we prove that the creation velocity of row combinations outpaces the logarithmic thinning of the primes. This ensures that the possible combination count never drops back down to zero past the final historical exception of 4,208, cementing the proof. Introduction Goldbach’s Original Conjecture states that every even integer greater than 2 can be expressed as the sum of two prime numbers. While the original conjecture allows any prime numbers to form the sum, restricting the pool strictly to twin primes—primes that belong to a pair with a difference of two—introduces a far more rigorous boundary condition known as Harvey Dubner's variation. Historically, this variation has remained unproven because it is doubly constrained: it relies completely on the unproven assumption that twin primes never stop appearing. My previous research resolved this limitation by proving the Twin Prime Conjecture through a local, deterministic base-6 smart sieve [1, 2].
