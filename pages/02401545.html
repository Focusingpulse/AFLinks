GC2026-PERM029 L-Shaped Arai–Nagata Diagrams in Ceramics: Air vs Argon Experiments and Numerical Modeling Roman A. Grachev1 , Olga V. Pilipenko2 , Valery I. Maksimochkin1 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University 2 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences grachevra@my.msu.ru L-shaped Arai–Nagata diagrams are often treated as a “red flag” of chemical alteration during Thellier-type palaeointensity experiments, especially oxidation of titanomagnetite [1]. However, pronounced curvature of NRM can also occur in the absence of apparent chemical changes. For instance, these effects may result from irreversible domain-state reorganization and stress relaxation during initial heating of the natural remanence [2] or from the presence of PSD/MD grains [3]. This creates a practical risk: pTRM checks can look excellent while the Arai slope (and palaeointensity) is biased. We carried out Thellier–Coe experiments in air on samples of ceramic fragments from the archeological monument Dyakovo ancient settlement. A ceramic fragment N8107 repeatedly shows L-shaped Arai–Nagata diagrams with a clear break at ~350–450 °C. The thermomagnetic cycling I s (T) for this sample and for other samples looks stable and highly reversible. There are no detectable Curie-temperature shifts, but the L-shaped Arai curvature remains. We also carried out Thellier–Coe experiments on samples of ceramic fragments from the archeological monument Grishinsky Istok III in both air and argon. Importantly, Thellier–Coe runs in argon on the ceramics also produce L￾shaped Arai–Nagata diagrams
