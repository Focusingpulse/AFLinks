Bauman Moscow State Technical University (National Research University) 3 Arctic and Antarctic Research Institute, Saint-Petersburg, Russia dm.trofimov@gmail.com The state of the ionosphere and understanding of the processes occurring in it are important for many practical applications, including satellite navigation. The delay of the navigation signal in the ionosphere is one of the main errors that affect the determination of coordinates. This problem is solved by transmitting the signal at two frequencies, which allows for the full consideration of this effect. However, the majority of GNSS equipment consists of single-frequency receivers, which do not allow for the full consideration of the ionospheric delay. The ionospheric delay is taken into account by using an ionosphere model, the parameters of which are included in the navigation signal. For GLONASS frequency-division signals, such a model is not available, which makes it necessary to create an ionosphere model that estimates the ionospheric delay based on the time and location of observations.
