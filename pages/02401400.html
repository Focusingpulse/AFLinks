GC2026-SGG010 Practice and prospects for the application of Microseismic Sounding Method in geological exploration of mineral deposits Andrei V. Gorbatikov1 , Alexey A. Tsukanov1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences avgor70@mail.ru Based on an analysis of the properties of the Microseismic Sounding Method (MSM) [1,2], it has been shown that zones of reduced and increased seismic wave velocities can be identified against the background of the average regional velocity model on the base of spectral amplitudes variations. Unlike many existing passive seismic exploration methods [3-5], MSM favors subvertical structures and contacts with horizontal dimensions comparable to or even smaller than the length of the sounding wave. Subvertical geological structures are characteristic of a significant number of deposits. These include diamond-bearing kimberlite pipes and a number of genetic types of gold deposits. Examples are given of exploration and pilot industrial work using MSM technology at diamond deposits in Botswana and orogenic-type deposits: at the Jerui deposit in Kyrgyzstan and on a private license near the Geita deposit in Tanzania. In all cases, the work was deemed successful. In particular, at the Jerui deposit, MSM was used to identify known fault structures, including those controlling ore bodies. At the Geita deposit, based on the results of MSM, the first exploration well revealed an ore intersection at a level of 1 gram per ton. References 1. A.V. Gorbatikov.
