Jacques Moret-Bailly Residence Dijon, France Nationality French Known for The CREIL alternative-redshift mechanism Scientific career Fields Physics , spectroscopy Institutions Université de Bourgogne, Dijon Jacques Moret-Bailly is a French physicist and retired professor at the Université de Bourgogne in Dijon, France. Originally a specialist in molecular spectroscopy, he is known in dissident cosmology for proposing the CREIL effect (Coherent Raman Effect on Incoherent Light) as a non-Doppler, non-expansion explanation of astronomical redshifts. (He is also listed on this wiki under the spelling "Moret-Bailey".) Biography Moret-Bailly spent his career at the Université de Bourgogne (University of Burgundy) in Dijon, where he worked as a professor of physics before retiring. His early research was in molecular spectroscopy. In later work he turned to theoretical optics and its application to astrophysics and cosmology, publishing a long series of papers from the early 2000s onward. Scientific work Molecular spectroscopy Moret-Bailly's original field was molecular spectroscopy, the study of how molecules absorb and emit light. This background in the interaction of light with matter informed his later work on light propagation through low-density gases. CREIL and alternative redshift Moret-Bailly is best known for the Coherent Raman Effect on Incoherent Light (CREIL), which he introduced around 2003 as an alternative account of cosmological redshift. He argues that when ordinary (incoherent) light propagates through a very low-pressure gas of excited atomic hydrogen, a coherent Raman-type interaction shifts the light's frequencies toward the red without blurring images or scrambling the order of spectral lines. Because the shift is smooth and image-preserving, he claims it can be mistaken for a Doppler shift or for cosmological expansion.
