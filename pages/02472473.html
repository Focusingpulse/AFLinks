MARCONI'S SYSTEM EASILY OUTDONE Reporter Assigned to See Famous Inventor Writes of What He May Have Dreamed. And now it is Eddy, the kite-flyer, who says he has been receiving electric signals from the planets. Hitching his imagination to the tail of his kite, he soared beyond the clouds yesterday in telling of these signals. They came, he said, in groups of three, and for the last two years he has been puzzled by the regularity of the flashes. He flies his kite with a steel wire, and when the wire is cut and the ends connected with silk strands, the electric sparks leap the silken cord at regular intervals of "one, two, three," much as if some Morse operator on Mars with a consuming thirst was telegraphing for relief so he could go out and get a drink. Then the amazing idea seized quick hold of the listeners to the Eddy lecture that the Marconi system of wireless telegraphy was like to a baby playing leap frog, to the hurtling of electric signals through illimitable space. In plain English, the Morse system is used in telegraphing in this country and in a modified form all over the world.
