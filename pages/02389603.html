Geodesic Paleogeography Report: The Richat-Centered Predictive Framework - A global, predictive treasure map centered on the Mauritanian hub

👤 Erydir Ceisiwr · · archived # 2,389,603
This archive presents the Richat-Centered Predictive Framework, a geodesic model for exploring large-scale archaeological patterning through a global coordinate system centered on the Richat Structure in Mauritania. The framework argues that conventional navigation-oriented projections, particularly Mercator-based cartography, can obscure great-circle relationships and broader spatial symmetries relevant to monument distribution. In response, the model re-anchors the global grid to Richat as a natural geodetic reference and applies a spoke-wheel structure composed of directional great-circle bearings and concentric distance radii. Within this system, recurring alignments are identified between Richat, Giza, Petra, Nazca, Easter Island, and a northern arc including Carnac, Stonehenge, and Newgrange. A predictive method termed &ldquo;mirror logic&rdquo; is then used to project known sites through the Richat-centered geometry in order to derive 927 candidate &ldquo;blind spot nodes,&rdquo; interpreted here as potential locations for undiscovered or obscured archaeological remains. These candidate zones are compared with paleogeographic contexts associated with post-glacial inundation and ice cover, including Doggerland, Sundaland, Atlantic ridge-facing shelves, and Antarctic and Arctic sectors. The archive further proposes targeted validation strategies using deep-sea sonar, ground-penetrating LiDAR, and ice-penetrating radar. Taken together, the framework is presented as a testable model for moving from descriptive spatial comparison toward predictive geodetic archaeology.
