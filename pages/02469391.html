Yost Electric Spacecraft Journal December 16th, 2002 The Free Energy Secrets of Cold Electricity , by Peter Lindemann, D.Sc., Clear Tech, Inc., P.O. Box 37, Metaline, WA, 99153, 2001, 130 pp., softcover, $29.95. In his book, Dr. Lindemann focuses on the work of Edwin Gray. Copies of the three Edwin Gray patents are included with three of Nikola Tesla's patents to show how closely they are related. In 1973, Gray invented an electric engine which ran off of high-voltage impulses, reminiscent of some of Tesla's experiments. Lindemann was introduced to Edwin Gray's work when it was first publicized, and became fascinated by the prospects of free energy. He eventually obtained copies of Gray's patents, and scrutinized them to try to figure out how the impulse circuit functioned. While the patents speak for themselves, it is Lindemann's thorough analysis that makes the book worth a careful read. Finding gaps in Gray's disclosures, he turned to Tesla's patents and historical accounts of his impulse devices. The most interesting part of the book is where Lindemann excerpts Gerry Vassilatos' description of the techniques Tesla employed to obtain extremely short electric impulses.
