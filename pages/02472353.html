O'Neill Brooklyn Eagle June 10th, 1930 Page number(s): A1, 6A Fantasy of Wonders Pictured by Science Editor of the Eagle “Ran across a copy of ‘A New Brooklyn Number’ that was published by the Brooklyn Daily Eagle back in 1930 while we were being transferred to our new home just the other day, and I was greatly amused at the way the folks in those days enthused over what they thought was modern. “The speaker was William Suffolk, a rising young man of 120, who in the years that passed since he reached manhood and the century mark has given evidence of being a person of considerable use to the community upon reaching maturity. He was talking to John Nassau, a still younger man who was still in his decades. The pair were passengers in the Sperry sky-gyro that was carrying them and many others from the suburbs in Montauk to the Nucleus, or what was formerly the Borough of Brooklyn. “They were bragging about Brooklyn being the ‘Greatest City’s Greatest Borough,’ and it then covered only 80 square miles. What do you suppose those people who got out the New Brooklyn Number would say if they could see Brooklyn today — the world’s greatest metropolis, embracing all of Long Island with its 1,500 square miles?
