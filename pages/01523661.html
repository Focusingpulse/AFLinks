Physical Reality VS Science Poetry - Open Discussion Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker David de Hilster Date 9 January 2021 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Physical Reality VS Science Poetry - Open Discussion was a live session presented by David de Hilster on 9 January 2021 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. David de Hilster leads an open discussion about how Critical Thinkers often use vocabulary like “Field”, “Energy” and other words without defining them physically. Summary An open discussion hosted by David de Hilster on the words physicists use and the mathematics they use them with. He credited James Keene with coining the phrase science poetry, and put the charge bluntly: he has been around critical thinkers since his early thirties and is now sixty-one, and he is tired of hearing a great many words — energy, fields, charge, dipoles — from people who, pressed on what the word stands for physically, have no answer. He was careful to distinguish that from naming. Calling a particle a G1 is not poetry, because a G1 can be described: what it is made of, what it does, what may be calculated with it. Poetry is a word with nothing physical behind it. Is most dissident work poetry? Bob Gray answered yes, particularly where there is no mathematics — which he takes to be an extension of natural language, letting a logical train of thought be transmitted and followed. He thinks physics needs both the mathematics and the natural-language model, and that denying either fails. He then pressed the harder question back: can anything be defined? An electron is made of particles, which are made of particles; at some level one has to stop and give properties obtained by experiment. Is an operational definition — an electron is what makes a beam, charges a pith ball, curves in a field — not enough? De Hilster's answer was no.
