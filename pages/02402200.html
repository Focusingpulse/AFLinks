Favorsky, Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Irkutsk, Russia 2 Irkutsk State University katdayneko@mail.ru Keywords: Baikal Region, development, environment, ecosystem, institutional approach, institutions, management, territory The concept of the Baikal Region is invariably associated with the lake Baikal, the unique nature object included in the World Heritage List by UNESCO, and the key strategic fresh water resource of the Russian Federation [1]. This relation has not only geographic but conceptual character too: the lake serves as the system core foundation, defining the region's ecological value, economic development, and sociocultural identity.The industries develop, and agriculture takes shape and traditional livelihoods of indigenous peoples are practiced in specific areas, the infrastructure, the key environmental protection regimes are established and recreational nature use is localized around the lake Baikal itself. At the same time, the territory of the Baikal Region extends beyond the lake's shoreline, encompassing its drainage basin, mountain systems, adjacent settlements, and economic zones. The conceptualization and regulation of all these components are grounded in their interrelationship with the lake Baikal.
