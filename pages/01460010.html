Jack Kilby

· archived # 1,460,010
1959, “chip” co-inventor Jack Kilby filed a patent application for 'Miniaturized Electronic Circuits'. Kilby worked for Texas Instruments. Having not earned a vacation, he was left alone in the laboratory during the summer of 1958, when he showed that it was possible to fabricate a simple integrated circuit in germanium, a commonly used semiconductor at that time. An integrated circuit, or chip, is built up of transistors and other components in a single piece of semiconductor material. Kilby was a successful inventor and was awarded some 60 patents. He is the co-inventor of the pocket calculator, which was one of the first applications using the integrated circuit. A survey run before the pocket calculator was manufactured showed that interest in it was negligible. After all, people had slide rules! Another young engineer, Robert Noyce , developed the circuit in the form that it would later be manufactured - with silicon. Kilby and Noyce are considered to be co-inventors of the integrated circuit, but Noyce died in 1990. Kilby was awarded the 2000 Nobel Prize in Physics. In the summer of 1958, Jack Kilby was the new employee at Texas Instruments in Dallas. He hadn't yet earned vacation time, so when most of the company left for summer break in July, Kilby stayed at his bench and thought.
