Title Real time gravity Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Joe Alexander Nahhas Keywords Gravity, Newton , Einstein , Kepler Published 1980 No. of pages 8 Abstract Defining gravity as mass different from zero and distance between object and observer greater than zero will produce observed force as a sign of the exitence of gravity Overview Real time gravity is an eight-page paper by Joe Alexander Nahhas , dated 1978 in its header and recorded here as published in 1980. Nahhas circulated many versions of the same argument, and its central claim is a strong one: that the anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion, 43 arcseconds per century, follows from classical mechanics plus a correction for light-travel delay, "without space-time fictional forces or space-time fiction." The framework is set out in the first paragraph and repeated in the summary. Everything in the universe is an object of mass m at a location r , so its "state" is the product S = m r . Differentiating twice gives what Nahhas calls the total momentum and the total force, P = d S /d t = m v + m ′ r , F = d² S /d t ² = m γ + 2 m ′ v + m ″ r , and the paper's stated conclusion is that gravity exists if and only if m ≠ 0 and r ≠ 0. Between those two statements sits the machinery. Nahhas distinguishes event time from real time: "we measure in present time what happened in past time", so that what is seen is the event plus a delay. He represents the delay by a complex exponential factor, writing every measured quantity as its event value multiplied by exp[λ + iω] t — λ a decay rate, ω a frequency, and separate pairs for mass and for distance. Applied to the two-body problem this yields a time-dependent orbit equation which he describes as "Newton's time dependent equation that is missed for 350 years", and from it a formula for perihelion advance which he evaluates for Mercury and for Venus.
