( click to enlarge ) Perpetual Motion - Cox's timepiece Forever engine - coke clock James Cox ( Ok . 1723-1800) - London jeweler and gold crafts master, inventor. He is also credited with making the Peacock clock, located in the Hermitage. The first information about Cox's watch can be found in a slightly insignificant book by Scottish astronomer James Ferguson, published in 1769. Testimony of the ingenuity of a wonderful English mechanic are the following words of Ferguson, mentioned in the above-mentioned catalog: "I have examined and studied this watch carefully. They continue to work continuously due to the rise and fall of mercury in a completely unusual barometer. There's no reason to fear that the clock will ever stop. They create such a supply of energy that makes it possible to work for hours throughout the year, even if the barometer is removed. Having studied the design of the device, I can say with full responsibility that this is the most wonderful mechanism I have ever seen. - James Ferguson, Bolt Court, Fleet Street, January 28, 1774 "Annual reference book for 1774" (t. 17, s. 248) anticipates the Ferguson recall with the following words: "Amongst other outstanding exhibits exhibited at Mr. Cox's Museum is the enormous barometer, an unusual design of which allowed its author to create a one-of-a-kind perpetual engine... “ A description of Cox's clock from the book: "Eternal Motion. A tale of one stubborn idea" Ord-Huma Arthur "The pictures show a sketch of the general appearance of Cox's "Eternal Engine" and the device of the "barometric mechanism of the sub-plant" The most important elements of the device are two swinging levers Aa and Bv. The F-hinge mounted frame is attached to the ends of these levers with the help of rods. An N barometer flask is attached to the frame, whose tube is lowered into a K glass vessel filled with mercury. The vessel is also hung by rods to swaying levers.
