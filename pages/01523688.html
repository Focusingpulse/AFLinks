Prism Experiments with Laser Beams

· archived # 1,523,688
Title Prism Experiments with Laser Beams Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Erich Wanek Keywords light , CMB anisotropy, solar apex, prism, laser, aether drift Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 3 Pages 600-602 Abstract Smoot and COBE have established anisotropy in the CMB microwave background. The solar system was found to move through CMB at a speed of 370 km/sec towards coordinates L 264, B 48 (galactic) = L 172, B -11 (ecliptic) near Tau Leonis. Earth travels within the solar system at 30 km/sec on its orbit around sun. The laser beam experiment should explain whether, and if so, to what extent, this motion affects direction and speed of a laser light beam. For a few days around both 4th of February and 20th of April (exactly 5 weeks before and after the opposition's constellation of Tau Leonis) a laser beam directed through a prism has shown slight shifts detected at a distance of 7.5m. The shifts during these days need to be confirmed by a professional laboratory. Overview A three-page report given to the 2010 Natural Philosophy Alliance conference at Long Beach, describing a home experiment carried out in Salzburg between 2007 and 2010. It is one of the last things Erich Wanek published; he died in December of the same year. The paper is unusual among the experimental reports collected here in how modestly it is pitched. Wanek does not claim a discovery. He reports an effect he could not eliminate, admits he lacks the means to measure it properly, and spends his last two sections asking a professional laboratory to repeat the observation on specified dates. The apparatus The setup is deliberately simple, and the paper gives enough detail to repeat it. A laser is clamped by two vices to a massive metal frame, together with an external switch so that it need not be touched during a run.
