Till a short time ago, Tesla was a name almost unknown to English ears. A few men had watched the announcements of his work in America, and Prof. S. P. Thompson, if we remember aright, introduced the name of the worker and his work in the borderland of science to the English public. But the man himself is now amongst us, and after his brilliant experimental lecture in the theatre of the Royal Institution on Wednesday the name will become as familiar as a household word. Geissler tubes have long been shown in the laboratory and the lecture-room, and many men have thought that in the dim and distant future something practical might arise from that root. De la Rue made many investigations; Spottiswoode, as will be remembered from his paper at the York meeting of the British Association, carried on the work; but perhaps above all others Crookes delved most deeply into this borderland of science.
