Title Centron – One of the Simplest Particles of Classical Electrodynamics (CED) Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Yuri N Keilman Published 2011 No. of pages 7 Abstract Overview Yuri N. Keilman presents an exact solution — which he names the Centron — within an extension of classical electrodynamics that replaces Newtonian point dynamics with what he calls the Dynamics of Material Continuum, set out in an earlier paper in Progress in Physics . His diagnosis of conventional electrodynamics is that it is inconsistent in structure. The electromagnetic field and the current density are treated as fields, but the dynamics still rests on Newton's mechanics of a material point, improved to relativistic form. A material point has an integrated charge and an integrated mass; it is not a field. Averaging many material points into a mass density gives fluid dynamics, which works macroscopically only because real fluids are made of particles and the densities and velocities are statistical representations. It cannot be used inside an elementary particle. His remedy is to abandon Newton's law and adopt a new field dynamics. He claims that the electromagnetic potential A k uniquely represents physical reality, and works from three equations: the Lorenz condition A k |k = 0, a second-order equation inside the particle involving two wave numbers, and the free equation outside. The Centron is a completely centrally symmetric solution, in two parts. The static part uses the spherical Bessel functions R 0 ( x ) = sin x / x and R 1 ( x ) = sin x / x ² − cos x / x , with x = k 0 r . Inside a sphere of radius x 1 the potential is a combination of these; outside it falls to zero at infinity. There is a volume charge density inside and a surface charge on the sphere — a delta function in the radial direction — so the electric field has a jump at the boundary.
