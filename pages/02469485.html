White, of New York, Arthur Wright, of Brighton, England, Professor Thompson, of Lyan, Mass., and Others Among the Speakers. Special Dispatch to Democrat and Chronicle. Niagara Falls, N. Y., June 9.—The second day’s session of the twentieth annual convention of the National Electric Light Association was interesting from the time of opening to the close. The two notable papers of the morning session were those of J. G. White, of New York, on the “Niagara Transmission Line,” and that of Arthur Wright, of Brighton, Eng., “On the Profitable Extension of Electricity Supply Stations.” Mr. White told of incidents in connection with the Niagara-Buffalo transmission line and of how the current had frequently burned off the cross-arms. The difficulties in securing proper porcelain insulators were well portrayed and he stated the question of suitable lightning arresters had not yet been fully settled. He felt that in the construction of long distance transmission lines all trees close to the line should be cut down in order that there might be no danger in their blowing over on the line.
