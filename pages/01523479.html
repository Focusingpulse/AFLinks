Moessbauer Effect and Tests of Relativity Book Author Ekhard Preikschat Language English Subject Mössbauer effect; tests of special relativity; aether drift Published 1968 Publisher Ph.D. thesis, University of Birmingham Pages 169 Mössbauer Effect and Tests of Relativity is the 1968 doctoral thesis of Ekhard Preikschat , submitted at the University of Birmingham . It reports a rotor experiment using the Mössbauer effect to search for a preferred frame of reference — an aether-drift test of the kind the Michelson–Morley experiment began, but conducted by measuring the frequency shift of gamma rays between a source and an absorber mounted on a high-speed centrifuge. The work was supervised by Professor P. B. Moon and Dr G. R. Isaak , and carried out in collaboration with Isaak and Dr J. H. Broadhurst. Synopsis In the author's own summary, the experiment is sensitive to anisotropies in the propagation of light and is therefore a direct test of the fundamental postulates of the special theory of relativity and of the Lorentz transformation. The limit it sets on effects arising from a preferred frame of reference, or a classical aether, is (2.0 ± 5.2) cm/sec , which the thesis describes as the most sensitive test of the theory to that date. Chapter I gives a short account of the aether-drift experiment and of resonance fluorescence; Chapter II covers those aspects of the Mössbauer effect relevant to the experiment; Chapter III deals with the practical problems of analysing the transmission profile and obtaining a narrow line width. Chapter IV discusses the significance of the experiment and reviews earlier work, both the Mössbauer experiments and those that preceded them. Chapter V describes the design and operation of the ultracentrifuge, with a semiquantitative analysis of the magnetic suspension and acceleration mechanisms.
