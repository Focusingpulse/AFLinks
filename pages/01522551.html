Title Bradley, Sagnac & Entrainment Read in full Link to paper Author(s) John-Erik Persson Keywords Global Positioning System, stellar aberration, Sagnac effect , Ether Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 4 Pages 358-361 Abstract New interpretations are given to stellar aberration and Sagnac effect. By strict following the wave model for light it is demonstrated that the wave motion depends on the ether-wind in one dimension only. This means that we cannot describe wave motion by the vector sum of ether-wind and wave velocity. Stellar aberration is therefore silent about the ether-wind, and the entrained ether is not ruled out. By utilizing the fact that the Sagnac effect can be locked in inside an optical fiber it is demonstrated that Sagnac effect is in reality a translational effect. The problem of synchronizing separated clocks forced Sagnac to use a rotating equipment to detect a translational effect. Together with the experiences from the Global Positioning System (GPS) we can find support for an ether that is entrained by our planet. Further confirmation can be found by detecting the ether-wind by translational equipment in a lab or in a satellite. Overview This is the earlier of two papers by John-Erik Persson in this collection, and it develops the same central thesis as Physics without Paradoxes — that a wave's motion depends on the ether wind in one dimension only, so that the vector sum of wind and wave velocity is not the right description. Here the argument is applied to two specific phenomena. The first is stellar aberration. If only the longitudinal component of the wind matters, then a transverse wind cannot tilt a wave front, and aberration measures nothing about the ether. It follows, Persson argues, that Bradley's 1728 observation does not rule out an ether entrained by the Earth — the objection that has stood against entrainment for three centuries is withdrawn.
