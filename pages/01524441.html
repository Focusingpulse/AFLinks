Experimental facts. Summary Hans Deyssenroth — who mentioned in passing that he would turn 88 within three weeks — presented a survey of experiments he takes to contradict relativity, built around one substantial piece of original analysis: a correlation he has found in the return rates of the Lunar Laser Ranging project. The argument about reflection The core claim is about what happens when light meets a moving mirror. A tennis ball striking a mirror tilted at 45° acquires momentum in the mirror's direction of travel, so its path becomes oblique. Lorentz and Einstein assumed photons behave the same way, and it is from that oblique path that time dilation is derived by Pythagoras — with length contraction then added to preserve the Michelson–Morley null result. Deyssenroth's objection follows Feynman: photons are not reflected at a mirror at all. They are absorbed by atoms in the glass and new photons are emitted a short time later. A mirror is therefore a light source — and by Einstein's own second postulate, light propagates independently of the motion of its source. So the emitted photons acquire no momentum in the direction of the mirror's motion. They travel straight, and may simply miss the detector. Lunar Laser Ranging The test he devised uses the one experiment where the mirror's motion is large and the geometry known to millimetres.
