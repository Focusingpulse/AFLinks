Zueva Mabtera83@gmail.com Affiliation: East-Siberian Institute of Medical and Ecological Research Position: Rheumatologist, Ultrasound Diagnostics Physician Abstract Background Humeral shaft fractures account for 1–3% of all skeletal fractures. Radial nerve neuropathy is the most common complication, occurring in 8–11.5% of cases [1]. The incidence depends on fracture location, reaching 11.5% in midshaft fractures [2]. The radial nerve is anatomically vulnerable within the spiral groove, where it lies in close proximity to the bone. Nerve injury may be primary (at the time of trauma) or secondary — iatrogenic, due to compression by hardware or callus formation. Following osteosynthesis, MRI becomes non-informative due to metal-induced artifacts, necessitating alternative imaging modalities [3]. High-frequency ultrasound enables assessment of nerve trunk integrity, detection of compression, and dynamic monitoring even in the presence of metal implants. Objective To demonstrate a diagnostic algorithm and treatment strategy selection in patients with post-traumatic radial nerve neuropathy following plate osteosynthesis of the humerus, and to define the role of high-frequency ultrasound in this process. Materials and Methods A retrospective case series analysis of 6 patients (5 males, 1 female; mean age 47.5 years) was conducted.
