Title Lessons from the Field: The 2013 John Chappell Memorial Lecture Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Cynthia Kolb Whitney Published 2013 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 10 No. of pages 5 Abstract Like most of you listening to this lecture, or reading this hardcopy later, I have been on a life-long journey, meandering to be sure, but progressing I think. I believe in the possibility of reaching a place where there will be a bit more understanding about what is going on in the physical universe. Along the way, I have gotten swatted down a lot. So will you. But there are some funny stories to tell about all those swat experiences, and laughter does overcome just about everything. So herein are my best ?Swat Stories'. I hope my stories will help you through your own journeys. Overview Lessons from the Field: The 2013 John Chappell Memorial Lecture is a five-page invited lecture by Cynthia Kolb Whitney , long-time editor of Galilean Electrodynamics , delivered at the 20th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in July 2013 and printed in volume 10 of the Proceedings of the NPA . Its declared subject is not a result but a career: Whitney offers what she calls her "Swat Stories", the episodes in which her work was rejected, and draws practical lessons from them. The memoir is nevertheless built around a specific technical objection, and the objection is the reason the lecture is worth reading as physics rather than only as autobiography. Whitney traces her route from an MIT physics bachelor's thesis on the hydrogen molecule under Walter Thorson, through an electrical engineering master's — taken on her husband's advice that it would make her employable whatever happened — to work at the Charles Stark Draper Laboratory on ring laser gyroscopes.
