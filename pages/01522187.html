Title What is Cold Fusion and Why Should You Care? (2012 John Chappell Memorial Paper) Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Edmund Storms Keywords Cold Fusion, nuclear Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 7 Pages 581-587 Abstract Cold fusion or low-energy-nuclear-reaction (LENR) has now been demonstrated to initiate various nuclear reactions in solid materials without application of high energy. This creates a significant challenge for science to explain and for industry to use in a rational way. Therefore, understanding what has been discovered is very important. This paper proposes to provide this understanding. Overview Delivered as the 2012 John Chappell Memorial Paper at the 19th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference in Albuquerque and published in the Proceedings of the NPA , volume 9, this is a review rather than a research report, written with Brian Scanlan of KivaLabs. Its author is not an outsider to nuclear science. Edmund Storms spent thirty-four years as a radiochemist at Los Alamos National Laboratory before turning to cold fusion , and he has since written the field's two standard monographs. The paper accordingly reads as a specialist's account of what he takes to be an established anomaly: "this paper is not designed to prove the phenomenon is real. Many detailed publications have been devoted to that effort… Instead, the general characteristics are summarized." Three things are attempted. A history of how the claim came to be rejected; a summary of the experimental evidence across three families of method; and a sketch of what any explanation would have to supply, organised around a concept Storms calls the nuclear-active-environment or NAE. The argument How the field was closed Storms's account of 1989 to 1993 is specific.
