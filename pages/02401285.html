GC2026-STP004 Experimental Studies of the Impact of Geomagnetically Induced Currents on a Single￾Phase Transformer Vera V. Vakhnina1 , Oleg V. Fedyai1 1 Togliatti State University vvvahnina@yandex.ru During periods of increased geomagnetic activity, high-voltage power systems with grounded neutrals may experience geomagnetically induced currents (GICs) of considerable magnitude [1,2]. GICs cause saturation of the magnetic core of power transformers and can exert a triggering effect on the power system, disrupting the stability of its operation due to the generation of harmonics, voltage drops at network nodes, and an increase in reactive power flows, which may result in significant damage [3,4]. Relative to the nominal 50 Hz alternating current frequency adopted in Russia, GICs can be regarded as direct current, for which protection of electrical equipment in power networks is not предусмотрена by existing regulatory documents. Consequently, there arises a need to study the saturation processes of power transformers in order to understand the operating behavior of electrical networks under the flow of GICs. Experimental studies employing actual power equipment have been carried out only in Finland [5] and the United States [6], where the characteristics of magnetizing current variation, its harmonic composition, and the heating of structural components were investigated. However, it should be noted that conducting such experiments in real power systems is impractical due to the risk of damage to transformer equipment.
