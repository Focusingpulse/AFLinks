Special New York Letter. The question of rapid transit for this city, which has long been under consideration by a commission appointed for the purpose, has at last been brought to a practical solution, and in rather an unexpected way. Through a decision of the Court of Appeals, the plans of the commission for an underground system are completely nullified, and an improvement and probable extension of the elevated system is the only hope at present for our overcrowded business population. There has been a pretty fight going on as between the elevated and underground systems, and the press generally has favored the latter, which affords the only complete solution of the problem. For the present, the Manhattan Elevated Railway seems to have won a substantial victory, and Messrs. Gould and Sage are prepared to negotiate with the commission for the building of extra tracks, making a double-deck system and providing facilities for through express trains.
