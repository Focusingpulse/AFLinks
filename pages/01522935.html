Forrest Bishop Born ( 1955-07-25 ) July 25, 1955 (age 71) Residence Seattle, WA, United States Nationality USA Known for Electromagnetism , Ivor Catt , Bishop Cubes , evolution , space propulsion , Electric Universe , Expanding Earth Forrest Bishop (born 25 July 1955) is an American engineer and independent researcher based in Seattle, Washington. He is the founder and chairman of the Institute of Atomic-Scale Engineering, and is best known outside dissident-physics circles for the Bishop Ring , a rotating space-habitat design he proposed in 1997. Space habitats and nanotechnology Bishop's work in the 1990s belonged to the molecular-nanotechnology community that formed around Eric Drexler's proposals, and he presented at the Fifth Foresight Conference on Molecular Nanotechnology. He developed a "Universal Assembler" concept as part of extended work on the nanofactory idea, and his illustrations are held in the Foresight Institute's nanomedicine art gallery. The Bishop Ring is his most widely cited contribution and carries his name in the space-settlement literature. It departs from the Stanford torus and the O'Neill cylinder designs of the 1970s by assuming carbon-nanotube construction rather than steel, which allows a far larger structure: roughly 1,000 km in radius and 500 km wide, rotating to give one gravity at the rim and enclosing some three million square kilometres of habitable surface — an area comparable to India. At that scale the habitat needs no roof. Atmosphere is retained by rotation alone, behind retaining walls about 200 km high, leaving the ring open to space along its axis. Physics and natural philosophy Bishop's contributions to the Natural Philosophy Alliance and its successor the CNPS are mostly an extension of Ivor Catt 's transmission-line reformulation of electromagnetism. In this account the fundamental physical object is the transverse electromagnetic step propagating at c , and the relation i = qc — current equal to linear charge density
