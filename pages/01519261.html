Accountability and Academic Freedom: The Battle Concerning Research on Cold Fusion at Texas A&M University

· archived # 1,519,261
Title Accountability and Academic Freedom: The Battle Concerning Research on Cold Fusion at Texas A&M University Read in full Link to paper Author(s) John M Bockris Keywords Funding, Academic Freedom, Research, Cold Fusion Published 2000 Abstract Directly after the March 1989 TV announcement by Fleischmann and Pons that they had achieved a nuclear reaction at electrodes in the cold, research began on the phenomenon at Texas A&M. The University was picked by EPRI as a recipient of funds to investigate the field. Thus, it possessed a Thermodynamic Research Center, a Cyclotron group, and three groups in Electrochemistry (Chemistry Department). In addition, there was the Center for Electrochemical Systems and Hydrogen Research in the Texas Engineering Experiment. Overview This is not a scientific paper but a first-person account of an academic dispute, published in the journal Accountability in Research in 2000, three years after its author's retirement. John O'M. Bockris was a figure of real standing. His Modern Electrochemistry , written with A. K. N. Reddy, is one of the discipline's standard texts; he published prolifically across five decades; and he is generally credited with putting the phrase "hydrogen economy" into circulation in 1970. He held a Distinguished Professorship at Texas A&M when Fleischmann and Pons made their announcement in March 1989, and he knew Fleischmann personally — they had overlapped at Imperial College in the late 1940s, when Bockris ran a research group there and Fleischmann was a graduate student. The paper narrates what happened to him over the following eight years: the tritium results his group reported, a journalist's suggestion that they were fraudulent, a graduate student forced to strip his published work from his thesis, a privately funded attempt at transmuting base metals to gold, two university investigations, and the isolation that accompanied them.
