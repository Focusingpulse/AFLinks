MolPhot2026-main013 Colloidal Quantum Dots as Phosphors for Nanophotonics Vladimir F. Razumov1,2 , Sergey A. Tovstun2,1 1 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (State University) 2 Federal Research Center for Problems of Chemical Physics and Medical Chemistry RAS razumovvf@list.ru Phosphors are widely used in science, technology, and everyday life, and also serve as an important building block for nanophotonics. The first generation of such materials comprised crystal phosphors, which are wide-bandgap semiconductors or insulators doped with luminescent ions. In such a system, the host matrix absorbs the excitation energy, while the activator ions act as radiative recombination centers, with the combination of their properties determining the luminescent characteristics of the resulting crystal phosphor. The second generation is represented by molecular luminophores, whose spectral properties are determined by the structure of the π￾conjugated framework and its substituents. The third generation is based on the so-called quantum-size effect, discovered in the early 1980s, which consists in the fact that the optical absorption and luminescence spectra of spherical semiconductor nanoparticles significantly depend on their size. Such objects became known as quantum dots (QDs). The term "quantum" reflects the fact that the size dependence of the optical properties is caused by the energy level quantization of electrons and holes due to their spatial confinement.
