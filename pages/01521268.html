Summary The first of John Hodge 's three recorded presentations, applying the Scalar Theory of Everything at the largest scale. Ian J Cowan hosted and opened with Locke on new opinions being suspected and opposed for no other reason than that they are not already common. Hodge was present throughout to answer questions. Sources and sinks Hodge's cosmology is built from the quasi-steady-state model of Hoyle and Narlikar, with one addition. Where the steady-state theory has matter continuously created — originally in intergalactic space, and after that was ruled out by the uniformity of the microwave background, at the centres of galaxies — Hodge adds annihilation : world lines terminate at sinks in elliptical galaxies as well as beginning at sources in spirals. That makes the universe finite and bounded without requiring a beginning, and he was explicit that the model says almost nothing about how the universe came to be, which may or may not be eternal. His argument for the addition is observational, drawn largely from Binney and Merrifield. Ellipticals are poor in cool gas, their interstellar matter being hot plasma distributed roughly spherically rather than in a disc, with X-ray brightness nearly proportional to optical brightness; the evidence suggests gas and dust are falling into them.
