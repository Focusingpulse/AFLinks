Eichler Born ( 1941-07-14 ) July 14, 1941 (age 85) Residence Little Rock, AR, United States Nationality USA Known for Expanding Earth , Cosmology , Computers Scientific career Fields Computer Scientist , Cosmology , Expansion Tectonics John B. Eichler is an American computer scientist and independent researcher known for his work on Expansion Tectonics — the view that the Earth has grown in radius and mass over geological time — and for proposing a physical mechanism, grounded in the electrical nature of the universe, by which the Earth could acquire that additional matter. A presenter with the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society , he argues that the standard plate-tectonic and gravitation-first accounts of Earth and cosmic history are incomplete, and that laboratory-testable electromagnetic processes deserve a far larger role than mainstream theory allows. Biography John Eichler earned a B.S. in mathematics and physics from the Illinois Institute of Technology in Chicago and later an M.A. in Interdisciplinary Studies from the University of Arkansas. He worked for the IIT Research Institute for eight years, and has worked with computers since the vacuum-tube era, doing both systems programming and digital/analog design. Among other projects he designed blast shields for nuclear reactors for the U.S. Atomic Energy Commission. He spent roughly a dozen years in computerized electrocardiography working on minicomputers and microcomputers, and in the mid-1980s designed and built what was then the fastest minicomputer-based ECG analysis system in the world. He resides in Little Rock, Arkansas. His master's thesis, Rhetoric and Paradigm Change in Science: Three Case Studies , examines three little-known scientific concepts, each advanced by a well-known and respected scientist, through the lens of rhetorical argument and Thomas Kuhn's model of paradigm shift.
