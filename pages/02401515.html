GC2026-SGG033 Geodynamics of the East European platform based on Russian satellite navigation network Pavel V. Movsesian1 , Victor L. Gorshkov2 , Natalia V. Scherbakova2 , Sergey D. Petrov1 , Sergey S. Smirnov1,2 1 St Petersburg University 2 Main (Pulkovo) Astronomical Observatory of the Russian Academy of Sciences movsesyanpv@gmail.com In 2023, the global navigation satellite system (GNSS) marked 50 years since development began and 30 years of actual operation, with the first permanent stations still functioning. Currently, over 25,000 stations are unevenly distributed across continents. The Pulkovo Observatory has created and maintains a velocity database (VDB) for stations approximately covering the East European Craton (EEC) to address scientific tasks, particularly studying EEC geodynamics. Global databases represent Russia's EEC territory with only dozens of stations, limiting geodynamic assessments. The Pulkovo VDB preserves data from over a thousand Russian stations otherwise inaccessible or gradually lost in commercial enterprises. The database description and processing methods are detailed in [1, 2]. The main geodynamic feature is Fennoscandia's postglacial uplift, with interaction between the Baltic Shield crystalline basement and the sedimentary Russian Platform producing several mm/yr crustal deformations [2, 3]. The RP contains numerous structures (Ukrainian Shield, aulacogens, syneclises, anteclises) potentially manifesting in deformations.
