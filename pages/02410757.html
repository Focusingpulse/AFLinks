Evaporation Caused by Light, Without Heat Discovered. Generated by a Photomolecular effect, it is even more efficient than heat-induced evaporation. Innovation will facilitate water desalination and access to water (PNAS Magazine. Technological Innovation) . Evaporation happens around us all the time, from the sweat that cools our bodies to the dew that disappears in the morning sun. So it may seem surprising that one aspect of this ubiquitous process has escaped the understanding of science until now. In recent years, some researchers have reported in their scientific papers that water in their experiments that was trapped in a sponge-like material known as a hydrogel evaporated at a higher rate than could be explained by the amount of heat - or energy. thermal - that this water was receiving. And the excess has been significant, reaching two to three times the maximum theoretical evaporation rate. Intrigued by the question, Yaodong Tu and colleagues at MIT in the USA designed careful experiments to check everything that was happening in these still unexplained cases. And they discovered nothing less than evaporation that does not depend on thermal energy. What has been discovered is that light can directly cause evaporation at the interface where water meets air, without the need for heat - in fact, light-induced evaporation, generated by a photomolecular effect , is even more efficient than heat-induced evaporation. In the experiments, water was retained in a hydrogel, but the team believes that the phenomenon can also occur in other conditions. In fact, the phenomenon of evaporation by light may play a role in the formation and evolution of fog, fog and clouds, and therefore it may be important to incorporate it into climate models to improve forecasts.
