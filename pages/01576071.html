Dipolar Spectral Arithmetic: Irreducible Modes, Factorization, and Operator-Theoretic L-Functions Alberto Forcada Abstract We introduce a new operator-theoretic arithmetic based on the dipolar operator acting on spherical harmonics. The dipolar spectrum forms a discrete, geometrically structured set of eigenvalues shaped by compactification, curvature, torsion, and helicoidal transport. Under natural algebraic assumptions, the dipolar spectrum admits a rigorous algebraic structure: each dipolar mode factors uniquely into irreducible modes (dipolar primes). This Fundamental Factorization Theorem establishes the dipolar spectrum as a commutative unique-factorization monoid. Building on this foundation, we develop a complete arithmetic parallel to classical multiplicative number theory: a dipolar Euler product, a Möbius function and convolution algebra, dipolar Dirichlet series, and dipolar L-functions. Helicoidal corrections introduce controlled oscillatory behavior, producing twisted Euler products, spectral interference, and a conjectural critical band for zeros. Compactification yields a dipolar prime number theorem analogue, with linear prime growth in the spectral parameter. The framework extends naturally to tensor networks, where higher-order multiplicativity and many-body prime structures arise. Dipolar spectral arithmetic demonstrates that arithmetic-like phenomena can emerge from operator geometry rather than integer structure. The theory provides a unified operator-geometric setting in which irreducibility, multiplicativity, analytic behavior, and many-body composition coexist, suggesting a broader program connecting spectral geometry, operator algebras, and analytic number theory.
