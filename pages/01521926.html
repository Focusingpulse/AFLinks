Title The Status of the Experimental Evidence for the B(3) Field Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Stanley Jeffers Keywords longitudinal component, electromagnetic fields Published 1997 Journal Apeiron Volume 4 Number 2-3 No. of pages 4 Pages 55-58 Abstract The current status of the experimental evidence for the existence of the proposed longitudinal component of classical electromagnetic fields is reviewed. Experiments are outlined which might provide more convincing evidence for the existence of this field. Overview The Status of the Experimental Evidence for the B(3) Field is a four-page review by Stanley Jeffers of the Department of Physics and Astronomy at York University, Toronto, published in Apeiron volume 4, numbers 2–3 (April–July 1997), pages 55–58, as part of a special issue devoted to the B(3) controversy. The B(3) field was proposed by Myron W Evans in 1992. The claim is that the classical electromagnetic field is incomplete: alongside the ordinary transverse Maxwellian components there exists a phase-free longitudinal magnetic field, obtained from the conjugate product of the transverse components, pointing along the propagation direction. Through the 1990s the proposal generated an unusually bitter literature, and Jeffers opens by cataloguing it — Barron's 1993 objection that B(3) violates CPT symmetry, and Comay's 1996 claim that including B(3) in the field of a rotating dipole violates Maxwell's equations outright, which Evans and Jeffers answered in 1996 by arguing that Comay had evaluated the curl of B(3) incorrectly. Jeffers' position is unusual and gives the paper its interest. He is a participant: co-author with Evans of volume 3 of The Enigmatic Photon , co-author of the reply to Comay, and co-designer of the experiment proposed here. The acknowledgement thanks Evans for his interest in the work. Yet the paper's conclusion is that the existing experimental case does not stand.
