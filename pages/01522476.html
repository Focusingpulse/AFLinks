Astrophysics is the application of physics to astronomical objects — stellar structure and evolution, compact objects, and the energetics of galaxies. On this wiki the disputes concern the models applied to the observations rather than the observations themselves: the photometry, the spectra and the redshift catalogues are largely common ground, and the argument is about what they are evidence for . This page is a guide to that material. Where a count is given, it is the number of pages on this wiki whose text touches the topic, which is a rough measure of coverage rather than of merit. How the subject is divided here Astrophysics on this wiki overlaps heavily with cosmology and shades into it at almost every point, because most of the disputes turn on the same question — whether an observed feature is intrinsic to the object or an artefact of the expanding-universe interpretation. As a rough division of labour: Astronomy (298 catalogued pages) — observation, instruments and measurement. Astrophysics — the physics of individual objects: stars, quasars, compact objects, galaxies. Cosmology (805 catalogued pages) — the universe as a whole, its origin and its large-scale structure. The formal Category:Astrophysics is small, with thirteen members. That undercounts the subject considerably: most of the relevant material is filed under Cosmology , Redshift (228 pages) or Plasma Cosmology . Redshift, quasars and galaxy association This is the largest and best-developed astrophysical dispute on the wiki, and the one with the strongest observational pedigree. Its central claim is that some redshift is intrinsic to the source rather than a measure of recession velocity or distance. Halton Arp is the principal figure — a professional observer who catalogued peculiar galaxies and argued that high-redshift quasars are found physically associated with low-redshift galaxies, sometimes bridged by visible filaments.
