Extraction of Electrical Energy Directly from Space: The N-Machine

Title Extraction of Electrical Energy Directly from Space: The N-Machine Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Bruce E DePalma Keywords Energy , Generator, Orgone, Prana, Od Published 1979 No. of pages 9 Abstract The extraction of energy directly from space has been suggested as a viable process for the solution of the energy problems of society. The accessibility of this energy has been limited by the necessity for the formulation of new energy paradigms. In the past, energy in space has been suggested by thoughts such as Orgone, Od, Prana, Bio-cosmic, Neutrino energy sea and so on, but the useful extraction of such energies has always awaited more explicit formulations of these ideas which could suggest the construction of simple practical energy extraction machinery. Overview Bruce E. DePalma proposes that electrical energy can be extracted directly from space by a rotating magnetised conductor, which he calls the N-machine, and presents the device as a generator that produces power without loading its drive. The framing is an energy medium. DePalma writes that space is perfused with a "fine substance" lying between a space-time construct and gross matter, which confers inertia on objects and can be polarised. He lists earlier names for the same idea — Orgone, Od, Prana, bio-cosmic energy, the neutrino sea — and argues that what has been missing is a formulation explicit enough to suggest practical machinery. His central physical claim is variable inertial mass. Experiments, he says, show that a rotating object's inertial mass becomes anisotropic: greater along the rotation axis and smaller in the plane of rotation, with complete polarisation when the in-plane inertial mass falls to zero at sufficient speed. The N effect combines this with magnetic polarisation. A cylindrical magnet rotated about its polar axis develops a cylindrically symmetric electric field internally, and current can be drawn between a contact on the axis and one on the rim.
