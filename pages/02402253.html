MolPhot2026-main010 1N-substituted organometallic 1,2,3-triazoles are promising scaffolds for the creation of photocontrolled bioactive molecules Lyudmila Telegina1 , Elena Kelbyscheva1 , Aleksey Rodionov1 1 A.N. Nesmeyanov Institute of Organoelement Compounds of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia popova-ln@mail.ru One of the main pharmacophoric groups, frequently used to create drugs with a broad spectrum of activity, is 1,2,3-triazole [1]. The introduction of organometallic fragments into the 1,2,3-triazole heterocycle helps solve the problem of resistance to existing drugs and opens the way to the production of prodrugs whose biological activity can be modified by external factors such as light, temperature, or ultrasound. Systematic analysis and comparison of results obtained using UV-visible and fluorescence spectroscopy, mass spectrometry, cyclic voltammetry, calculation of non-covalent interactions, and the study of cytotoxic biological activity in the dark and under irradiation opens the way for the rational design of photocontrolled bioactive compounds and the prediction of their properties. To this aim, ferrocenyl and cymantrene-containing derivatives of 1,2,3-triazoles, as well as their organic analogs, were synthesized, and a comprehensive study of their physicochemical, photophysical, electrochemical, and biological properties was conducted. A study of the optical properties in ethanol solutions revealed that all the obtained compounds exhibit broadband fluorescence in the 300-500 nm range.
