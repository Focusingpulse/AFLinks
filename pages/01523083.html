Has Antimatter Been Misunderstood? Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Roger J Anderton Date 22 February 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Has Antimatter Been Misunderstood? was a live session presented by Roger J Anderton on 22 February 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Antimatter was originally conceived as having negative mass based on trying to combine special relativity with quantum physics. But the Physics Establishment then changed it to having positive mass. Is this another example of physicists making a mess of things when dealing with relativity? Summary Roger J Anderton introduced this one frankly as another grumble about relativity, this time following the consequences into antimatter. His standing complaint is the building process: mainstream physics rests on what Einstein said in 1905, everything since has been piled on top of it, and if the foundation is wrong then so is everything raised on it — antimatter included, because the modern concept came out of an attempt to marry relativity to quantum theory. Antimatter before and after Einstein He worked from the Wikipedia account. The modern theory begins with Paul Dirac 's 1928 paper, in which the relativistic version of the Schrödinger equation predicts anti-electrons; Dirac did not at first see the implication of his own equation and gave the credit to Oppenheimer, whose 1930 paper argued for a positively charged electron of the same mass. Anderton's reading of that transition is that the theory was quietly altered between 1928 and 1930 — from something that might have carried negative mass to something with positive mass — and he was careful to say he was waving his arms and would need to check the detail. The prehistory interested him more.
