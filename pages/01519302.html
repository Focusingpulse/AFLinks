Overview An Absolute Space Interpretation (with Non-Zero Photon Mass) of the Non-Null Results of Michelson-Morley and Similar Experiments: An Extension of Vigier's Proposal is a three-page note by Hector A Munera of the Centro Internacional de Física in Bogotá, published in Apeiron volume 4, numbers 2–3 (April–July 1997), pages 77–79. It responds directly to a paper by Jean-Pierre Vigier in the same issue. Vigier had proposed taking the small residual drifts in the Michelson–Morley experiment and Miller's repetitions at face value, and argued that within a relativistic interpretation admitting absolute space this implies a non-zero photon rest mass. Múnera's purpose is twofold: to show that the same conclusion follows from a Galilean–Newtonian theory of motion in absolute space that he had proposed in Physics Essays in 1993, and to read modern Michelson–Morley experiments as evidence for photon mass. His framework recasts Newton's first law as the conservation of an "inertial energy" I ( V ), giving a velocity dependence formally like the relativistic one but derived without Lorentz transformations, so that Galilean velocity addition still applies.
