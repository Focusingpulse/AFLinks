A team of Israeli and French physicians recently tested a new device, called the Rhinoderm, that delivers humidified air at 109 degrees Fahrenheit into a patient's nostrils. Of those allergy sufferers receiving three 30-minute treatments spaced two hours apart, about three-quarters reported that they were FREE of symptoms a week later; two-thirds said their relief lasted for up to a month. The Rhinotherm was effective against allergies to pollen, molds, dust and animal hair. The researchers speculate that in raising the temperature of the nasal passages, the procedure somehow rallies the patient's defenses against the factors causing allergic attacks. They also note that it may effectively suppress symptoms of the common cold as well. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ Vanguard Note The speculation as to why this treatment works on allergies is more logically due to flushing of the nasal passages by the hot moisturized air.
