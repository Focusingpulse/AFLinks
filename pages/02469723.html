MILLIONS SAVED YEARLY. Refuse of Great Cities Can Be Utilized to Produce Power, Light and Heat. GREAT PLANT AT SHOREDITCH. Among the passengers by the Campania yesterday wore Lord and Lady Kelvin and Professor G. F. Fitzgerald, the gentlemen being en route to Canada to participate in the meeting of the British Association for the Advancement of Science. Lord Kelvin is better known as William Thompson, an eminent electrician and scientist, who was knighted on the completion of the second Atlantic cable, and raised to the peerage in 1892. He was at one time president of the Royal Society. His scientific achievements are too well known to need recapitulation. Though now an aged man, Lord Kelvin is still vigorous both in mind and body. He is going to-day to Montreal, thence to Toronto, where the British Association meets. He said he would in all probability make a trip to Vancouver before he returned to England. HE IS HERE TO LEARN. I asked Lord Kelvin for his opinion about Nikola Tesla's new discovery of telegraphing without wires. "The accounts I have seen of it," said His Lordship, "have been so meagre that I cannot say anything about it. Whether it is possible or not I do not know.
