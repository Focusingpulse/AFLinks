GC2026-STP053 The role of the ring current in the substorm development from the point of view of Stokes' theorem Vladimir S. Semenov1 , Igor V. Kubyshkin1 , Marina V. Kubyshkina1 1 St Petersburg University v.s.semenov@spbu.ru Numerous satellite measurements have revealed that the primary process responsible for a magnetospheric substorm is magnetic reconnection in the magnetospheric tail. It has been shown that during the reconnection process, plasma flows accelerated to Alfvén speeds (BBFs — Bursty Bulk Flows) are formed; these flows transport reconnected magnetic flux, mass, and energy from the tail toward Earth. An important new element is that BBFs also carry a portion of the tail’s electric current and the corresponding electric field. Upon penetrating the inner magnetosphere, BBFs create plasma injection and a Substorm Current Wedge (SCW ) with a strong inductive electric field inside the region of emerging dipolarization. Using Stokes’ theorem, it can be shown that the experimental manifestation of a strong electric field in the loop region is the rapid motion (~1 km/s) of the polar arc of the auroral bulge. Protons and electrons crossing the SCW region due to gradient and centrifugal drift undergo betatron acceleration and gain energy. As a result, an enhanced ring current pulse forms in the loop region. This current, drifting westward with protons and eastward with electrons, transports the reconnected magnetic flux.
