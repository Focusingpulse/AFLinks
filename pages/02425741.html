Misaligned Circumstellar Disks and Orbital Motion of the Young Binary XZ Tau Takanori Ichikawa,1 Miyu Kido,1 Daisuke Takaishi,1 Yoshito Shimajiri,1, 2 Yusuke Tsukamoto,1 and Shigehisa Takakuwa1, 3 1 Department of Physics and Astronomy, Graduate School of Science and Engineering, Kagoshima University, 1-21-35 Korimoto, Kagoshima,Kagoshima 890-0065, Japan 2 National Astronomical Observatory of Japan, Osawa 2-21-1, Mitaka, Tokyo 181-8588, Japan 3 Academia Sinica Institute of Astronomy and Astrophysics, 11F of Astro-Math Bldg, 1, Sec. 4, Roosevelt Rd, Taipei 10617, Taiwan (Received; Revised June 23, 2021; Accepted) Submitted to ApJ ABSTRACT We report our analyses of the multi-epoch (2015–2017) ALMA archival data of the Class II binary system XZ Tau at Bands 3, 4 and 6. The millimeter dust continuum images show compact, unresolved (r . 15 au) circumstellar disks (CSDs) around the individual binary stars; XZ Tau A and B, with a projected separation of ∼ 39 au. The 12 CO (2-1) emission associated with those CSDs traces the Keplerian rotations, whose rotational axes are misaligned with each other (P.A. ∼ -5◦ for XZ Tau A and ∼ 130◦ for XZ Tau B). The similar systemic velocities of the two CSDs (VLSR ∼ 6.0 km s−1 ) suggest that the orbital plane of the binary stars is close to the plane of the sky. From the multi-epoch ALMA data, we have also identified the relative orbital motion of the binary. Along with the previous 00 +0. 002 00 ◦ NIR data, we found that the elliptical orbit (e = 0.742+0.025 , and ω = -54.2+2.0 −4.7 ) −0.034 , a = 0. 172−000 . 003 is preferable to the circular orbit. Our results suggest that the two CSDs and the orbital plane of the XZ Tau system are all misaligned with each other, and possible mechanisms to produce such a configuration are discussed.
