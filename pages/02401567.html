GC2026-PERM031 Synchronicity of geomagnetic field variations and global mantle plume activity: Implications for the nature of the Devonian and Vendian periods of GAD disturbance Dmitry V. Metelkin1,2 1 Novosibirsk State University, Russia 2 A.A. Trofimuk Institute of Petroleum Geology and Geophysics, Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences metelkindv@ipgg.sbras.ru Based on an analysis of proprietary paleomagnetic data for magmatic complexes of Siberia and the High Arctic potentially related with mantle plume activity, a relationship is inferred between mantle plume magmatism and changes in geomagnetic field characteristics, such as reversal frequency and its absolute value for the last 600 Myr. A repetition of typical minima and maxima is shown for every ~100 Myr. At the same time, in the general record of geological events it can be observed that plume activity periods are preceded by increasing reversal frequency accompanied by a decrease in geomagnetic field intensity. We propose a hypothesis explaining this effect through the mode of thermal convection in the outer core, where plumes are considered as regulators of the state of the hydromagnetic dynamo [1]. “Overheating” of the core increased the turbulence of convective currents, which therefore led to increasing numbers of reversals, during which the value of the main component of the geomagnetic field – the axial dipole – first fell to zero and subsequently returned to high values only after full reversal of the poles.
