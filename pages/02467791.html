FREE CHOICE IN ST.MAXIMUS THE CONFESSOR With a forward by His Grace the Right Reverend Kallistos (Ware) Bishop ofDiokleia St. Tikhon's Seminary Press South Canan, Pennsylvania 18459 1989 - -1 - Acknowledgments The idea and plan of this essay cannot correctly be said to be entirely my own. Indeed, it is the product of discussion with friends and professors going back nine years. The original seed of the essay was planted by two friends, the Rev. Bryan George Fulwider of the United Methodist Church, and John D. Harrell. I am indebted to them for pointing the way back to the christological controversies of the seventh century, for listening to my earliest formulations of some of the ideas presented in this essay, and for their criticisms and encouragement. I am also grateful to Dr. J. Steven O'Malley for his many insights which helped me to put flesh on an otherwise skeletal outline. The Very Rev. John Meyendorff, of St. Vladimir's Theological Seminary, during many private discussions, carefully and patiently elaborated the concept of the gnomic will in St. Maximus, and helped to correct many of my misconceptions. If there is any fault with the analysis of the gnomic will presented here, it is entirely my own. My deepest gratitude must be expressed to the Right Reverend Bishop Kallistos (Timothy Ware) of the University of Oxford for his inexhaustible patience in checking the translations, and for his unceasing encouragement and confidence in this project. To the typists, typographers, and proofreaders -Jane Drum, Gretta Illot, Alexander Slepukhof, and Saralyn Kincaid- who have labored so long over the difficult format and sometimes necessarily pedantic style, and who so quickly and accurately learned how to type Greek, goes my profound gratitude.
