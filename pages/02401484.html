GC2026-U004 The 25th solar activity cycle: Forecasts and actual implementation Andrei Tlatov1 1 Main (Pulkovo) Astronomical Observatory of the Russian Academy of Sciences Ponencia invitada tlatov@mail.ru Forecasting solar cycles years and even decades into the future is crucial for ensuring the stability of space infrastructure, the operation of communications and navigation systems, and for understanding the long-term dynamics of solar magnetism. Following the anomalously low minimum between cycles 24 and 25, the strength of the current cycle has become a subject of intense scientific debate. The NOAA/NASA/ISES forecast team's 2019 sunspot index forecasts for solar cycle 25 yielded a value of ~115. They used a combination of methods, including physical solar dynamo models based on poleward magnetic flux transport, machine learning, and statistical predictors such as polar magnetic field strength at cycle minimum. In October 2024, we reached the maximum of Cycle 25, which was at 160.8. In addition to classical solar dynamo models, a number of alternative approaches exist. These often yield more accurate results for anomalous cycles, as they consider the Sun not only as an isolated magnetic system but also as part of more complex physical interactions. This report presents an overview of solar activity forecasting methods and the overall picture of solar cycles, including "extended" solar activity cycles.
