Report: The Physical Theory of the Levitron Authors: H. Eckardt and C. Kellum Paper Title: "Simple Theory for the Levitron" Publication: Journal of Applied Physics, Volume 82, Issue 2 (1997) Key Concepts: Magnetic levitation: The suspension of an object using the repulsive force of magnets. Earnshaw's Theorem: States that stable levitation cannot be achieved with static magnetic fields alone. Gyroscopic stabilization: Uses the spinning of an object to provide stability, overcoming Earnshaw's Theorem. Dipole force model: A mathematical model describing the forces between a spinning magnetic top and a base magnet. Summary The Levitron is a popular toy where a magnetic top floats seemingly in defiance of gravity. Eckardt and Kellum's paper explains the physics behind this phenomenon, clarifying how the Levitron cleverly circumvents Earnshaw's Theorem. Their work provides a mathematical framework (the dipole force model) for analyzing the forces and stability of the Levitron.
