Title Re-Examination of Newton's First Law Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Mitch Emery Keywords Newton , Motion, Inertia, Gravitation, Equivalence Principle Published 2008 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 5 Number 1 No. of pages 2 Pages 46-47 Abstract Trouble with Newton's First Law was found in my original study, but the trouble is not easy to see. In fact, most readers fail to see it. Old ways of thinking stand in their way, and so the purpose of this note is to reexamine Newton's First Law, and to put its trouble in perspective with the state of modern physics. Overview Re-Examination of Newton's First Law is a two-page paper by Mitch Emery of Maumee, Ohio, presented at the 15th conference of the Natural Philosophy Alliance and published in the Proceedings of the NPA volume 5, number 1 (2008), pages 46–47. It is a companion to his " Electron Spin and the Emission of Photons ", and rests on the same foundation: a paper of his called "New Physics Based on Force-Free Circular Motion." Where that one applied the idea to the electron, this one applies it to mechanics and gravity. Emery opens with the state of fundamental physics, quoting Lee Smolin's The Trouble with Physics — "The one thing everyone who cares about fundamental physics seems to agree on is that new ideas are needed" — and NPA colleagues Francisco Müller and Peter Marquardt, the latter asking: "What are the iron principles that never have been found to fail and still leave room for novel discoveries?" His candidate is the first law. "Newton's First Law says that a body in motion tends to move in a straight line, and yet all things in the Universe move about in circular motion. Gravity is thought to be the cause, but the cause itself is a mystery. According to Newton, a body's tendency to move in a straight line is just an act of Nature. The principle has no real cause." He proposes inverting it: "Instead of straight-line motion, we can postulate circular motion to be the natural thing." Force
