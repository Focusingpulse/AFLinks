Title The Structure of Aether and a Structural Method for Calculating Phi and Planck's Length Read in full Link to paper Author(s) William R Hohenberger Keywords aether , Planck , length Published 2010 No. of pages 8 Abstract A mathematical octahedral model for the tetrahedral fine structure of aether can be developed, from which the mathematical constants Phi and Planck's Length can be calculated, and then reconciled with the Seven Twist-Loop Fractal model. Overview This paper builds a geometrical model of the aether out of interlocking rings and derives from it two numbers: the golden ratio φ, and a value for the Planck length. It is one of a series by the same author, and follows directly from an earlier paper on calculating the Planck length and the fine structure constant ; the octahedral model developed here is described as the reciprocal of the "seven twist-loop fractal" model of that earlier work, the two said to converge at the Planck length. The underlying picture is an "energy cell": four rings interlocked in a tetrahedral arrangement, which can function as a three-dimensional spring. Flattened, the cell is negatively charged; stretched, positively charged; at rest, neutral. Viewed along any of its three polar axes the cell presents the same figure, which the author models with an equilateral triangle inscribed in a single loop, dimensioned with the values 1, 2 and √3 — chosen, he says, because they are "whole prime number integers" rather than halves or multiples, and because "only these numbers will solve the equations correctly". The argument From rings to √3 The same √3 is then obtained a second way, by drawing space diagonals from corner to opposite corner within a cube, producing what the author calls a square-root-of-three pyramid built from four internal squares.
