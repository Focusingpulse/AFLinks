Overview Quantum Gravity is a six-page paper by Faheem Murtaza , presented at the 16th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference in May 2009. It proposes a law of quantum gravity, argues that gravitation is electromagnetic in origin, and derives Newton's law, a relativistic correction and a relation between gravity and time from it. Section A states the law as J·p·g = (dm)²√(c²/v² − 1), with J = h/c⁴, p and g the momentum and gravitational acceleration of a particle, and dm a mass differential satisfying dm·c² = h/dt. Murtaza then observes that the right-hand side can be written as (dq′/Y)², where Y is "a Lorentz-invariant factor with dimensions as coulomb/kg" and dq′ is an "induced charge (due to motion)". Integration gives the central relation: q′ = (3.87113×10 8 )·mvr/M with mvr the orbiting body's angular momentum, M the mass of the central body, and the constant carrying units of coulomb·second/m². Since angular momentum is conserved, he notes, so is the induced charge.
