09/05/13 - Energy-Capture Fuel Saver Research Project I would like to tell you about my invention, the High Velocity Low Pressure engine. It uses exhaust energy from an engine to add power to a crankshaft. It delivers smooth rotary power from a gas flow by diverting the flow to multiple pistons or bellows with a frictionless rotary valve. I've spent several years researching power plants and heat engines thinking there must be a more efficient type of heat engine. I learned that modern jet engines use the Brayton cycle, which was originally invented as a piston engine. Very long story short, I made a piston engine that is powered by a jet stream. It is similar to a power recovery turbine, but captures much more energy because it has a positive displacement. Now for this project-- I am asking for your help to fund an ambitious Project! I plan to build a HVLP engine and install it in the bed of a truck. Using the power of the exhaust, the engine will add torque to the driveshaft, increasing fuel efficiency. Estimates are that with moderate driving of 60 miles per hour, average fuel consumption will be cut in half! Here in this video is my latest test model HVLP engine hooked up to a 12 volt generator. This 750 watt (1 horsepower) leaf blower simulates an engines exhaust. Notice the easy start up! I used sheet metal and leather bellows for this model. For the project, I plan to use this air compressor for its pistons and crankshaft. Adding my patent pending frictionless rotary valve and a few other details will transform it into a HVLP engine. The purpose of this Project is to research and develop better ways of using the available energy we have, and to show the government, the oil companies, and the world- that even a few Kansas farmers can make a big difference! I have friends and business associates who are ready to start working with me on this historic project! They include engineers, electricians, machinists, mechanics, welders, and engine performance specialist
