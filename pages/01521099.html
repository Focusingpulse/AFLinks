Title Quantum Fluctuations and the Action of the Mind Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Jean E Burns Keywords Mind, Matter, Position, Momentum, Order, Uncertainty principle, Quantum fluctuations Published 2002 Journal Noetic Journal Volume 3 Number 4 No. of pages 6 Pages 312-317 Abstract It is shown that if mental influence can change a position or momentum coordinate within the limits of the uncertainty principle, such change, when magnified by a single interaction, is sufficient to order the direction of traveling molecules. Mental influence could initiate an action potential in the brain through this process by using the impact of ordered molecules to open the gates of sodium channels in neuronal membranes. It is shown that about 80 ordered molecules, traveling at thermal velocity in the intercellular medium in the brain, can break an ionic or covalent bond, and that the number needed to initiate an action potential is relatively small. If mental influence can act within the brain, it is reasonable to suppose it can act to some extent outside of it. If mental influence could not only order the direction of individual molecules, but coordinate this effect to produce a longitudinal pressure wave which is reasonably coherent across a macroscopic surface, only 10 4 molecules need be simultaneously affected to produce a detectible sound wave. Such an effect is not ordinarily observed, which suggests that if mental influence acts by ordering the direction of molecules, it acts at the level of individual molecules, but does not coordinate their motion. Overview Jean Burns, an independent physicist in San Leandro with refereed publications in Foundations of Physics and the Journal of Consciousness Studies , asks a precise question: if mental influence acts on matter, and if it is confined to the limits set by the uncertainty principle, how many molecules would it have to order to do anything?
