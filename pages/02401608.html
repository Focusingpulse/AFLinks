GC2026-PERM041 The first results of the separation of magnetite and pyrrhotite in banded iron formation of the Kostomuksha greenstone belt of the Karelian craton based on petromagnetic data Timur R. Zaripov1 , Natalia V. Lubnina1 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University zaripov.tim000zaripov@yandex.ru Magnetite and pyrrhotite are the main magnetization-bearing minerals in banded iron formation (BIF) of the Kostomuksha Greenstone Belt (KGB) of the Karelian craton. The separation of the contribution of these minerals to the total magnetization is key to the correct calculation of the extracted iron ore, excluding the magnetization introduced by pyrrhotite. This paper presents the first results of the separation of magnetite and pyrrhotite based on thermomagnetic analysis, magnetic cleaning, and measurement of magnetic susceptibility anisotropy (AMS). Thermomagnetic analysis was performed for all types of BIF found in KGB. In all the samples studied, magnetite and pyrrhotite are distinguished by their characteristic peaks and declines in magnetic susceptibility. So the Curie point (Tc) of magnetite is 575℃, Tc of monoclinic pyrrhotite is 325℃. Pyrite (Tc = 450℃) and hematite (Tc = 680℃) are also isolated in BIF-3, while only hematite is isolated in BIF-4. A large role in the magnetization is played by multi-domain magnetite grains, which are also isolated in all samples. During heating and cooling, phase transitions and transformations of some minerals into others are recorded, which is reflected in detail in our work.
