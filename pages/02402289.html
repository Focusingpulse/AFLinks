MolPhot2026-main045 Methods for Studying Solutions of Multicomponent Luminophores Sergey A. Tovstun1,2 1 Federal Research Center for Problems of Chemical Physics and Medical Chemistry RAS 2 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (State University) tovstun@icp.ac.ru When studying liquid solutions of luminophores, a typical situation is that they contain more than one luminescent substance, while their photophysical properties are unknown. In the case of molecular luminophores, this can occur due to the presence of impurities, conformers, protolytic forms, or aggregates. In the case of colloidal quantum dots, this is true in most cases due to polydispersity and an indefinite state of the surface. In such a scenario, it is usually impossible to reliably and directly measure the emission spectrum, excitation spectrum, and luminescence lifetime of the individual components. A general approach to solving such problems is the acquisition and analysis of multidimensional experimental data. A conventional luminescence spectrum or decay kinetics at a specific observation wavelength are considered one-dimensional data in this context. Examples of two-dimensional data in this context include the excitation-emission matrix (EEM) and the time-resolved emission spectrum (TRES). The excitation-emission matrix represents the luminescence intensity at a given wavelength as a function of the excitation and emission wavelengths.
