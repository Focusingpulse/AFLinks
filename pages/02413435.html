Sympathetic Vibratory Physics

· archived # 2,413,435
Sometimes in between 1011-1027, Ibn al-Haytham published his most famous book Kitab al-Manazir or the Book of Optics. This book drastically transformed the understanding of light and vision. Ibn al-Haytham explained that vision occurs when light reflects from an object and then passes to one's eyes. Ibn al-Haytham also studied what eyes are made of and named important parts of the eye. He also drew a sketch of the human optical system. Ibn al-Haytham is credited with explaining the nature of light and vision, through using a dark chamber he called “Al-Beit Al-Muzlim”, which has the Latin translation as the “camera obscura”; the device that forms the basis of modern photography/camera. Ibn al-Haytham studied how light moves and experimented using lenses and mirrors. He studied reflection and refraction concluding that light refracts when it moved through different materials. Ibn al-Haytham was a prolific author. He wrote more than 200 works on a wide range of subjects, of which at least 96 of his scientific works are known, and approximately 50 of them have survived to date. Nearly half of his surviving works are on mathematics, 23 of them are on astronomy, and 14 of them are on optics, with a few on other areas of science. In International Year of Light 2015, the 1000th anniversary of the publication of Kitab al-Manazir was celebrated. He had set new standards in experimental science back in the 11th century. For his remarkable contributions to the science of light and vision, Ibn al-Haytham has been described as the father of modern optics.
