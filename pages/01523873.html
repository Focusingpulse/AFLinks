Science Chat Saturday sponsored by the CNPS Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Dennis Allen , James Keene , Ian Cohen and Bill Lucas , intended as a mailbag session and diverted when Hu's screen sharing crashed his browser repeatedly. Keene opened by pointing to a new video on the inertial propulsion mechanism, correcting his presentation of the previous week, and credited Allen by name for his contribution to the argument. Lucas on the length of the year Lucas — audible only with difficulty throughout — described work on coral and limestone. Coral lays down annual rings and, within them, ripples marking the days, so the oldest coral records years of some 486 days against the present 365. He reads this as the earth having expanded, on his own account through a decay in gravity, which he says he can predict; and he identifies two intervals in the record where the rate of change was different, which he associates with events in the biblical narrative. He also reported that papers on the subject, some produced under NOAA contract, have been taken down from the internet, and that he has kept downloaded copies — drawing the parallel with what he sees in other fields the group works in. Asked what causes the decay, he gave his own mechanism: within an atom each proton vibrates with respect to an electron, giving dipole, quadrupole or higher oscillations, and the net force between two atoms is proportional to the number of multipoles and the amplitude — most terms cancelling under spherical symmetry, which is why gravity is so many orders weaker.
