Chubb Born ( 1953-01-30 ) January 30, 1953 Died March 25, 2011 (2011-03-25) (aged 58) Residence Burke, VA, United States Nationality USA Known for New Energy , Cold Fusion Scientific career Fields Research Scientist , Editor of Infinite Energy Scott R. Chubb (30 January 1953 – 25 March 2011) was an American theoretical and computational physicist at the Naval Research Laboratory, and for the last two decades of his life one of the principal theoreticians working on low energy nuclear reactions. Education and early career A.B. in physics — Princeton University, 1975 Ph.D. in physics — State University of New York at Stony Brook, 1982, with research carried out at Brookhaven National Laboratory After his doctorate Chubb received several offers and went first to Northwestern University as a postdoctoral researcher, then to the Naval Research Laboratory on a National Research Council postdoctoral appointment, joining Barry Klein's Condensed Matter Theory Branch. David J Nagel , then head of the Condensed Matter and Radiation Sciences division, later recalled that Chubb "stood out immediately after his arrival at the Lab". Career Chubb's NRL work covered an unusually wide range of scales. His early appointment was in solid-state physics, chiefly surface-science computation. He then moved to the Remote Sensing Division, where he worked on computational analysis of satellite data on ocean waves, including measurements of currents at the edge of the Gulf Stream in the western Atlantic. He also wrote on the relativistic corrections required by the Global Positioning System — where the gravitational and kinematic terms combine to about +38.6 microseconds per day, enough to accumulate some 11.6 km of ranging error daily if uncorrected — and obtained a patent on a device to correct for them.
