Dollard Born 1952 (age 73–74) Residence San Francisco, CA, United States Nationality USA Known for Replication of Nikola Tesla 's wireless transmission work; versor algebra; the analysis of electricity as dielectric and magnetic components Scientific career Fields Electrical engineering, high-frequency electricity, telluric transmission Eric P. Dollard (born 1952) is an American electrical engineer and researcher, and among the very few people credited with successfully reproducing Nikola Tesla 's experiments in wireless power transmission — including, on his supporters' account, the only working Magnifying Transmitter built since Tesla's death. His central contention is that conventional electrical theory contains only half the story, because it treats electricity as a single quantity rather than as two incommensurate components. His theoretical work links Tesla with Steinmetz, and his broader corpus connects the thought of Tesla, Viktor Schauberger and Wilhelm Reich . Borderland Sciences, which published most of his papers, describes that corpus as among the most important material it has issued. Education and early career Dollard's training was practical rather than academic, and unusually early. As a teenager he was given access to RCA's Bolinas radio facility on the northern California coast — then one of the largest radio stations in the United States — for his own investigations into high-frequency alternating current. He worked there from about the age of seventeen, and left high school holding engineering certifications. Bolinas is the thread running through his work. The Marconi–RCA station there, established around 1913, is the subject of his paper Theory of Wireless Power , which diagrams the installation as it stood about 1919, and of his later book Wireless Giant of the Pacific .
