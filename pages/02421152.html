Nichelson Born 1944 (age 81–82) Residence American Fork, Utah, United States Nationality American Alma mater Kalamazoo College; Harvard University Known for The argument that Nikola Tesla designed a self-sustaining electrical generator; measurements on Tesla's bifilar coil Scientific career Fields History of science; Tesla research; antenna engineering Oliver D. Nichelson (born 1944) is an American historian of science and independent researcher, and one of the most cited authors on the claim that Nikola Tesla designed a machine capable of producing electricity without fuel . He is not primarily a theorist: his method is documentary, working from Tesla's patents, articles and lecture notes to argue that a particular set of designs was intended for a purpose Tesla never stated openly. He is unusual among the authors collected here in having taken his case to mainstream engineering meetings — he presented at the Intersociety Energy Conversion Engineering Conference in 1991 and again in 1993 — as well as to the Tesla Centennial Symposium of 1984 and the IEEE Tesla symposium held at Columbia University in 1976. Education Kalamazoo College — history of science Harvard University — graduate study in the history of science He subsequently worked as a consultant to an antenna research and development company, which is the background to his experimental work on coil geometry. The fuelless generator argument Nichelson's central paper appeared in 1984 as "Tesla's Self-Sustaining Electrical Generator and the Ether" and was developed for the 1991 conference paper "Nikola Tesla's Later Energy Generation Designs". The argument proceeds by identification: Tesla spoke repeatedly of a machine that would draw energy from the environment, and Nichelson asks which of the surviving designs fits that description.
