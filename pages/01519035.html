4th Phase of Water - Dr. Gerald Pollack Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Date 20 March 2021 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording 4th Phase of Water - Dr. Gerald Pollack was a live session on 20 March 2021 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Water is known to have three phases, solid, liquid, and vapor. However, Pollack discovered a fourth phase, lying in between solid and liquid. While the evidence for this phase is relatively new, the concept of a fourth, ordered, phase of water dates back at least a century. This recently identified phase carries with it considerable explanatory power for diverse disciplines, a sampling of which is offered here. Summary Gerald Pollack of the University of Washington on the fourth phase of water. David de Hilster hosted. The two spent something over ten minutes failing to get Pollack's slides onto the stream — the browser refusing screen capture, the file too large to email, Dropbox not working at either end — and settled on playing a recorded lecture Pollack had given to a conference on the physics, chemistry and biology of water instead, with discussion afterwards. Ling Pollack's account of where the work came from begins with Gilbert Ling, one of three scholars — a biologist, a physicist and a chemist — selected in the first cohort sent from China to the United States, and by common assent the cleverest of them, though the physicist went on to a Nobel Prize. Ling thought water important, and concluded from his experiments that the water inside a cell is structured: there are many surfaces within a cell, the water next to each takes on an order, and since essentially all of the cell's water is interfacial, essentially all of it is structured. Pollack was candid that Ling's writing is hard to digest and that the two were never really friends.
