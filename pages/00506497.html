On the Diophantine Equation x 2 + k = 2n : Prime Constraints and Computational Evidence Durga Shankar Akodia∗ November 29, 2025 Abstract We study integer solutions to the exponential Diophantine equation x 2 +k = 2n where k and n are primes. We prove that for any solution with n ≥ 2, x must be odd, and k must be an odd prime. Furthermore, we establish the strict congruence k ≡ 7 (mod 8) for all solutions with n ≥ 3. We identify a trivial family of solutions corresponding to Mersenne primes (x = 1) and demonstrate the existence of non￾trivial solutions for x > 1. Computational evidence is presented for 9 out of 11 prime values of n ≤ 31, revealing 16 distinct non-trivial solutions. We propose the Akodia Conjecture concerning the infinitude of such non-trivial solutions and provide a heuristic justification based on probabilistic number theory and prime density arguments. 1 Introduction We investigate the exponential Diophantine equation: x 2 + k = 2n , (1) where x ∈ Z, and k, n are primes (∈ P). This equation generalizes the celebrated Ramanujan–Nagell equation (x 2 + 7 = 2n ), which has finitely many known solutions [3], by allowing the additive prime k to vary. Our focus is on deriving necessary conditions and providing computational evidence for solutions. The structure of this paper is as follows: In Section 2, we establish fundamental parity and modular constraints. Section 3 identifies the Mersenne prime family of solutions.
