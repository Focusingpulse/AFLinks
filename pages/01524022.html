The Atom Book Author Albert Cushing Crehore Language English Subject Atomic structure; spectra; gravitation; dimensional analysis Published 1920 Publisher D. Van Nostrand Company Pages 161 The Atom is a 1920 book by Albert Cushing Crehore setting out an alternative to the Bohr model of the atom. It is not an exposition of accepted atomic physics but an argument against the theory that had just become standard, together with a rival theory of Crehore's own — one in which the hydrogen atom has two electrons rather than one, a whole series of spectral lines is emitted in a single operation rather than one line per jump, and gravitation is derived from the motion of the electrons inside atoms . The book is the mathematical statement of a programme Crehore had described without equations three years earlier in The Mystery of Matter and Energy: Recent Progress as to the Structure of Matter (1917), and it was followed the same year by The New Physics . Why the book was written Crehore explains in the first pages that he would ordinarily have published this work as a series of papers in the technical journals, as he had done for many years, but that the argument needed more space than a journal would give and that condensing it would weaken rather than strengthen it. He is explicit about what he is doing: It would be false modesty to try to disguise the fact that the subject of atomic theory is treated in a new form from the beginning to the end of the volume… This has made it necessary to emphasize the points where the current theory of the atom, that due to Dr. N. Bohr, is deficient. He adds that Bohr's work is "most suggestive, and as marking an epoch in the progress of atomic theory." The dispute is with the theory, not with its author. The case against the Bohr atom Crehore's central objection is one that several physicists of a conservative cast made at the time, and he states it plainly.
