Hendershot Born ( 1898-06-03 ) June 3, 1898 West Elizabeth, Pennsylvania, United States Died April 19, 1961 (1961-04-19) (aged 62) Residence Pennsylvania, United States Nationality American Known for The Hendershot "fuelless" generator Scientific career Fields Inventor Lester Jennings Hendershot (3 June 1898 – 19 April 1961) was an American inventor , best known for the so-called Hendershot fuelless generator (or "Fuelless Motor"), a device he unveiled in 1928 that he claimed produced electrical power without any fuel. The invention drew national press attention and the interest of prominent backers before commercial and political difficulties led Hendershot to abandon it. Its operation was never explained or independently verified, and its claimed fuelless output is not accepted by mainstream science. Education Attended Cornell University briefly, taking a few courses; he earned no degree, and his formal schooling was limited. Biography Lester Hendershot was born on 3 June 1898 in West Elizabeth, Pennsylvania. He had little formal education and was largely self-taught as an inventor, achieving modest success with electronic toys and selling some of his ideas to small manufacturers. In the 1920s, while working on a new type of fuelless aviation compass, Hendershot reported that he had stumbled upon a method of generating electrical energy. He developed this into what he called a "magnetic induction" motor, first announced in March 1928. The device — quickly dubbed a "fuelless motor" by the press — attracted intense publicity and the attention of well-known figures, including Major Thomas Lanphier, commandant at Selfridge Field, and Henry Breckinridge, the attorney for Colonel Charles Lindbergh, who was among those said to have taken an interest in the invention.
