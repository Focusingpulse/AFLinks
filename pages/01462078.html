Small Community Prosperity

· archived # 1,462,078
Why Rural Towns Are Failing — and the Simple Way to Fix It The real problem isn’t bad politics or lack of jobs. The cause is simple: More money leaves the community than comes in. Most people buy everything from outside — food, clothes, tools, vehicles, insurance, taxes, services — but produce nothing that brings money into the town. Our ancestors made things: food, lumber, bricks, clothing, furniture, and tools. That kept wealth circulating locally. When money changes hands many times inside a community, everyone prospers. When it leaves after one or two transactions, the town slowly dies. The Solution Start producing again — locally. Trade with each other. Buy from your neighbors. Sell to your neighbors. Sell what you make to nearby towns or online. Each trade adds a little “profit” that multiplies as it moves through the community. Look at Ithaca, New York’s HOURS system — people bought and sold with one another using local credits. The same money changed hands dozens of times, and the town grew wealthy while others struggled. The Core Principle Circulating money creates prosperity. Exporting money creates poverty. Until we fix that cause, no amount of outside aid, programs, or politics will heal rural America. [Dale Pond, October 30, 2025]
