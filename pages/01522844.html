Einstein's Relativity Mistaught Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Roger J Anderton Date 14 June 2025 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Einstein's Relativity Mistaught was a live session presented by Roger J Anderton on 14 June 2025 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. There are many different points-of-view that can be taken of Relativity; but physics students are not told about that and as consequence Relativity is being mistaught to them. Hosted by Roger Anderton. Summary A sequel to Roger J Anderton 's session two weeks earlier on the omission of metaphysics from physics teaching, narrowing the charge to a specific one: that students are taught a single interpretation of special relativity as though it were the only one, and are never told the others exist. Five points of view He put the question to ChatGPT and cross-checked the answer against Gemini, which broadly agreed. The five it returned: Einstein's 1905 formulation. No preferred frame, invariant light speed, relative simultaneity, operational definitions in terms of what clocks and rods measure. The textbook version. The Lorentzian interpretation. A preferred frame exists but is undetectable; contraction and clock retardation are real physical effects of motion through it; the one-way speed of light is constant only in the ether frame, and appears constant elsewhere because of those effects and the synchronisation convention. Mathematically identical to Einstein's, conceptually very different. The Minkowski view. Space and time merged into a four-dimensional geometry, with emphasis on symmetry and invariance — the groundwork for general relativity, though absent from the 1905 paper. The operational or instrumentalist view. Avoids metaphysical claims entirely and treats simultaneity and dilation as measurement conventions. Used in metrology and engineering, GPS included.
