Reginald Irvan Gray Born ( 1917-03-18 ) March 18, 1917 Died November 28, 1996 (1996-11-28) (aged 79) Residence Myrtle Beach, SC, United States Nationality English / USA Known for Electrodynamics , Experiments Reginald Irvan Gray , O.B.E. (18 March 1917 – 28 November 1996), was an English-born research physicist and former Royal Air Force officer who, after emigrating to the United States, worked as an experimental and theoretical physicist for the United States Navy . He is best remembered among independent-science circles for his lifelong study of classical electromagnetism in the tradition of James Clerk Maxwell , for a series of experiments probing fundamental questions such as the finite mass of the photon and the possible presence of neutrinos in the vacuum, and for his book Unified Physics (1988). Biography Gray served during the Second World War as a Group Captain in the Royal Air Force and was appointed an Officer of the Order of the British Empire ( O.B.E. ). He was counted among "the few," the RAF airmen of the wartime generation, and by contemporaries was described as an exceptionally modest man who seldom spoke of his wartime service. After the war Gray emigrated to the United States, where he was employed as a physicist at a U.S. Navy laboratory. He became known within the community of independent physicists from the late 1960s onward, carrying out carefully crafted experiments—initially on his own initiative and later with some support from his laboratory. He settled in Myrtle Beach, South Carolina, and continued both experimental and theoretical work through his years of retirement until his death in 1996. Scientific contributions Gray's work centred on classical electromagnetic theory and its experimental application. He was noted for a detailed command of nineteenth-century physics, including Maxwell 's Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism and the work of J. J. Thomson , which he combined with an interest in contemporary developments in physics. Hi
