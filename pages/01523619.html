Paramahamsa Tewari Born ( 1937-01-06 ) January 6, 1937 Died 27 November 2017 Residence Karwar, India Nationality Indian Known for Space Vortex Theory; Space Power Generator; new energy Scientific career Fields Electrical Engineer ; Chief Project Engineer, Kaiga Atomic Power Project Institutions Nuclear Power Corporation of India, Department of Atomic Energy (India) Paramahamsa Tewari (6 January 1937 – 27 November 2017) was an Indian electrical engineer, nuclear-project executive and independent theorist who developed the Space Vortex Theory (SVT), in which matter, mass, electric charge and the fields of physics are derived from vortex motion in a fluid, non-material space. Professionally he rose to Executive Director (Nuclear Projects) of the Nuclear Power Corporation of India and was Project Director of the Kaiga Atomic Power Project; privately he spent four decades building the theoretical and experimental case that space itself is the primary reality and that energy can be drawn from it. He also designed and tested a "Space Power Generator" (SPG), later developed as a reactionless AC synchronous generator (RLG), which he and several engineers who measured it reported as operating above unity efficiency — a claim that places his work squarely outside mainstream physics. Biography Paramahamsa Tewari was born on January 6, 1937, and graduated in Electrical Engineering in 1958 from Banaras Engineering College, India, and held responsible positions in large engineering construction organizations, mostly in Nuclear Projects of the Department of Atomic Energy , India. He was also deputed abroad for a year at Douglas Point Nuclear Project, Canada. He retired in 1997 from his position as Executive Nuclear Director, Nuclear Power Corporation, Department of Atomic Energy, India, and is the former Project Director of the Kaiga Atomic Power Project .
