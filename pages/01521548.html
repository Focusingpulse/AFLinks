The Death of Modern Aether? - With David de Hilster Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Date 16 April 2022 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording The Death of Modern Aether? - With David de Hilster was a live session on 16 April 2022 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Has David de Hilster discovered a hidden assumption in modern aether models that could finally lead to aether’s demise? Summary David de Hilster put an objection to the luminiferous ether that he had arrived at a fortnight earlier and had not seen made before, and spent as much of the session on how such objections are received as on the objection itself. He was careful and repetitive about the framing: the ether could be right, he could be wrong, and he was not there to tell etherists they are stupid. Preliminaries He gave his own background — computer scientist, artist, filmmaker, a bachelor's in mathematics and a master's in linguistics, work in natural language processing at LexisNexis, brought into the subject by Carezani in the early 1990s and into this organisation by John Chappell in 1996 — and a statement of method: he gives himself a hundred per cent right to be wrong, attacks his own model as well as everyone else's, and does not defend scientific beliefs. His illustration was from M*A*S*H : "you can trust me" — "I know, sir" — and the man goes on counting the money. He listed the moments that had convinced him of things: Neal Adams 's expanding earth videos; Ionel Dinu 's underwater experiments producing what look like magnetic fields; the infinite levels in Puetz and Borchardt 's Universal Cycle Theory , after which he telephoned Borchardt and talked for an hour and a half; his father's model of light, which he understood only when Bob put his hands up and mimed it; and Jeff Yee 's account of why electrons sit in the s, p, d and f orbitals.
