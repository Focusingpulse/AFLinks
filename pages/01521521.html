Title The Cause of Gravity: A Concept Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Duncan W Shaw Keywords gravity, Theory of Relativity , aether Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 9 Pages 541-549 Abstract Einstein's General Theory of Relativity sets out a theory of gravity which does not postulate a physical force as the cause of gravity; rather, it provides a geometric explanation for gravity. The present paper makes a proposal to fill this gap with a concept of gravity that is strictly physical. The proposal is premised upon the propositions that a sub-atomic substance ("aether") permeates space and that stars, planets and other cosmic bodies absorb aether. The absorption of aether is seen as part of the process of the operation of cosmic bodies. The mechanism which this paper offers as the cause of gravity is that the absorption of aether by a cosmic body causes a flow of aether towards the cosmic body, and the inflow of aether exerts ram pressure upon any matter in its path. The result is gravity. This paper also considers several phenomena which may be explained by the proposed aether and gravity concepts. Overview The Cause of Gravity: A Concept is a nine-page paper by Duncan W Shaw of Vancouver, presented at the 17th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in Long Beach in June 2010 and printed in volume 7 of the Proceedings of the NPA at pages 541–549. Its starting point is the complaint that general relativity supplies the geometry of gravitation but not its machinery. Shaw quotes Newton's letter to Bentley — that gravity innate and inherent in matter, acting at a distance through a vacuum, "is to me so great an absurdity that I believe no Man who has in philosophical matters a competent faculty of thinking can ever fall into it" — and Feynman's remark that Newton "made no hypotheses about this" and that "no one has since given any machinery."
