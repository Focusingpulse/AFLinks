Title Gravitational Energy and the Flatness Problem Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Ronald R Hatch Keywords General Relativity Published 1999 No. of pages 5 Abstract The frequency of an atomic clock is driven by the energy difference between excited states of an atom. Since the frequency of an atomic clock is a function of the gravitational potential, the energy difference must likewise be a function of the gravitational potential. Thus, the Pound-Rebka experiment rather than showing that a falling photon picked up energy, simply showed a higher frequency by comparison to a lower reference frequency. The frequency (energy) of a falling photon is unchanged. This shows that the General Theory of Relativity is wrong?gravity does not act on all forms of energy. This revision of gravitational effects is explored. Significant implications arise and potential explanations for significant ongoing problems in cosmology are developed. Overview Gravitational Energy and the Flatness Problem is a five-page paper by Ronald R Hatch , written in 1999 while he was at NavCom Technology in Torrance, California. Its argument begins from a genuine subtlety about what the Pound–Rebka experiment measured. An atomic clock's frequency is set by the energy difference between atomic states, and that frequency depends on gravitational potential. Hatch reasons that the energy levels themselves must therefore depend on potential, so a photon falling to a lower potential is not gaining energy — it is being compared against a reference that has been lowered. "The frequency (energy) of a falling photon is unchanged." From that he draws a general conclusion: "gravity does not act on all forms of energy", contrary to general relativity. He then develops the consequences.
