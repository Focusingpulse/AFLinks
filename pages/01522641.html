Electromagnetic fields, electric charges, and light possess momentum and energy that we can experience with our sense organs. Therefore, these are real physical entities (objects). All physical objects are subject to the action of gravity, and so are those entities, too. Implications for Special Relativity Theory. Hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary The first of two talks by Sankar Hajra of Kolkata — this one on special relativity, its sequel a fortnight later on general relativity. He set out his method in advance, in a letter Ian J Cowan read at the start: he proposes no theory of his own, will use only Newton and Maxwell, and will say nothing about anyone else's alternative. The session was hampered throughout by the speaker's partial hearing loss; he asked for questions in writing, and much of the discussion had to be conducted through the chat. The premise The argument rests on one claim stated at the outset. Electromagnetic fields, electric charges and light possess momentum and energy, and are therefore real physical entities. All physical objects are subject to gravity. Therefore so are these. Hajra's contention is that this single consideration collapses both relativities. Its immediate consequence is that the Earth carries the electromagnetic fields in its vicinity along with it — they translate, rotate and spin with the planet as any other physical object does, and experience the Coriolis force when in motion relative to its surface. The Earth's surface is then exactly equivalent to free space for electromagnetic purposes, which is why every optical and electromagnetic phenomenon observed on it is independent of the planet's motion.
