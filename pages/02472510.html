The people still continue to throng to the White City, and from 100,000 to 200,000 sight seers daily wander among the many marvelous objects, and yet there is no crowd. The immense grounds and buildings absorb all and there is still room for as many more. There are ways and there are ways to see the fair. The veteran comes to the city with a well laid out plan for his week’s or two weeks’ campaign. He has read of what there is to be seen, and knowing that there is chaff among the wheat, he has selected a few things that he wants to see and study, and he goes about it in a business-like way. His country cousin, with visions of the county fair hovering in the realms of memory, has read in a careless manner of the World’s Fair, and comes to do or die, and he generally dies, or wishes he could, before he is half through. To begin with, he fails entirely to realize the magnitude of the thing. He comes expecting to see all in three or four days, and starting in at the first gate he finds, begins to go through it like a whirlwind. He spends half his time looking at thousands of things he cares little about and the other half in gazing at a few things he finds that do interest him.
