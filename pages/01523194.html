Jack Scholze Born ( 1958-03-06 ) March 6, 1958 (age 68) Residence Honolulu, HI, United States Nationality USA Known for Experiments with exotic inductor geometries and pulse motors Scientific career Fields Electromagnetism , experimental energy devices Jack Scholze (born March 6, 1958) is an American independent experimenter based in Honolulu, Hawaii, who builds and tests electromagnetic devices in the amateur "free energy" tradition. He documents his work on a personal YouTube channel, where he presents pulse motors, motor-generators, battery chargers, and energy-capture apparatus wound with unconventional coil geometries. Biography Scholze lives in Honolulu, Hawaii, and pursues his experimental work outside of the mainstream academic and industrial physics community. He shares his experiments publicly through online video, positioning himself within a wider community of hobbyists and dissident researchers who investigate claims of over-unity and vortex-based energy. Work Scholze's experiments center on direct-current and alternating-current pulse motors, motor-generators, battery chargers, and other energy-capture devices. Much of his work explores exotic inductors and inductor winding geometries, including Rodin coils, Starship coils, conical coils, and designs he associates with names such as Powell Abha, Matthews, and Hendershot coils. Many of these coils are toroidal or donut-shaped and carry several separate windings. His interest in the geometry of inductors draws on "vortex math" ideas popularized by Marko Rodin, in which winding patterns are arranged according to numerical sequences said to concentrate or channel electromagnetic effects. Scholze presents his devices as investigations into whether such geometries can capture or amplify energy, a premise that lies outside established electromagnetic theory.
