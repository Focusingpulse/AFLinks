It consists of a thin polished metal tube acting as reflector and a base equipped with switch and connecting terminals and carrying spaced resistor wires concentric with the tube and at a certain distance from the inner surface of the same. In this arrangement the diffuse radiation is virtually eliminated, and the heater operates as if the resistor were not present, the rays being projected from the reflector radically to the central or focal region occupied by the boiling pot. The principal advantages thus secured are the following: A very high efficiency, as much as 96% being attainable. The efficiency is practically the same whether the pot is large or small since the density of the rays is inversely as the diameter of the vessel. Due to these features the current consumption is hardly more than half of that in the best heaters of the type referred to. The resistor has a relatively much longer life and can be made to last almost indefinitely in some cases. Also less wire can be used if desired. The heat being largely confined to the range, the kitchen remains comparatively cool. Another practical advantage is greater safety from a variety of accidents frequently occurring with ordinary ranges.
