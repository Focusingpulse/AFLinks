J. Lynde Gives a Practical Demonstration of It to Students. Yesterday and to-day Prof. C. J. Lynde has been giving his classes a demonstration of the methods used in wireless telegraphy. Prof. Lynde constructed the apparatus during vacation and it consists of a complete transmitter and receiver. The demonstrations before the classes consisted of sending messages through space, through obstructions such as brick walls, of starting motors, lighting electric lights, and the exploding of gun powder. Prof. Lynde gave a history of the steps leading up to the discovery of the wireless telegraphy. In 1861 Faraday propounded the theory that heat, light and electricity are related and are all due to disturbances in the ether. Maxwell developed this theory mathematically. These electrical waves are generated wherever an electrical spark occurs. They travel in all directions unless they strike some metallic substance which will act as a reflector. In 1889 and following years Prof. Lodge, of Liverpool, perfected an apparatus for receiving these rays and using them to reproduce signals. Lodge can therefore rightly be called the father of wireless telegraphy. He, however, put the limit of the distance through which a message could be sent at half a mile.
