By 1944 he had significantly improved his invention and applied for a patent in 1944. The feds issued him patent No. 2377835A in June 1945. Alexander Weygers sent info about Discopter to the military, aircraft manufacturers, helicopters and even automakers to gauge interest. In response he received some encouraging notes in which engineers said the discopter seemed reliable but too advanced for the time. (This will required lighter materials and more efficient power equipment.) Most of the responses were disappointing. "Our technicians studied the structure and stated that they were not interested," one of the US Air Force colonel wrote. So the Weygers discopter, though it caused a rush in the press, it remained only an unfulfilled prototype. And in 1947, a swarm of stories appeared in the popular press discussing UFO sightings and the popularization of flying saucers.
