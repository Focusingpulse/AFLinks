International Journal of Hydrogen Energy 31 (2006) 77 – 92 www.elsevier.com/locate/ijhydene Efﬁciency of hydrogen production systems using alternative nuclear energy technologies Bilge Yildiz∗ , Mujid S. Kazimi Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Nuclear Engineering Department, Center for Advanced Nuclear Energy Systems, 77 Massachusetts Avenue, Cambridge, MA 02139, USA Available online 10 May 2005 Abstract Nuclear energy can be used as the primary energy source in centralized hydrogen production through high-temperature thermochemical processes, water electrolysis, or high-temperature steam electrolysis. Energy efﬁciency is important in providing hydrogen economically and in a climate friendly manner. High operating temperatures are needed for more efﬁcient thermochemical and electrochemical hydrogen production using nuclear energy. Therefore, high-temperature reactors, such as the gas-cooled, molten-salt-cooled and liquid-metal-cooled reactor technologies, are the candidates for use in hydrogen production. Several candidate technologies that span the range from well developed to conceptual are compared in our analysis. Among these alternatives, high-temperature steam electrolysis (HTSE) coupled to an advanced gas reactor cooled by supercritical CO2 (S-CO2 ) and equipped with a supercritical CO2 power conversion cycle has the potential to provide higher energy efﬁciency at a lower temperature range than the other alternatives. 䉷 2005 International Association for Hydrogen Energy.
