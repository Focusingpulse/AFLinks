Underground Moon living

· archived # 2,446,543
09/24/14 - Underground Moon living Today, over 200 holes, pits and a lunar land bridge may potentially lead Global Space Organization crews into massive underground caverns, or lunar lava tubes. These holes have been located and photographed by NASA�s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) spacecraft and are found in many prime locations for potential lunar settlement. Naturally, many do not see living underground as very exciting. With all of the natural beauty of the solar system and space on the surface, why would anyone choose to live underground? Despite the grand views and vistas of the lunar surface, its also very dangerous and offers many challenges to those wanting to live on its surface. The chief problem among many is the constant solar and cosmic radiation bombardment of its surface. To live on the surface of the Moon would require a shelter buried under several meters of lunar regolith to reduce radiation exposure. In a way, this is the same as living underground, only with lunar lava tubes, nature did the work for us. Lunar lava tubes also provide stable temperatures and may even contain water ice similar to the ice found in the deep craters that never see the suns light. Living on the surface or underground without ready access to open space and outdoors can present other problems. Living in enclosed spaces for long periods of time can effect even the hardiest of explorers with cabin fever. The Global Space Organization is examining many ways to increase the quality of life in space for our future crews. When the time comes to begin engineering our GSO Lunar Station One, design implementations will include more open spaces, sounds, smells, structural modifications for realistic but artificial windows that will provide surface views controlled by occupants so that they feel they are closer to the surface.
