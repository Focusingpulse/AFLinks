Resonant Cavity Magnetron

· archived # 1,461,756
Hardware Update: The copper block that won the war. In 1940, radar was a massive technology. Literally. To spot incoming planes, the British had to build 350-foot-tall steel towers along the coast. They desperately needed to shrink radar so it could fit inside the nose of an airplane to hunt U-boats at night. The Engineering Miracle: The Resonant Cavity Magnetron . Invented by British physicists, it was incredibly small (about the size of a hockey puck) but possessed world-altering physics. The Math: Inside a solid block of copper, engineers machined a central hole surrounded by smaller circular cavities. A magnetic field was applied, and a cathode shot electrons into the center. The magnetic field forced the electrons to spin rapidly in a circle, sweeping past the openings of the cavities. Just like blowing air over the top of a glass bottle to make a whistle, the electrons "blew" over the cavities, generating a massive, highly powerful, ultra-high-frequency radio wave (microwaves). It shrank radar from the size of a skyscraper into a box small enough to fit in a night-fighter.
