Introduction and Foundational Constraints The architecture of discrete mechanics can be rigorously derived from an unusually austere foundation: a single operational mandate governing execution, formalized as the requirement that every executable state must possess a distinct legal successor. This principle, recognized as the continuability constraint (𝐶ଵ), asserts that stasis is not merely unlikely, but fundamentally excluded from the dynamical domain. A state that maps to itself possesses no continuation, generates no difference, and accrues no history. Therefore, it is forbidden. Before continuability can be enforced, the foundational gap across which change is measured must be established. This is defined structurally as the 𝐶଴ constraint, or the "comma" identity. The difference between a state that cannot change and one that can is the interstice that serializes a superposition into a readable distinction. A boundary walk on a cylinder yields no distinction and generates no time.
