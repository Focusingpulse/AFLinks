Hannah Herbst

· archived # 1,459,739
At just fourteen years old, most teenagers are thinking about school and hobbies, but Hannah Herbst was thinking about how to change the world. In 2015 the Florida student captured national attention after inventing a device called the Ocean Energy Probe, a small but powerful machine designed to generate electricity from the natural movement of ocean currents. Her idea was simple and brilliant: instead of relying on expensive fuel or large power plants, energy could be created using the constant motion of the sea. What made her project even more impressive was that it was built from inexpensive, everyday materials such as PVC pipe, a small generator, and a 3D-printed propeller. With this design, Hannah proved that clean and renewable power could be produced in an affordable and practical way. The inspiration behind her invention came from a very personal place. Hannah had a pen pal in Ethiopia who often wrote about living without reliable electricity. Hearing how difficult daily life was without power motivated her to search for a solution that could help people in similar situations around the world. She wanted to create something that could work in developing nations, remote villages, or disaster areas where traditional electricity systems were unavailable. When tested, her Ocean Energy Probe was able to produce enough power to light LED bulbs and operate small devices, showing real potential for uses such as medical equipment, water purification, and basic lighting.
