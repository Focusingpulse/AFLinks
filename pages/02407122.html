Printed in the United States of America PREFACE The material presented in this manual is meant to serve as a guide to students in gaining a general knowledge of the biology of some parasitic animals. In the selection of study types, considerable attention was given to the avail¬ ability of specimens. Almost all the genera and species chosen for study can either be collected by students and teacher or be purchased from biological supply houses. I wish to emphasize the importance and desirability of study¬ ing living parasites. Seeing a live tapeworm, trypanosome, or an amoeba in its natural environment, in natural color, and undergoing movement is far more vivid and impressive to the student than the restricted study of stained mounts. The demonstration of life cycles and larval stages in the laboratory is also a valuable part of any parasitology course. Although some emphasis is given to the parasites of human beings, certain parasites from other vertebrates are also included, especially if they demonstrate more clearly some important biological features. An example of this is the use of acanthocephala of birds and fish in preference to those from mammals because they show internal struc¬ tures more clearly.
