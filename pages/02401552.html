GC2026-SGG039 Geochemical contamination and induced seismicity in shale hydrocarbon development Mikhail V. Rodkin1,2 , Svetlana A. Punanova1 1 Institute of Oil and Gas Problems of the Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Institute of Earthquake Prediction Theory and Mathematical Geophysics, Russian Academy of Sciences rodkin@mitp.ru The paper examines the environmental risks associated with the development of unconventional mega-reservoir hydrocarbon (HC) accumulations – shale formations. The risk of development of giant shale formations includes the danger of both geochemical contamination and the danger of induced seismicity. The shale formations are complex source both of HC and industrially significant, valuable, but also potentially toxic chemical elements such as Co, As, Zn, Cs, Ag, Ni, Au, V, Se, Re, U, Pt, Mo, Hg, Cu, and others [1,2]. Examples of industrial-scale extraction of U, Ge, V, Mo, and other metals from oil are known from international practice (USA, Canada, China). Hydrocarbon production in shale formations involves horizontal drilling with hydraulic fracturing, proppant injection, and thermal stimulation. When extracting shale hydrocarbons using such methods, strong induced seismicity often occurs [3-5]. For example, the seismicity in Oklahoma, a region of intensive shale oil production, was evaluated to be systematically higher than in California, which is known for its strong earthquakes [3].
