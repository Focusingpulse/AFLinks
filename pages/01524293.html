Thomas Edison Born ( 1847-02-11 ) February 11, 1847 Died October 18, 1931 (1931-10-18) (aged 84) Residence West Orange, NJ, United States Nationality American Known for The phonograph, the first commercially practical incandescent lamp, direct-current central-station supply, the Edison effect , the war of the currents against Nikola Tesla and Westinghouse Scientific career Fields Electrical engineering, invention, industrial research Thomas Alva Edison (11 February 1847 – 18 October 1931) was an American inventor and industrialist who developed the phonograph, the first commercially practical incandescent lamp, and the first central-station electricity supply systems. He was not a dissident scientist and made no contribution to natural philosophy in the sense documented on this wiki; he appears here for three specific reasons. The first is Nikola Tesla . Edison employed Tesla in 1884, lost him within a year, and then spent four years fighting the alternating-current system Tesla had designed – and losing. Almost every Tesla source collected on this wiki passes through that episode, and it cannot be read without knowing what Edison actually did and did not do. The second is the Edison effect : in the course of the lamp work Edison observed a current crossing a vacuum, patented it, and set it aside without explanation, leaving the discovery to become the vacuum tube in other hands. The third is his method. Edison worked by exhaustive experiment and was openly contemptuous of mathematical theory – a working style that recurs throughout the independent-research tradition documented here, and one reason his name is invoked so often in it. Biography Edison was born in Milan, Ohio, in 1847 and raised in Port Huron, Michigan, with almost no formal schooling; he was taught at home by his mother and educated himself by reading.
