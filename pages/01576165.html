Nichols Radial Injection Model (RIM) XIV Could Relativistic Processes near Sgr A∗ Contribute to the Galactic 511 keV Line? Lawrence William Nichols August 26, 2026 Abstract The Galactic 511 keV emission line from electron-positron annihilation shows a prominent bulge-dominated morphology with limited temporal variability, as mapped by two decades of INTEGRAL/SPI observations. While distributed astro￾physical sources can supply positrons, the observed spatial distribution and high bulge-to-disk ratio remain difficult to reproduce fully. This paper extends the Radial Injection Model framework developed in RIM IX. We examine whether the same near-horizon environment — where material accel￾erated from the inner accretion flow reaches v ≳ 0.9c and undergoes progressive electromagnetic stripping — can also generate electron-positron pairs that con￾tribute to the Galactic 511 keV line. Quantitative predictions require GRMHD simulations coupled with particle transport. Current data are consistent with a sub-dominant central contribution during past higher-accretion episodes, but do not yet distinguish it definitively from distributed sources. Light dark-matter annihilation in a central density spike remains an alternative channel for the central component. 1 Introduction The 511 keV gamma-ray line from positron annihilation in the Milky Way has been ob￾served for decades. INTEGRAL/SPI has provided high-resolution mapping over more than twenty years (6; 4). The emission is dominated by a bright, roughly spherical bulge component centred on the Galactic Centre, with a fainter extended disk. The bulge-to￾disk luminosity ratio is higher than expected from ordinary stellar population tracers, and the line is narrow, consistent with annihilation in a warm, partially ionised medium with a high positronium fraction (fPs ≳ 90%).
