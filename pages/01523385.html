Martin Kokus Born ( 1950-05-16 ) May 16, 1950 (age 76) Residence Union City, PA, United States Nationality USA Known for Gravity , Cosmology , Fundamental Particles Scientific career Fields Physics , Geophysics , Cosmology Martin Kokus (born May 16, 1950) is an American physicist and independent researcher whose work spans gravitation , earth physics and cosmology . He is known for arguing that astronomical redshift quantization and earth expansion are complementary phenomena, and for studies of solar, lunar and planetary periodicities in earthquakes and volcanism. Kokus has been a longtime participant in the Natural Philosophy Alliance and in the New Concepts in Global Tectonics research community. Biography Kokus was trained in gravitation and earth physics. Beginning in 1986, at the age of 36, he undertook a serious study of periodicities in earthquakes and increasingly questioned mainstream scientific consensus. Over the following fourteen years he came to reject a number of established theories and to view academic science with skepticism, following the observational data wherever it appeared to him to lead. By 1996 he believed he had assembled the basis of a grand unified theory. He obtained an invitation to a conference of astronomers and physicists at the University of Arizona, where he presented his ideas. He later recounted that his approach combined theories unfamiliar to that audience in order to explain phenomena they regarded as established, an experience that reinforced his sense of standing outside conventional academic physics. Education B.S. Physics, University of Pittsburgh, 1971. Minors in mathematics and education. Pennsylvania Secondary Instructional I Teaching Certificate in physics, 1973. M.S. Engineering Physics, University of Virginia, 1975; minor in environmental science. Advanced study, University of Denver, economics and mathematics (40 graduate quarter hours in economics).
