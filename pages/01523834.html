Saturday Morning Science Chat Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with Ian Cohen, James Keene , Bob Gray , Carl and Alex, the whole of it on dark matter. Hu's microphone was barely audible for the first half — a Windows update had reset the level — so Ian carried much of the discussion, and Hu twice handed the chair over while attending to his mother. Ian: are spiral galaxies spiral? Ian's proposal, which he has raised before and thinks neglected, is that what we see in the night sky is not a contemporaneous snapshot. An object is visible only if it happened to be at a distance of X light years at the moment X years ago when it emitted the light; if it was elsewhere then, the light has already passed us or has not yet arrived. Looking at a nebula whose near side is a million light years away and whose far side is two million, we are combining events separated by a million years. From that he draws two conclusions. First, that the gravitational effects attributed to dark matter may simply be matter we cannot see — ordinary matter, of the kind found in a laboratory, that is not at the right distance for its light to reach us now. He prefers to call this dark stuff , declining the mainstream term. Second, and more provocatively, that spiral galaxies may not be spiral. His tentative position is that they are elliptical or spherical bodies whose apparent shape is the composite of parts seen at different epochs as the object rotates.
