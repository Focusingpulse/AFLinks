GC2026-SGG043 Paleoseismological studies of the Kyzyl-Adyr uplift in the Talas depression Andrei A. Strelnikov1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences aas@ifz.ru Abstract In 2025, reconnaissance paleoseismological studies were conducted on the Kyzyl-Adyr uplift in the Talas depression, with the aim of identifying ancient strong earthquakes. Research Methods Traditional paleoseismological methods were used [1]. An assessment of paleoearthquake magnitudes and intensities was conducted based on empirical relationships between them and the parameters of seismogenic ruptures [2]. Neogene-Quaternary deposits composing intra-depression uplifts were studied within natural outcrops. An analysis of modern geodynamic activity and the formation settings of the newest structures in the Talas Depression and adjacent territories was conducted, based on GNSS data and solutions for earthquake focal mechanisms. Description of the Region The Talas Depression is located in the northwest of the Kyrgyz Republic, in the Northern Tien Shan. The depression is an asymmetrical graben with a gently sloping northern limb and a steep southern limb complicated by marginal faults. It lies at an altitude of 600–2000 m between the Kyrgyz Range to the north and the Talas Ala-Too to the south and extends from west to east for 250 km. The Talas Depression is framed by two mountain ranges: the Talas Ala-Too, composed primarily of metamorphic schists and granites, and the Kyrgyz Range, composed primarily of sedimentary and metamorphic rocks, porphyrites, and granites.
