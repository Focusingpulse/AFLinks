Donald Reed Residence West Seneca, NY, United States Nationality USA Known for Beltrami vector fields; vortex topology; torsion-field research Scientific career Fields Mathematics, electrodynamics, energy research Institutions State University of New York at Buffalo Donald Reed is an American mathematician and independent researcher on the foundations of electrodynamics. He is known for a long series of papers on Beltrami vector fields and vortex topology, and for introducing English-language readers to the Russian torsion field research that became visible in the West after the collapse of the Soviet Union. Biography Reed has a background in engineering and physics as well as mathematics, and has worked as an adjunct professor and lecturer at the State University of New York at Buffalo, teaching pre-calculus mathematics. Alongside that he has been a long-standing researcher into advanced energy topics, contributing journal articles and conference papers over several decades — principally to the International Symposia on New Energy, the Natural Philosophy Alliance conferences, and the Space, Propulsion and Energy Sciences International Forum. Education M.A. in Mathematics No institution or year is recorded for the degree in the sources available. Research Beltrami fields and vortex topology Reed's earliest and most sustained subject is a class of vector fields rarely treated in the physics literature: Beltrami fields , in which the curl of the field is everywhere parallel to the field itself. These arise in fluid dynamics and as force-free magnetic fields in plasma physics, and Reed's argument across a sequence of papers from 1993 onward is that they are not a special case but an archetype — that the vortex is a fundamental topological form in nature, and that the standard formulation of electrodynamics has overlooked a whole family of solutions with it.
