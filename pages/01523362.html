M. A. Piestrup Residence Palo Alto, CA, United States Nationality American Known for X-ray and radiation sources Scientific career Fields Physics Institutions Adelphi Technology, Inc. Melvin A. Piestrup (M. A. Piestrup) is an American physicist and engineer based in the Palo Alto / Redwood City area of California, best known for his work on compact sources of x-rays, extreme ultraviolet light, and neutrons produced by relativistic electron beams. He is the founder of Adelphi Technology, Inc., a company he established to develop and commercialize novel radiation sources for medical, industrial, and scientific applications. Biography Piestrup earned a PhD in Electrical Engineering from Stanford University in 1972. His early research included work on the use of lasers to accelerate high-energy electrons. Over a career spanning more than forty years he specialized in the development of electron and ion sources and their application to the generation of x-rays and neutrons. In 1984 he formed Adelphi Technology, Inc., incorporating it in California in 1986; working with colleagues at Adelphi and at Stanford University, he developed novel sources of vacuum ultraviolet, soft, and hard x-rays, and later compact neutron generators. He has served the company as founder, president, and Vice President of Research and Marketing. Scientific work Piestrup's research centers on the physics of electromagnetic radiation emitted when fast (relativistic) electrons interact with matter, and on turning these processes into practical, compact radiation sources. Transition radiation. When a charged particle crosses the boundary between two media with different optical properties, it emits radiation. Piestrup and colleagues showed that by passing relativistic electrons through targets made of many thin metal foils, a narrow, forward-directed cone of x-rays can be generated, and proposed the technique as a soft-x-ray source for x-ray lithography.
