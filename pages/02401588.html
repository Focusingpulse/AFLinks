GC2026-PERM037 Reconstruction of the Miocene volcanic activity within the Malko-Petropavlovsk zone of transverse dislocations (Kamchatka) based on rock-magnetic and paleomagnetic data Anton Latyshev1,2,3 , Olga Bergal-Kuvikas3,4 , Elena Latanova2,3,5 , Maiia Anosova2 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University 2 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences 3 Institute of Volcanology and Seismology of the Far Eastern Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences 4 Vitus Bering Kamchatka State University 5 National Research Technological University MISIS anton.latyshev@gmail.com Assessment of volcanic risks is a critical issue for Kamchatka. The most densely populated area of Kamchatka, including its capital Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky, the Elizovo international airport, and numerous touristic centers, is located near the active Avachinsky and Koryaksky volcanoes. Furthermore, Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky lies within the Malko-Petropavlovsk zone of transverse dislocations, where volcanic activity has occurred from the Miocene Zavoiko volcanic complex to the Quaternary monogenetic cinder cones and extrusive domes [1]. The most ancient volcanic manifestation in the studied area is related to the Miocene igneous rocks of the Zavoiko (or Pribrezhny) complex. These rocks are widespread along the shores of the Avacha bay and compose so-called Zavoiko paleovolcanic edifice [2].
