1 The Universe Has One Address Homogeneity as a Statement About What Can Be Said Dean Kulik QuHarmonics Research Group · ORCID 0009-0003-3128-8828 August 2026 Abstract The cosmological principle states that on sufficiently large scales the universe is homogeneous and isotropic: no location is privileged and no direction is preferred. This is ordinarily read as a modelling assumption that makes the field equations tractable. It is also, read literally, a statement about symmetry, and symmetry statements have consequences for what can be said about the thing they describe. A space whose symmetry group acts transitively has exactly one orbit, and therefore its quotient under that group is a single point. Applied to the largest scales, the cosmological principle asserts that the universe supports precisely one intrinsic address class. Every coordinate system used in cosmology — comoving position, cosmic time, the frame in which the microwave background is isotropic — is accordingly a structure supplied by the observer rather than found in the subject. Cosmologists have long treated these as gauge choices; the present reading is stronger and more specific. On a homogeneous space the observer's coordinate surplus is not merely conventional but maximal, and a surplus of that kind must become degenerate somewhere. Three consequences follow and each already has an independent counterpart in the literature.
