Universal Principles of Space and Matter: A Call for Conceptual Reorientation Book Author Paramahamsa Tewari Language English Subject Space Vortex Theory; the structure of the electron; gravitation; cosmology Published November 2001; printings 2002 and 2007 Publisher Crest Publishing House (a Jaico enterprise), New Delhi Pages 206 ISBN 8124202818 Universal Principles of Space and Matter: A Call for Conceptual Reorientation is a book by Paramahamsa Tewari , the Indian nuclear-project engineer who developed Space Vortex Theory . It is the mature statement of a programme he began in the 1970s, and the volume in which the theory's founding postulates are set out as three principles. Its aim, in the author's summary, is a physics in which the universe is built "with a single substance, a single field, a single fundamental particle, and a single universal constant." The three principles The book develops three Cosmic Principles : The space of the universe is an eternally existent, nonmaterial, continuous, isotropic and fluid substratum. The space has a limiting speed of flow, equal to the speed of light relative to the absolute vacuum; and a limiting angular velocity, when in a state of circulating motion. The universal space is eternally inherent with motion. The second is where the theory does its work. Given a fluid space that cannot flow faster than c , a vortex circulating at the limit must break down at its centre — and it is that breakdown, a void in the medium, from which Tewari derives the electron, and from the electron mass, charge and the fields. Where the argument starts The preface gives the origin of the whole programme, and it is a classroom demonstration. In the mid-1950s, as a student at Intermediate Science College, Tewari watched a lecturer place a paperweight on a slip of paper and pull the paper briskly away; the paperweight scarcely moved.
