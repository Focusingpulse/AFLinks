Karin Westdyk Nationality USA Known for Documenting the hydrogen-generator work of Francisco Pacheco Scientific career Fields Journalism; environmental and energy writing Karin Westdyk is an American writer on environmental and energy subjects, best known within the new energy community for documenting the work of the hydrogen inventor Francisco Pacheco . She is a writer rather than a scientist, and her two pieces catalogued here are works of journalism — a magazine article and a book chapter — rather than technical papers. Writing Westdyk's account of Pacheco appeared in two places: " US Patent Granted to West Milford Resident for Unique Hydrogen Energy Systems " ( The Messenger: Environmental Health Journal , March/April 1992) — a report, from Pacheco's own New Jersey area, on the granting of his 1992 US patent. " The Story of Francisco Pacheco and the Suppression of Hydrogen Technology " (1994) — a chapter in the anthology Suppressed Inventions and Other Discoveries , which frames Pacheco's device within that book's theme of technologies allegedly kept from the public. Subject: Francisco Pacheco and his hydrogen generator Francisco Pacheco (a Bolivian-born inventor based at Hewitt, in the West Milford area of New Jersey) built and patented a "Bi-Polar Autoelectric Hydrogen Generator" — US Patent 3,892,653 (1975) and later US 5,089,107 (1992), with corresponding patents in Germany, Brazil and Japan. The device generates hydrogen from seawater using a galvanic cell: sacrificial magnesium and aluminium plates as the anode, stainless steel as the cathode, and seawater as the electrolyte, producing hydrogen both by electrolytic action at the cathode and by chemical reaction at the anode. The demonstrations were real and are well attested.
