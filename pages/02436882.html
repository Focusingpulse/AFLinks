Introduction: The Universe as a Self-Extending Computational Substrate The conventional reductionist view of physics posits that fundamental particles exist within a pre-defined geometric arena, interacting through forces prescribed by fixed, external symmetries. However, an exhaustive synthesis of theoretical physics, information theory, and formal logic—encapsulated within the Recursive Harmonic Architecture (RHA), the Nexus framework, and Helix Gravity—suggests a radically inverted ontology. In this paradigm, reality is a singular, self-extending computational substrate capable of generating a virtually infinite array of conditional interactions. Computation is not an abstract overlay constructed by human engineers; rather, it is formally defined as matter arranged so that it conditionally changes what other matter is permitted to do next. This framework asserts that the physical world is not defined by static "values" or fundamental "nouns," but by a growing vocabulary of callable "verbs" or transformations. Learning and physical evolution are thus isomorphic to parameter discovery. At a coarse level, invoking a physical system like water appears as a low-arity method. Deeper investigation reveals hidden conditional interactions—temperature, pressure, phase, flow—where each added parameter is not a mere label but a newly discovered handle on a conditional verb.
