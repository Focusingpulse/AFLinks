SHETTLE. Lond. Elec. Rev. and Elec. Eng. March 6. —He claims that the cause of the phenomena discovered by Röntgen (referring to the photographic effect of the rays) is the direct result of electric stress and not, as was supposed, of invisible light. He describes an experiment in which a cotton-covered wire “was bent five times upon itself in parallel lines” and then fastened to a cardboard and placed directly on the sensitive surface of a photographic plate; a very thin magnetic strip was fastened to the back of the plate by strips of paper, the whole being wrapped in ruby and in black paper; an alternating current of eight amperes at a frequency of 40 was passed through the wire for about 45 minutes; a complete and distinct image of the magnetic strip and the paper fastening it was formed on the plate.
