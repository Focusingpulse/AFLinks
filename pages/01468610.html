(Citation marker: Acoustic Cavitation) SUMMARY OF THE DOCUMENT Thresholds of Acoustic Cavitation James E. Barger, ONR Contract Nonr-1866(24), Harvard University (April 1964) Acoustic Cavitation This 200-page technical memorandum is a comprehensive study of acoustic cavitation thresholds in water, examining how cavitation begins, how bubbles behave, and what physical parameters control their formation, growth, and collapse. Barger’s approach is unusually thorough for 1964: he studies frequency, pressure, dissolved gas, particle contamination, bubble nucleation theories, experimental design, dynamic bubble motion, and statistical behavior of cavitation events, and then builds a full laboratory system capable of producing extremely high acoustic pressures (10–200 bars). HIGH-LEVEL FINDINGS 1. Cavitation Threshold Depends on Three Domains (A, B, C) These “regions,” defined experimentally, are a central organizing concept: (Chapter I Abstract, p. 3–4) Acoustic Cavitation Region A — Low frequency, low pressure, high dissolved gas f < 200 kHz Pa < 3 bars Ps (dissolved-air saturation) > 600 mm Hg → Small gas bubbles grow by rectified diffusion, collect at pressure nodes (“stable cavitation”).
