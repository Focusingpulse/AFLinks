GTM2026-main004 Emotionally Bounded-Rational Agents in Repeated Games: Dual-System Architecture and Simulation Study Pavliuchenkov Dmitri1 , Gubanov Dmitri2 1 Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (State University) 2 V.A. Trapeznikov Institute of Control Problems of the Russian Academy of Sciences. dima1567p@gmail.com We present a computational framework for simulating emotion-guided decision-making in repeated strategic games such as the Prisoner’s Dilemma, the Battle of the Sexes, and the Ultimatum Game. The model is designed to explore how the coupling of emotional and rational processes shapes adaptive behavior and social outcomes over time. Our main hypothesis is that emotionally–rational (E–R) agents—those with balanced affective and cognitive systems—will perform more effectively and maintain greater subjective well-being than purely rational agents (with an atrophied but computational emotion module) or purely emotional agents (with a weakened rational component). Additional hypotheses address specific behavioral dimensions: (1) E–R agents are expected to adapt more flexibly to partners with fixed strategies; (2) their gameplay trajectories will approximate human￾like decision patterns; and (3) their dispositions—pessimistic, optimistic, or emotionally intense—will produce distinctive, interpretable interaction styles. In contrast to classical game-theoretic models that assume static preferences and perfect rationality, our approach treats emotion as a dynamic process intertwined with learning, evaluation, and action.
