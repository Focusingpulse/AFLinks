The Origin of the Universe: An Examination of the Big-Bang and Steady-State Cosmogonies Book Author Harold S Slusher Language English Subject Cosmology; the Big Bang and steady-state theories; young-earth creationism Published 1978; ICR Technical Monograph No. 8, 1980 Publisher Institute for Creation Research, San Diego Pages 50 ISBN 0932766005 The Origin of the Universe: An Examination of the Big-Bang and Steady-State Cosmogonies is a 50-page monograph by Harold S Slusher , issued by the Institute for Creation Research as No. 8 in its Technical Monograph series. It does two things. It argues that both of the twentieth century's principal cosmogonies — the Big Bang and the steady-state model — fail on empirical and theoretical grounds. And it advances the proposal for which Slusher is best known in astronomy: that the universe is not Euclidean but Riemannian , and is in consequence very much smaller than it appears. The argument Against the standard cosmogonies The first part of the monograph is critical rather than constructive. Slusher argues that the Big Bang and the steady-state theory alike rest on assumptions that cannot be tested — matter eternally existing, or matter appearing from a singularity or from nothing — and that neither accounts satisfactorily for the observations. The intended conclusion is not that one of the two should be preferred but that both should be set aside, leaving room for a created origin. The Riemannian universe The distinctive claim is geometrical. Slusher proposes that space is Riemannian — positively curved and closed — rather than Euclidean, and assigns it a radius of curvature so small that no point in the universe is more than about 15.71 light-years away . On this account the immense distances derived by astronomers are an artefact of the wrong geometry: light travelling on a curved path in a small closed space is mistakenly interpreted, under a Euclidean assumption, as having crossed an enormous Euclidean distance.
