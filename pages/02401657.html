GC2026-PERM055 Preliminary results of multidisciplinary studies of Lake Turgoyak sediments Lina Kosareva1 , Nuriia Nourgalieva1 , Pavel Krylov1 , Mikhail Glukhov2,1 , Anastasia Yusupova1 , Inna Chernova1 1 Kazan (Volga Region) Federal University 2 Zavaritsky Institute of Geology and Geochemistry, Ural Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences lina.kosareva@mail.ru Lake Turgoyak, located on the eastern slope of the Southern Urals, features sedimentary deposits that form a unique natural archive, holding information about sedimentation processes and paleoclimatic changes. Its geological history spans over 32.5 thousand years. Key characteristics — such as a complex bottom topography, an uneven distribution of sediment thickness, an oligotrophic status, and freshwater of the hydrocarbonate class — create unique conditions for reconstructing paleogeographic events [1, 2]. Our study aims to reconstruct lacustrine sedimentation by analyzing the material composition of six cores, ranging from 4.2 to 5.8 meters in length, sampled from the central deep-water zone of the lake. To assess the role of sediment sources and transport pathways, as well as the mechanisms of sediment distribution within the lake basin, we analyzed cartographic materials detailing the area's geology, topography, drainage networks, and watersheds [3]. A digital elevation model (DEM) of the lake and its surrounding area was constructed using GIS software, based on available geological, topographical, and bathymetric maps.
