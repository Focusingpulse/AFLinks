Title New Energy News (September 2000) Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Patrick G Bailey Keywords New Energy Published 2000 Journal New Energy News Volume 7 Number 4 No. of pages 12 Abstract Overview This is not a research paper but a complete issue of the newsletter of the Institute for New Energy — New Energy News , Volume 7, Number 4, ISSN 1075-0045, September 2000, edited by Patrick G Bailey . It opens with an editor's note announcing that from this issue onward NEN will be distributed by email in two forms, plain ASCII and PDF, with a mailed Adobe Acrobat CD-ROM offered to subscribers without internet access for $2 postage. The remainder is a compilation of forwarded news items, contributed reports and event announcements, each credited to whoever sent it in. As a document its interest is documentary rather than scientific: it is a snapshot of what the new-energy community was reading and circulating in autumn 2000. The mix is characteristic — a patent-office employment case treated as a proxy for institutional suppression, a Russian over-unity claim posted to a mailing list, a trip report on a rotating machine in India, and, alongside these, straight wire-service coverage of hydrogen fuel cells and nuclear-waste encapsulation that has nothing unorthodox about it at all. Nothing in the issue is peer-reviewed and nothing carries error bars. Contents of the issue The EEOC ruling and the "Robert Park" list The lead item, credited to Richard Shamp of Washington D.C. and drawn from a Washington Post story of 23 August 2000, reports that a patent examiner fired from the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office appealed through the Commerce Department to the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, which "did not rule as to whether the technology of cold fusion was scientifically correct or not but did rule that the Commerce Department acted improperly."
