We will therefore seek to establish the commonality between these three phenomena. Overview Electric Current (Cable Telegraphy and Wireless Telegraphy) is a five-page paper by David Tombe , dated 7 April 2012 and published in the General Science Journal . It returns to the subject of his 2008 The Telegrapher's Equations from a different angle, taking Poynting's theorem as the common ground between guided and free-space propagation. The organising idea is a distinction between two things an electric circulation can do when energy is added: "the circulation may either dilate or angularly accelerate, depending on the constraints." Cable telegraphy is the dilating case, wireless the angularly accelerating one. In cable telegraphy , a step wave advances between two conductors, the return limb sometimes "static like a caterpillar track on the ground", with a transverse electric field at the step and a surrounding magnetic field. Tombe notes that "particles don't cross the gap at the step", and takes this as grounds to "rule out the conventional theory that electric current is a flow of charged particles", reintroducing "the old vitreous fluid of Franklin, Watson, and DuFay". He then draws a consequence he states plainly: because the displacement current in a cable arises from linear polarisation of the dielectric and so belongs to Gauss's law rather than to induction, "we cannot therefore use the linear displacement current in cable telegraphy in order to derive the electromagnetic wave equation.
