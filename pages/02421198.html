The Philosopher's Stone: Alchemy and the Secret Research for Exotic Matter Book Author Joseph P Farrell Published 2009 Publisher Feral House Pages 360 ISBN 1932595406 The Philosopher's Stone: Alchemy and the Secret Research for Exotic Matter is a 2009 book by Joseph P Farrell , published by Feral House. It was Farrell's first book with that publisher; his earlier work appeared chiefly through Adventures Unlimited Press. Overview The book argues for a continuity between the aims of historical alchemy and certain strands of twentieth-century physics, on the premise that both were concerned with altering the constitution of matter itself rather than merely rearranging it. Farrell's method throughout is to read alchemical texts as descriptions of a physical medium, and then to look for modern counterparts to what he takes them to describe. Three lines of material are drawn together. The first is the claim, associated with David Hudson, that the platinum-group metals can exist in a monatomic or "high spin" state with anomalous properties — the substance often called monatomic gold. The second is Soviet and Russian work, principally that of the astrophysicist Nikolai A Kozyrev on rotation, time and what later writers termed torsion physics, together with the "red mercury" legend that circulated after the collapse of the Soviet Union. The third is Farrell's continuing subject: German wartime research, here the device known as Die Glocke ("the Bell") and the substance identified in that literature as "Serum 525," approached alongside isotope enrichment and laser work. The Bell and the wartime material connect this book to its predecessor, Secrets of the Unified Field: The Philadelphia Experiment, The Nazi Bell, and the Discarded Theory (2008), which treats the same device in relation to Einstein's abandoned unified field theory.
