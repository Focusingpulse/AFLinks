Title EPR Paradox and the Physical Meaning of an Experiment in Quantum Mechanics Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Vesselin C Noninski Keywords quantum mechanics , paradox, Quantum Theory , distance Published 2003 Journal PhilSci-Archive No. of pages 9 Abstract It is shown that there is one purely deterministic outcome when measurement is made on the state function chosen by EPR to describe the combined two-particle system - the distance between the two particles is preserved the same. Further, it is shown that, surprisingly, the psi-function designed according to QM leads to the following paradox - despite the fact that the two particles move in opposite directions, in time the distance between them becomes shorter and shorter. Overview EPR Paradox and the Physical Meaning of an Experiment in Quantum Mechanics is a nine-page paper by Vesselin C Noninski , posted to the PhilSci-Archive in 2003. It examines the particular two-particle wavefunction that Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen wrote down in 1935 and argues that it has two properties that have gone unnoticed: one deterministic and one paradoxical. EPR's state is Ψ(x 1 ,x 2 ) = ∫ dp exp[i(x 1 − x 2 + x 0 )p/ℏ] which Noninski rewrites as an expansion of Ψ along the continuous basis exp(ix 1 p/ℏ), with the exponentials in x 2 as coefficients. A position measurement on particle 1 collapses this to δ(x 1 −x)δ(x 2 −x+x 0 ), from which x 2 = x 1 + x 0 . A momentum measurement instead yields p for particle 1 and −p for particle 2. His first observation is that although repeated measurements at the same time t give different values of x 1 and different values of p, the separation is always x 0 . He finds this striking: the two particles are flying apart at speeds that differ from run to run, yet their separation is always the same, and since t is arbitrary the separation is the same at every time.
