Title Bohr Quantum Theory Yields the Relativistic Schroedinger Equation Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Yuri N Keilman Keywords Quantum Theory , Bohr, longitudinal waves, wave function Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 2 Pages 255-256 Abstract Niels Bohr's 1913 approach to Quantum Theory yields the relativistic Schroedinger Equation, suggesting that the physical meaning of the wave function is not probabilistic. The existence and physical meaning of longitudinal waves of E-M Potential are discovered. Overview Bohr Quantum Theory Yields the Relativistic Schroedinger Equation is a two-page paper by Yuri N Keilman of Valley City, North Dakota, presented at the 17th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference and published in the Proceedings of the NPA (vol. 7, pp. 255–256, 2010). Its starting point is Bohr's 1913 quantisation, read as a resonance condition: an orbiting electron radiates at its de Broglie frequency, that radiation "feeds" a standing wave, and the orbit is allowed only when its circumference holds a whole number of de Broglie wavelengths. Keilman then asks what the standing wave is made of, and answers that it is not electromagnetic but a longitudinal wave of electromagnetic potential , which he calls a "Dummy Wave". The construction rests on three positions he states explicitly. First, "everything in the realm of E-M Potential is physically important", even though the potential cannot be measured directly. Second, such waves "do not produce electric or magnetic fields and consequently have no energy or momentum", so a charge can radiate them continuously without loss — which is offered as the reason an orbiting electron does not spiral in. Third, "obviously we deny 'gauge invariance'. Different 'gauges' represent different physical situations."
