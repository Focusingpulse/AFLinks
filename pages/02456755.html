Can't Get There from Here Two thousand years ago the Greek philosopher Zeno noted that an object moving from one place to another must first reach a halfway point, and before that a point half of the way to the halfway point, and so on. Any movement involves an infinite number of intermediate points, and so any motion must require an infinite amount of time. Motion, Zeno concluded, is logically impossible. In fact, things do move. Zeno did not consider that an endless series could have a finite sum. But in the counter-intuitive realm of quantum physics, something akin to Zeno's paradox can occur: atoms can be paralyzed if they are closely scrutinized.
