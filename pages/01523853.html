Saturday Science Chats lead by Franklin Hu sponsored by the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society Summary An open chat with no scheduled speaker, hosted by Franklin Hu with Harry Ricker , Ian Cohen in Dublin and Alistair Riddoch — ranging widely, and closing with David de Hilster 's announcement of the society's new website. Hu's own summary at the end was that they had covered the west coast fires, climate, covid, population and gold. The wide-ranging first half The session opened on the California and Oregon fires and stayed on causes: Riddoch attributed most of the risk to reduced logging on government land over fifty years, leaving unmanaged undergrowth and fewer access roads, and pointed to the El Niño oscillation as what determines a bad fire year; Ricker cited the US Forest Service estimate that eighty to ninety per cent of fires are human-caused, mostly accidentally. Ian noted that Ireland's gorse fires are small by comparison. From there to population : Riddoch's objection to overpopulation arguments is that they are made without numbers — that one cannot predict a collapse without predicting when, and that innovation has repeatedly raised what the earth can carry. Ian set the Malthusian version against it: subsistence rising arithmetically against a geometrically rising population. They looked at the growth-rate curve and noted the annual increase is already falling. On covid the discussion was speculative and the participants said so.
