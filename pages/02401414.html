GC2026-SGG012 Characterization of Permeability and Strength Anisotropy in Arctic Gas-Condensate Reservoirs Using Multiscale Digital Core Analysis and Geomechanical Modeling Valerii V. Khimulia1 , Svyatoslav O. Barkov1 , Yuri F. Kovalenko1 , Vladimir I. Karev1 1 Institute for Problems of Mechanics named after A.Yu. Ishlinsky of the Russian Academy of Sciences Ponencia invitada Valery.khim@gmail.com This research is dedicated to the development and application of an integrated laboratory methodology for studying weakly cemented, high-porosity sandstones from a gas condensate field on the Russian Arctic Shelf. The study focuses on the interrelated processes of rock failure, filtration, and sand production, with a particular emphasis on understanding the anisotropy of petrophysical properties. A multidisciplinary approach was employed, combining several advanced techniques. Direct physical modeling using a true triaxial loading apparatus was conducted to simulate near-wellbore stress evolution [1]. Specialized geomechanical tests, including hollow cylinder-type experiments [2], were performed to investigate sand production initiation. The core of the methodology involved comprehensive digital rock analysis aimed at quantitative characterization of microstructural and filtration properties.. High-resolution X-ray computed microtomography (CT) was used for non-destructive imaging of the pore space structure.
