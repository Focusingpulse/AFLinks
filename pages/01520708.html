Title New Energy News (June 2000) Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Patrick G Bailey Keywords New Energy Published 2000 Journal New Energy News Volume 7 Number 1 No. of pages 10 Abstract Overview This is not a paper. It is an issue of New Energy News — the newsletter of the Institute for New Energy, edited by Hal Fox and archived by Patrick G Bailey — and it consists almost entirely of a conference report. The subject is ICCF8, the Eighth International Conference on Cold Fusion, held at Lerici in Italy over four days in May 2000, with about 150 attendees, some thirty presentations and ninety poster papers. The issue reproduces abstracts from the meeting with the editor's own comments appended to each, and opens with his overall impression. That impression is worth quoting in full, because it is more sceptical than the venue would lead one to expect: "There is a lot of excellent scientific work being done to improve the various so-called cold fusion experiments … However, there is no PRESENTLY-PLANNED cold fusion device that is ready for commercialization. This editor's judgement is that the basic hydrogen-in-metals process is not sufficiently robust enough to become a major new-energy commercial success." He then names the two technologies he does think have "strong commercial potential": Randell Mills's work at BlackLight Power, and the high-density charge cluster patents issued from 1992 onward, which he notes are "the first … in which the patents claim to be tapping space energy or zero-point energy". The value of the document is as a primary source: it records, in detail and at the time, what the low-energy nuclear reaction field looked like at the turn of the century.
