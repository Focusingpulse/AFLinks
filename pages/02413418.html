( click to enlarge ) This is Constantine. He is often called Constantine The Great, which was probably self styled judging by the ego on him. He’s frequently hailed as the great (there’s that word again) first Roman ruler to embrace Christianity. But he wasn’t. He was a pagan. Born one, from a family of pagans, lived as one, and the matter of whether he died as one is still up for debate. He was proclaimed emperor (Caesar) here in York in 306AD and I often pass him as I go about my biz in the city. With the Council of Nicaea in 325AD he settled the Christological issue of the divine nature of Jesus as God The Son. Up to this point Jesus was regarded as a sort of guru-prophet. So in order to compete in a Roman pantheon populated with gods and goddesses for every hour of every day, he had to be Divine. From the Council of Nicaea we get the (early) Nicene Creed and the roman mandated date of Easter. And Jesus was essentially elevated from being a man to becoming a God. That always stuck in my craw! On Constantine’s say so...the forms of Christianity we all know today originated here. With him. We got 2000 years of the Holy Roman Empire, suppression and oppression, wars too numerous to count, bloodshed, “my gods better than your god” imperialism, slaughter and untold pillaging, an amassing of wealth, treasure and power that solidified the forms of Christianity that we know today. And to boot (probably one of those fancy lace up Roman boots) all traces of pagan practice, all the holy sacred groves, the stone circles and shrines, springs and treasured trees were either destroyed or overlaid with the “new religion.” And even worse, all memory of these precious ancient spiritual traditions were brutally suppressed and lost from living memory. So when I see Constantine’s smug supercilious face all I can see is a shrewd political propagandist.
