Title Impulse Power Secrets Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Philipp M Kanarev Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 4 Pages 241-244 Abstract Water is the main source of future environmental-friendly power; that's why the search for ways to tap this resource increases continually. But there exist forces which seriously prevent advancement. Overview Philipp M. Kanarev, professor emeritus of physics at Kuban State Agrarian University in Krasnodar, argues that water is the energy source of the future and that impulse — rather than continuous — power systems are the key to tapping it cheaply. The paper opens with an unusual framing. Citing the fate of the American inventor Stanley Meyer, Kanarev writes that "it is clear from it that I am the next to be done away with," and states that his life is dearer to him than the scientific secrets he possesses. He therefore declines to reveal what he calls the electric route to cheap electrolysis, and reveals the electromechanical route "only partially." The technical argument runs as follows. Continuous energy production is uneconomical; the heart, which works one third of the period and rests two thirds, is the model. Water decomposes into hydrogen and oxygen under impulse excitation of its ions, and stand-alone inertial impulse electromechanical sources do this most economically — magnetic field excitation being ten to a hundred times more expensive per watt. He states that the direct cost of producing one cubic metre of the hydrogen–oxygen mixture is less than the cost of natural gas. The delay in implementation he attributes to "an error in understanding the larger part of Newton's dynamics," corrected by his new laws of mechanodynamics, which treat accelerated, uniform and decelerated motion separately.
