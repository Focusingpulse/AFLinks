Bridging the Standard Model and Aether Physics Model: A Summary Overview David W. Thomson III June 2025 1 Introduction The Standard Model (SM) of particle physics and General Relativity stand as monumental achievements, offering precise predictions for phenomena ranging from the Higgs boson’s discovery [3] to gravitational wave detection [1]. These frameworks have shaped modern physics, providing a robust mathematical structure validated by experiments like neutrino oscillations [6] and beta decay spectroscopy [16]. Yet, unresolved questions persist: the SM struggles to unify gravity with quantum mechanics, explain dark matter and dark energy, or provide a physical basis for particle structures [16]. Its reliance on abstract constructs—point-like particles, virtual bosons, and a continuous linear timeline—reveals ontological biases that limit its scope [24]. The Aether Physics Model (APM) emerges as a complementary framework, reinterpreting SM evidence through a unified ontology grounded in a quantized, non-material Aether medium [26]. By viewing magnetic, electrostatic, and gravitational fields, semiconductor p-holes, and vacuum effects as direct manifestations of the Aether, the APM offers a structural, geometric perspective that bridges quantum and cosmological scales, aligning with empirical data while challenging SM assumptions [18]. 1.1 Purpose and Scope This summary overview presents the APM as a unified framework that reinterprets SM evidence through an Aether-based ontology, synthesizing insights from nuclear physics, stellar dynamics, thermodynamics, and biological mechanics [18]. Its purpose is to facilitate dialogue between APM proponents and SM physicists by highlighting shared mathematical foundations, empirical successes, and testable predictions, while addressing ontological divergences.
