ABSTRACT A qualitative electrochemical model for ball lightning was developed during the 1990’s (Turner, 2002). The key requirement was electrochemical refrigeration at the surface of an air plasma. The cooling was shown to result from the conversion of metastable, fully ionized, gas-phase nitrous acid to its stable molecular form. If the refrigeration cools the plasma surface to below 15 0 C, aerosols of nitric acid can be produced in subsequent oxidation processes. These particles restrict the inflow of air toward the plasma and provide the ball with a very effective surface tension. This helps explain several unusual characteristics of lightning balls and also their close relatives, such as earth-lights and Unpredictable Flying Objects.
