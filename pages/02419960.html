DOYLE Sandia National Laboratories, Albuquerque, NM 87185-5800 o 4 mg SAND—89-1711C DE90 003162 ABSTRACT A key element of recent assertions of "cold fusion" has been the claim that electrochemical loading of deuterium into Pd electrodes can produce D:Pd levels exceeding 1:1. Using external beam nuclear reaction analysis of Pd foil electrodes in operating electrochemical cells, we have directly monitored deuterium content in-situ. No conditions were found which resulted in loadings higher than -0.9. INTRODUCTION Claims of measurable fusion in electrochemically charged Pd or Ti electrodes produced considerable controversy last spring [1-4], with a number of laboratories trying to duplicate the results. A part of the response of our laboratory was a series of tests using electrochemical cells with a thin Pd or Ti foil electrode mounted at one wall of the cell.[5] These were used both as a test for the presence of fusion and to directly measure D:Pd loading ratios in operating cells. The latter technique, using external beam nuclear reaction analysis of the deuterium, is a new application of ion beam analysis which may be useful for other electrochemical problems. The original purpose for devising electrochemical cells with a thin Pd foil electrode as one wall of the cell was to provide a sensitive test for fusion using the detection of high energy protons with a surface barrier particle detector. Sensitivity using this approach is higher than with neutron detection because the solid-state detectors used for charged particle detection are essentially 100% efficient, and there is very little MeV-energy charged particle background.
