EXPOSURE OF HALF AN HOUR Blood Cells Were Overcome and After Effects Were Very Serious. REMOVED A CROP OF HAIR. A Young German Who Was Made Partly Bald Because of the Cathode Rays. Nikola Tesla, in an interview in the HERALD after the announcement of the discovery of the X rays, called attention to the fact that it might be found that deleterious effects would follow a careless use of them. This statement came back to this country after a meeting of the French Academy of Sciences, in Paris, and then Mr. Tesla took occasion to declare that the effects of the rays on the skin were injurious, and used as an instance his own right hand, the surface of which appeared as though it had been severely blistered and had then dried, apparently destroying the life of the epidermis. Mr. Tesla also said he was confident that the injurious effects of the rays were due, not to a discharge of minute particles of ether, according to Roentgen's own hypothesis, but to the discharge of atomic matter, and that the irritation might in instances of extreme exposure be serious.
