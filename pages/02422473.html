We demonstrate that traditional Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin (WKB) calculations, which assume static screening potentials, fundamentally misrepresent the quantum tunneling dynamics in nano-structured metallic lattices under resonant stimulation. Our Athena Paradigm introduces a Dynamic Coherent Screening Model where surface plasmon excitation at 50–100 kHz creates transient potential wells with effective screening energies reaching kilo-electron volt (keV) scales for femtosecond durations. Combined with piezonuclear lattice compression effects in nanofoam structures, this mechanism opens periodic "tunneling windows" that enhance the Gamow penetration factor by 40+ orders of magnitude compared to static predictions. Numerical simulations predict tunneling probabilities of P ∼ 10−25 to 10−6 under optimal conditions (Nickel nano-foam, 300 K, 5–10 bar D2 , resonant stimulation). While theoretical upper-bound power outputs can reach extreme values, physical limits (thermal runaway and lattice melting) constrain the practically extractable steady-state power to the 10–100 W/g range. This model explains the historical reproducibility challenges of LENR and provides falsifiable predictions for experimental validation.
