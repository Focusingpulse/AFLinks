Ionel Dinu Born ( 1972-06-01 ) June 1, 1972 (age 54) Residence Focsani, Vrancea, Romania Nationality Romanian Alma mater University of Bucharest, Tohoku University Known for Underwater rotating-cylinder experiments (the "Dinu effect"), Aether , inertia , gravitation , electrodynamics Scientific career Fields Physics teaching, Aether hydrodynamics, Electrodynamics Ionel Dinu (born 1 June 1972) is a Romanian physicist, physics teacher and experimentalist, best known for his underwater experiments with rotating disks and cylinders , which demonstrate a purely mechanical model for magnetic attraction and repulsion. Dinu's programme is to show that the forces normally attributed to fields acting across empty space are in fact fluid-mechanical effects in a liquid aether : that electrostatic and gravitational attraction correspond to Bjerknes forces , and that magnetic attraction and repulsion occur between vortices . His experiments are offered as the demonstration that action apparently at a distance is in every case transmitted through the intervening liquid. His rotating-cylinder result was adopted by Robert de Hilster and David de Hilster as the experimental basis for the magnetic component of the Four Universal Motions , where it is named the "Dinu effect" — a name coined by David de Hilster in Principia Mathematica 2 . The underwater experiments The experiments are simple enough to be shown on video and are the reason Dinu is cited elsewhere on this wiki. Cylinders (and in other runs, disks) are set rotating while submerged in water, and their mutual behaviour is observed. The result is that rotating cylinders in water attract or repel one another in the same pattern as permanent magnets : Opposite directions of rotation → attraction. The flows between the two cylinders reinforce one another and the cylinders move together. Same direction of rotation → repulsion. The opposing flows between them create a disturbance that forces the cylinders apart. No magnets, charge
