George Staniforth Coyne Born ( 1952-10-02 ) October 2, 1952 (age 73) Residence North Delta, BC, Canada Nationality Canadian Known for Notfinity Process , the notfinity concept, consciousness as an abstraction Scientific career Fields Cosmology, Theoretical physics, Philosophy of mind Institutions Progressive Science Institute (Vancouver office), John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society George Staniforth Coyne (born 2 October 1952) is a Canadian philosopher, science communicator and counsellor, best known for his book Notfinity Process and for the term it takes its name from. Notfinity is Coyne's proposed replacement for "infinity". His argument is that infinity cannot be conceptualised — no one can hold an actually infinite quantity in mind — whereas its negation can be stated without difficulty. Notfinity is simply negated finity : the denial that the universe has a boundary, a beginning, a smallest constituent or a largest container. Nothing positive need be imagined; one need only decline to impose a limit. He pairs it with two further terms drawn from univironmental determinism : microcosms , meaning all things and portions of the cosmos, and process , meaning microcosms in motion. Hence the book's title and subtitles — Notfinity Process: Microcosms-in-Motion and Matter-in-Motion . The resulting worldview is univironmental, deterministic, and built from classical mechanics and systems philosophy. In it Coyne critiques relativity theory, the Copenhagen interpretation of quantum mechanics and Big Bang cosmology, and sets out alternatives he holds to be free of paradox and contradiction. He also proposes a treatment of consciousness requiring nothing beyond brain function. He is a board member of the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society , a scientific society for the philosophical study of nature and the physical universe.
