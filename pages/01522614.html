Charles Chandler Residence Baltimore, MD, United States Known for Philosophy , Astrophysics , Electromagnetism Scientific career Fields Software Developer Charles L. Chandler is an American independent researcher and software developer based in the Baltimore, Maryland area. Largely self-taught in physics, he develops and publishes theories on astrophysics , geophysics and atomic structure that emphasize the role of electromagnetic forces, working outside the mainstream scientific community and within the Electric Universe and plasma-cosmology milieu. Biography Chandler describes having dedicated himself to philosophy at the age of 16. Before turning to science, he spent about five years in the merchant marines and roughly ten years as a carpenter, after which he moved into computing. Starting as a CNC operator, he taught himself programming, AutoCAD and AutoLISP, and worked in desktop and application support, eventually holding positions as a help-desk manager, network operations manager and CAD developer. His shift toward physics followed an F4 tornado that struck Southern Maryland on 28 April 2002. Seeking to understand the storm, he studied meteorology part-time and later left his career to work on tornado theory full-time, concluding that tornadoes arise from a combination of fluid dynamics and electromagnetism. He subsequently broadened his approach to astrophysics and geophysics, posting his work to Internet forums and revising it in response to critics. Chandler maintains a personal website and the online "Quick Disclosure Lite" (QDL) discussion platform hosted at scs-inc.us, where he compiles his writings. Chandler's interest in the Electric Universe grew out of reading on Immanuel Velikovsky around 2000, which led him to the work of authors such as David Talbott and Wal Thornhill .
