Title The Basic Space-Time Equation of the Universe (Cosmic Dynamics) Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Peter Kohut Keywords space-time, universe, quantum Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 5 Pages 260-264 Abstract The elementary building unit of the Universe - quantum connection of opposite poles (quantum dipole) represents the elementary quantum of space. The Universe pulsates thanks to its dialectic contradiction between attraction and repulsion. The cosmic expansion is caused by systematic expelling of new quantum dipoles accompanied with the transition of the Universe from one quantum state to the next by elementary quantum jump, which represents the elementary quantum of time. This fact can be expressed by simple space-time equation of the Universe, which has important consequences and enables to calculate such cosmic characteristics like mass and energy of the Universe, its volume, average density, radius of cosmic curvature and others. Overview Peter Kohut builds a cosmology from a single combinatorial postulate. The universe consists of poles, positive and negative, and every positive pole is connected to every negative one; each connection is a "quantum dipole" and constitutes an elementary quantum of space. Expansion proceeds by expelling new pole pairs, and each such expulsion is an elementary quantum of time. The arithmetic follows immediately. In symmetric state k there are k positive and k negative poles, hence k ² connections, so the volume goes as the square of the elapsed time: V k = k ², and in dimensional form V = zt ². This is what Kohut calls the basic quantum space-time equation of the universe, and the rest of the paper works out its consequences: the Hubble parameter, the age, the relation between the speed of light and the rate of cosmic expansion, the total mass and energy, and the radius of curvature of a universe treated as the three-dimensional surface of a four-dimensional sphere.
