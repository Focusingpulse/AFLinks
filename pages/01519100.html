Title A Few Notes on Gyroscopes and Antigravity Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Mario Ludovico Keywords Gyroscope, plenum, velocity circulation, electron/coil magnetic fields Published 2011 No. of pages 9 Abstract Classic Mechanics does not provide explanation for certain lifting and/or lowering effects observed in spinning gyroscopes. A possible interpretation might rely upon the hypothesis of velocity/acceleration fields associated with vortices of cosmic plenum and relevant velocity circulations. In an analogy, the magnetic field created by a coil conducting continuous electrical current might also be viewed as a special gyroscopic behavior of the plenum involved. Overview Mario Ludovico argues that classical mechanics cannot account for the lifting and lowering effects observers report in spinning gyroscopes, and proposes that these arise from the interaction between the spinning body and the plenum — the continuous fluid substance he takes physical space to be, developed at length in his book Vacuum, Vortices and Gravitation . A spinning disc drags the local plenum into circulation, and that circulation either opposes or reinforces the pre-existing gravitational vortex, changing the effective weight. The evidence assembled is threefold. Eric Laithwaite's 1974 Royal Institution demonstration, in which an 8 kg flywheel on a 2.7 kg shaft was lifted with one finger, implying a loss of at least 90% of the system's weight. The 1989 report by Hayasaka and Takeuchi in Physical Review Letters of a weight reduction of about one part in 7,000 for clockwise-spinning gyroscopes. And more recent videos by the engineer William Alek showing a gyroscope pulled upward when spun one way and pushed down when spun the other. The paper's most useful contribution is a piece of ordinary mechanics.
