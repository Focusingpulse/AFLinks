A Novel Interpretation of the Ideal Gas Law and Its Relation to Black Body Radiation Using the Aether Physics Model Introduction The Ideal Gas Law, a cornerstone of thermodynamics, has long been used to describe the behavior of gases under various conditions (Çengel & Boles, 2015). However, the advent of quantum mechanics and the development of alternative physical models have opened new avenues for reinterpreting this fundamental law. This paper presents a novel approach to understanding the Ideal Gas Law through the lens of the Aether Physics Model (APM) and its associated Quantum Measurement Units (QMU). The APM developed as an alternative to standard quantum mechanics, proposes a structured, quantized Aether as the fundamental fabric of the universe. This model offers a geometric, intuitive approach to understanding physical phenomena, potentially bridging the gap between classical and quantum physics. We argue that reformulating the Ideal Gas Law using QMU can give us new insights into its underlying principles and connections to other phenomena, such as Black Body Radiation. This approach provides a more fundamental understanding of these phenomena and suggests a unifying framework for seemingly disparate physical processes. Traditional Ideal Gas Law The Ideal Gas Law is traditionally expressed as: PV = nRT Where: ● P is pressure ● V is volume ● n is the number of moles of gas ● ● R is the gas constant (8.314 J/(mol·K)) (NIST CODATA, 2022) T is the temperature in Kelvin This formulation has remarkably successfully predicted gas behavior under various conditions. However, it obscures some fundamental physical relationships, particularly regarding the gas's mass.
