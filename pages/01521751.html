Title The Long History of the Mass-Energy Relation Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Hartwig Wolfgang Thim Keywords E = mc² Published 2006 No. of pages 4 Overview The Long History of the Mass-Energy Relation is a four-page talk by Hartwig Wolfgang Thim of the Microelectronics Institute, Johannes Kepler University Linz, given at the 2006 annual meeting of the Austrian Physical Society in Graz and printed in the proceedings of the European Physical Society's History of Physics Group. Its thesis is a priority claim: that "the discovery of the mass-energy-relation should not be ascribed to a single person but rather to a group of scientists including Poincaré, Abraham, Hasenöhrl, Kaufmann, Einstein und Planck", and that "this new view should be adopted by all Physical Societies". The argument proceeds in four parts. The pre-1905 work. Poincaré (1900) and Abraham (1903–04) introduced S/c as the energy density of radiation and S/c² as its momentum density; Kaufmann (1902) measured the velocity dependence of the electron's mass; Hasenöhrl (1904) obtained E = (3/8)Mc² for a cavity filled with radiation, "after some corrections" E = (3/4)Mc². A derivation from the Poynting vector. Thim shows that a plate of mass M totally absorbing radiation gains mass and velocity together, yielding M = M 0 /√(1−v²/c²) and, on integration, W = M 0 c² + ½M 0 v² + (3/8)M 0 v 4 /c² + … An ether interpretation. He argues, following Hasenöhrl, that the v in these equations is "an absolute rather than a relative velocity", requiring a preferred frame. He then asserts that Penzias and Wilson (1965), Smoot and colleagues (1977) and Marinov (1979) have "falsified experimentally" the isotropy condition x²+y²+z²−c²t² = x′²+y′²+z′²−c²t′² = 0 on which the Lorentz transformation rests, and that Marinov's "coupled mirrors experiment" was "the first experimental falsification of special relativity in 1975".
