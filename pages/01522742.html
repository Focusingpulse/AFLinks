David Talbott Born ( 1942-10-28 ) October 28, 1942 (age 83) Oregon, United States Residence Portland, OR, United States Nationality USA Known for Solar System , Cosmology , Electric Universe , The Saturn Model Scientific career Fields Comparative Mythology , Author , Founder of the Thunderbolts Project David N. Talbott (born October 28, 1942) is an American self-taught comparative mythologist whose work offers a radical new vantage point on the origin of ancient cultural themes, myths and symbols. Working in the tradition of Immanuel Velikovsky , his research has been the primary catalyst behind the "Saturn Model" of recent planetary history, and is the subject of the feature documentary Remembering the End of the World . He is the author of The Saturn Myth and co-author (with Wallace Thornhill ) of Thunderbolts of the Gods and The Electric Universe . Talbott is the founder and director of The Thunderbolts Project . Education B.S. in education and political science — Portland State University Talbott was raised in Oregon. Scientific History Talbott's interest in ancient catastrophe grew out of the controversy surrounding Immanuel Velikovsky , author of Worlds in Collision . In 1970, together with his brother Stephen L. Talbott as editor, he revived the student magazine Pensée . Late in 1971 the brothers decided to devote a feature issue to Velikovsky's ideas; the response was so great that it grew, between 1972 and 1974, into a landmark ten-issue series titled Immanuel Velikovsky Reconsidered , reaching a circulation of between 10,000 and 20,000 subscribers and bringing renewed scholarly attention to Velikovsky's work. Beginning in 1971, and motivated in part by Velikovsky's then-unpublished manuscript In the Beginning , Talbott set out to test the Velikovskian thesis through the systematic comparison of the world's oldest myths, symbols and sacred images.
