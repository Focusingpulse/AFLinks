Travers at the Marine Trust Co. It’s brought out from time to time and carried to a meeting, where it is opened and referred to and then closed up again. The folder is neatly labeled: “Adams Plant Project.” It tells a story. The first entry is a list of 22 industrialists or businessmen organized in 1958. Most of them headed their respective firms at that time. Some still do. Others have died or moved out of the city. Committee Organized They were brought together in June 1958 to serve on a committee organized to save the historic Adams Plant, where the first alternating current electricity was produced on a mass basis in the United States. The committee was organized by the late James G. Marshall, 88-year-old retired Electro Metallurgical Co. Works manager. Mr. Marshall, cognizant that the plant would be taken out of service when the Niagara power project became operative, also feared that the site which gave Niagara Falls the “Power City of the World” title would be doomed. Mr. Marshall died several years ago. But his idea is still being kept alive by a committee. Mr. Travers still heads it.
