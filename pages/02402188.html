GTM2026-main048 Discrete-time pursuit in a reach-avoid game Segrei V. Ivanov1 1 St. Petersburg Branch of the V. A. Steklov Mathematical Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences svivanov@pdmi.ras.ru We consider a classical reach-avoid differential game (also known as "lifeline game") where a single evader E plays against a team of pursuers. The evader aims to reach a fixed goal area G before being captured, or at least approach G as close as possible. Pursuers are equipped with capture radii and the capture occurs if the distance between E and one of the pursuers becomes smaller than the respective radius. All players move freely in a convex region F of the plane or space subject to certain speed bounds, and we assume that the maximum speed of the evader is no greater than those of pursuers. It is known that under these assumptions the evader can win if and only if its naturally defined dominance region D has a nonempty intersection with the goal area G. If the intersection is empty, pursuers can win by means of an optimal strategy developed by Dutkevich and Petrosyan. However this strategy is not positional as the pursuer velocities are determined by both the position and velocity of E. If we assume in addition that G is convex, then there is a positional strategy where pursuers move towards the point of D nearest to G. This strategy was justified by Isaacs in the case of one pursuer and G being a half-plane. We generalize the Isaacs strategy to the case of several pursuers and arbitrary convex sets F and G, and show that it remains optimal even if pursuers check evader
