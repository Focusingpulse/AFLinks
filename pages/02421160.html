Perpetual Motion: The History of an Obsession Book Author Arthur W J G Ord-Hume Language English Subject History of technology; perpetual motion Published 1977; reprinted 2006 Publisher George Allen & Unwin, London (1977); Adventures Unlimited Press (2006) Pages 235 ISBN 9780046210243 Perpetual Motion: The History of an Obsession is a history of perpetual-motion machines and the people who built them, by the English mechanical and aeronautical engineer Arthur W J G Ord-Hume . First published by George Allen & Unwin in London in 1977 , with 235 pages and 95 illustrations, it has stayed in print through several reissues and is generally treated as the standard work on its subject. It is an unusual entry in this collection, because it is a sceptical book. Ord-Hume's subject is a four-century record of failure, and his thesis is that the failures mattered: "without their early experiments the science of mechanics would not have developed so rapidly." The argument The book takes the proposition at its plainest — "make a machine which gives out more work than the energy you put into it and you have perpetual motion" — and follows its pursuit from the medieval overbalancing wheel to the twentieth century. Two threads run through it. The first is how the impossibility was established : the attempts, and the increasingly precise reasons for their failure, are part of how mechanics arrived at the conservation laws. The inventors were, in Ord-Hume's account, the unwitting experimental arm of a science that had not yet been formulated, and the basic tenets of engineering grew out of their defeats . The second is the character of the pursuit, which the subtitle names. Ord-Hume is interested in why the problem grips people — and he distinguishes carefully between the self-deceived, the merely mistaken, and the outright frauds , of whom there were many.
