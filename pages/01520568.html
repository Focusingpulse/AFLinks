Title Magnetic Repulsion and the Gyroscopic Force Read in full Link to paper Author(s) David Tombe Published 2014 Abstract The counterintuitive gravity defying behaviour that is exhibited by a pivoted gyroscope suggests the involvement of an active spin-induced force, similar in nature to the magnetic force, F = qv×B. The phenomenon of gyroscopic stability exhibits a strong spin-induced reactance which cannot be accounted for by the moment of inertia alone. The physical connection between the inertial forces and magnetic repulsion will be investigated. Overview David Tombe argues that the behaviour of a spinning top is not explained by the standard treatment, and that what actually holds it up is a real physical force of the same family as the magnetic force F = q v × B . From there he builds outward: the Coriolis force and centrifugal force are real forces acting in inertial frames, not artefacts of a rotating frame of reference; the rattleback's spin reversal demonstrates it; and all three — magnetic repulsion, Coriolis and centrifugal — have a common cause in Maxwell 's sea of molecular vortices filling space. The chain of reasoning is compact. A charged particle falling through a horizontal magnetic field loops sideways instead of dropping, and falls straight down if its sideways motion is blocked. A spinning top precesses instead of toppling, and topples if its precession is blocked. The parallel, Tombe argues, is too close to be coincidence: "This suggests that there is an active spin-induced force at play, similar in nature to the magnetic force." The paper's closing sections set out the mechanism. Space is filled with tiny aether vortices which press outward against one another; whenever motion makes that pressure asymmetric, the imbalance appears at large scale as a "compound centrifugal force", which is what we call Coriolis force in mechanics and magnetic force in electromagnetism.
