Did Einstein Get it Wrong? Book Author Michael J Kelly Language English Subject Aether , Relativity , gravitation Published 2006 Publisher Trafford Publishing Pages 80 ISBN 1412029015 Did Einstein Get it Wrong? is a 2006 book by Michael J Kelly in which the author argues that the aether is real, that Einstein's abandonment of it was a mistake with serious consequences for the special theory of relativity , and that a medium filling space can be made to account for mass, for gravitation and for the wave-particle duality of light and matter. It is a short book of eighty pages, self-published through Trafford Publishing and written for a general readership rather than for specialists. Summary Kelly's starting point is that the nineteenth-century aether was discarded too readily. In his account the null result of the Michelson-Morley experiment was taken as proof that no medium exists, when what it actually showed was that the particular kind of medium being looked for — a stationary aether through which the Earth ploughs unimpeded — was the wrong one. Kelly offers his own explanation of why that experiment failed to register a drift, and argues that once the failure is understood the case for removing the medium from physics collapses. Einstein, on his reading, then built relativity on the absence of something that was in fact there, and the repercussions run through the whole of the special theory. In place of the discarded medium Kelly proposes that space is pervaded by antimatter, and he develops the consequences from two stated axioms: that matter attracts antimatter, and that antimatter repels itself.
