Introduction The flint hill series is the elementary series of the form X∞ n=1 1 (sin2 n)n3 . The series seems to have gained much popularity in part due to the work of Pickover (See [1]), who studied and questioned its convergence. It is unknown whether the flint hill series converges or diverges and it is an open problem to determine the state of convergence of such series. As with most open problems finding a solution almost reduces to finding possible connections of the existing problem to other problems that seems quite amenable to current tools. For a fixed positive real number x, we call the smallest value of m such that 0 < x − p q < 1 qm the irrationality measure of x if the inequality holds only for a finite number of co-prime positive integers p, q. Alekseyev has shown that (See [2]) the convergence of the flint hill series is equivalent to asserting the irrationality measure of π as µ(π) ≤ 2.5. Nonetheless studying the convergence of the flint hill series via this approach can be quite hard, since establishing the irrationality measure µ(π) ≤ 2.5 is a terribly hard problem in its own right.
