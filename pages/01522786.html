Dissident Science Live News and Discussion with David de Hilster Summary A Dissident Science Live broadcast — David de Hilster alone, working through recent articles and answering the chat for two hours. He was joined by a hostile commenter he declined to engage and by several viewers who asked real questions, and the session is essentially a tour of his positions with the evidence he keeps to hand. On a black hole paper The first article was a study of marginally outer trapped surfaces merging inside a binary black hole, from institutes he pointedly listed by name — the naming of institutions being, in his reading, how such papers establish that the authors are intellectuals rather than critical thinkers. His objection was to the whole enterprise rather than the mathematics. Nobody knows what a black hole is: the object rests on space-time, which is given a physicality it does not have by calling it a fabric or a foam, and which terminates in an infinite density at a point. He accepts that there are very dense objects — something evidently sits at the centre of the galaxy — and would call them dark dense objects, perhaps nucleons without orbitals, until anyone can say more. To discuss the surfaces of such things, he said, is to study the migration patterns and digestive systems of unicorns; the rules can be internally consistent, as the rules of a video game are, without the object existing. He noted that jets were observed coming out of things nothing could escape from, and that Hawking after thirty years reported that they were not much like what he had first described.
