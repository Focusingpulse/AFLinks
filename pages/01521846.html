Title The Power of URL to Delineate the Cosmos Author(s) Satya Pal Asija Keywords Universal Relationship Law (URL), cosmos, gravitation, absolute space, special relativity , Aristotle, Galileo, Newton , Einstein Published 2010 "The Power of URL to Delineate the Cosmos" is a 2010 conference paper and presentation by Satya Pal Asija , delivered in the Natural Philosophy Alliance setting. In it Asija introduces what he calls the "URL" — the Universal Relationship Law — and argues that it is "comparable in scope to Newton's UGL (Universal Law of Gravitation)" as a tool for understanding the universe. The title is one of Asija's characteristic puns . "URL" is borrowed from the web address (" as my URL is still the same OurPal.com ", he notes elsewhere), but here it stands for his Universal Relationship Law , part of the larger philosophical system he calls One Reality . What the "URL" is In Asija's scheme the Universal Relationship Law rests on the existence of an absolute space and a preferred state of motion. He defines a quantity V n — a "Normal Earth Velocity in absolute space", which he puts at about 0.00125 c — and divides all motion into two regimes: Range I — velocities from 0 up to V n Range II — velocities from V n up to the speed of light c He proposes simplified formulas for the Lorentz gamma factor in each range, and treats the URL as a "ratio of ratios" correction that, he claims, resolves what he regards as an internal inconsistency in special relativity between length contraction and mass increase. The central argument The body of the paper is a matrix of verdicts on who was right and who was wrong about how bodies of different mass move and fall, evaluated separately in the two velocity ranges. In compressed form, Asija's claims are: In Range II (from V n to c ), Aristotle and Einstein are both wrong , Galileo "and everybody to date" also wrong, and the URL correction applies.
