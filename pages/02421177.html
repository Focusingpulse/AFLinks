Haralick (born 1943) is an American computer scientist, Distinguished Professor of Computer Science at the Graduate Center of the City University of New York, and one of the founding figures of computer vision, pattern recognition and image analysis. Alongside a long and highly decorated mainstream academic career, he has since the mid-2010s pursued an independent experimental programme on the properties of water — including magnetically and frequency-treated water and what he terms subtle energy — which he has presented at the Tesla Tech ExtraOrdinary Technology Conference, at the Energy Science and Technology Conference, and at the Conference on the Physics, Chemistry and Biology of Water. Education B.A. in mathematics — University of Kansas, 1964 B.S. in electrical engineering — University of Kansas, 1966 M.S. in electrical engineering — University of Kansas, 1967 Biography Haralick was born in 1943 in Brooklyn, New York. He completed his Ph.D. at the University of Kansas in 1969 and joined the faculty of its electrical engineering department, serving as professor there from 1975 to 1978. In 1979 he moved to the electrical engineering department at Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University, where he was a professor and director of the Spatial Data Analysis Laboratory.
