"Every hero is a Samson. The strong man succumbs to the intrigues of the weak and the many; and if in the end he loses all patience he crushes both them and himself. Or he is like Gulliver at Lilliput, overwhelmed by an enormous number of little men." [ Arthur Schopenhauer ] Hero - The Inventor Sometime in the first century AD, a man in Alexandria sat down and wrote instructions for building a device that would spin a hollow bronze sphere using steam. He described it carefully, drew diagrams, and filed it in a book alongside seventy-seven other inventions. Then he moved on to the next thing. His name was Hero, and the next thing was a vending machine. It dispensed holy water. A worshipper inserted a coin into a slot at the top of a bronze box. The coin landed on a small pan attached to a lever. The weight of the coin tilted the lever, which opened a valve, which released a measured amount of water. When the coin slid off the pan, the lever returned to its original position and the flow stopped. Hero had thought through the mechanism precisely enough that the dispensed amount was consistent regardless of how long it took the coin to fall. He built it for temples, where worshippers needed water for ritual purification and priests needed a reliable way to manage the supply. This is the same man who invented automatic temple doors.
