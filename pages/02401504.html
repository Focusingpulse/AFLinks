GC2026-STP036 Behaviour of “substorm current wedge” in course of the substorm explosive phase Oleg Troshichev1 , Nikita Stepanov1 1 Arctic and Antarctic Research Institute olegtro@aari.ru Active (expansive) phase of magnetic substorms is characterized by explosive development of the westward electrojet (DP11 magnetic disturbances) in the midnight auroral zone. The westward electrojet formation is promoted by the “substorm current wedge” (SCW) − strong field-aligned currents, which flow into the ionosphere in the after-midnight sector and flowing out of ionosphere in the pre-midnight sector of the auroral zone. It is reckoned that the substorm current wedge provides closure of the magnetotail neutral sheet currents through auroral ionosphere when the neutral current sheet comes to the crucial level of instability. Analysis of the SCW location and its longitudinal displacements in course of substorms was carried out with use of SML indices, which present intensity of negative magnetic disturbances in each 2-hour MLT interval at latitudes from 55° to 85° CGLat (available at SuperMAG database). Behavior of the 1-min SML indices has been examined in the night (20- 22, 22-24, 24-00, 00-02, 02-04 MLT) and in the morning (04-06, 06-08, 08-10 MLT) sectors for 147 magnetic substorms with intensity from 400 nT to 1600 nT observed in 1998-2016. Results of analysis have demonstrated that the substorm sudden onset (SO) can occur either in a single MLT sector (usually before midnight) or in several MLT sectors (up to the extent of 20 −04 MLT).
