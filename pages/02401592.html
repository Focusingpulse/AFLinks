GC2026-STP068 Decomposing the Hall term contribution to magnetic field evolution using a specialized induction equation solver Igor Paramonik1 , Andrey Divin1 , Vladimir Semenov1 1 St Petersburg University igorparamonik@gmail.com Expansion of plasma into an ambient magnetic field is a fundamental problem in astrophysics, space plasma physics, and magnetospheric physics. Examples of such events occurring at different scales include supernova explosions, coronal mass ejections, jets, stellar and solar wind outflows, cometary coma formation, as well as ions releases in active experiments in the Earth's magnetosphere (AMPTE, CRRES) and micro-impact events caused by dust particles striking spacecraft. A simple and classical formulation of the problem is the spherically symmetric expansion of a plasma cloud into a uniform ambient magnetic field, displacing it and forming a diamagnetic cavity, which has been studied for several decades [1]. One important unresolved question is the mechanism of the rapid collapse of the diamagnetic cavity during sub￾Alfvénic expansion of a plasma cloud in the regime of unmagnetized ions, which occurs faster than predicted by classical magnetic field diffusion.
