Sympathetic Vibratory Physics

· archived # 2,430,882
Robert MacDougall is a Canadian-based historian of science, technology, communication, and American history. He is Department Chair and Associate Professor in the Department of History at Western University (University of Western Ontario) in London, Ontario. He received his PhD in History from Harvard University in 2004. MacDougall's research focuses primarily on the United States in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries, with particular attention to the ways information, communication, science, and technology interact with political, economic, and cultural change. He is the author of The People’s Network: The Political Economy of the Telephone in the Gilded Age (University of Pennsylvania Press, 2014), a study of the independent telephone movement in the United States and Canada and its struggle with the expanding Bell System. The book received the 2016 Albert B. Corey Prize, awarded jointly by the American Historical Association and the Canadian Historical Association. MacDougall has also conducted extensive historical research into nineteenth-century scientific controversy, pseudoscience, thermodynamics, and attempts to develop so-called perpetual-motion machines. A major focus of this work has been Philadelphia inventor John Worrell Keely and the Keely Motor. His article, “Sympathetic Physics: The Keely Motor and the Laws of Thermodynamics in Nineteenth-Century Culture,” was published in Technology and Culture in 2019 and received the Society for the History of Technology’s 2022 Abbott Payson Usher Prize. MacDougall describes his continuing Keely research as part of a broader cultural history of thermodynamics and the nineteenth-century dream of perpetual motion.
