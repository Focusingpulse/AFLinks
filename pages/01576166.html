China, 300401 *Corresponding E-mail: zhangbing970641@qq.com; azhjia@hebut.edu.cn Abstract We propose a unified framework in which Newton's gravitational constant emerges from the coherent collective oscillations of a two-component glueball condensate under ambient conditions — room temperature, atmospheric pressure, and weak terrestrial gravity. The condensate comprises 75\% glueballs (mass 1.5~GeV) and 25\% glueballs (mass 2.0~GeV), with a Hausdorff fractal dimension serving simultaneously as the coarse-graining weight. By replacing the Boltzmann collision term with a Gross–Pitaevskii-type (GPE) oscillating momentum flux, we construct a three-tier coupling from microscopic quantum bound states through mesoscopic hydrodynamics to macroscopic weak-field Einstein equations. The effective gravitational constant acquires a density￾dependent correction , where and are the glueball and baryon number densities, respectively. Three laboratory￾accessible targeted experimental signatures are derived: (1) anomalous power-law scaling of underground gravitational gradients, (2) non-local correlations in torsion-pendulum measurements, and (3) modified ultra-cold neutron (UCN) storage lifetimes. The construction deliberately excludes QCD phase transitions, quark mixing, and classical hard-sphere assumptions as unnecessary ingredients in the ambient regime; this approach is complementary to, rather than in competition with, cosmological scenarios that invoke QCD phase transitions.
