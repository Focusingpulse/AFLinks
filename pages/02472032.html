The value of the description in question is much enhanced by the numerous illustrations in which Mr. Tesla himself appears very frequently as operating or supporting the various luminous bodies. The article is chiefly devoted to vacuum-tube lighting, and in the first illustration a bulb 12in. in diameter is shown held in the hand of its inventor, in which a light equal to 1,500 c.p. is said to be generated by the vacuum bulb being held in front of a coil of four turns through which a condenser is discharging. Mr. Tesla adds that "such a powerfully energised coil, when the frequency, as in this instance, is measured in millions per second, shows little repellent action, but when the frequency of the impulses is low, closed conductors, as washers of conducting material, are thrown off with a force of a magnitude which can be only explained on the assumption that the currents have maximum values of many hundred thousand amperes."
