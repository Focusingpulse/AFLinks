On Search For The Largest Prime Island Marcin Barylski Created: October 4, 2024 The last update: July 26, 2025 Abstract There are several interesting ways to depict distribution of primes like Ulam Spiral [1], Klauber Triangle [2] or the Sacks Number Spiral [3]. In all cases, Prime Number Theo￾rem describes the asymptotic distribution of such numbers among the positive integers. This work is devoted to illus￾tration of primes of form p × q ± C in a way that allows to search for clusters (so called islands of primes). The direct goal of this experimental work is to locate islands with the largest surface area and potentially discover some further patterns in distribution of primes. 1 Introduction Work presented in this paper consists of three major parts: illustration of primes with detection of interest￾ing clusters, formulation of patterns and examination of correctness of formulated patterns in spirit of finding new primes. Experiments are planned against various sequences of form p×q±C, where p and q are integer variables (p, q>0) and C is constant. Primes in the sequences are depicted as a non-zero values in X-Y graph, creating a single tile of coordinates (p, q). If two tiles are adjacent to each other on both sides, they form an island of size 2. Discovery of big islands (at least of size 4) is the first goal of this work, depicting co-located primes. For clarity, the following definitions are in charge in this work: Definition 1. A prime tile is a location of coordinates (x, y) on X-Y graph with prime tile value. Primality of tile is result of a function: isprime(F(x, y, C)) where F() is a function of form x × y ± C. Definition 2. A prime island is an area composed of at least two prime tiles that have a common side. Lemma 3. Formula p × q + C does not produce prime islands if C is even and positive (p, q>1). Proof.
