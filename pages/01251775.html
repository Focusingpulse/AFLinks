New formulation of equations of compressible fluids on analogy of Maxwells equations - FDR2010

Res. 42 (2010)‡ New formulation of equations of compressible fluids on analogy of Maxwell’s equations Tsutomu Kambe Higashi-yama 2-11-3, Meguro-ku, Tokyo, Japan 153-0043 E-mail: kambe@ruby.dti.ne.jp Received 27 february 2009, in final form 12 April 2010 Communicated by E Knobloch Abstract. A compressible ideal fluid is governed by Euler’s equation of motion and equations of continuity, entropy and vorticity. This system can be reformulated in a form analogous to that of electromagnetism governed by Maxwell’s equations with source terms. The vorticity plays the role of magnetic field, while the velocity field plays the part of a vector potential and the enthalpy (of isentropic flows) plays the part of a scalar potential in the electromagnetism. Evolution of source terms of fluid Maxwell equations are determined by solving the equations of motion and continuity. Equation of sound waves can be derived from this formulation, where time evolution of sound source is determined by the equation of motion. Theory of vortex sound of the aeroacoustics is included in this formulation. It is remarkable that the forces acting on a point mass moving in a velocity field of an inviscid fluid are analogous in their form to the electric force and Lorentz force in the electromagnetism. Significance of the reformulation is interpreted by examples taken from fluid mechanics. This formulation can be extended to viscous fluids without difficulty. The Maxwell-type equations are unchanged by viscosity effect, although the source terms have additional terms due to viscosities. ‡ ⃝c 2010 The Japan Society of Fluid Mechanics and IOP Publishing Ltd Printed in the UK 2 1.
