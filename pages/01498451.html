Johnson Chemistry & Materials Branch, Research & Technology Division Naval Air Warfare Center Weapons Division China Lake, CA 93555-6100, USA * and M. Ashraf Imam Physical Metallurgy Branch, Materials Science & Technology Division Naval Research Laboratory Washington, DC 20375-5320, USA Abstract Excess power production and other anomalous effects have been observed during the electrolysis of heavy water using palladium and palladium-boron alloys as the cathode materials. This study focused on hydrogen and deuterium loading into palladium and palladium-boron alloys. Improved calorimetry provided for the detection of the exothermic heat of absorption of deuterium into palladium and palladium-alloy cathodes. The addition of boron to palladium does not significantly affect the initial loading rate but slows further loading to higher levels. The presence of boron in the palladium significantly slows the rate of the deloading process. Cracks or other surface defects prevent high loading levels of hydrogen or deuterium into palladium or palladium-boron alloys. 1.
