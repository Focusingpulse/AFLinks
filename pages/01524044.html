Title The Centrifugal Force Paradox Read in full Link to paper Author(s) David Tombe Keywords centrifugal force , paradox, gravitational field, magnetic field, inertia Published 2011 Journal General Science Journal No. of pages 4 Abstract It is commonly taught nowadays that centrifugal force doesn't exist, except as a fictitious force that is only observable from a rotating frame of reference. This belief is based on Newton's law of inertia which states that a body undergoing straight line motion at constant speed experiences no net force, and that curved path motion involves only a centripetal force. However, if we split the net zero force of straight line constant speed motion into polar components, we find that one of these is a centrifugal force component which can physically react with constraints, hence revealing an underlying pressure associated with inertia. Further evidence that centrifugal force is a real physical force arises when inertial pressure becomes asymmetrical, as happens in a radial gravitational field or in a solenoidal magnetic field. Overview The Centrifugal Force Paradox is a four-page paper by David Tombe , dated 27 March 2011 and published in the General Science Journal . It argues that centrifugal force is a real physical force rather than a fictitious one, and that both of the standard textbook accounts of it are wrong. The paper opens with the case of straight-line motion at constant speed viewed from an off-axis origin. Relative to such an origin "we will observe an acceleration that is directed radially outwards… This acceleration is the centrifugal acceleration which can be shown to obey an inverse cube law with respect to the radial distance from the origin." Tombe notes that a counteracting term associated with the rotation of the radius vector brings the resultant to zero, which is why some regard the outward term as fictitious.
