Title The "Thought Experiment" with Two Observers Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Time Dilation , Autodynamics , proper time, thought experiment, light clock Published 2004 Website https://www.autodynamics.org/two-observers/ "The 'Thought Experiment' with Two Observers" , dated June 2004, is a white paper by Ricardo L Carezani attacking the standard textbook derivation of time dilation — the light-clock argument with one observer on a sidewalk and another on a moving truck. The version he analyses is taken from Franklin Miller's College Physics , fifth edition (1982), page 667. Overview The textbook argument is reproduced in full before it is criticised. Boy A on the sidewalk flashes a light at boy B on a passing truck; the light reflects from B 's mirror and returns. A measures a round trip of 2 a / c , the proper time. B , who regards himself as stationary and A as moving backwards, sees the light travel a longer, diagonal path, and by Pythagoras obtains a longer interval — from which the factor γ follows. Carezani's objection is that the two figures do not describe the same experiment, and that the geometry in the second one is wrong. The argument The geometrical objection The core claim is about aiming. "It is not true that B , in case (b), sees the flashlight send the flashlight pulse in the ARROW direction. The light will not reach boy B ." To hit a moving target, A must aim ahead of it, with an angular deviation. B therefore sees light arriving at an angle, and an angle is all he can measure. From this Carezani draws his conclusion: the longer interval is a longer path , not a stretched time.
