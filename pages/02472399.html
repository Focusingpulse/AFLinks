State Officials Pleased at the Result — Electricity Will Take the Place of Steam and Mules Hereafter. Rochester, Nov. 25. — Electric propulsion for boats upon the Erie canal has been shown to be practicable. The trolley wires are stretched along nine-tenths of a mile on a portion of the canal with several sharp turns, and this section presents one of the severest tests. The boat used in the test Saturday was a full sized canal boat called Frank W. Hawley, and is named after the vice president of the Cataract General Electric Company, under whose supervision and direction the first application of electric power on the canal is made. It uses the old style dishpan propeller wheels, which are not nearly as efficient as the present style of screw wheel used on the canal steamers. It is contemplated, if electric power on the canal is adopted, to have but one motor on each boat which will take up a space about four feet square. This will insure quite a saving in space. The boat is fitted with two motors of the standard street railway company type, each of 25 horse power, with the standard Westinghouse series multiple controller placed in the stern of the boat.
