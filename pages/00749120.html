Double serial operators theory. Juan Elias Millas Vera juanmillaszgz@gmail.com Zaragoza (Spain) September 2021 0- Abstract: This paper is an advance of my work in serial operators. Here you can see 216 theoretical combinations of two serial operators with a 2-variable operation. The theory covers addition, subtraction, product, division, power and root. Moreover I am going to present 48 numerical examples. 1- Introduction: Based in my paper “Resume of the serial operators theory” [1], in which I put the examples of the six combinations of serial operations, this idea is, as I presented in the conclusions of that paper, the next step in the structure of a theory of calculus of operations. Doubling the variables we can go further of the main idea, and if we double the variables we also double the operator. I am going to use uppercase Greek letters in the corresponding operators, I am going to use sigma for summation, rho for restory, pi for productory, delta for divisory, zeta for exponentory and dseda for rootory. 2- Theoretical combinations with examples in the easy ones: 2.1.1.1- Summation of summation of the sum: (1) Σ m= j Σ n=i i+ j Example: (2) 4 Σ m=2 5 Σ n=3 i+ j= 4 Σ m=2 (3+ j)+(4+ j)+(5+ j)=((3+2)+(4+2)+(5+2))+((3+3)+(4+3)+(5+3)) +((3+4)+(4+4)+(5+4))=(5+6+7)+(6+7+8)+(7+8+9)=18+21+24=63 2.1.1.2- Summation of summation of the rest: (3) Σ m=j Σ n=i i−j Example: (4) 4 Σ m=1 3 Σ n=1 i−j= 4 Σ m=1 (1− j)+(2−j)+(3− j)=((1−2)+(2−2)+(3−2)) +((1−3)+(2−3)+(3−3))+((1−4)+(2−4)+(3−4))=((−1)+0+1)+((−2)+(−1)+0) +((−3)+(−2)+(−1))=0−3−6=−9 2.1.1.3- Summation of summation of the product: (5) Σ m=j Σ n=i i⋅j Example: (6) 5 Σ m=3 2 Σ n=1 i⋅j= 5 Σ m=3 (1⋅j)+(2⋅j)=((1⋅3)+(2⋅3))+((1⋅4)+(2⋅4))+((1⋅5)+(2⋅5))=9+12+15=36 2.1.1.4- Summation of summation of the division: (7) Σ m=j Σ n=i i÷j Example: (8) 5 Σ m=3 4 Σ n=2 i÷j= 5 Σ m=3 (2÷ j)+(3÷j)+(4÷j)=((2÷3)+(3÷3)+(4÷3)) +((2÷4)+(3÷4)+(4÷4))+((2÷5)+(3÷5)+(4÷5))=( 2 3 +1+ 4 3 )+( 1 2 + 3 4 +1)+( 2 5 + 3 5 + 4 5 )= 141 20 2.1.1.5- Summation
