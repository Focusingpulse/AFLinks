Handbook of LEGUMES of Wbrld Economic Importance James A. Duke Handbook of LEGUMES of World Economic Importance James A. Duke United States Department of Agriculture Beltsville, Maryland This timely volume — the result of extensive computer searches combined with recent research and the au¬ thor’s own extensive knowledge — fills the need for a concise source of detailed information on nearly 150 economically important species of legumes. Each plant is listed under its scientific name with re¬ cent significant synonyms included. General uses of plants are covered. Folk medicine — often the first guide in the quest for new medicinal uses - is given a separate section. Plants discussed include some legumes containing compounds useful against ex¬ perimental cancers. Also listed are toxic plants. A section on germplasm covers the important cultivars, and offers comments on centers of origin, breeding mechanisms, tolerances, and chromosome counts. Among the nutritional data presented is a tabulation of nutrients on a zero-moisture basis and amino acid compositions - data which facilitate comparisons be¬ tween plants. Since modern distribution of many economically important legumes bears little relation to their historic distribution, a useful section on this subject summarizes facts about current and historic distribution. An ecology section lists data on life zones, annual precipitation, annual temperature, and pH, while a section on cultivation offers information encompassing land preparation, planting, fertilization, intercropping, cultivation, and maintenance of the legume until harvest — information intended for both developed “mechanized” societies and developing “manual” societies. Methods of harvesting are re¬ ported, with notes on processing the harvested plant, conditions for harvest, and data on optimum states of growth.
