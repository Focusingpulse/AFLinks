Consideration of the competing explanatory mathematical models for the MMX. Presented by James Arathoon, hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary James Arathoon 's subject was not the Michelson–Morley experiment itself so much as the century and a half of mathematics built to account for it — and the fact that, on his reading, none of it works. The session was dogged by connection failures on all sides, including one that took the speaker offline entirely for several minutes. Two families of theory He set the whole question inside an old division: Newton's absolute space and time against Leibniz's relationalism, in which everything is measured relative to observable matter points and unobservable space is not worth discussing. Fresnel's stationary ether, Stokes's dragged ether, Einstein's principle-based approach, Walter Ritz 's ballistic emission theory, and the modern absolute theories of Tangherlini, Franco Selleri and Horst Eckardt all fall on one side or the other. Einstein's method, he suggested, was borrowed from classical thermodynamics, which states impossibilities — no perpetual motion, hence energy conservation — and asserts by analogy that nothing about an underlying absolute space can be measured within an inertial frame. He was careful about which parts of the light postulate he accepts.
