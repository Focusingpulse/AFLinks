GC2026-STP034 Variations in geophysical characteristics associated with the solar Hale cycle and their possible contribution to bidecadal climatic oscillations Svetlana V. Veretenenko1 , Pavel B. Dmitriev1 , Vladimir N. Obridko2 1 Ioffe Institute, Russian Academy of Sciences, St Petersburg, Russia 2 IZMIRAN, Moscow, Russia svevereten@yandex.ru Bidecadal oscillations are observed in a large number of atmospheric characteristics (atmospheric pressure distribution, regional temperatures, precipitation, occurrence of droughts and hurricanes, etc.), which allows suggesting a possible connection between these oscillations and the Hale magnetic cycle on the Sun. In this work, we study the manifestations of the Hale cycle in different geophysical factors, which may be considered as connecting links between processes on the Sun and those in the Earth's atmosphere. It was found that ∼22-year variations associated with the Hale cycle are observed in the intensity of galactic cosmic ray fluxes entering the lower atmosphere, as well as in geoeffective parameters of solar wind (the Bz and By components of interplanetary magnetic field, solar wind speed) and indicators of geomagnetic activity (annual occurrences of magnetic storms, geomagnetic aa-indices). The greatest differences between temporal variations of the studied characteristics in even and odd solar cycles take place, as a rule, during the declining phase and the minimum of a cycle.
