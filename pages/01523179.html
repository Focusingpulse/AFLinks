Title Is There a Higgs' Boson? Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Athanassios A Nassikas Published 2010 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 7 No. of pages 3 Pages 343-345 Abstract In the near future on the basis of the CERN experiment it will be known whether the standard model is valid or not. This model requires the existence of Higgs' boson; if not detected a new model should be found. In this paper the model proposed is based on the Minimum Contradictions Physics while experimental and theoretical data as the ones related to Santilli's Etherino and a new proposal as well, can show that Higgs' boson is not necessary for the nature's function explanation. Basic element of this point of view is the interaction of the gravitational (g) with the electromagnetic (em) space-time. Overview Is There a Higgs' Boson? is a three-page paper by Athanassios A Nassikas of the Technological Institute of Larissa, Greece, presented at the 17th Natural Philosophy Alliance conference at Long Beach on 23 June 2010 and published in Proceedings of the NPA volume 7, pages 343–345. It was written in the window when the Large Hadron Collider had begun operating but had not yet reported, and it asks what should replace the Standard Model if the Higgs boson were not found. Nassikas's answer is drawn from his own framework, Minimum Contradictions Physics, set out at length in his 2008 book of that name. The framework rests on a methodological principle — "what includes the minimum possible contradictions is accepted as valid" — together with the claim that physics has minimum contradictions when founded only on classical logic, the principle of sufficient reason, and an "anterior-posterior axiom". From these he develops a Hypothetical Measuring Field, a Euclidean reference space-time onto which the real, deformed space-time is mapped, and a stochastic space-time whose properties are distributed according to the probability density of the relativistic Schrödinger equation.
