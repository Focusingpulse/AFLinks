GC2026-SGG038 Application of the Boundary Control Method for Solving the Inverse Problem of Signal Velocity Reconstruction for a One-Dimensional Wave Equation Vladimir S. Semenov1 , Igor V. Kubyshkin1 , Vasiliy A. Dragunov1 , Mikhail I. Belishev2 1 St Petersburg University 2 St. Petersburg Branch of the V. A. Steklov Mathematical Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences v.s.semenov@spbu.ru The Boundary Control (BC) method is one of the few methods for solving inverse problems that does not rely on any a priori information about the medium being studied. It turns out that many geophysical equations have the property of complete controllability. This means that any function in a domain can be approximated by waves generated at the boundary of this domain. This property allows for the development of efficient algorithms for reconstructing the velocity of the medium in a domain based on the inverse problem data at its boundary. To reconstruct the velocity in the medium to a certain depth corresponding to the wave travel time T, it is necessary to collect inverse problem data for a time of 2T, allowing the wave to reach the bottom, be reflected, and return. This paper provides details of the algorithms applied, using the Dirichlet and Neumann problems for a one￾dimensional wave equation as examples. The problems of wave basis formation, solving the resulting system of linear equations, and determining the signal velocity are discussed.
