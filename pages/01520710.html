Title New Energy News (March 2001) Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Patrick G Bailey Keywords New Energy Published 2001 Journal New Energy News Volume 8 Number 3 No. of pages 21 Abstract Overview New Energy News was the monthly newsletter of the Institute for New Energy (INE), edited by Patrick G Bailey and distributed by email to members in both plain ASCII and PDF form. This issue — Volume 8, Number 3, ISSN 1075-0045 — is not a research paper but a compilation: a president's editorial, a policy piece by Hal Fox , forwarded news items, a book review, two speculative articles, correspondence and a meetings calendar. It is of interest chiefly as a primary document of how the new-energy community understood its own position in early 2001. Two things give the issue its character. The first is the California electricity crisis, then at its height, which Bailey reads not as a shortage but as "a well-planned and executed business operation put into motion by a few people to make the most money in the shortest amount of time" — and, simultaneously, as the opening that would finally bring funding to alternative energy conversion research. The second is an insistence on measurement discipline that runs against the grain of the field: a substantial part of the editorial is devoted to explaining why many advertised over-unity devices are fraudulent, and how to tell. Contents of the issue President's Perspective: devices, fraud and funding Bailey's editorial surveys the roughly 130 devices then listed in the INE database, ranked by both scientific and commercial interest, and sorts their status into seven categories. Most are still at the replication stage and "are not ready". Some "will probably never work" but are kept for reference. Some are known frauds. Some are under proprietary agreements; some have been placed under Secrecy Orders by military review boards within the U.S. Patent Office; and some are simply hidden, awaiting funding.
