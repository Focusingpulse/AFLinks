Title Efficiency Author(s) Leslee A Kulba Keywords Efficiency, coefficient of performance, Carnot limit, power factor Published 2010 Journal Electric Spacecraft Journal Volume 48 Pages 12-15 Efficiency is a four-page article by Leslee A Kulba , published in 2010 in issue 48 of the Electric Spacecraft Journal , the journal's final double issue. It is the fourth part of the ten-part handbook on perpetual motion and its testing that Kulba, the journal's technical editor, contributed to that issue, and it follows immediately on " Conservation Laws ". The text of the article has not been recoverable, and this page describes its subject and its place in the series rather than its argument. The subject Efficiency is the ratio of useful output to total input, and the reason it earns four pages here — more than the conservation laws themselves — is that the ratio is much easier to get wrong than it looks. An over-unity claim is a claim that this number exceeds one. Most of the ways it can exceed one have nothing to do with new physics. Efficiency is not coefficient of performance The commonest confusion in the whole subject is between efficiency and coefficient of performance . A domestic heat pump delivers three or four joules of heat into a house for every joule of electricity it consumes, and its COP is therefore 3 or 4. This is not a violation of anything: the extra energy is not created but moved , pumped from the cold outside air into the warm inside, and the electricity pays only for the pumping. A device whose output is of a different kind from its input — heat moved versus work supplied — can have a ratio above one all day long. Several claimed over-unity devices are of exactly this character, drawing on an unmeasured ambient reservoir.
