Geoff Hunter Born ( 1933-01-01 ) January 1, 1933 Died December 18, 2018 (2018-12-18) (aged 85) Residence Malibu, CA, United States Nationality USA Known for Gravity , Autodynamics Scientific career Fields Mechanical Engineer Geoff Hunter (1933 – 18 December 2018) was an American mechanical engineer who worked for the aerospace company Rocketdyne , and who in later life brought that engineering skill to the gravity experiment devised by Dr. Ricardo Carezani to test whether gravity is carried by a particle. He designed the mechanical arm at the heart of the apparatus, co-authored the resulting paper, and appeared as one of the central figures in the feature-length documentary Einstein Wrong - The Miracle Year . Outside the laboratory he was a percussionist of some standing, playing for nearly twenty years with a Los Angeles samba school and performing in the Sambadrome in Rio de Janeiro. Professional life Hunter spent his career as a mechanical engineer at Rocketdyne , the rocket-engine manufacturer. He described the work there with characteristic understatement: that they used to blow up rockets all the time, and then go and find the pieces to work out what had gone wrong — a description that captures the empirical, failure-driven engineering temperament he later brought to the gravity experiment. In 1966 he bought a remote house in Malibu, a decision his colleagues questioned at the time given the distances he would have to commute. He kept it for the rest of his life. The gravity experiment Hunter took part in the gravity experiment designed by Dr. Ricardo Carezani , the originator of Autodynamics , whose purpose was to test whether gravity is caused by a particle — a graviton — by attempting to detect its effect directly. Hunter's contribution was the mechanical arm : the pendulum assembly on which the detection depended, and precisely the part of the apparatus that demanded a professional engineer rather than a physicist.
