Abstract An observable is ordinarily treated as a property carried by a state: the object has a mass, an area, a charge, and measurement reads it off. This paper develops the alternative, in which an observable is an invariant of the orbit a state traces under its admissible transformations, and shows that several unrelated results become one statement under it. The template is minimal. Given a configuration and a group of admissible transformations, form the orbit — the union of all transformed copies — and take the invariant on that orbit. The observable belongs to the orbit, not to the configuration. Euclidean distance, relativistic mass, spin magnitude, and the swept measure introduced here are all instances.
