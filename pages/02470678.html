R. Hearst.) It is reported that Thomas Edison is about to retire from the world and go to Florida to catch fish. Possibly he is already there, stringing the game tarpon or persuading the coy mud cat to leave its congenial ooze for the more strenuous but brief life of the creel. We shall all miss Mr. Edison, and if he should unhappily be caught by an inconsiderate tarpon and towed out to sea, no more to glad us with automatic fortresses, crewless battleships and flying machines, that are to soar by back action gravity, there would be mourning in “the land once proud of him.” The land is still proud of him. It recalls his eminent services to journalism in the matter of interviews: admitting us to glimpses of the glories to be when he shall have perfected the particular device that he happens to be “working on.” We remember with gratitude his project of defending New York harbor against the navies of the world by pointing an electrified finger at them as they steam through the Narrows unconscious of their doom. We cannot forget his noble proposal to beat Tesla’s scheme for communicating with Mars.
