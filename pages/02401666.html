GC2026-STP093 Coronal hole influence on coronal mass ejection propagation during the 15 February 2011 event Anna A. Vakhrusheva1,2 , Yulia S. Shugay1 , Ksenia B. Kaportseva1 , Vladimir V. Kalegaev1 1 Skobeltsyn Inctitute of Nuclear Physics, Lomonosov Moscow State University (SINP MSU), Russia 2 Faculty of Physics, Lomonosov Moscow State University (SINP MSU) vakhr.anna@gmail.com Coronal mass ejections (CMEs) are eruptions of plasma from the Sun into interplanetary space. CMEs are one of the sources of geomagnetic disturbances. The most powerful magnetic storms are caused by interacting solar wind streams: interactions of CMEs with high-speed solar wind streams (HSS) originating from coronal holes and interactions between multiple CMEs [1]. When interacting with the ambient solar wind, CMEs may accelerate or decelerate, change their direction of propagation, and modify their shape and magnetic structure [2]. Deflection of CMEs from radial propagation, as well as deformation of the CME front, may occur not only during their propagation through the heliosphere but also closer to the Sun, if the coronal source of the CME is located close to a coronal hole. Most CME deflections occur at the early stages of propagation [3]. In this work we examine interaction of coronal mass ejection observed in the coronagraph on 15 February 2011 with a coronal hole. Data from SDO, SOHO and STEREO spacecraft were used. Numerical modeling was performed using the Drag-Based Model. References 1. Scolini et al.
