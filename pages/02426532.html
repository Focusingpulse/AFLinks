Pearson - Science News They're All Part Fungus Grass blades, coffee or cacao leaves … probably all plants Susan Milius "You've mistaken a fungus for a pine tree" can be a ticklish thing for one botanist to say to another. Yet, in the 1990s, one respected university researcher made that very accusation to another. Stories such as this have spiced botanist gossip for years, but in this case, the two scientists resolved their differences and published a paper telling the whole story. In the mid-1990s, Aaron Liston of Oregon State University in Corvallis was studying the evolutionary history of pine trees and managed to sequence a long stretch of DNA from pine needles. "It was still a big deal in those days," he says. He searched databases for genetic sequences from similar pine trees and found some that didn't match his results at all. After more work, he became confident that his lab had the real pine-DNA sequence. He contacted Anita Klein of the University of New Hampshire in Durham, whose graduate students had contributed the other sequences to the database and used them in a journal paper. WHAT LIES WITHIN.
