GC2026-STP032 Principal characteristics of the terrestrial magnetosheath based on results of empirical modeling Nikolai A. Tsyganenko1 , Nikita T. Gubaidulin1 , Vladimir S. Semenov1 , Nikolai V. Erkaev1,2,3 1 St Petersburg University 2 Institute of Computational Modeling, Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences 3 Siberian Federal University nikita.gub22@gmail.com This work presents an empirical model of key scalar plasma characteristics — proton volume density and thermal energy in Earth's magnetosphere. The model is built from satellite data spanning 2001–2024, including about 1,150,000 one-minute measurements from the THEMIS, Cluster, and MMS missions. Input parameters include solar wind proton density, velocity, and temperature, components of the interplanetary magnetic field (IMF), and the geodipole tilt angle. The results align well with theory, including confirmation of the density and temperature jump at the bow shock, as well as dependencies on solar wind conditions. Asymmetries in magnetosphere parameter distributions—driven by IMF orientation and geodipole tilt—were examined, highlighting the role of magnetic reconnection in shaping these structures. The findings can support further analysis of magnetosphere dynamics and its response to interplanetary medium variations. References 1. N. A. Tsyganenko, N. T. Gubaidulin, V. S. Semenov, and N. V. Erkaev, Magnetosheath Proton Density and Temperature as Derived From Data‐Constrained Modeling, JGR Space Physics, doi 10.1029/2025JA034343
