Hannes Alfvén Born ( 1908-05-30 ) 30 May 1908 Norrköping, Sweden Died 2 April 1995 (1995-04-02) (aged 86) Djursholm, Sweden Nationality Swedish Known for Magnetohydrodynamics, Alfvén waves, the plasma universe Awards Nobel Prize in Physics (1970) Scientific career Fields Plasma physics, Astrophysics, Cosmology Institutions Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm; University of California, San Diego Hannes Olof Gösta Alfvén (30 May 1908 – 2 April 1995) was a Swedish plasma physicist who founded magnetohydrodynamics and received the 1970 Nobel Prize in Physics "for fundamental work and discoveries in magnetohydrodynamics with fruitful applications in different parts of plasma physics." He is the single most important ancestor of the plasma-cosmology and Electric Universe material collected on this wiki. Alfvén's core discovery, published in 1942, is that a magnetised conducting fluid can carry a transverse wave in which the magnetic field lines oscillate like plucked strings — the Alfvén wave , now a standard tool in solar, magnetospheric and fusion physics. He introduced the guiding-centre approximation for charged-particle motion, developed the theory of magnetic double layers, and was the first to argue that the aurora is driven by field-aligned electric currents flowing along the Earth's magnetic field — the currents predicted by Kristian Birkeland and now called Birkeland currents . Every one of these ideas was resisted for years and every one is now standard. His major books are Cosmical Electrodynamics (1948; second edition with Carl-Gunne Fälthammar, 1963), Worlds–Antiworlds (1966) and Cosmic Plasma (1981). The plasma universe Alfvén's later position, and his quarrel with astrophysics, can be stated compactly.
