GC2026-STP084 Ionospheric and geomagnetic responses to the X8.1 solar flare on February 1, 2026 Svetlana Riabova1,2 , Surkov Vadim1 , Denis Zinkin1 , Polina Kostina1 1 Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia 2 Sadovsky Institute of Geosphere Dynamics of Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia riabovasa@mail.ru We analyze variations in the total electron content (TEC) and variations in the geomagnetic field components accompanying the recent powerful solar flare on February 1, 2026, peaking at X8.1. This flare was the most powerful since October 2024 and ranked 19th among the strongest in the history of scientific observations. The flare peaked at 23:57 UTC originated from active region 4366. This powerful flare was followed by two more flares (X1.5 at 00:22 UTC and X2.8 at 00:36 UTC on February 2). An analysis of TEC variations obtained from data from the GNSS receiving stations revealed that the X8.1 solar flare and subsequent flares, accompanied by a significant increase in extreme ultraviolet (according to SOHO Monitor data) and X-ray (according to GOES-18 data), caused ionospheric variations synchronous with flux variations in the ultraviolet wavelength range. The dynamics of TEC variations were calculated using a model of the impact of ultraviolet radiation on the state of the ionospheric F￾region. The modeled TEC variations derived from SOHO Monitor EUV radiation measurement demonstrated the same dynamics both in amplitude and signal waveform as actual TEC variations recorded by the GNSS receivers.
