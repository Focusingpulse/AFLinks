Motorin1 1 Kola Branch of Federal Research Center Geophysical Survey of Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia 2 Institute of Earthquake PredictionTheory and Mathematical Geophysics of Russian Academy of Sciences Ponencia invitada bars.vl@gmail.com The ability of earthquakes to trigger other seismic events (or aftershocks) is characterized by productivity. Productivity determines the number of events within the spatiotemporal interval resulting from a stress disturbance caused by another earlier earthquake. This concept was used by Utsu [1] to develop a model for the occurrence of aftershocks. Since the advent of epidemic seismicity models (e.g., ETAS [2]), the study of productivity properties has become a major task. Using Japanese earthquake statistic, it was shown that the productivity law is satisfied over a wide magnitude range (ΔM = 6) [8].
