Phil Sokolof

· archived # 1,461,282
In 1966, Phil Sokolof had a heart attack in Omaha, Nebraska. He was 43. His doctor told him, in the language of the era, that saturated fat and cholesterol were to blame. It was 1966. This was the settled science. Ancel Keys was on the cover of Time magazine. The American Heart Association had been recommending reduced saturated fat for five years. Nobody in the mainstream medical establishment was questioning the framework. Sokolof believed it completely. He was a self-made millionaire, drywall manufacturing, with the energy of a man who has recently been reminded he is not immortal and the conviction of a man who believes he has found the cause. He sold his business. He founded the National Heart Savers Association. He spent $15,000,000 of his own money over twenty years on newspaper advertisements, television appearances, and direct corporate pressure campaigns targeting saturated fat in the American food supply. His primary target was McDonald's. McDonald's had been frying their french fries in beef tallow since Ray Kroc first fell in love with the McDonald brothers' product in the 1950s. Kroc himself described the fry as almost sacred. The formula, 93% beef fat, 7% cottonseed oil, had been arrived at accidentally, when a supplier stretched their product with tallow to keep costs down, and produced something extraordinary.
