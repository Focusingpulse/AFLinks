Spiritual Foundations Book Author Paramahamsa Tewari Language English Subject Space ( aakaash ) as the ground of both physics and metaphysics; Vedantic cosmology; the concept of God Published 1996 Publisher Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan, Mumbai Pages 104 ISBN 8172760701 Spiritual Foundations is a 1996 book by Paramahamsa Tewari , the Indian nuclear-project engineer who developed Space Vortex Theory . It is the one book in his output that is explicitly metaphysical rather than physical, and it states the conviction that underlies all the rest: that the aakaash — the space of the Upanishads — is the single substantial reality, and that neither science nor religion can get hold of its subject until that is granted. It is a short book, 104 pages, and it is not a treatise. It collects essays written during the years in which Tewari was building his physical theory, in a period during which, by his own account, he was also undergoing spiritual experiences. The two lines of thought are presented as one enquiry. The argument The book opens by refusing to take its nominal subject first. From the preface: What is the reality of the universe? This question should be first answered before the concept of God can be analysed. Science is still in search of the basic entity that constucts the cosmos. God, therefore, will be a system too complex for science to discover. Unless the basic reality of aakaash (space) is recognized, neither science nor spirituality can have a grasp of the Creator, Sustainer and Destroyer of this gigantic phenomena that the Vedas named as Brahman. The move being made here is the same one Tewari makes in his physics, and it is worth stating plainly because it is easy to miss. He is not arguing from scripture to a conclusion about nature.
