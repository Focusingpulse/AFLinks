GC2026-PERM010 New Eurasian Late Cretaceous paleomagnetic pole and some its applications Vladimir Pavlov1 , Andrey Guzhikov2 , Evgenij Baraboshkin3 , Georgiy Zaika1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences 2 N.G. Chernyshevsky Saratov National Research State University, Russia 3 Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow, Russia pavlov.ifz@gmail.com Surprisingly, the number of Eurasian Late Cretaceous paleomagnetic poles that meet modern reliability criteria is extremely limited, which makes it difficult to build a high-quality Late Mesozoic APWP segment for Eurasia proper. Obviously, this hampers the solution of numerous geological and geophysical problems, such as, for example, paleotectonic reconstructions of various regions currently belonging to the folded-thrust margins framing the Eurasian plate. Of course, in the absence of an accurate Eurasian APWP, the Master APWP combining paleomagnetic poles obtained for most major tectonic plates can be applied. However, this approach is somewhat risky because it does not take into account a range of hidden uncertainties related to the way the Master APWP is built. Thus, the identification of new reliable paleomagnetic poles of the Late Cretaceous for Eurasia is an important and urgent task. This task is not easy to accomplish, since there are not many geological objects of this age that have preserved a good-quality primary paleomagnetic record.
