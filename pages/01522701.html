As know from my former paper, Neptune is the "lonely planet". But I found more. In this paper, I come to the curiosum, that the sequence order of the gas-planets, at the very beginning of its existence as proto-planets, should have been as follows: proto-Jupiter, -Neptune (!) , -Saturn, and -Uranus. The basis for this research lays in the existence of the remarkable Titius-Bode law, and the unexpected successive matter composition of our gas-planets. Overview Thierry De Mees extends a planetary formation model he calls the solar protuberance hypothesis, developed across several papers, in which the planets were ejected from the Sun in a single enormous eruption rather than condensing from an interstellar cloud. The ejected material — ionised hydrogen and helium following a magnetic field line out of a sunspot and back — formed a spirally wound ring segment which fragmented into proto-planets. His reason for preferring an eruption to a cloud is the regularity of the spacing: "there exists only one planet per orbit, and these orbits lie on certain distances of each other, as follows from the empiric Titius' law," which he writes as a = 0.4 + 0.3 × 2 n and states is "also valid for the distances of Uranus and Neptune to the sun."
