Everyone has ideas about it and there is a website which has a couple of interesting animations, showing how the center of gravity is deflected by an tilted ovoid type of motion. >From what I've seen, the biggest problem would be resistance, though with modern components it would seem to be more a matter of selection of material and lubricants to minimize the friction. Teflon, dry lubricants and such. You'd think if they could build something like that way back in 1712 using crude axle grease, we should be able to duplicate it at least in a very low power version. Bessler does indicate the weight determined the torque so something like 80-100 pounds was the max he achieved and that was with a 12 foot diameter wheel! What I find so intriguing about it is the relationship to size and torque, as if gravity, as induced in the weights through the length of the spokes, makes the size quite critical. But I guess its that way on any energy generator...i.e. size does matter...<g>..a kind of gravity tap where the distance of the weight falling under the influence of gravity determines the ultimate thrust/torque you could achieve.
