Physics theory restarting from Newton’s speculations and then describing the experiments of the 19th and 20th centuries results in a model of the big, the small, and the four forces (GUT) – the Scalar Theory of Everything (STOE). Summary John Hodge 's presentation of the Scalar Theory of Everything, which he abbreviates STOE. Ian J Cowan hosted, with Franklin Hu assisting technically. Hodge's connection failed entirely, as it would again in April: he was never able to speak. The session was built instead from two recorded videos he sent by email, and he answered every question typed into the chat. The framework Hodge presents the STOE as a Kuhnian paradigm shift and claims for it the standard virtues — accuracy, internal and external consistency, breadth, simplicity and fertility — while noting that rival paradigms are incommensurable, so that big bang thinking will not help in understanding it. Its fundamental postulates are a fractal, self-similar universe, so that equations from our own size scale may be applied to larger and smaller ones; and the spherical principle, giving inverse-square forces and inverse-radius potentials.
