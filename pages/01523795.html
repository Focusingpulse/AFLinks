Golka Residence Brockton, Massachusetts; Wendover, Utah; Leadville, Colorado Nationality USA Known for Project Tesla, the Wendover magnifying transmitter, ball lightning research, large Tesla coils Scientific career Fields High-voltage engineering, Plasma Physics , Tesla technology Robert K. Golka (died 2018) was an American independent researcher and high-voltage experimenter, best known for Project Tesla — his decades-long effort to rebuild and extend Nikola Tesla 's Colorado Springs magnifying transmitter and to produce and study ball lightning in the laboratory. Working almost entirely outside universities and corporate laboratories, largely self-funded and using military surplus and salvaged parts, he built what was for years described as the world's largest Tesla coil, in an aircraft hangar at the former Wendover Air Force Base in Utah. He took seriously two propositions that mainstream physics had set aside — that ball lightning is a real, reproducible, self-contained plasma phenomenon with possible relevance to fusion, and that Tesla's scheme for wireless power transmission through the Earth-ionosphere cavity deserved to be tested rather than dismissed. Biography Golka was a native of Brockton, Massachusetts. His interest in Tesla was lifelong, and to obtain primary data he travelled to the Nikola Tesla Museum in Belgrade — reportedly on three separate occasions — to copy the inventor's Colorado Springs laboratory notes. Those notes became the working basis for his own reconstruction of Tesla's apparatus. In the early 1970s he moved to Wendover, Utah, taking up residence in a large aircraft hangar at the deactivated Wendover Air Base, where he assembled his magnifying transmitter. He remained there for roughly nine years. In 1980 the U.S. Air Force transferred the inactive base to the town of Wendover; a very large increase in his rent followed, and after a period of disputes and litigation he left the site.
