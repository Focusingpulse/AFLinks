Correa & Correa Giza Great Pyramid © Akronos Publishing, Canada ISSN 1915-8408 1 Giza Great Pyramid J Aetherom Res 4, 14: 1-48 (2026) "Heretic" models of the Great Pyramid of Giza as an energy machine Paulo N. Correa & Alexandra N. Correa Aurora Biophysics Research Institute, Concord, Ontario, Canada Abstract In the first of a series of reports on the possible reverse engineering of the Great Pyramid - or other pyramids and their complexes - as power plants, we first examined the hydraulic models of the Great Pyramid that were proposed by Kunkel (suction pump) and Cadman (ram pump). We find that neither hydraulic model fits the structural data of the pyramid. Further, their proposed engineering could not have resulted in a plausible hydraulic pump. Next, we examine Dunn's model of the Great Pyramid. This is a complex hybrid hypothesis that claims the pyramid amplified some fundamental geo￾acoustic signal which Dunn has persistently failed to identify. The hypothesis further claims that the Queen's Chamber housed a chemical generator of hydrogen, with the hydrogen being driven by the acoustic oscillations of the Grand Gallery (Vawter's acoustic model) to set up a maser receiver and emitter in the King's Chamber. We find that all three claims are at odds with the pertinent structural data of the pyramid; they lack operationality and the fixed-angle maser has no apparent utility. Further, the required hydrogen cannot be generated by the proposed chemical reactions, nor can it be simply driven by undefined acoustic oscillations.
