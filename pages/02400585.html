Kulik September 2026 Abstract A sphere is not an object situated in space. It is the orbit of a single point under the rotation group, and every property conventionally attributed to it follows from that fact alone. This paper takes the orbit as primary and derives the rest, then prices what a metric costs when it is not assumed. Three consequences are immediate. An orbit has no privileged element, so a sphere supplies no coordinate and no address until a mark is made. The centre is the unique fixed point of the group action, so every direction there lies in a single orbit, no direction is distinguishable from any other, and no gradient is definable without first breaking the symmetry that defines the centre. And dimension is orbit dimension: the (n−1)-sphere is what the rotation group of n-space does to one point, so the familiar progression from line to circle to sphere is one generator applied repeatedly rather than three objects assembled in order of complexity. Quotienting three-space by the rotation group leaves the half-line.
