NEW YORK city has not given birth to most of its great men. The country has furnished energy and brains for the metropolis. Though 272 years have passed since Peter Minuet purchased Manhattan Island from the Indians the very great majority of those who have been most active in its welfare — the controlling spirits, the men of prominence and the men of accomplishments — have come from beyond its borders. They have not been urban by birth, but rural. Write a score or more of names that come first to your mind when you think of the prominent men of New York, then go to your biographical dictionary and you will find that ten out of every dozen were born outside the city, and that eight out of the ten came from rural districts. Not Even Native Mayors. It is so now, and it has been so from the beginning. Though about 1,800,000 persons have been born in New York during the present century, and in that time the city has had thirty-nine Mayors, beginning with Richard Varick and ending with Robert A. Van Wyck, only twelve of these Mayors were born on Manhattan Island. This same thing has run through the city’s history.
