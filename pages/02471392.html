The 24-foot-high, roughly one-third-scale gold-leaf-on-bronze replica of the Statue of the Republic (popularly known as the Golden Lady) stands in Jackson Park at the Hayes-Richards Circle (6300 South). It is a replica of the 65-foot-high gold-leaf-on-plaster original that stood in the central focus of the Grand Basin gateway Court of Honor (southwest of the present Beach House at 63rd Street/Hayes Drive) at the World’s Columbian Exposition of 1893. The replica occupies the site of the exposition’s Administration Building, on the opposite side of the Grand Basin from where the original stood. Both the original and the replica were by Daniel Chester French. French and architect Henry Bacon produced the 24-foot replica and pedestal, which were dedicated on May 11, 1918, to honor both the 25th anniversary of the Exposition and the 100th birthday of the entry of the State of Illinois into the Union. The statue was designated a Chicago Landmark by the City Council on June 5, 2003 (final recommendation by the Commission on Chicago Landmarks approved April 3, 2003, and sent to the Chicago City Council).
