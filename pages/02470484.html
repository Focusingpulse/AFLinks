He comes forth with the announcement that he has practically perfected a "world system," whereby, through the use of half a dozen great stations at various parts of the globe, all the inhabitants of this fair earth can not only talk to one another but can also see one another. Tesla declares that his apparatus will transmit pictures without wires, and the faces of persons talking will be accurately revealed when his "system" is put in operation. His mode of transmitting pictures is based on a plan used in ordinary telegraphy. But he insists that he has refined and improved that plan until it is entirely feasible. The transmission of photographs by telegraph has been accepted as possible for half a dozen years. To Professor Korn of Munich goes much of the credit for the invention of such transmission. While the idea is said to be commercially practicable, little has been done in the matter of its application to every day use, however.
