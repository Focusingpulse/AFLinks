GTM2026-main066 SHAP-based Warehouse Management Model. Anna V. Melnik1 , Maksim Y. Kenzin 1 OOO BIATECH, Russia anna.v.melnik@bia-tech.ru Machine learning models have become an integral part of modern business processes, being widely used for prediction, clustering, and classification to support operational and strategic decision-making. The automation of business processes through such models significantly increases the speed of decision-making, improves the quality of services provided, and reduces company costs. One of the key requirements for deploying machine learning models in real-world business environments is the ability to interpret and explain their predictions. In this context, interpretability tools play a crucial role in ensuring trust, transparency, and accountability of automated systems. Recently, a method based on the Shapley value from cooperative game theory — SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) — has gained significant popularity. SHAP assigns to each feature a contribution value reflecting its impact on the model’s output, thereby providing both local and global interpretability. Beyond its primary function as an explanation tool, SHAP can be extended to causal analysis and root-cause identification, transforming it into a powerful decision-support instrument applicable across various business domains. This work explores the use of SHAP not only as an interpretation method, but also as a tool for causal analysis.
