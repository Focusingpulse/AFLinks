08/10/16 - 1985 G-Force inertial drive Harnessing Nature To Do The Work - April 21, 1985 - By Jon Van, Chicago Tribune - CHICAGO � The force at work when you twirl a ball around your head on the end of a string can be harnessed and put to work to propel a boat through water. To many engineers, the notion of harnessing gravity to drive a vehicle smacks of the impossible, something like a perpetual motion machine. But Jack Gilbert and Steve Witter of Suffren, N.Y., have built a device that does just that. The device consists of two whirling weights analogous to balls on strings. The difference is that these weights are mounted on rotating plates with eccentric pivots so that at one point in the rotation the weights are extended far from the center of the plate and at the opposite point of rotation, they are close to the center. Because the plates rotate in opposite directions, the effect is to cancel out all the opposing centrifugal forces except those pushing in a single direction. By mounting this unit on a tripod of wheels, Gilbert has harnessed the force to push the unit forward in a series of pulses as the weights spin. In the next year Gilbert plans to build a bigger version of the centrifugal force propulsion system and to mount it inside a boat 18 feet long. He expects that the unit, driven by a standard gasoline engine, can propel the boat up a river at more than 20 mph without using a propeller or any other device external to the boat. That will reduce drag on the boat and allow it to move through very shallow waters, Gilbert said. ''Anytime you can harness a force of nature and make it work for you, you will use less energy, and that's the idea here,'' he said. Though it is doubtful that the unit will ever replace auto engines, Gilbert said another use might be in propelling a craft upward from the ground, a hovercraft configuration.
