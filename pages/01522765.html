Title Determining Age, Expansion, and Dark Matter for Our Universe Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Morton F Spears Keywords dark matter , expanding universe Published 1999 Journal Unpublished No. of pages 3 Abstract This report demonstrates that a changed model of the universe results when the assumption is made that permittivity reduces with time in an expanding universe. Age, expansion, and dark matter are all affected. Overview Dated 24 February 1999 and never published in a journal, this three-page report by Morton F Spears offers a single mechanism for three cosmological puzzles then in the news: a universe apparently younger than its oldest stars, an expansion that appeared to be accelerating, and the missing mass called dark matter . Spears states his aim as explaining them "without resorting to speculations or new theories… Only facts and physical laws are used", and writes deliberately for non-technical readers, with almost no algebra. The argument is a chain of six numbered "Facts". Permittivity falls as the universe expands Matter of any kind placed between capacitor plates raises the measured capacitance above its vacuum value, so the effective permittivity of a volume of space rises with the amount of matter in it. Spears offers a "little bang" demonstration — insulated metal balls glued around an explosive charge, with capacitors along a radial path measuring permittivity as a ball passes — and then scales it up, imagining plates 10,000 light years square separated by 100 light years, enclosing the Earth. Long ago, when everything was closer together, such a measurement would have returned a larger capacitance. Hence "perceived permittivity in the space of our Universe is decreasing with time and distance from the big bang origin." Therefore light speeds up, and mass falls Since c 2 varies inversely with permittivity, a falling permittivity means a rising speed of light (Fact 2).
