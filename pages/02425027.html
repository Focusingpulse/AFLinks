Evidence for the innermost inner core: Robust parameter search for radially varying anisotropy using the Neighbourhood Algorithm J. Stephenson1, H. Tkalčić1 and M. Sambridge1 joanne.stephenson@anu.edu.au Key Points: • The “Neighbourhood Algorithm” provides a robust methodology for testing layered anisotropy in Earth’s inner core. • There is no significant change observed in strength of anisotropy with depth in the inner core. • The innermost inner core is defined as a gradual change in the slow propagation direction of anisotropy. 1 Research School of Earth Sciences, The Australian National University, 142 Mills Road, Canberra, ACT, 0200, Australia This article has been accepted for publication and undergone full peer review but has not been through the copyediting, typesetting, pagination and proofreading process, which may lead to differences between this version and the Version of Record. Abstract The model of cylindrical anisotropy in the inner core (IC) states that seismic rays traveling parallel to the Earth’s rotation axis travel faster than parallel to the equator. There have been continuing discrepancies in estimates of strength and orientation of anisotropy, with some evidence suggesting such a model may not be supported by available data. Here, we scrutinise the radial dependence of anisotropy within the IC, where the nature of anisotropy has been shown to change anywhere between 300 and 800 km radius. We use recent travel time data from the International Seismological Centre (ISC) in conjunction with the Neighbourhood Algorithm to provide a robust means of testing this idea, through examination of an ensemble of models that satisfactorily fit the data.
