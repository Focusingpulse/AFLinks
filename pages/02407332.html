Girolamo Cardano’s De Subtilitate (published in 1550) is a work that covers a wide range of topics in natural philosophy, science, and metaphysics. The title De Subtilitate can be translated as On Subtlety or On Subtle Matters, and it reflects Cardano’s exploration of the intricate, fine, and often mysterious aspects of the natural world. Here’s an overview of the main themes and subjects discussed in De Subtilitate: 1. Natural Philosophy and Physics: Cardano investigates the principles of nature, including elements such as air, water, fire, and earth. He discusses various natural phenomena such as magnetism, heat, light, and celestial bodies, trying to understand the subtle forces governing the world. The book delves into physical phenomena, focusing on the relationship between matter and the properties of materials. Cardano examines the composition and behavior of objects in nature, both from a scientific and metaphysical perspective. 2. Alchemy and Chemistry: Cardano was deeply influenced by the alchemical tradition, and in De Subtilitate, he explores the transformation of materials, including metals and minerals. He discusses alchemy in terms of both practical applications (like metallurgy) and symbolic meanings. He looks at the subtle qualities of different substances, contemplating how they interact and transform, aiming to understand processes like sublimation, condensation, and transmutation. 3. Metaphysics and Occult Philosophy: The book also covers metaphysical subjects, including the nature of the soul, the divine, and the hidden forces that influence the physical world. Cardano is interested in the idea that there are subtle, invisible forces, both physical and spiritual, that govern life.
