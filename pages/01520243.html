Title Gravity & The Spinning Ball Experiment Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Bruce E DePalma Keywords gravity, inertia, spinning ball, field Published 1977 No. of pages 6 Abstract The spinning ball experiment consists of the observation of the interaction of gravitational and inertia forces on a rotating material object. In the interaction of material force on a rotating physical object, four experiments are possible: # Inertial forces acting on non-rotating material objects in field-free space Inertial forces acting on rotating material objects in field-free space Inertial forces acting on non-rotating material objects in a gravitational field Inertial forces acting on rotating material objects in a gravitational field Overview Gravity & The Spinning Ball Experiment is a six-page paper by Bruce E DePalma dated 17 March 1977. It is the theoretical companion to the experiment DePalma is best known for, in which two steel balls — one spinning at high speed, one not — are launched vertically and photographed by strobe. The paper's claims are: A rapidly rotating object gains inertia. DePalma names the additional inertial property od , a term borrowed from Karl von Reichenbach's 1845 "odic force". A spinning ball thrown upward rises higher than a non-spinning one launched at the same speed, because the energy stored in od is available to do work against gravity. A spinning ball falls faster , with greater acceleration, because od is "an additional repository for the extraction... of work from a gravitational field". Newton's laws are a special case , valid only for non-rotating objects in field-free space, and the equality of inertial and gravitational mass is an artefact of Newton's failure to distinguish rotating from non-rotating bodies. Antigravity and energy extraction follow. "Here is the possibility of the conversion of rotational energy to work done against the gravitational field."
