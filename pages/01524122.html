Title The Gravitational Superforce Hypothesis Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Robert J Heaston Keywords gravity Published 2003 Journal Journal of New Energy Volume 7 Number 3 No. of pages 8 Pages 25-31 Abstract The gravitational superforce hypothesis states: The gravitational force is always equal to a constant superforce for the same mass and distance that exists at the mass energy equivalence of the gravitation potential energy. A specific superforce that impacts a broad range of theoretical physics and cosmology is defined by this hypothesis. This superforce is suggested as a baseline for comparison with any future superforce. Overview Robert J. Heaston proposes that a specific quantity, c 4 / G , deserves the name "superforce" — the single unifying force Paul Davies described in his 1984 book of that title — and offers it as a baseline against which any future candidate should be measured. The derivation is short. Setting a mass's rest energy equal to its own gravitational potential energy, mc ² = m ² G / r S , defines a length r S = mG / c ², which Heaston calls the gravitational collapse limit — the radius at which all matter becomes energy. He notes this is half the Schwarzschild radius, and introduces a parameter n = mG / c ² r as a measure of relativistic effects, quoting n = 10 −5 for the Sun, 10 −3 for a white dwarf and 0.3 for a neutron star, with n ≥ 0.5 at the Schwarzschild radius and n = 1 at the Big Bang. Substituting that length back into Newton's law gives m ² G / r S ² = m ² G ( c ²/ mG )² = c 4 / G Mass cancels, so the result is the same whether the interacting objects are electrons, bowling balls or galaxies. The quantity has the dimensions of force and the value 1.21 × 10 44 N. Rearranged, the same relation gives mG / r S = c ², which Heaston reads as the gravitational potential of any mass at the superforce.
