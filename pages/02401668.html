GC2026-STP094 Comparative Study of Ionospheric Effects during the Extreme Geomagnetic Storms of November 2025 and January 2026 Alexander Chernyshov1 , Sergey Pulinets1 , Yury Yasyukevich2 , Dmitry Chugunin1 , Sergey Svertilov3 , Artem Padokhin3 , Vitaly Bogomolov3 , Andrey Bogomolov3 , Konstantin Tsybulya4 , Ekaterina Danilchuk2 , Mikhail Mogilevsky1 1 Space Research Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Institute of Solar-Terrestrial Physics, Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Irkutsk, Russia 3 Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow, Russia 4 Fedorov Institute of Applied Geophysics, Russia achernyshov@cosmos.ru In terms of both frequency and strength of extreme geomagnetic events, the current 25th solar cycle already stands out as remarkable. Whereas the only outstanding event of the previous, 24th cycle was the "St. Patrick's Day Storm" of 2015, the current cycle is exhibiting a concerning series of powerful storms. The most recent extreme events were the storms of November 2025 and January 2026, which serve as the focus of the present study. In this research, we investigate the consequences and ionospheric effects of these two storms. To achieve this, we utilize both ground-based infrastructure and satellite data from four Russian Ionosphere-M sattelites. The first pair of these satellites was launched in November 2024, followed by the second pair in July 2025, providing comprehensive coverage for analyzing the ionospheric response to extreme space weather events.
