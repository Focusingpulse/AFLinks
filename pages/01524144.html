Title The Lagrange Function Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Autodynamics , Lagrangian, Dirac equation, Sommerfeld, quantum electrodynamics, Bohr atom Published Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Website https://www.autodynamics.org/lagrange-function/ "The Lagrange Function" is a white paper by Ricardo L Carezani answering a question from Greg Oganessian of the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research: "Has anyone succeeded in applying AD to quantum mechanics, and obtaining an analogue to well known Dirac equation in special relativity quantum mechanics? Also, does AD allow field-theoretical formulation in the guise of action principle? Can you write the Lagrangian for AD?" Overview Carezani's answer is that a Lagrangian can be written, and that the more interesting question is what a Lagrangian is . His claim is that Sommerfeld's relativistic Lagrangian was not derived but invented to make an integration work, that the same is true of the weak-interaction Lagrangian, and that Autodynamics is entitled to invent one on the same terms. The argument Sommerfeld's Lagrangian was constructed The historical section traces the Dirac equation back through Sommerfeld's relativistic treatment of the Bohr atom to the Balmer formula. Carezani's technical observation is that Sommerfeld could not define the conjugate momentum as the partial derivative of the relativistic kinetic energy, "because this 'will not yield the momentum equation' and the variable separation" — and that the classical Lagrangian is no help either. What Sommerfeld did was write down L = − m 0 c 2 1 − β 2 − U Carezani's comment: "If we really tell the truth, this Lagrangian is an invented solution." The coincidence Carezani draws attention to He then notes that the first term of Sommerfeld's Lagrangian, m 0 c 2 1 − β 2 , is exactly the Autodynamics expression for mass variation written as an energy.
