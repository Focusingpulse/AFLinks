A Novel Interpretation of the Photoelectric Effect Using the Aether Physics Model Abstract This paper presents a novel interpretation of the photoelectric effect using the Aether Physics Model (APM) framework. While Einstein's 1905 explanation of the photoelectric effect has been foundational to quantum mechanics, treating light as discrete photon particles, we propose an alternative mechanical explanation that maintains the predictive power of Einstein's model while providing a more intuitive geometric understanding of the underlying processes. In the APM framework, light is treated as a continuous phenomenon rather than discrete particles, with photons redefined as quanta of action-at-a-distance (h·c). The photoelectric effect is explained through angular momentum accumulation in atomic valence positions within a quantized Aether structure. This model accounts for all key observations - including the frequency threshold, instantaneous emission, and the linear relationship between frequency and electron kinetic energy - while offering a clear mechanical picture of the interaction process. The paper introduces the APM's Quantum Measurement Units (QMU) system and explores various expressions of the light unit, providing multiple perspectives on electromagnetic radiation's nature. This framework bridges quantum and classical descriptions of light-matter interactions, potentially resolving some conceptual difficulties in quantum mechanics while maintaining consistency with experimental observations. We propose several experimental tests to validate this new interpretation and discuss its implications for quantum optics, photovoltaics, and other technological applications. This reinterpretation of the photoelectric effect demonstrates how the APM's geometric approach to physics can offer fresh insights into fundamental phenomena while maintaining the predictive success of existing models.
