Menahem Simhony Born 1922 Died 29 June 2015 Residence Jerusalem, Israel Nationality Israeli Known for The epola (electron-positron lattice) model of space Scientific career Fields Physics , Aether , Relativity , Cosmology Institutions Hebrew University of Jerusalem Menahem Simhony (1922 – 29 June 2015) was an Israeli physicist at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem who developed the epola model — the electron-positron lattice theory of the vacuum. In this model what physics calls empty space is a real, crystalline solid built of alternating bound electrons and positrons, from whose measurable elastic properties Simhony derived the velocity of light, inertia and gravitation. Working outside the mainstream for more than forty years after his papers were repeatedly declined by the major physics journals, he argued that twentieth-century physics had abandoned physical explanation in favour of mathematical formalism. Biography Simhony was born in 1922. He joined the physics staff of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem in January 1960, working in its Physics Section 5 and serving successively as Instructor, Lecturer and Senior Lecturer. His experimental background was in solid-state and semiconductor research, in particular the physics of crystals — the field from which the epola concept later grew, since it was the well-measured behaviour of ionic crystals such as sodium chloride that gave him the quantitative model for a lattice of charges. He began work on the electron-positron lattice in 1973, submitting a thirty-page paper titled "The Electron-Positron Lattice" to Physical Review D in March of that year. It was declined, as were many subsequent submissions, generally without any specific error being identified — the stated grounds being that the work "contradicts accepted scientific knowledge" or was "too speculative."
