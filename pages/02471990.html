W. Phillips Criterion, The November 19th, 1898 Tall, spare man entered from the side door. He had a keen face - the dome of the head seeming to overbalance the sensitive chin. This was the man who jounced the reading world out of its usual apathy several days ago by declaring that war would be abolished - the means being an invention of his, by which a torpedo or boat could be controlled from shore, although entirely cut off from ordinary means of communication - the governing impulses to be sent through the air. "You must excuse me," he said, with a pleasant foreign accent, "but I cannot be interviewed. There is so much put down that I do not say, and the subject is of too great importance to be treated lightly." "But, Mr. Tesla, am I to understand that your invention remains to be perfected, or is now ready for operation?" "It is now!" he answered, eagerly. "Within three days can be made ready a launch, torpedo boat, or what you will. There are four - five - men in the world who could apply these principles - not more - but they, by taking a piece here and something there, now that I have disclosed my idea, could utilize the invention at once. I do not care to make it quite so strong for publication, as every new idea arouses antagonism, and many people may call me fanatic, for that statement, but it is true."
