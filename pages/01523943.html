Stefan Marinov Born ( 1931-02-01 ) February 1, 1931 Sofia, Bulgaria Died July 15, 1997 (1997-07-15) (aged 66) Graz, Austria Residence Graz, Austria Nationality Bulgarian Known for Absolute space-time theory, Aether drift measurements, Marinov motor, New Energy , anti- relativism Scientific career Fields Physicist , Inventor Stefan Marinov (1 February 1931 – 15 July 1997) was a Bulgarian physicist, researcher, writer and lecturer who promoted anti-relativistic theoretical viewpoints and, later in his life, defended the ideas of perpetual motion and free energy. He is best known for his attempts to disprove Albert Einstein's special theory of relativity through experiments intended to detect a direction-dependence in the one-way velocity of light, and for his claim to have measured the absolute velocity of the Earth through the aether. His views did not find acceptance within the scientific mainstream. Marinov died in Graz, Austria, on 15 July 1997, in what authorities recorded as a suicide. Education 1948 – Completed studies at the Soviet College, Prague Studied physics at the Czech Technical University, Prague Studied physics at Sofia University, Sofia, Bulgaria Biography Early life Marinov was born on 1 February 1931 in Sofia, Bulgaria, into a family of intellectual communists. In 1948 he finished the Soviet College in Prague and went on to study physics at the Czech Technical University in Prague and at Sofia University. Career Marinov was an Assistant Professor of Physics from 1960 to 1974 at Sofia University. In 1966–67, 1974 and 1977 he was subjected to compulsory psychiatric treatment in Sofia because of his political dissent. In September 1977 he obtained a passport and emigrated, moving first to Brussels. In 1978 he moved to Washington, D.C., and later lived in Italy and Austria, eventually settling in Graz.
