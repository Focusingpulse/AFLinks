The proper interpretation requires an internal reduction in the energy of matter for any velocity imposed. The law of the conservative field is applied to correct the Einstein equation for free fall. It is found that the action of contact forces is conservative as long as no radiation occurs. These facts are applied to show that the Black Hole as envisioned in the study of Cosmology has no existence in the real universe. Overview The Black Hole is a three-page paper by Robert L Carroll of Summerville, South Carolina, published in Apeiron no. 17 (October 1993), pp. 18–21. Its argument proceeds in four steps before reaching black holes at all. Carroll accepts time dilation as an experimental fact: "the period of the frequency is increased by the act of putting the clock into motion", f = f 0 √(1−v²/c²). He then multiplies by Planck's constant, hf = hf 0 √(1−v²/c²), and reads the result as "an expression of the fact that the internal energy of the mass comprising the clock is reduced when a velocity is imposed upon it", writing e = m 0 c 0 ²√(1−v²/c²). Since a conservative field cannot increase a body's total energy, he replaces the relativistic energy law with one in which the rest energy is conserved, arriving at m 0 c 0 ² = m 0 c 0 ²√(1−v²/c²) + m 0 c 0 ²(1 − √(1−v²/c²)) He extends this to contact forces by arguing that action and reaction do equal work, so "the accelerated object lose[s] just as much energy to the source as it received", and concludes that "there is no mass increase with velocity in the case of free fall or by acceleration by a contact force" — mass changes only through radiation.
