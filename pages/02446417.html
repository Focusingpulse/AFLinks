Discovery of chemical compound that reverses ageing

· archived # 2,446,417
09/20/14 - Discovery of chemical compound that reverses ageing Researchers from the University of NSW have discovered a chemical compound which they claim can reverse the ageing process. The compound, known as nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) could also cure cancer, diabetes and some inflammatory diseases within five years. Studies on older mice have shown that, within a week of being injected with the compound, they displayed muscle improvement to such an extent that they were indistinguishable from much younger mice. Dr Nigel Turner, co-author of the study, describes the findings as "something like a 60 year old being similar to a 20 year old on some measures". Speaking on Channel 7's Sunrise this morning, Dr Ginni Mansberg explained that NMN works by boosting the levels of a coenzyme known as nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD). Dr Mansberg says NAD works as the "communications control tower" within the cell and when NAD levels drop, the ageing process happens. But with NMN injections, that process could potentially be reversed. NAD also works to promote a healthy metabolism, especially in the pancreas and liver as well as muscles and fat tissue. Human trials of the compound could begin as early as next year, however the treatment does not come cheaply. The compound costs around $1,000 per gram and researchers were using the equivalent of 500mg per kilogram of body weight every day. This means that an average sized woman would be paying around $35,000 daily for the treatment. Lead researcher, Professor David Sinclair, says that he hopes to find a way to produce the compound at a more affordable price.
