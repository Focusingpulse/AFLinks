Saturday Science Chats lead by Franklin Hu and sponsored by the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu , with Ian Cohen, Bill Lucas , James Keene , Alistair Riddoch , Roger and Carl. Hu opened it as a mailbag session, working through the society's email list, where one argument had crowded out everything else for a fortnight. Covid The first quarter hour was the participants' own views on the pandemic, offered as opinion. Carl's argument was that lockdowns slow the case rate without changing how many are ultimately infected, that the justification is keeping hospitals from being overrun, and that sequestering the vulnerable while the rest of the population acquired immunity would have reached the same place. Ian drew the parallel with the 1918 influenza, which came in successive waves and then subsided of its own accord, and added the cynical reading that governments needed to be seen doing something. Hu described his own experience of a covid ward when his mother died, which he found calm and well within capacity, and noted that the ventilator shortage feared in the United States had not materialised. Displacement current The substance of the session was an argument running on the email list between Hu, Akinbo Ojo , Cornelius, Harry Ricker and David de Hilster about displacement current and what an electromagnetic wave is. Hu set out the background from a first reading. Maxwell added the displacement current — a time-varying electric field acting as a source of magnetic field — to Ampère's law, and it is the term that makes electromagnetic waves possible.
