Overview Stellar Metamorphosis: An Alternative for the Star Sciences is a five-page paper by Jeffrey Joseph Wolynski of Cocoa, Florida, dated 2012 and presented at the 20th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in July 2013. Its thesis is a single identification: "Planet formation is star evolution… Planets are ancient stars and stars are young planets." Wolynski notes that "planet" is from αστήρ πλανήτησ, wandering star, and argues that treating the two as distinct is "the wrong root assumption", from which "there is no possible way to understand anything." The proposed sequence is a chain of phase transitions. Quasars create matter as newborn galaxies (citing Arp and Ambartsumian); a gas cloud is ionised and compressed by a Z-pinch into a hot blue star; the plasma cools and recombines to a gas, giving first a red dwarf and then a "gas giant planet"; the gas deposits as solids and liquids, giving a rocky planet like the Earth, with the oceans condensing from its atmosphere and the Grand Canyon's layers laid down by direct deposition of gaseous silicon dioxide; and finally the dead star collides with others, producing asteroid belts and the rings and "proto-planetary disks" that are, on this reading, debris rather than nurseries. Two further claims carry the framework. Gravitation is redefined: "This decreasing enthalpy is what gravitation is… It is not a force, is not constant… The faster the lowering of enthalpy the higher the 'gravitation'."
