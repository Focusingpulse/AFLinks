Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu on the Fourth of July, with Ian Cohen, Alistair Riddoch , James Keene and Bob, who had prepared slides on the question Hu had been putting to the group for some weeks: what is a magnetic field, and why does an electron deflect in one? Bob's answer: there is no magnetic field Bob's case, developed over a long presentation, is that the magnetic field is not a physical field at all. He began with terminology — field means several different things, and if one defines an electrodynamic field as force per unit charge on a test body, then B does not qualify, its units being wrong. His alternative comes from Weber electrodynamics, where the force between two charged bodies is the Coulomb force multiplied by a coupling coefficient with a constant term, a velocity-dependent term and an acceleration-dependent term. His analogy was static against kinetic friction: nobody finds it strange that the coupling between two objects differs according to their relative motion. On this reading the magnetic field is simply that dynamic coupling, and the Coulomb field is the only real physical field. He applied Occam's razor twice. First to William J Hooper , who identifies three electric fields — Coulomb, motional and transformer — which Bob tabulated to show that they differ precisely in how many time derivatives they carry: none, one, two. Second to the multipole expansion of the scalar potential, whose infinite series of terms would, on the same logic, give an infinite series of fields.
