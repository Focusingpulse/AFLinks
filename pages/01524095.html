Title The Experimental Measurements of the One-Way Light Velocity and Its Possibilities for Absolute Velocity Measurement Author(s) Stefan Marinov Keywords one-way light velocity, absolute velocity, aether drift, special relativity , clock synchronisation Published 1980 Journal Speculations in Science and Technology Volume 3 Number 1 No. of pages 6 Pages 57-62 "The Experimental Measurements of the One-Way Light Velocity and Its Possibilities for Absolute Velocity Measurement" is a six-page paper by Stefan Marinov , published in 1980 in Speculations in Science and Technology , the journal founded three years earlier by William M Honig to carry work that established journals would not take. Overview The paper belongs to the burst of publication that followed Marinov's first positive results, when versions of his argument appeared within about two years in General Relativity and Gravitation , Foundations of Physics , Physics Letters A , the Indian Journal of Theoretical Physics , the proceedings of the Second Marcel Grossmann Meeting and here. Its subject, as the title states, is the distinction on which Marinov's entire programme rests. Every classic determination of the speed of light — and the Michelson–Morley experiment with it — measures a two-way value, sending light out and back over a closed path, so that any anisotropy in the outward leg is cancelled by the return. Only a one-way measurement over a single trip could reveal a direction dependence, and any such measurement requires two spatially separated clocks to be synchronised. In special relativity that synchronisation is conventionally performed with light signals, which makes the isotropy of the one-way speed a definition rather than a result.
