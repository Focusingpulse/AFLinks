Title The Significance of the Inertial Forces in Electromagnetism Read in full Link to paper Author(s) David Tombe Published 2019 Abstract The centrifugal force and the Coriolis force will be described. There is a controversy over whether these forces are real or fictitious. This controversy will be examined in conjunction with its significance to electromagnetism. Overview The Significance of the Inertial Forces in Electromagnetism is a five-page paper by David Tombe , dated 7 May 2019. It argues that centrifugal and Coriolis forces are real rather than fictitious, that the reason they are taught otherwise is a misreading of Newton's first law, and that recognising them as real reveals the magnetic force to be an inertial effect in a medium of rotating electron–positron dipoles. Sections I–III set out the kinematics. Tombe notes that with respect to any chosen origin, the centrifugal term in the radial acceleration of a body moving in a straight line is exactly cancelled by a second radial term, and that in the transverse direction the Coriolis term is cancelled by an equal and opposite term, giving conservation of angular momentum. He takes the cancellation to be the reason people call these forces fictitious, and argues that they are nonetheless real because they "can cause physical reactions with other bodies, they can act in tandem with other forces, they can defy gravity, and they can cause a rotating rattleback to reverse its angular momentum". He then proposes that the straight-line inertial path is itself the consequence of a body interacting with an all-pervading medium, generating "a uniform centrifugal pressure on all sides, leading to a cylindrically symmetric field pattern in the likeness of the magnetic field that surrounds an electric current", and remarks that this is never discussed because it leads into the banned territory of the luminiferous aether.
