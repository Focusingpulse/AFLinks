Einstein's Aether Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker Roger J Anderton Date 14 May 2022 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording Einstein's Aether was a live session presented by Roger J Anderton on 14 May 2022 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. Roger Anderton, a long-time critic of Einstein's special relativity, will be talking about how the math in Einstein's famous 1905 paper shows there is an aether, even though claiming aether is superfluous. Join David he listens to Roger's arguments and discusses this interesting claim from Anderton. Summary Roger Anderton , a semi-retired telecommunications engineer in Southampton with a degree in mathematics and physics, argued that the mathematics of Einstein's 1905 paper contains an ether frame despite the paper's own statement that the ether is superfluous. David de Hilster introduced him and handed the session to Bob de Hilster partway through. Anderton gave his own route in: he gave up A-level physics as too difficult, spent a career in telecommunications and electronics, took a degree with the Open University, and abandoned the idea of a doctorate on finding that he disagreed with too much of what he was studying — and that the literature did not agree with what he had been taught. In memory of Neal Adams Before the talk de Hilster marked the death of Neal Adams at eighty, a few weeks earlier, and played much of his growing-earth animation, which has some two million views — probably, de Hilster thought, more than any other dissident science video. Adams is why he knows about earth expansion at all, and he had persuaded him to write a paper for the society despite his saying he was not a scientist. He was candid about the man — cantankerous, the first speaker he heard swear like a sailor, and unwilling to entertain other accounts of why the earth grows — and about the animations, which James Maxlow does not regard as exactly right.
