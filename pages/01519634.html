Title Derivation of the Schrodinger Equation from the Laws of Classical Mechanics Taking into Account the Ether Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Nina B Sotina Keywords Ether , particle Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 7 Pages 572-578 Abstract In the present work the author suggests a to the idea of "hidden variables" as a physical field (ether). It is shown below that the Schrödinger equation can be derived from the deterministic laws of classical mechanics under the assumption that the ether exists. The reasoning is based to a great extent on the works of N.G. Chetaev. Formulas derived in the present work for the velocity of an elementary particle and for the forces exerted on the particle by the ether coincide precisely with those derived by V.A. Kotel'nikov using the standard probabilistic interpretation of the Schrödinger equation. Therefore, the proposed classical approach agrees with the probabilistic one, and hence with standard experiments. The mathematical development of the proposed model when applied to an atom leads to the idea that structures are formed in the ether inside the atom. From the standpoint of this model, De Broglie's "law of phase harmony" has a new physical interpretation. Overview This paper argues that the Schrödinger equation need not be taken as a postulate of quantum mechanics but can be derived from deterministic classical mechanics, provided one admits an ether exerting an additional force on the particle. The hidden variables that Einstein , Podolsky and Rosen sought are here identified not with properties of the particle but with a physical field. The mathematical route is unusual and is the paper's distinctive feature. It does not start from a fluid model or a stochastic process, as most classical reconstructions of quantum mechanics do, but from stability theory — the branch of classical mechanics concerned with whether a given trajectory persists under small perturbations.
