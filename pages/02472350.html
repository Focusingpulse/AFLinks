At few funerals in this city has greater homage been paid the memory of a man than was displayed to-day when the final services were read over the body of George C. Boldt, proprietor of the Waldorf-Astoria Hotel. Following the simple and impressive private service in the Boldt apartment at the hotel there was a public service in St. Thomas’ Episcopal Church, at Fifth avenue and Fifty-third street. Nearly two thousand persons can be seated there and the edifice was thronged, those present including some of the best known men in the country and some of the humblest employees of the Waldorf-Astoria. As the cortege moved up Fifth avenue from the hotel to the church, and after the service from the church to 110th street on its way to Woodlawn, traffic in Fifth avenue was halted, while from every hotel flags were lowered to half mast. Traffic in Avenue Halted. The coffin, of bronze and silver, was carried from the hotel to Thirty-fourth street, although the funeral party started from the Thirty-third street side.
