GC2026-STP077 The Role of Low Polar Orbit Experiments in Monitoring Space Weather in Near-Earth Space Irina N. Myagkova1 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow, Russia irina@srd.sinp.msu.ru Monitoring, analyzing, and forecasting space weather phenomena have become increasingly important in recent decades, as all human activities become increasingly closely linked to the space industry, both due to the miniaturization of electronic components used in space and the increasing demands for reliable communications required for robotics in various fields. We list the space weather events that directly or indirectly impact near-Earth space: • ionizing electromagnetic radiation from solar flares, which causes heating and additional ionization of the upper atmosphere, as well as flare-induced hard X-rays and gamma radiation; • solar cosmic rays (protons, electrons, and nuclei) generated on the Sun and reaching Earth's orbit after interacting with the interplanetary medium; • magnetospheric storms and substorms caused by the arrival of an interplanetary shock wave at Earth, associated with CMEs and high-speed solar wind streams from coronal holes; • variations in the fluxes of relativistic electrons in Earth's outer radiation belt during geomagnetic storms. As can be seen, observations of three of the four phenomena listed must be observed in space, and experiments in low polar orbits ( or low Earth orbit - LEO) play a special role here.
