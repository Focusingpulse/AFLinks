GC2026-PERM036 New data on the weak magnetic field in the Proterozoic and an analysis of the field geometry in the Precambrian Valentina Shcherbakova1 , Valeriy Shcherbakov1 , Grigoriy Zhidkov1 , Natalya Aphinogenova1 1 Geophysical Observatory "Borok", O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences valia@borok.yar.ru The evolution of the Earth's magnetic field at various stages of its geological history is a fundamental problem in geophysics. In particular, the question of when the Earth's solid core (ICN) was formed remains among the most complex and debated topic. According to modern geodynamo theories, after the formation of the solid core, the geomagnetic field begins to be additionally supported by compositional convection, which is more effective than the thermal one. Consequently, it is assumed that the formation of the inner solid core should be accompanied by a sharp increase in magnetic field strength. With this idea in mind, [1] suggested that an analysis of the set of experimental palaeointensity measurements (Banc ) presented in the international databases (IDBs) could serve as an independent method for estimating the formation time of the ICN. Another fundamental problem in geophysics is the geometry of the ancient geomagnetic field. Paleomagnetic reconstructions of positions of continents and crustal blocks rely on the geocentric axial dipole (GAD) hypothesis.
