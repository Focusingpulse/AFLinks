Title A Novel Approach To The Bound Muon Decay Rate Retardation: Metric Change Nearby The Nucleus Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Tolga Yarman Keywords Muon, Mass Deficiency, Special Theory of Relativity , Electric Charge, Metric Change Published 2008 No. of pages 25 Abstract We show that, just like the gravitational field, the electric field too slows down the internal mechanism of a clock, entering into interaction with the field. This approach explains substantially, the retardation of the decay of the muon, bound to a nucleus. Overview A negative Muon captured into an atomic orbit around a nucleus lives measurably longer than a free muon, and the retardation grows with atomic number. Tolga Yarman argues that this is not a specialised nuclear-physics effect requiring phase-space corrections and Coulomb factors, but a direct consequence of energy conservation combined with the mass–energy equivalence: a bound particle has given up its binding energy, so its rest mass is smaller, so whatever internal mechanism sets its clock rate must run slower by exactly the same fraction. Decay is treated as a clock, and the muon's half-life is the reading of that clock. The claim generalises: any field a particle is bound in — gravitational, electric, magnetic — slows its internal dynamics by the ratio of binding energy to rest energy. Yarman stresses that the same red-shift factor that General Relativity obtains for a particle in a gravitational field is here recovered "through a totally different set up," without assuming the Equivalence Principle . His conclusion is that "electric charge too, slows down clocks," and that this "induces the metric change nearby a nucleus, just like the metric change nearby a gravitational source."
