Title On the Forces and Knowledge of the World Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Joseph J Smulsky Published 2012 Journal Proceedings of the NPA Volume 9 No. of pages 3 Pages 569-571 Abstract The paper continued clarifications on the validity of Newton's laws of gravity. Opponents of the law and of Newtonian mechanics led a number of arguments in their favor. Based on the solution of a number of problems by the program Galactica it is show that these arguments are untenable. Overview Joseph J. Smulsky, of the Institute of Earth's Cryosphere of the Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences, replies to Charles W. Lucas, Jr., who had argued that Newton's law of gravitation is defective and should be replaced by a universal electrodynamic force. The exchange had run through several papers in Foundations of Science , and this contribution answers Lucas's latest round. Smulsky opens by noting that he had previously shown Lucas's tables — offered as evidence against Newtonian gravity — to follow directly from Newton's law, and that Lucas raised no objection to the demonstration. He treats that point as settled and turns to the eight phenomena Lucas holds Newtonian gravity unable to explain: orbits not lying in the Sun's equatorial plane, the Titius–Bode spacing, the expansion of bodies and galaxies, the source of gravitational force, the Hubble redshift, the microwave background, redshift quantisation, and action at a distance. His answers rest on numerical work with Galactica, an n-body integrator he has developed and made publicly available.
