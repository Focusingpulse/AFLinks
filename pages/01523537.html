Norbert Feist Born ( 1941-10-02 ) October 2, 1941 (age 84) Residence Gersthofen D-86368, Germany Nationality German Known for An acoustic analogue of the Michelson–Morley experiment ; ether theory; criticism of special relativity Scientific career Fields Chemical engineering ; electrochemistry Norbert Feist (born 2 October 1941) is a German chemical engineer and amateur experimental physicist based in Gersthofen, Bavaria. He is distinctive among the researchers recorded on this wiki for having built and run experiments rather than argued from theory alone: his best-known work is an acoustic analogue of the Michelson–Morley experiment , performed with an ultrasonic range finder mounted on the roof of a moving car. His position is that light possesses an inertial component, that the two-way velocity of light is isotropic in a moving system without any need for Lorentz contraction, and that a luminiferous ether and an absolute frame of reference exist. Biography Feist was born in 1941 in Germany and worked in the chemical industry from 1966 to 1996, his main fields being electrochemistry and chemical engineering. Physics has been his hobby since 1974, concentrated on a single question — ether and/or special relativity. He lives in Gersthofen, near Augsburg. No degree or institution is recorded in any source located for this article; the World Science Database gives his occupation but not his qualifications. Work The 2001 papers Feist's first published statements were two German-language preprints deposited on arXiv in April 2001, both under General Physics. A speech for the luminiferous ether ( Plädoyer für den Äther ), arXiv physics/0104047, submitted 16 April 2001 and revised on 20 April — 20 pages with 15 figures. The propagation of light and sound in moving systems ( Über die Lichtgeschwindigkeit in bewegten Systemen ), arXiv physics/0104059, submitted 20 April 2001 — 25 pages.
