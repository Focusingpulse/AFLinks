Title Tidal Asymmetry Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Raymond H Gallucci Keywords Tides, Symmetry, Gravity, Barycenter, Moon Published 2016 Journal Proceedings of the CNPS Volume 10 No. of pages 10 Abstract The Earth’s diametrically opposed, presumably symmetric, tides are due to the Moon’s differential gravitational force varying across the Earth. This is not intuitively obvious, but becomes clear when the physics is examined mathematically. The presumed symmetry is due to an approximation that holds when the radius of the affect ed body (e.g., the Earth) is much less than its center-to-center distance from the affect ing body (e.g., the Moon). The exact solution indicates an asymmetry, which becomes more pronounced as the assumption loses its applicability. Overview Tidal Asymmetry is a ten-page paper by Raymond H Gallucci , printed in volume 10 of the Proceedings of the CNPS for 2016. Its claim is unusual in this collection in being straightforwardly correct: the two tidal bulges raised on the Earth by the Moon are not equal, and the symmetry usually asserted for them is an artefact of a leading-order approximation. Gallucci sets out the standard account first, quoting it at length.
