Overview The Gravitational Stellar Constant Allows for an Improved Description of Stellar and Black Hole Dynamics is a four-page paper by Thierry De Mees , published in the General Science Journal in 2011. It builds on his earlier work in "gyrotation", his term for the gravitational analogue of magnetism. The starting claim is a relation he says holds for the Sun, connecting its rotation frequency to its mass and radius through G and c — his equation (1), ν eq ⇐ Gm Sun / c ² R eq ², where the arrow marks what he calls unilateral validity. From the stellar life cycle he extrapolates this to any active star, and rewrites it so that the product Gm can be replaced by an expression in the star's angular velocity and radius. This is the "gravitational stellar constant" of the title, and the point of it is to eliminate one parameter. The consequences he draws concern rapidly spinning stars. A fast-rotating white dwarf, he argues, will partially explode, with gyrotational compression holding it together from the equator up to a latitude of about 35°16′ while material above that latitude is lost.
