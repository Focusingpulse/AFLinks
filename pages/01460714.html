Methylene Blue

· archived # 1,460,714
Methylene blue is an FDA-approved medication and synthetic dye used primarily to treat methemoglobinemia, a blood disorder, and as a surgical stain. It acts as an antioxidant and metabolic enhancer, with potential, though limited, evidence for anti-aging and cognitive benefits. Side effects include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and potential serotonin syndrome if taken with antidepressants. Key Aspects of Methylene Blue Medical Uses: It is used to treat methemoglobinemia, cyanide poisoning, and vasoplegic shock (low blood pressure). It acts as a diagnostic stain during surgery and was historically used for malaria. Mechanism of Action: It functions as a redox agent, facilitating electron transfer in the mitochondrial electron transport chain, which can boost energy production and act as an antioxidant. Potential Benefits & Hype: While some studies suggest it may improve memory and reduce neurobehavioral impairment in animal models, its use as a "brain booster" or anti-aging supplement is not widely supported by high-quality human evidence. Risks and Side Effects: Common side effects include gastrointestinal issues (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea). More serious risks include potential for serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs/SNRIs, and high doses can cause neurological complications.
