GC2026-STP052 Observations of Solar Flare Activity and Charged Particle Flux Variations during Strong Geomagnetic Storms on Ionosfera-M Satellites in 2025 – 2026 Sergey Svertilov1 , Vitaly Bogomolov1 , Andrey Bogomolov1 , Oleg Marchenko1 , Irina Myagkova1 , Ivan Yashin1 , Aleksandr Chernyshov2 1 Lomonosov Moscow State University, Moscow, Russia 2 Space Research Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow, Russia sis@coronas.ru A constellation of four Ionosfera-M spacecraft is currently deployed, designed, among other goals, to monitor physical processes in the Earth's atmosphere, ionosphere, and magnetosphere, as well as to patrol solar flare activity. Each of these spacecraft carries the SG/1 and SPER/1 instruments. The SG/1 instrument is a scintillation gamma-ray spectrometer based on cylindrical CsI(Tl) crystals with a volume of 8 x 8 cm³. It is designed to monitor gamma rays from the Earth's atmosphere, as well as solar flare activity in the hard X-rays and gamma rays. The SPER/1 instrument is a plasma and energetic radiation spectrometer designed to measure fluxes and differential energy spectra of low-energy electrons and protons in the energy range of 0.05–20 keV, as well as charged particles of medium and high energies, i.e. electrons in the energy range of ~0.15–10 MeV and protons in the energy range of ~2.0–160.0 MeV.
