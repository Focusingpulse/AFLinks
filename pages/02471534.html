FOUR TABLES WERE FILLED Club Members Appeared as “Rosencrantz and Guildenstern” Between the Other Acts. The disturbance in upper Broadway this morning was caused by the Twelfth Royster of the Strollers Club at Browne’s chop house. It began about 1 o’clock, and lasted until exhausted. The affair was a cross between an east side political convention and a meeting of the Paterson Anarchists. The programme was drinks interspersed with efforts by actors from the various theatres and a play written by W. S. Gilbert. It wore the title of “Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.” The author calls it a tragic episode in three tableaux. It was. All the players were amateurs, J. Cheever Goodwin being the star of the production. As an actor he is a wonder, and what a satisfaction it would have been to hundred of members of “the profession” whom he has accused of murdering his lines if they could have seen him as the librettist who acts. As it was a tableau production, most of the players became ossified so they could do the work properly. Score to Move Them. It took a score of waiters to move them. As tableau artists, they are dreams of perfection. Senator Chauncey Depew and Bourke Cockran was expected, but they were busy making political speeches and could not come. But W. K.
