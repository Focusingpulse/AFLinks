MolPhot2026-main015 Surface and Interface Engineering of Nanosized Oxides for Enhanced Photocatalytic and Photothermocatalytic Activity Xintong Zhang1 1 Northeast Normal University, China Ponencia invitada xtzhang@nenu.edu.cn The surface of photocatalysts plays as site where photocatalytic reactions occur, and significantly influences the reaction pathways and charge recombination. Rational design of the photocatalyst surface is therefore essential for the enhancement of photocatalysis.1 This issue is even more important for photothermocatalysis of which the effective coupling of photo-stimulated and thermally activated processes is required.1 In this talk, I will present our recent works on surface and interface engineering of nanosized oxide materials, focusing on TiO2 and CeO2 . A simple and scalable solution plasma processing (SPP) technique is developed to introduce hydrogen shallow donors to the surface of Ov -rich yellow TiO2 , which increases the CO2 photoreduction by 300 times at elevated temperature (393K).2 Surface amorphorization of TiO2 photocatalysts, induced by ultrasonics,3 and SPP,4 respectively uplifts the surface electronic states and prolongs the charge separation, and consequently enhances the performance of photocatalytic hydrogen evolution. In the case of CeO2 , we prepare noble metal single/dual atom catalysts on CeO2 nanosheets by SPP.7 Under Xe lamp irradiation, Au1 Rh1 /CeO2 reached over 50% CO conversion rate at ambient temperature for the water–gas shift reaction.
