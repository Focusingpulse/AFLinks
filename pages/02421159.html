Jensen Residence Santa Barbara, CA, United States Nationality USA Known for The Unidirectional Transformer; New Energy Scientific career Fields Inventor ; electrical engineering Paul Raymond Jensen is an American independent inventor, active in the new energy community in the mid-1990s, who claimed to have built a transformer that delivers more power to its load than is drawn from its primary supply. He called it the Unidirectional Transformer (UDT), on the grounds that the magnetic reaction of the load current does not act back on the primary circuit. He contributed two papers to the 2nd International Symposium on New Energy at Denver in May 1994 — " Examination and Analysis of the Series Resonant LC Circuit " and " The Unidirectional Transformer " — and published a further account of the device in Fulcrum later that year. He gave the design away, publishing full construction details and asking explicitly that no one patent it. Biography Very little is recorded about Jensen beyond the work itself. The Institute for New Energy device database gives his last known address as Santa Barbara, California, and the papers carry no institutional affiliation, degree or employer. He appears in the record between 1994 and 1996 and then not at all; the Institute's entries for both of his devices were last modified in July 1997 and stand with most fields blank. His own writing gives some sense of the man. He states that he developed the UDT alone, "without benefit of any knowledge of other free-energy devices, if they exist", releases the design without restriction — "Please feel free to use this information as you desire. However, I hope that no one will attempt to patent and control this type of transformer" — and closes on the note that gives his work its urgency: The time on Planet Earth is 15 minutes before midnight; there is no time left to waste.
