Kelvin scale

· archived # 1,460,214
The Kelvin scale is the main temperature scale used in science all over the world. It is different from Celsius because it does not start at the freezing point of water. It begins at absolute zero which is the lowest possible temperature in nature. Absolute zero is the point where all motion of particles becomes as small as it can ever be. On the Kelvin scale this point is written as 0 K. Scientists use this scale because it is based on the true physical behavior of heat and energy. The Kelvin scale is created by shifting the Celsius scale downward by 273.15 degrees. This means that 0 degrees Celsius equals 273.15 K. From that point onward the steps on both scales are the same size. This scale is very important in physics chemistry and space science. It helps scientists measure gas behavior energy transfer and atomic motion with great accuracy. Many scientific laws only work correctly when Kelvin is used. Because it starts at absolute zero and follows natural energy limits the Kelvin scale is recognized as the international standard for scientific temperature measurement across the world.
