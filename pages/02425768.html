Food & Function View Article Online PAPER Cite this: DOI: 10.1039/c5fo01475a View Journal Morinda citrifolia edible leaf extract enhanced immune response against lung cancer Swee-Ling Lim,a Yong-Meng Goh,b M. Mustapha Noordin,b Heshu S. Rahman,a Hemn H. Othman,b Nurul Ain Abu Bakara and Suhaila Mohamed*a Lung cancer causes 1.4 million deaths annually. In the search for functional foods as complementary therapies against lung cancer, the immuno-stimulatory properties of the vegetable Morinda citrifolia leaves were investigated and compared with the anti-cancer drug erlotinib. Lung tumour-induced BALB/c mice were fed with 150 mg kg−1 or 300 mg kg−1 body weight of the leaf extract, or erlotinib (50 mg kg−1 body-weight) for 21 days. The 300 mg kg−1 body weight extract signiﬁcantly (and dose-dependently) suppressed lung tumour growth; the extract worked more eﬀectively than the 50 mg kg−1 body weight erlotinib treatment. The extract signiﬁcantly increased blood lymphocyte counts, and spleen tissue B cells, T cells and natural killer cells, and reduced the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) which is a lung adenocarcinoma biomarker. The extract also suppressed the cyclooxygenase 2 (COX2) inﬂammatory markers, and enhanced the tumour suppressor gene ( phosphatase and tensin homolog, PTEN). It inhibited tumour growth cellular gene (transformed mouse 3T3 cell double minute 2 (MDM2), V-raf-leukemia viral oncogene 1 (RAF1), and mechanistic target of rapamycin (MTOR)) mRNA expression in the tumours. The extract is rich in scopoletin and epicatechin, which are the main phenolic compounds.
