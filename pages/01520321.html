Published 1997 Journal Apeiron Volume 4 Number 2-3 No. of pages 6 Pages 48-54 Abstract The structure of the Poincare group gives, under all conditions, an equation of field helicity which reduces to the Maxwell equations and also gives cyclic relations between field components. If the underlying symmetry of special relativity is represented by the Poincare group, it follows that the Maxwell equations and the cyclic equations are both products of special relativity itself, and both stem from the equation of helicity. This means that the symmetry of special relativity demands the existence of longitudinal solutions of Maxwell's equations under all topological conditions. In particular, the fundamental spin component of the electromagnetic field is B(3), a longitudinal magnetic flux density which is free of phase and which is a topological invariant. Overview Myron Evans was a Welsh chemical physicist with a substantial mainstream record — a Junior Research Fellowship at Wolfson College, Oxford, several hundred papers on molecular dynamics and spectroscopy, and the editorship of two volumes of Advances in Chemical Physics — before he became known for the claim this paper defends. Published in Apeiron in 1997, it argues that special relativity itself requires a longitudinal component of the electromagnetic field , which Evans calls B(3) . The argument is presented as one of pure symmetry. If the symmetry underlying electrodynamics is that of the Poincaré group, and if the group's generators are matched to field quantities in the right way, then both Maxwell's equations and a set of "cyclic relations" between field components follow from a single equation of helicity.
