An article by Nikola Tesla, the electrician, on ' The Problem of Increasing Human Energy, with Special Reference to Harnessing the Sun's Energy ,' published in a recent number of the Century , has aroused sharp criticism from men versed in electrical science; one of these criticisms, understood to come from the pen of a leading professor of electrical science in this city, is published in Science to-day. A summary of it follows: The present writer has lately gone over the text of the article, pen in hand, and herewith presents a small portion of his notes. They seem to him to show that the article in question is composed, essentially, of three different kinds of writing. The first kind describes Mr. Tesla's experiments in electricity and shows what he has already accomplished. . . . . . . . The second describes what Mr. Tesla expects to accomplish in the future. His prophecies are no sanguine, in many cases, that even a general reader may presume to compare them with the comparatively small things already accomplished, and to point out how very unlikely it is that any great part of his expectations will be realized within any reasonable future — within Mr. Tesla's own lifetime, for example.
