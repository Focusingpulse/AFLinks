Title Low Level Measurements Author(s) Leslee A Kulba Keywords Low level measurement, noise floor, guarding, thermoelectric EMF, triboelectric Published 2010 Journal Electric Spacecraft Journal Volume 48 Pages 23-26 Low Level Measurements is a four-page article by Leslee A Kulba , published in 2010 in issue 48 of the Electric Spacecraft Journal , the journal's final double issue. It is the seventh part of the ten-part handbook on perpetual motion and its testing that Kulba, the journal's technical editor, contributed to that issue, and at four pages it is one of the two longest practical articles in the set. The text of the article has not been recoverable, and this page describes its subject and its place in the series rather than its argument. The subject Low-level measurement is the art of measuring voltages, currents, charges and resistances small enough that the measurement apparatus is a significant part of the phenomenon. It has a substantial professional literature — the standard work is Keithley Instruments' Low Level Measurements Handbook , which shares this article's title and was in its sixth edition at the time — and it is relevant here because the anomalies claimed in this field are characteristically small differences between large numbers, which is the regime in which the errors below are decisive. The noise floor There is a floor below which no amount of care helps, and it is set by physics rather than by the instrument. Johnson–Nyquist noise, present in any resistance at any temperature above absolute zero, has a root-mean-square voltage of √(4 kTRB ) — about 1.3 microvolts across a one-megohm resistor in a 1 Hz bandwidth at room temperature, and growing as the square root of both resistance and bandwidth. Shot noise sets a corresponding limit on current measurement, and 1/ f or flicker noise dominates at low frequencies, which is exactly where slow drifting measurements live.
