Unlike the Big Bang Model, which assumes a practically infinite, homogeneous, and isotropic Universe expanding from an initial singularity, World-Universe Cosmology envisions a three-dimensional finite, boundless observable World as a Patchwork Quilt composed of ≳10^3 Luminous Superclusters that formed independently in different regions and at different cosmological times. Presented by Vladimir Netchitailo, Ian Cowan hosting. Summary Vladimir S Netchitailo 's tenth presentation to the society, drawn from a paper accepted for the April 2026 issue of the Journal of High Energy Physics, Gravitation and Cosmology — his forty-second there. The argument is that the Hubble tension is not an anomaly to be resolved but the expected consequence of the structure his World-Universe Cosmology attributes to the visible world. The tension Determinations of the Hubble constant between 1994 and 2025 span 66.6 to 76.9 km/s/Mpc, with disagreements far exceeding the quoted uncertainties and no dependence on any single data set or group. Netchitailo took the 2019 Kavli Institute workshop's conclusion — that the mismatch between early- and late-universe determinations may require extending the standard model — as the starting point. The patchwork quilt Where the big bang model assumes a nearly infinite, homogeneous and isotropic universe expanding from an initial singularity, WUC describes a three-dimensional finite boundless world: the hypersphere of a four-dimensional nucleus expanding along a fourth spatial dimension at c , which produces cosmic expansion by stretching without requiring dark energy.
