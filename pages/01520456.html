Title Is Gravity Just the Electrostatic Force? Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Franklin Hu Keywords gravity electrostatic force Published 2013 No. of pages 2 Abstract This a paper explores the idea that gravity is just a fairly straight forward application of the well known electrostatic force. The usual objections to an electrostatic gravity are refuted and additional evidence supporting gravity as the electrostatic force are provided. It is shown that gravity isn't the mysterious force we think it is. Gravity is simply the electrostatic force. Overview Is Gravity Just the Electrostatic Force? is a two-page paper by Franklin Hu , presented at the 20th Natural Philosophy Alliance Conference in July 2013. Its proposal is direct. Astronomical bodies lose electrons to space and so acquire a small net positive charge. A positively charged object attracts neutral matter, not merely negative charge — the comb-and-paper demonstration — and Hu identifies the mechanism as dielectrophoresis: the field orients the dipoles in the neutral body so that their negative ends are nearer and therefore more strongly pulled. Because every atom is a combination of positive and negative charges, the force couples to the number of charges and hence to mass, which Hu offers as the reason gravitational mass tracks the amount of matter. He supports this with the fair-weather atmospheric field of about 120 V/m, and with lightning, which he reads as negative charge flowing from cloud base to a positively charged Earth. Because a spherical shell of charge acts as a point charge at its centre, a very slight and very diffuse imbalance spread through the Earth produces a strong field with no large local charge anywhere. Hu then takes up three standard objections and answers each.
