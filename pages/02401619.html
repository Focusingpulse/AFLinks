GC2026-STP076 Response of the Radiation Dose Rate Model to Extreme Solar Events of November 10–11, 2025 Kirill Kholodkov1,2 , Vyacheslav Burov1 , Andrey Kondratov1,2 1 Fedorov Institute of Applied Geophysics, Russia 2 Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth of the Russian Academy of Sciences, Russia k.kholodkov@space-weather.ru Exposure to ionizing radiation at cruising altitudes for civil aviation flights poses a well-recognized occupational hazard for aircraft crews and frequent passengers. Galactic cosmic radiation (Galactic Cosmic Rays, GCR) typically contributes a background dose rate of 4–8 μSv/h, but extreme solar energetic particle events can elevate this dose rate by two to three orders of magnitude within minutes [1, 2]. To address this risk, Amendment 82 to Annex 3 of the Convention on International Civil Aviation: Meteorological Service for International Air Navigation [3] mandates the issuance of space weather advisories whenever forecasted or observed effective dose rates exceed 30 μSv/h (MODERATE) or 80 μSv/h (SEVERE) in designated atmospheric regions. Solar activity was low at the end of October, featuring only isolated C-class flares. By November 3, active region No. 4274 had rotated back onto the northeast limb of the Sun. This region expanded rapidly and generated multiple M- and X-class flares, along with halo coronal mass ejections (CMEs). The peak occurred with an X5.2 flare on November 11 from 09:55–10:04 UTC, which was accompanied by a powerful proton event. The first protons exceeding 500 MeV were detected at 10:15 UTC.
