The Mysteries in Goethe’s “Faust”: Esoteric [ 1 ] In one of the versions Goethe intended for his *Faust*, in the second part, at the end of the third act, Mephistopheles—who had worn the mask of Phorkyas in that act—was to step forward onto the proscenium, drop the mask, step down from his buskins, and deliver a sort of epilogue. As the stage direction—which has now lost its meaning—tells us, the intention was for this epilogue to hint at how Faust’s final appearance should be interpreted.
