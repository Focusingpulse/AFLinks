Toivo Jaakkola Born ( 1941-03-11 ) March 11, 1941 Pelkosenniemi, Finland Died May 24, 1995 (1995-05-24) (aged 54) Residence Finland Nationality Finnish Known for Electrogravitational coupling; a Machian, statistically stable universe Scientific career Fields Extragalactic astronomy; cosmology Institutions University of Oulu; University of Helsinki Toivo Jaakkola (11 March 1941 – 24 May 1995) was a Finnish astronomer who worked on extragalactic astronomy and cosmology, and who spent his career arguing that the redshift of distant galaxies is not evidence of expansion but the mechanism by which a stable universe holds itself together. He was one of the founders of Apeiron in 1987 and contributed to it repeatedly. Most of what is known of his life comes from the obituary written by Jean-Claude Pecker of the Collège de France, who had sat on his doctoral jury, and published in Apeiron the year after his death. Education Student's examination — University of Oulu, 1960 Phil. Cand. (roughly equivalent to a Master of Science) — 1967 Doctorate — Université Pierre et Marie Curie, Paris, 1977, for a thesis titled Études sur la nature des décalages vers le rouge dans les galaxies , awarded the mention très honorable Biography Jaakkola was born at Pelkosenniemi in eastern Finland in March 1941, close to the battle lines of the Winter War. He was an assistant at the Observatory of the University of Oulu from 1966 to 1969, and then at the Observatory of the University of Helsinki from 1970 to 1988, where he taught extragalactic astronomy and cosmology from 1971. He became a docent of astronomy at Helsinki in 1978 and at Oulu in 1979, and was acting Professor of Astronomy at Helsinki in 1979–80.
