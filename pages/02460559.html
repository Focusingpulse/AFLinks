According to accepted teaching signals cannot be transmitted faster than light. Some even believe that Einstein's Theory of Relativity could be broken if light moved faster. At the University of Cologne physicists have begun to shake up this theory. The spectacular experiment took place in a laboratory of the second Institute of Physics. Prof. Dr. Nimtz and Mozart's 40th symphony are our main protagonists in this experiment. At first the music is put piggyback onto a carrier wave, in this case a carrier wave of ten gigahertz. The carrier wave travels over a cable into a funneling antenna then a little through the air and into the reception antenna. It follows another short thin tunnel - where the signal should stop. According to the laws of physics the microwave does not fit through the tunnel, it ought to be reflected back. Despite this the signal mysteriously slips through the tunnel.
