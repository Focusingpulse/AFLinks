GC2026-PERM038 Magnetic fabric of loess deposits: physical principles, experimental data and paleoclimatic implication Ekaterina Kulakova1 , Varvara Dudanova1 1 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences liverpool.town.uk@gmail.com Magnetic fabric, i.e., the orientation of magnetic particles in an oriented sample, is formed in sedimentary rocks during deposition under the influence of several factors and can therefore reflect the conditions of their formation. In loess-paleosol sequences, which serve as continental climatic paleo-archives, the primary factors controlling the development of magnetic fabric are gravity, the direction of dust-transporting winds, and the geomagnetic field. Magnetic techniques applied to loess-paleosol sequences are particularly insightful for investigating dust transport dynamics and variations in the intensity of hydroclimate. Anisotropy of magnetic susceptibility (AMS) in particular measures the magnetic fabric, which can provide insights into palaeowind directions and post￾depositional processes. However, for reliable paleoclimatic reconstructions it is necessary to accurately understand the physical basis and mechanisms of formation of the magnetic fabric in loess. This report investigates the dual origin of the magnetic signal in loess deposits, distinguishing between wind￾aligned particles that contribute to the magnetic fabric and field-aligned particles that carry the natural remanent magnetization.
