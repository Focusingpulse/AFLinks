GC2026-PERM056 Paleomagnetic Evidence for Controls on Quaternary Sedimentation in the Deep Arctic Basin Daria Elkina1 , Alexey Piskarev1,2 , Tansylu Khosnullina1,2 , Irina Bazhenova1,2 , Kirill Getman1,2 1 Gramberg All-Russian State Research Institute for Geology and Mineral Resources of the Ocean (VNIIOkeangeologia), Russia 2 St Petersburg University, St. Petersburg, Russia darielfly@gmail.com For the deep part of the Arctic Ocean, the problem of determining the age of the upper sedimentary section is particularly acute. Various methods of absolute and relative dating applied to sediment cores collected here do not yield unambiguous results and do not correlate with one another. Our paleomagnetic studies of marine sediment cores up to 8 m long, recovered in the Amerasian Basin during a series of high-latitude expeditions to the Mendeleev Ridge, supported by results from radiometric methods, reveal the presence of a boundary between the Quaternary and the Pliocene [1], confirming earlier results reported by David Clark and colleagues for the Alpha–Mendeleev Rise [2]. In contrast, while the number of sediment cores collected in the Amerasian Basin of the Arctic Ocean is already approaching several hundred, the cores from the Eurasian Basin remain scarce. During the “North Pole-41” expedition, in the course of the drift of the self-propelled ice platform “North Pole” [3], numerous cores from the Eurasian Basin, 2 to 4.5 m long, were collected.
