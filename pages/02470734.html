On Sunday, November 11, a notable gathering of scientific men witnessed the accomplishment of an achievement which marks a new milestone in the history of electric marvels. A microphone and an almost microscopic mirror working together translated a photograph record into terms of light. The microphone carries the needle, which passes over the graduated surface of the picture to be sent. A tiny arc lamp in a bulb is an important part of the mechanism. It was made in England especially for experimental purposes. The tiny arc is so intense that it gives 104 candle-power at what is virtually a point. Such a little lamp lasts only 10 hours and costs $5. The photograph of the Indian chief was sent by wire from the office of a newspaper in St. Louis to the office of a well-known New York newspaper. After the receipt of the picture M. Belin sent two photographs by wire to St. Louis. The tests were pronounced highly successful.
