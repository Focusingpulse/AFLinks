Liperovskaya3 1 Institute of Earthquake Prediction Theory and Mathematical Geophysics, Russian Academy of Sciences 2 Institute of Marine Geology and Geophysics of the Far Eastern Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences 3 O.Yu. Schmidt Institute of Physics of the Earth, Russian Academy of Sciences liper@ifz.ru A catastrophe (bifurcation) is understood as something unexpected and terrible. Earthquakes are not only a spectacular example of bifurcation but also a good example of research. Earthquakes are numerous and have been studied for a long time. The global scientific community has come a long way in this area, from initial hopes of receiving of a forecast, through the idea that forecasting was impossible [1], to real-time monitoring that the best forecasting algorithms yield results better than random guessing [2; and others]. The main obstacles to obtain an effective earthquake forecasting are believe to be strong background variability of the seismic regime, the absence of the developed physical model of the seismic process and the lack of data [2; 3; and others]. Strong background variability complicates the timely identification of forecast anomalies, and the absence of a developed physical model of the earthquake complicates the selection of informative forecast features.
