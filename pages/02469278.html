Included With Notables Schenectady Gazette March 2nd, 1932 Thomson, Whitney, Langmuir, Coolidge Among 13 Invited to Opening of Patent Office Among the 13 most notable American inventors and scientists of inventive talents now living, as reflected by invitations to the opening of the new patent office at Washington on April 11, are four men in the organization of the General Electric Company: Professor Elihu Thomson, Dr. Willis R. Whitney, Dr. William D. Coolidge and Dr. Irving Langmuir. The group of 13 will be entertained at the ceremonies by a committee of national scope which is preparing the program for the occasion. Professor Thomson, who heads the General Electric contingent, is one of the most conspicuous of America’s electrical scientists. He alone holds more than 700 patents for various inventions, chiefly electrical. Dr. Whitney is a vice-president of General Electric and director of its famous research laboratory. He himself holds a number of patents, and directs an institution which has provided the patent office with an immense amount of business. Drs. Coolidge and Langmuir are associate directors with Dr. Whitney in the research laboratory. Dr. Coolidge is the inventor of ductile tungsten wire and of the portable and high-tension types of X-ray tubes.
