The Everest Hypothesis: mate selection for replicative fidelity Patrick D Shaw Stewart Newbury, Berkshire, UK. E-mail: patrick.ss.home@gmail.com 2 February 2022 Abstract Mutations must occasionally arise in all genes, including the genes that encode the proteins that replicate genetic material – the nucleic acid polymerases and their associated proteins. Some of these mutations must reduce the fidelity of DNA replication (or RNA replication in RNA viruses), without actually being fatal. Lineages that replicate with reduced fidelity must therefore exist in all biological kingdoms, although we do not know how common they are. A fundamental problem at the heart of evolutionary biology is, therefore, to explain how high-fidelity replication originally evolved, and what selective pressures now maintain it.
