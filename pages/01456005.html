Baron von Steuben

· archived # 1,456,005
In 1777, the American Revolution was dying. The soldiers at Valley Forge were starving, barefoot, and lived in their own filth. They were less an army and more a desperate gathering of men waiting for the end. Washington knew his troops were brave, but they were not professionals. They couldn't march. They couldn't use their bayonets. They didn't even know how to set up a camp that didn't breed disease. Then, a man named Baron von Steuben arrived in camp. He was a Prussian officer who claimed to be a Lieutenant General. In reality, he was a Captain who had been cast out of his own country and was nearly broke. But the Baron was a magician of the battlefield. He didn't speak a word of English when he arrived. He had to rely on a young aide to translate his instructions into shouts the men could understand. He didn't stay in a warm tent like other high-ranking officers. He got down in the mud with the farm boys and the tailors. He barked orders until his voice was raw. He saw their hunger. He saw their rags. He saw their resolve. But he knew resolve alone wouldn't win a war against the most powerful empire on earth. He changed how they slept. He changed how they marched. He changed how they fought. Baron von Steuben focused on the basics. He taught them that a bayonet wasn't just for cooking meat over a fire. It was a weapon of war.
