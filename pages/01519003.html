1979 Brillet-Hall Experiment Might Have detected Aether Watch this session on YouTube Talk Type Live session Speaker James Marsen Date 18 July 2026 Platform YouTube live stream Recording Recording 1979 Brillet-Hall Experiment Might Have detected Aether was a live session presented by James Marsen on 18 July 2026 in the John Chappell Natural Philosophy Society live session series. The 1979 Brillet and Hall experiment was a precision test of the isotropy of space. It reported a signal that was consistent with the velocity of Earth's rotation with respect to the Earth Centered Inertial Reference frame (the ECI). It was dismissed as a systematic but never properly explained. It is proposed to repeat the experiment with modern equipment to test the systematic conclusion. How and why it should be repeated. Presented by Jim Marsen and hosted by Ian Cowan. Summary The presentation Jim Marsen undertook to give after his May session was criticised for talking around the Brillet–Hall experiment rather than presenting it. This time he set out the argument from the beginning, surveyed the experiments that have followed, and described the repeat he wants someone to perform. The step that was skipped Michelson and Morley in 1887 looked for the Earth's 30 km/s orbital motion through what everyone assumed was a universal aether, and found nothing above the sensitivity of their apparatus — about a fortieth of a fringe. Marsen's central historical point is that a null below the threshold of detection was converted into a null to infinite precision , which is what Lorentz's contraction and then Einstein's 1905 paper were constructed to explain. Nobody had shown the second thing. Following Petr Beckmann 's Einstein Plus Two of 1986, and Ching-Chuan Su's later local aether model, Marsen takes the medium to be tied to the Earth's gravitational field — a halo that travels with the Earth but does not rotate with it.
