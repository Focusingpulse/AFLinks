Title The Revolutionary Plasma Power Technology of Josef Papp Read in full Link to paper Author(s) Barry Springer Keywords Papp, plasma, plasmic transition, overunity, Rohner, Inteligentry, noble gas Published 2012 No. of pages 50 Abstract In 1972, Josef Papp patented a Method for Converting Atomic Energy into Utilizable Kinetic Energy. His method modified standard internal combustion engines by eliminating the intake and exhaust systems and filling the cylinders with a mix of noble gases (Helium, Neon, Argon, Krypton and Xenon) which were excited by an arc discharge that converted the normally inert gasses into a plasma. The plasma transition produced an enormous amount of energy, measured to be 80 times the energy input that excited the gases to plasma. Papp appears to have demonstrated a clean, powerful new energy technology that could become the power source for our future. Overview Barry Springer presents, as a fifty-slide NPA web seminar of November 2012, the case for Josef Papp's noble-gas plasma engine as a working over-unity power source about to enter production. Papp, born in Hungary in 1933 and emigrating to the United States via Canada in 1957, developed the device through the 1960s and patented a "Method for Converting Atomic Energy into Utilizable Kinetic Energy" in 1972. The engine is a modified internal combustion engine with intake and exhaust removed, its sealed cylinders filled with a mixture of helium, neon, argon, krypton and xenon. An arc discharge excites the gases into a plasma, and the "plasma transition" is said to release around eighty times the electrical energy used to trigger it. Springer notes that the mixture also contained small quantities of thorium, rubidium and phosphorus, "but insufficient for known nuclear power reactions". The framing is Nassim Taleb's black swan — something that "lies outside the realm of regular expectations, because nothing in the past can convincingly point to its possibility".
