Saturday Science Chat Summary An open chat hosted by Franklin Hu with James Keene , Ian Cohen, Alistair Riddoch , Carl and Bob Gray . It began with the clocks — Ian had arrived an hour early, the American and European changeovers being a fortnight apart — and with his account of daylight saving as a German wartime measure of 1916 to save coal, copied by Britain a month later. From the clocks it went to time itself and stayed there. Is time quantized? Keene holds that space, time and energy are all quantized, and that this is what makes his binary mechanics work: with those three quantities discrete, the quantities usually presented to students as fundamental physical constants become derivable rather than merely measured. He put the criterion plainly — by their fruits ye shall know them — and treated the quantization as a postulate justified by its utility, declining to go behind it. Asked why the world should be quantized, he said that is where science shades into philosophy and religion, and that he would leave it to others; he noted that Bill Lucas , asked the same, answers that God made it so, and that this is not where he himself will go. Hu wanted a physical cause rather than a postulate, and offered one: that we are inside a computer. Nothing in a computer happens faster than the clock; each tick fixes a state; a bit is here or there and never half-way. Keene was sympathetic but said this is only another way of saying that space, time and energy are quantized.
