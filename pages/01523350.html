Title Lorentz Invariance Read in full Read in full Author(s) Ricardo L Carezani Keywords Autodynamics , Lorentz invariance, Maxwell's equations, Lorentz transformation , electrodynamics Published Society for the Advancement of Autodynamics Website https://www.autodynamics.org/lorentz-invariance/ "Lorentz Invariance" is a short white paper by Ricardo L Carezani answering a question from a physicist at the University of Virginia: "I glanced at the page. It seemed interesting, but I see no mention of AD allowing Maxwell's equations to remain invariant. Did I miss this??" Overview Carezani's answer is that the question does not arise. Autodynamics discards the two frames in relative motion on which the notion of invariance is built, replaces the Lorentz equations with his own, and treats the phenomenon and the observer as forming a single physical system. Once that is done, he argues, "the concept of 'invariance' becomes meaningless." The argument Invariance as a semantic question Carezani's substantive point is that "invariance" as usually stated conceals a change in what is being described. Maxwell's equations at rest, transformed to a system in relative motion, are the same as saying that the phenomenon they describe is in motion with respect to the observer. Working the classical equations into general form, he notes the standard result: an observer in motion who sees an electric field E will also see a magnetic field H . His question is whether that counts as invariance. "The equations are 'invariant,' but a different phenomenon appears when the observer, or the phenomenon, is in motion. Are the equations 'invariant'?"
