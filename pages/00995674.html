Therefore, the idea that the biofield is created by the mass of DNA in the organism remains a hypothesis. We will first briefly summarize the existing evidence and then briefly review our studies, in which we used computational genomics to reveal the traces of resonance signaling in the genome and provided statistical evidence for this resonance signaling. The idea of the biofield is ancient. Indian yoga teaches about prana as life energy and chakras as centers of resonant vibration. Traditional Chinese medicine teaches about the meridians, which work as conduits of information, and herbal medicine for balancing the energies. Yoga and Chi-Gong teach the art of mastering the energies and redirecting them. Chi-Gong practitioners use body movements to send and direct the biofield energy through the air. Brief history of biofield research Scientific biological experimentation with biofields ran in parallel with the development of electromagnetic science. Franz Mesmer called it "animal magnetism". In his experiments, water and plants were charged by him with magnetism, and hypnotized (mesmerized) people were able to detect the presence of this energy [1,2]. The chemist Jöns Jakob Berzelius proposed in the early 19th century that a regulative force exists within and maintains the functions of living matter [3].
