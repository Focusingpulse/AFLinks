MolPhot2026-main018 FTIR study of lateral interactions in zeolites Yaroslav V. Shergin , Alexey A. Tsyganenko ia.iara@yandex.ru INTRODUCTION: Lateral interactions in the adsorbed layers play important role in adsorption, catalysis, in excitation energy dissipation and other chemical and physical processes at the surfaces. Such interactions, well studied for metals [1] or oxides [2], were thought to be not important for zeolites, because metal cations are weakly bound to the framework and interaction between molecules adsorbed on them cannot be strong. However, it was found that molecules of fluoroform CHF3 adsorbed directly on the bridged oxygen atoms of the framework exhibit not a weak interaction between each other [3]. We have studied this phenomenon in more detail. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION: NO+ ion is a perfect test for the basicity of framework oxygen atoms of zeolites [4]. Using the shifts of absorption band of NO+, we have shown that adsorption of different electron-accepting or proton-donating molecules on framework oxygen atoms results in a serious lowering of oxygen basicity, manifested in the frequency increase of N-O vibration frequency by up to 107 cm-1 for SO2 addition. The shift is not stepwise, but gradual. This means that it is not the result of direct interaction of one molecule with each NO+ ion, but is due to adsorption of molecules at several adjacent oxygen atoms.
