Recursive Ethics A Framework for Preserving Fragile Patterns Author: Aureon R. Vale PREFACE: OVERVIEW This work presents a structural theory of ethics rooted in systems behavior, recursive modeling, and awareness in time. It proposes that ethical action is not defined by intention, rules, or outcomes alone, but by a configuration's capacity to model itself recursively and act in ways that preserve fragile patterns within itself and the systems it affects. The theory draws a distinction between consciousness (coherent behavior in the present) and awareness (a system modeling itself across time). It argues that ethical behavior only becomes possible when all three criteria for awareness are met. This is not a moral code or an ideology. It is a structural lens. Ethics, in this framework, is not based on what is right or wrong in a universal sense, but on what is preserved. This manuscript exists to preserve the structure it describes. It is not an attempt to convince or persuade. It offers a model. What others choose to do with that model is up to them. PURPOSE AND CONTEXT This began as a private inquiry. The question was simple but unresolved. Can ethical behavior be defined structurally, rather than emotionally or morally? Can such a definition apply to both biological and artificial systems? The result is this theory. Recursive Ethics. It is a systems-based view of ethical action. It does not rely on conscience, belief, or authority. It is not universal. It is not complete.
