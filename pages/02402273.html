MolPhot2026-main031 New photonic materials based on arrays of oriented open vase-like microcapsules with walls of complex metal oxides Tatiana A. Moskvina , Khattra Amar , Anastasia A. Mariinskaya , Valery P. Tolstoy v.tolstoy@spbu.ru Hollow microspheres, or microcapsules as they are known, exhibit unique optical and photocatalytic properties and are used, for example, as highly effective light-reflecting and thermal-insulating materials. Open microcapsules occupy a special place among them, providing conditions for the most complete absorption of light within the microcapsules and the most intense diffusion exchange between the microcapsules' interior and their external environment. In [1-3], it was first shown that when an aqueous solution of a metal salt is sprayed onto the surface of an alkali solution, a layer of open microcapsules with a vase-like (VLM) morphology is formed on its surface, and this layer can be transferred to the surface of a planar substrate. Moreover, depending on the transfer method, this layer can consist of two types of VLM arrays, namely, an array in which all the VLMs are oriented with the opening toward the substrate and an array with the opposite orientation. An example of such VLMs with an orientation toward the opposite substrate is shown in Fig. 1. It was also established that the dimensions of the VLMs in this layer can be specified by varying the synthesis conditions, resulting in microcapsules of virtually similar sizes, specified in the range from 2 to 10 μm, and with wall thicknesses of 50-200 nm.
