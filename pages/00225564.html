Our approach aims to significantly improve the efficiency and feasibility of fusion energy, bringing us closer to a sustainable energy future. 1 Introduction Fusion energy, the process of combining light atomic nuclei to form heavier nuclei, has the potential to revolutionize the world’s energy landscape. The most promising fusion reaction involves the isotopes of hydrogen, deuterium (D), and tritium (T), which produce helium and a high-energy neutron. This reaction releases a tremendous amount of energy, primarily in the form of kinetic energy of the neutron, which can be converted into electricity. The primary challenge in harnessing fusion energy lies in confining and sta￾bilizing the high-temperature plasma, a state of matter consisting of charged particles, in which the fusion reactions occur. To achieve the conditions nec￾essary for fusion, the plasma must be heated to temperatures exceeding 100 million degrees Celsius, which is several times hotter than the core of the sun.
