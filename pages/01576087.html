The Fatal Flaw in Special Relativity: A 120-Year-Old Overlooked Contradiction Zhongyi Zhang Los Angeles, CA 90049, U.S.A. zhongyizhang1962@outlook.com ORCID: 0009-0009-5582-465X Abstract Special relativity is built on the postulate that the speed of light is the same in all inertial frames. This paper re-examines Einstein’s train-and-embankment thought experiment and reveals a logical contradiction that has gone unnoticed for over a cen￾tury. In the experiment, a single light pulse from one lightning strike is shown to arrive at two spatially separated points — the midpoint of the embankment and the midpoint of the moving train — at the same time. This is impossible for a finite-speed light pulse. The contradiction arises within the framework of relativity itself, using its own assumptions. No external experiment is needed. The paper concludes that the constancy of the speed of light in all frames leads to a physical impossibility, and that the relativity of simultaneity cannot resolve this self-contradiction. Introduction Special relativity was proposed by Albert Einstein in 1905[1]. It rests on two postulates: 1 1. The laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames of reference (the principle of relativity). 2. The speed of light in vacuum is the same for all observers, regardless of the motion of the source or observer (the principle of light constancy). Special relativity reshaped our understanding of time and space by showing that they are not absolute, but relative and interwoven into a single fabric called spacetime.
