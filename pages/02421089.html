Hotson (25 February 1935 – 11 June 2014) was an American independent researcher, Shakespearean scholar and professional land surveyor, best known for the three-part series Dirac's Equation and the Sea of Negative Energy published in Infinite Energy between 2002 and 2009. His argument is that Dirac's equation was stripped of half its solutions in 1934, and that restoring them yields a unitary theory in which the whole physical universe is built from four kinds of electron. Education Undergraduate study in physics, discontinued — Hotson intended a career in physics but was told that with his "attitude" he had no prospect of completing a graduate programme, and advised to save his money Paris-Sorbonne University, Paris — French literature Biography The family business, and the awkward question Hotson's family, as he put it, was in the "family business" of English literature: his father and uncle both held Harvard doctorates in the subject, and his uncle Leslie Hotson was a celebrated Shakespeare scholar. Hotson nevertheless intended a career in physics, and by his own account could not resist asking awkward questions. The question that ended that intention concerned pair production . He had been taught that conservation of mass–energy is the never-violated foundation of physics: a photon of at least 1.022 MeV creates an electron–positron pair, each with 0.511 MeV of rest energy, the excess appearing as momentum, so that the books balance.
