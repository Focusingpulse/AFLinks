Log in I forgot my password Sympathetic Vibratory Physics &raquo; Aztec whistles Page actions PDF Print Share Send link Aztec whistles Swiss neuroscientists analyze the effects of Aztec whistles: sounds that evoke "extreme fear" A group of neuroscientists in Switzerland has devoted their efforts to study a fascinating and enigmatic instrument of ancient Mesoamerican cultures: the Aztec whistles. This peculiar object, known for producing a sound similar to a human scream of panic, has attracted the attention of not only archaeologists and historians, but also scientists interested in the psychological and neurological effects it can generate. The psychological impact of sound Aztec whistles, crafted mainly in ceramic, emit a sharp, heart-wrenching, and deeply disturbing sound that resembles a human being's scream of terror. According to Swiss experts, this sound has a unique ability to evoke responses of "extreme fear" in those who hear it. In their research, recordings of these instruments were analyzed and various participants were exposed to their sounds in a controlled environment. Through neuroimaging techniques and physiological measurements such as heart rate and galvanic response of the skin, scientists identified that whistles trigger a strong activation in areas of the brain related to fear and stress response, such as the tonsil. This finding coincides with the hypothesis that whistles were not only used as ceremonial instruments, but also as psychological tools in war or ritual contexts. Cultural origins and ritual uses In pre-Hispanic Mexico, these whistles would have played a crucial role during religious ceremonies and war events.
