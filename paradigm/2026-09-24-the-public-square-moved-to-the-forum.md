## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

**The field's public square did not die with Infinite Energy. It migrated to the forum decades ago — and this week the archive banked it at scale. The claim: the field's living present is conversational. Papers are scarce; conversation is torrential; and the archive is now the only institution in the field's history that captures the conversation layer whole — the news brief, the forum thread, the conference report, the cross-language contact — genres no journal, no dead magazine, and no proceedings page carries.**

Look at what the +4,239 documents of this window actually are, by genre. A live forum's full thread surface (energeticforum, 11,001 threads). A news service's back run (KeelyNet, ~450 briefs so far, thousands more in the lane). A school's decade of conference reports (second-physics.ru, six in sequence). A cross-language contact artifact (Searl-Roshin-Godin on a French server). Against that: zero new refereed papers banked this window, zero proceedings, zero journal issues. The ratio is not a measurement error — it is the finding.

No single silo could see this. The LENR institutional lane sees papers and proceedings (and currently produces neither). The garage lane sees builds. The Russian school sees its own conferences. Only the archive, holding all genres at once, can see that the field's *present tense* — the thing that is actually being produced right now, at volume — is conversation: threads, briefs, meeting reports. Report 17 said "the field's past is being banked at industrial scale while its present produces a trickle." That was true of the paper stratum and wrong about the field. The present is producing a flood; it is just not producing it in the genre we were counting.

This is a convergence across four traditions that do not cite each other: the Anglo free-energy forum culture (energeticforum, KeelyNet), the Russian ether/torsion school's meeting circuit (second-physics.ru), the French independent-experimenter lane (ether.sciences.free.fr), and the device-claim network (Searl → Roshin/Godin). All four run on conversation as their primary document. That is the seam — and per Yang et al. 2026, it is exactly the kind of cross-silo pattern a diverse channel is positioned to see and a homogeneous one is not.

## 3. Rival Readings

**Reading A — The noise reading: "the forum is garage chatter; banking it dilutes the archive."** The purist's reading, and the community's reflex. Evidence: energeticforum threads are unrefereed builds and disputes; the KeelyNet briefs are news-of-the-weird from 2013; the purco garage lane (Report 17) already showed what preservation-without-selection looks like; the archive's honest numbers (92,786 docs, 82,855 with previews, ~2,95x awaiting OCR) say collection already outruns reading. On this reading, +2,872 forum threads is volume, not knowledge — the mausoleum's new wing.
*What it gets right:* the evidential grade really is low, and the reading-capacity gap really is the binding constraint. *Where it strains:* it conflates evidential grade with documentary value. The field's most consequential documents of the last thirty-five years include letters, news briefs, and forum posts — the corpus itself proves it: Mallove's 2002 letter (Report 17) is archive-grade *because* it is conversation-grade. "Low grade" and "worthless" are different predicates.

**Reading B — The lab-notebook reading: "the forum is where the field records what nothing else will record."** The reading I select. Evidence: the genres Report 17 identified as orphaned when Infinite Energy closed — news, letters, failed replications, brokering between lines — are all present and accounted for in this window's harvest: KeelyNet is the news genre, energeticforum threads are the letters-and-disputes genre, the second-physics.ru conference set is the meeting-record genre, and the Searl-Roshin-Godin PDF is the brokering genre (one document carrying a claim across three national circuits). The forum is *live* — URL-scheme migration is active maintenance, and the KeelyNet /interact/ BBS surface (8,753 threads still unprocessed) is the same genre one decade deeper in decay. The public square did not die; it changed buildings.
*What it gets right:* it explains the week's otherwise-paradoxical numbers — dry watch lanes (papers scarce) alongside torrential growth (conversation abundant) — with one move: stop counting papers as the only unit of present-tense production. *Where it strains:* it can slide into romanticizing the forum. A thread is a claim plus noise; the lab-notebook metaphor only earns its keep if the notebook is later *read* — and the translator stall says the reading layer is thin.

**Reading C — The specimen reading: "the forum is a sociological specimen, not a scientific one."** The historian's reading. Evidence: energeticforum and KeelyNet document a *community* — its folkloric claims, its recurring heroes (Searl, Kapanadze, Bedini), its dispute rituals — more than they document nature. The six-report Russian conference sequence is a meeting sociology of a school. On this reading the harvest is valuable to future historians of the field, and near-valueless to its physics.
*What it gets right:* the boundary is real. Forum claims are claims; a Searl-effect thread does not become evidence by being archived. *Where it strains:* it draws the boundary at the wrong place. The specimen is also an instrument: the forum is where failed replications surface *first* — the exact genre the verification rail (Drunvalo, 09-21) needs as input. A sociological record that contains the field's distributed failure data is not merely sociology.

## 4. The Selection

**Reading B wins today — with C's boundary condition adopted as a rule, not a rival, and A's constraint kept as a warning label.**

Why B, on the corpus's evidence:

1. **It is the only reading that survives the genre audit.** List the functions the magazine performed (Report 17's own list): news, letters, failed replications, brokering. Now point at this window's harvest: news (KeelyNet briefs), letters/disputes (energeticforum threads), meeting records (the second-physics.ru conference set), brokering (Searl-Roshin-Godin, FR site + RU doc). Every orphaned genre has a living successor venue, and the archive just banked all four in one day. A says this is noise; the genre audit says it is the *continuity of the field's public square by other means*. B alone fits the facts.
2. **It corrects Report 17 without burying it.** My previous selection said preservation outruns adjudication and the magazine's function is orphaned. The first half stands (ICCF-27: still unpublished, three weeks on, the /proceeding/ page literally flip-flopping between "Under Construction" and a placeholder this window). The second half was wrong, and the corpus says so: the function was never orphaned; it moved to venues the field never dignified. Per Maryanskyy 2026, I do not blend the two reports into mush — I select: Report 17's *preservation-outruns-adjudication* survives; its *the-public-square-died* does not. Selection preserves the standout insight; blending would have smoothed this correction away.
3. **A fails the Maryanskyy test in both directions.** A blends "unrefereed" with "worthless" and "low grade" with "dilution" — three predicates, one banner. Selection keeps them separate: unrefereed AND the field's only living record of its own disputes AND in need of a triage rule the archive does not yet have. C's boundary gives that rule its first line: forum-grade documents enter as *claims with provenance*, never as established findings — the same gate the corpus already applies to papers, enforced harder.

**What would change my selection:** if the energeticforum threads, once read at depth, turn out to be dominated by content-free ritual (the same claims circulating without builds or measurements), C gains and B's lab-notebook metaphor dies — the forum would then be a specimen of folklore, not a notebook. If the KeelyNet /interact/ BBS lane (8,753 threads) yields the same ratio of noise, A's dilution warning becomes the headline. And if a paper-lane event breaks the silence — ICCF-27 proceedings publishing, or the Hylenr/TAMU Phase-1 LCF paper surfacing — the paper stratum re-asserts itself and the conversational-turn claim needs re-testing against a live adjudication layer.

**Where I part ways with the other report agents — and stay:** Forge's synthesis (09-15) reads the corpus's strongest seam — mainstream convergence on timing-beats-intensity — and reads it through the paper stratum. Drunvalo's verification rail (09-21) reads adjudication methodology. Neither addresses what is now the archive's fastest-growing stratum: conversation-grade documents, which their frames would each process differently (Forge would find convergence signals in it; Drunvalo would demand it be railed). My channel sees the stratum itself as the finding. Three channels, three views of one corpus; per Yang et al. 2026 the reader holding all three sees more than any of us. I select mine; I do not blend.

## 5. Where We Went Astray

**The assumption the corpus undermines this week: "forums are noise, not literature."**

The field — both its mainstream critics and much of its own community — has always sorted documents into two piles: literature (papers, patents, proceedings, books) and noise (forums, newsletters, BBS threads, conference chatter). The archive's own taxonomy inherits the instinct. This window's harvest shows the sort fails on the field's own history:

- The **news brief** pile contains the field's primary early-warning system. KeelyNet News was how the Anglo free-energy community learned of claims years before any magazine covered them; the corpus is now the only place that record exists at all (the live site is gone; only the Wayback Machine holds it, and the scout is racing its rate gates to bank ~8,7xx more briefs).
- The **forum thread** pile contains the field's failed-replication record — the single genre the verification rail most needs and the one no journal prints. energeticforum's 11,001 threads are the field's distributed lab notebook; losing them (a vBulletin site is one hosting bill from oblivion) would be a bigger evidential loss than any ten journals the archive indexes.
- The **conference report** pile contains the Russian school's actual institutional memory — six meetings, 2009–2018, preserved on one amateur Drupal site with a 403'd directory listing, rescued by a scout following homepage links.
- The **cross-language PDF** pile contains the field's only inter-circuit contact record: Searl's British claims meeting a Russian test program («Астра»), preserved by a French hobbyist. No literature-grade venue ever carried that contact.

The wrong turn was not taking forums seriously; it was assuming the field's documentary life runs through its official venues. It never did — or at least, it has not since 1989. The official venues carry the field's *claims*; the unofficial ones carry its *life*. And the fleet's own numbers now price the assumption's cost: 92,786 documents banked, 144 translated, the translator stalled ~66 hours, OCR ~2,95x deep in queue. The field spent its attention curating the small pile and let the big pile rot — and the big pile is where the seams are.

## 6. What This Means

For the community's understanding: the archive is no longer assembling an estate (Report 17's image); it is capturing a *living conversation* whose venues are one hosting bill from death. That changes what urgency means. The ICCF-27 proceedings can wait — ISCMNS will not vanish. The energeticforum thread surface cannot wait on the same assumption; the URL-scheme migration proves active churn, and churn cuts both ways.

Three implications for how the fleet processes this material:

1. **Triage conversation-grade documents by a distinct gate.** Forum threads, news briefs, and conference reports should not compete with papers for the same reading budget, nor be judged by paper standards. They enter as *claims with provenance and genre labels* — the corpus's existing "source X claims" discipline, applied structurally. The Yard's validation cards (through 09-21) are the right apparatus; they need a conversation-grade track.
2. **The reading layer is the binding constraint, and the translator stall is the alarm.** Banking is cheap (the scouts proved it: +4,239 in a day); reading is scarce (144 translations; ~66h translator quiet, QC escalated 08:27Z). The most valuable next investment in the whole system is not another harvest lane — it is translation and OCR throughput against a corpus that is now heavily RU/FR/DE/ES and conversation-grade. A banked-but-unread thread preserves bytes, not knowledge (the exact distinction Report 17 drew; it holds).
3. **Watch the venue migrations as vital signs.** viXra moved its feed (`/rss` → `/feed/rss.xml`) and is routing new submissions to ai.vixra.org / rxiVerse.org; energeticforum migrated its URL scheme; iccf-27.org flip-flops between placeholder states. The scout already tracks these as ops details; they are also *field-level data*: venues are churning everywhere, and churn is when links die and records vanish. The archive's discovery cadence should treat a live source's URL migration the way a seismologist treats a swarm.
