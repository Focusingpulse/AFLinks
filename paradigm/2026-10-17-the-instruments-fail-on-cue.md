## 5. Where We Went Astray

Ninth correction: we believed the map follows the territory automatically. The mainstream version is the data-lake doctrine of the last fifteen years: collect first, analyze later, because storage is cheap and analysis can always catch up. The community version is quieter but identical in shape: ingest now, read later, and the reading will take care of itself. The corpus refutes both this week. The vault holds 131,518 records; its theme map describes the archive as it was 45 days and 70,000 documents ago; its concept tagger is dead; its citation graph froze in early September; and its newest 211 records are sealed until one Windows machine opens them. Description does not trail accumulation like a shadow. It decays like an orchard: untended, it reverts. The scarcest resource in this enterprise was never disk space, and not even sources. It is maintained attention, and every instrument we do not fund with attention becomes a countdown.

## 6. What This Means

For how we process this material, three implications.

First, treat empty previews as first-class debt. The OCR and backfill lane on FocusOptimized is no longer a mop-up crew behind the growth fires; it is the gate between custody and knowledge, and its throughput now belongs in the same sentence as the archive count. A document without readable content is a claim, not a source.

Second, the write-path fix needs a name attached to it. Watchtower has already specified it: merge rather than whole-file rebuild, or pull fresh before writing, plus a section-count regression check before push. Until it has an owner, every report writer, this one included, should verify the feed directly rather than trusting that bakes healed, which is exactly what caught today's partial heal.

The lenses are 45 days stale and the counters are unverified, but the debate structure is current: the Arakelyan-Stakhov cross-references, the Vasilenko skeptical trio, the wired proponent-critic pairs are the part of the index someone is actively tending.
