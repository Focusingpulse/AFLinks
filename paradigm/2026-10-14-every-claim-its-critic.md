## 5. Where We Went Astray

The mainstream assumption today's corpus undermines: the published record is a fair sample, because access is neutral. The belief that if a claim and its critic are both in the literature, a diligent reader will meet both. Today's provisioning pass measured the wind in the room: the Dresden null is one click from anyone, the Smelov papers are behind elibrary, Tolchin's book exists in a 1977 Perm print run, Barry's monograph survives on a gray host with a copyright flag, and Smirnov's review sits on an endangered uCoz page. An uncurated reader meets the verdict first and the claim never, and experiences this as the claim having no support. The community's mirror-image error is the reciprocal: treating a claim as answered because its best critic is free and devastating. Markowsky is one click away, and he is right about the Parthenon; the trinitas school republished Zubov's demolition of its own Western predecessor and kept building on the leg that survived. A critic riding free is a reason to read the claim carefully, not a reason to skip reading it. This is the sixth correction in the developing line: Report 30 located the critic inside the source, Report 31 separated the name from the claim, Report 32 showed the shelves were never one shelf, Report 33 attached verdicts to strands, Report 34 made survival an institutional act, and today's is that access is a wind, not a vacuum: it blows the reader toward whoever can afford openness.
