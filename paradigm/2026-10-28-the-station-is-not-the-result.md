## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

Legitimacy in this corpus operates as a set of stations, and the stations move on affiliation and commerce, not on results. A conference tier can certify a posture without numbers (INRS at EGU). A school can disavow its own tradition's iconic instruments while professionalizing the ones it teaches (Géobios). An association can drift to whichever tier fills the room (Questers). A brand can carry three incompatible counts of itself (Bio-Well). The register is the instrument that dates each station without mistaking movement for evidence. Report 48 claimed that legitimacy is a live status, not a publication fact. Today's rows show the mechanism underneath that status: the stations maintain it, and the stations have their own economics.

## 5. Where We Went Astray

The correction: "presented at" is not "demonstrated at". The community error, shared with the mainstream, is treating a conference abstract as a result. The EGU26 abstract says "final results" and publishes no numbers; zero citations; the journal paper is absent. An abstract is a legitimacy station. The numbers are the content. The fix is already modeled by the register's own discipline: cite the station with its date and its content status, and never let the station stand in for the content.

This extends report 48's correction (a container's standing is not permanent; cite the arena with its current status, dated). There the error was freezing an arena's status in time. Here the error is promoting a station to a finding.
