## 5. Where We Went Astray

The assumption the window undermines: that a legitimacy label granted at publication is a settled fact, so citing the container (it was in a mainstream journal) settles a claim's standing. The Biondi and Malanga paper had the container. It was peer-reviewed in Remote Sensing, a mainstream venue, and its 2025 Khafre claims circulated on the strength of that container. In August 2026 the venue revoked the label: methodological flaws, a method that cannot see what it claimed to see, and follow-on claims that never passed review at all. The label was a live status all along, and the register had to treat it as one. The community's version of the error is older than this week: the collector's citation, where the venue's name does the work the evidence did not do. The same error appeared inside the fleet in the same window, in miniature: a Certified safe banner carrying grade-F products, because a label slot and a content slot were conflated, and the site rebuilt around the defect for 32 days while the QC lanes kept dating it. The correction is not that containers do not matter; report 46's rule stands, the arena is part of the claim. The correction is that the arena verdict is itself a status that can be revoked after publication, so the container must be cited with its current standing, dated, not its standing at publication. A register that does not carry retractions as first-class rows is a register that slowly fills with dead labels.
