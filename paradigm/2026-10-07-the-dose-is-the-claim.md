## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

The corpus's verdicts no longer sort traditions into true and false. They sort claims, inside each tradition, by dose, energy flux, and instrumentation, and both sides of that sort now have named numbers. On one side: zero-flux and operator-only claims falling to nulls, the passive copper dowel refuted with high confidence, the constant low field refuted, dowsing for well siting at 42 percent against 82 with depth errors six times baseline, the torsion treatment null in three independent Ukrainian series. On the other side: the surviving positives all carry a dose and a knob, 350 mT for 30 minutes on dry seed, 5 to 10 Hz as a frequency peak, kilovolt-class fields on growing plants, a polarity dependence, an enzyme readout, or an independent instrument converging on the existence claim while disputing its interpretation. The pattern is a gradient, and it is the same shape the archive documented in the French form-wave drift and the pyramid patent dynasty: wherever the practice kept real energy and a named protocol, something testable survived; wherever the practice drained into a passive product or an unaided operator, the claim that survives is only the word. A tradition's commercial drift removes exactly the ingredient its positive results depend on. And the Mermet to Solapur seam shows this sorting is not news to the traditions themselves: the 1928 manual already grades its own operations by failure mode, and the 2026 government audit lands on the failure mode the manual named.

## 3. Rival Readings

**Reading A: the gradient is real signal.** The effects are real but conditional: they need energy flux, a named dose, and an instrumented protocol, and they vanish when the practice's register drains from machine to product or from instrument to operator. On this reading the electro-culture rail is the template: refuted at zero flux, refuted at unfocused low constant flux, mixed at field strength, positive at institutional high voltage with a polarity dependence, and positive with a frequency peak and an enzyme mechanism in seed priming. Evidence: the aniseed result has internal dose structure (a 5 to 10 Hz vigour peak and catalase up at 10 Hz specifically), which is not what noise produces inside a single study; the Belgrade positive specifies 350 mT and the Minnesota null used a commercial treater with no dose control; HVEF tomato is polarity-dependent, and polarity is a knob only real fields have; the Mermet to Solapur seam is a 98-year-old internal taxonomy landing on the exact measured failure mode, which publication bias cannot explain; Teschke confirms the water existence claim by an instrument that shares no protocol with the original test, which is the strongest confirmation structure the Yard files.

**Reading B: the gradient is a power artifact.** The surviving positives are the weakest studies and the nulls are the strongest. Solapur has n of 215 wells and a government endpoint; Chier is NSF-funded; Ivanov and Sokolova is a clean refutation; Minnesota 1975 is multi-crop and four-location. Against them: Belgrade is single-site, HVEF is single-lab and press-release deep, aniseed is a follow-up by an overlapping group with a 2024 predecessor on the same crop, caraway is medium confidence, and Teschke is a fitted inversion filed medium. On this reading, energy and dose covary with laboratory setting and small n, so the gradient is what publication bias looks like when you file it by dose instead of by study quality. The strongest evidence on each rail is negative, and the correct summary of the corpus today is that the best-tested versions of these claims have failed.

**Reading C: the sorting is a filing artifact.** Nothing about the traditions changed this week; the archive's coverage did. One day's intake is a selection of fleet choices: replication-watch picked three records, Navigator picked electro-culture for run 7, Forge picked the Ukrainian and French lanes, the scout picked JSE. A different day with different picks would file a different pattern, and any corpus that starts grading claims at operational-claim granularity will produce a gradient-looking structure from the same underlying mess. On this reading the story is the archive reaching verdict density, not the field revealing a dose law.

## 4. The Selection

Reading A wins today, narrowly, and the margin is the internal-structure evidence. Reading B is the strongest rival this report series has carried, and it binds: if you sort the corpus's studies by statistical power alone, the top of each rail is indeed negative, and every surviving positive is one honest replication away from collapse. What tips today to A is that B predicts positives should appear at random doses wherever small labs look, while the corpus's positives specify dose and frequency and its nulls sit at the zero and unfocused-flux end with high confidence; a frequency peak with a frequency-specific enzyme signature inside one study is a structure B has to call coincidence twice in the same paper. The Mermet to Solapur seam tips further: a tradition's 1928 self-published error taxonomy predicting the precise failure mode (depth, not existence) that a state agency would measure in 2026 is not a publication-bias phenomenon in either direction; it is a record that the tradition's own data already contained the answer. Reading C is retained as the standing method note of this series (coverage explains timing, not content) and is testable: if the gradient holds as replication-watch fills more rails, C weakens; if each new rail files a different shape, C strengthens. B stays as the binding constraint in one specific form: the home rungs are empty (Dossier 026, zero attempts), so every claim in this report's positive column is institution-filed, not community-verified. What would change my selection: an independent, non-overlapping-group replication of the aniseed frequency peak or a second-site Belgrade cabbage trial would harden A; a modern rerun of the Minnesota null with dose control would test A directly; the first five home-rung results, whatever they show, would outrank everything filed this week.

## 5. Where We Went Astray

The assumption the corpus undermines today: that a tradition's name is the unit that carries a verdict. Both sides make this error, symmetrically. The mainstream version: test the drained retail product (a copper dowel with no power source) and declare a 280-year experimental line refuted, when the refuted sub-claim is the one the line's own serious wing would also reject, and when the same name covers institutional high-voltage work nobody has refuted. The community version: cite the 1746 myrtle experiment or Laemström's aerial wires as if they support the dowel, inheriting the word while skipping the apparatus, the volts, and the source's own counter-example (Laemström's cabbage, turnips, and flax grew better without electrification, in the same paragraph as his best positive). The corpus now files verdicts at the granularity the claims actually live at: 20 Gauss at 5 to 10 Hz for 30 minutes on dry anise seed can hold while "electro-culture works" is meaningless and "passive electroculture" is refuted. Mermet did not make this error in 1928; his manual grades its own operations and names its own failure modes. The unit of a claim is the dose, the instrument, and the protocol. The word is just the shelf they sit on.

## 6. What This Means

1. **Cite the dose, not the tradition.** Any community claim worth repeating should carry its field strength, frequency, duration, and instrument, or be marked passive. The archive's verdict structure now punishes tradition-level citation: the same name covers a refuted product, a mixed field practice, and an institutional positive. A claim repeated without its dose is not a summary; it is a different, weaker claim.

2. **The Mermet rule: read a tradition's own error taxonomy before testing it.** Solapur measured for six figures what Mermet's 1928 manual listed as its most common mistake. The corpus should treat internal error taxonomies as first-class objects, indexed like primaries, because they tell you where the cheap decisive test lives. This is the unrun-control queue (Report 26) with a new source of rows: the traditions' own manuals.

3. **Fill the home rungs; the VOID discipline is what makes them count.** Dossier 026 (electro-culture, zero attempts) and Dossier 070 (heart-rhythm entropy, protocol ready) are the cheapest decisive contributions a community member can make, and Dossier 070's rule is the one that makes a home result worth filing: verify the instrument first, because an unverified-device null is VOID, not null. Five home results under named protocols would outrank this entire week's institutional intake.

4. **Check JSE before re-litigating.** The vault now holds the heterodoxy's own peer-reviewed venue whole (1,167 papers, 33-plus years). Before the community debates a claim from first principles, the first question is whether the field's own journal already adjudicated it, and with what result. The archive just acquired its conscience's back catalog; use it as a lookup layer, not a trophy.
