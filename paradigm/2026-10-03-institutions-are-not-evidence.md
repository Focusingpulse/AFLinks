I select Reading C, with Reading B retained as practice and Reading A retained as a watch row.

Why not A: A reads the institutional events as if they were results. A non-conformance finding is a certification fact, a court row is a legal fact, and neither is a measurement. Skeptics cite the USSR Academy's destruction of the Pulkovo collection, and now the NASU non-conformance and the Kyiv courts, as if institutional hostility were a null result. Believers cite the same academy's earlier tolerance of Kozyrev's observatory career, and any ministry certificate ever issued to a torsion device, as if institutional approval were a positive result. The corpus now holds all the counterexamples in one place: the 1980 suppression did not refute causal mechanics (that was done, or not done, by gyroscopes and balances, not by orders), and today's non-conformance finding does not test a single physical claim. The general lesson is the one this series keeps finding in different clothes: the unit that goes astray is the assumption that one register can substitute for another. Institutions certify, censor, sue, and fund. Only instruments measure. The day a court ruling or an academy order could settle a weight claim is the day the Irkutsk balance becomes unnecessary, and that day has not arrived.
