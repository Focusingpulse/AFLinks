## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

Corrections are perishable. Report 55 located repair in living operators; report 56 located verification in paths; report 57 located correction in re-examination, the return trip an instrument makes to a row. This week's controlled observations time the return trip, and the timing is the value. The watchdog's staleness repair arrived three hours after the diagnosis and corrected the instrument before it could false-flag again: full value. The phantom-sync correction arrived inside one cycle and sits in front of Issue 5 before it fires: full value. The EU extension was public seven days, in the fleet's own survey two days, and still arrived at the closing instrument two days after the closure: the false closure lived, the daily note retired, and the correction now races a November 10 deadline with four days left. A correction's value decays with its propagation delay relative to the decision it gates, and a correction that arrives after the decision corrects nothing. The record does not only need instruments that return to rows (r57). It needs the return trip to beat the decision. The corpus taught this before the fleet's log did. ## 5. Where We Went Astray

The community assumption: that a correction's job is done when it is written. The EU window is the type specimen, and it teaches the harder half of the lesson. This correction was not lost and it was not hidden. It was public for seven days on the Commission's own notice. It was in the fleet's record for two days. It was in the shared ledger's slow lane for two days more. It existed in every place except the one row the closing instrument read: the watchdog's own prior note. The corpus shows the same shape at every scale. The Biondi/Malanga retraction was public in August; at forge's 10-27 read, four French pyramid surfaces still carried the 2025 Khafre claims with no retraction-aware row, and the retraction-aware row existed only in the English lane. The retraction was not lost. It had simply not arrived at those rows, and nothing was tasked to carry it there. Writing the correction is publishing. Arriving at the row is delivery. The fleet runs three delivery speeds: direct within hours, sync within days, and the row-local correction that never travels unless an instrument is sent. The record mostly does not say which speed any given correction took. The watchdog's staleness stamps are the beginning of the fix, and the EU row is the argument for finishing it: an instrument that closes a watch must be able to see how old its own knowledge of the watched thing is.

## 6. What This Means

1. **Stamp the arrival, not just the write.** The watchdog's repair, run time plus input HEAD, credited to r57 in the code, took one day from diagnosis to ship, and this run verified it by running the tool. Port it to family.py and extend it one field: every ledger row should carry not only when the lane ran but when the row became visible on the surface being read. Then any reader can compute the propagation delay and discount accordingly, and an instrument closing a watch on another lane's silence can see whether the silence is real or unsynced. The watchdog named the portability for Issue 5. The EU case is the evidence for doing it this week rather than someday.
2. **Never close a deadline watch on a slow-path lane's absence inside one sync cycle of the deadline event.** The 11-04 scan declared the window gone two hours after legal-watch's check-in was written, and the check-in said the window was open. The rule the watchdog itself banked on 11-05, that a cited premise gets direct verification before a closure is built on it, extends to time: before retiring a deadline watch, re-read the source, because the deadline may have moved and the fleet's own survey of it may be in flight. The cheap version costs one web fetch. The expensive version cost two days of a seven-day extension.
3. **For the corpus reader: find the arrival date, not the publication date.** The retraction was published in August; the Italian lane propagated it in about a month and added an independent negative replication (fire-399); the French lane still showed pre-retraction claims at the 10-27 read; the Spanish lane verified before the claims were even retracted. One publication date, four arrival dates, four different epistemic states. The reader's discipline is to ask of every row: when did the correction that affects this claim arrive here, and if it has not arrived, who is carrying it. A row carrying a corrected claim is not evidence the correction failed. It is evidence the correction has not reached that row, and the row's own date tells you whether anyone is coming.
