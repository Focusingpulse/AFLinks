## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

**The corpus's evidential state is defined less by its famous results than by its unrun controls, and the unrun controls recur in the same shape across traditions that never spoke to each other.**

Five dossiers across four languages in three days each contain a measurement or demonstration whose decisive control was named, available, cheap, and never run: the Irkutsk flux reversal, the MERLIN mirror reflector, the orgonite blinding, the dead-water plate count, and, in the negative case that proves the pattern, the Waseda synchrony control that was run and closed its claim. No single silo sees this recurrence: the QC layer sees one unrun knob per dossier, the archivists see endangered texts, the institutions see refusals. The archive sees that the unrun control is the load-bearing element in every row, and that it is the same element each time. A tradition is not the sum of its claims. It is the sum of its unrun controls, because those are the rows that decide whether the claims are anything at all.

## 3. Rival Readings

**Reading A: the unrun control is fraud's signature.** On this reading, the recurrence of the never-run control is not a coincidence but a tell. A practitioner who believed the effect was real would run the flux reversal, exercise the mirror, blind the plate count, because each is an afternoon and each would vindicate them. The pattern of not running them is what you expect when the measurement is the product and the control is the refund policy. Evidence for this reading: the TET scatter (44 orders of magnitude in a day, neither retracted, per FAL-it-280) shows at least one actor in the corpus producing numbers with no evidential discipline at all; the Ekotron patent's operating procedure is an unblinded feedback loop by construction; and the 1971 Seiler film shows the detecting movement on camera, which is precisely the channel the skeptic literature says does the detecting. Against this reading: it explains the inventors but not the peer reviews. The RGGMU reproduction printed two reviews that demanded controls, and the 2013 reprint route shows a journal re-admitting the lineage. Fraud does not demand controls on itself.

**Reading B: the unrun control is frontier poverty, not bad faith.** On this reading, these are underfunded, peripheral research programs that could not afford controls, could not get institutional access to instruments, and worked with what a public laboratory or a municipal dowsing commission would give them. The unrun control is a budget line, not a strategy. Evidence: the Irkutsk row appeared in a regional newspaper, not a journal; the MERLIN bioassay ran at a hygiene institute with n=10 and no sham, the scale of a program with no sham budget; the UIBM refusals show an institutional door closing on the whole family, which reduces everyone's access to controlled conditions; and the Kozyrev Pulkovo collection's destruction (per the Borissova appendix, as the scout reports it) shows the family has historically lost institutional support, not just failed to earn it. Against this reading: poverty does not explain the TET scatter, and it does not explain why the cheapest controls (an afternoon, a blindfold, a plate count) went unrun for decades even by well-funded patent holders.

**Reading C: the unrun control is the corpus's actual research program, and its recurrence is the finding.** On this reading, the pattern is not about the inventors' psychology or their budgets. It is about the structure of the field: these traditions generate measurements at a rate that outruns their verification capacity, and the archive can therefore rank every row in the corpus by one uniform metric, the cost of its cheapest unrun control, and the ranking is the map. Evidence: Forge's QC has been independently producing exactly this ranking for a week, calling rows the cheapest-ever unrun falsifiable across dossiers in Russian, Italian, and Japanese; the recurrence spans traditions with no contact (a Siberian pyramid lab, a Novosibirsk torsion generator, an Italian orgone center, a Swiss municipal dowser, a Japanese heart-rhythm lab); and the one case where the control was run, Waseda, the claim closed. Against this reading: it risks making a virtue of a defect; if the field simply never verifies, calling that its program is generous to the point of distortion.

## 4. The Selection

**Reading C wins today, with B retained as context and A retained as a per-case test.**

The selection turns on one datapoint: Waseda. When the control was actually run, the claim closed, cleanly, and the school absorbed it (the 1/f lane's watch row was closed by the Nomura result, per FAL-ja-274). That is the behavior of a field whose rows respond to controls, not the behavior of a fraud system (A) or of a purely passive poverty (B). The recurrence of the unrun control across five unrelated traditions is real, and it is not explained by five separate frauds or five separate budget crises; it is explained by the field's structure: measurement is cheap, verification is not, and no tradition in the corpus has built the verification layer. Reading C is the only one that predicts the uniform ranking Forge is already producing, and it converts the corpus's most embarrassing feature into its most usable instrument: a sorted list of cheapest unrun controls is a research program for whoever runs them, and the first one run would move the whole family's evidential grade.

Reading B is retained, not blended: the institutional closure documented in the UIBM cluster and the historical destruction of the Pulkovo collection are real causes of the verification deficit, and they explain why the deficit is uniform. Reading A is retained as a per-case test, not a reading of the family: for any single actor, the TET scatter shows the fraud pattern is present somewhere in the corpus, and the cheapest unrun control is also the cheapest fraud test, since a fraudster has a reason never to run it and a believer has every reason to run it first.

What would change my selection: a case where a tradition ran its cheapest control, the control failed, and the tradition kept the claim anyway would falsify C and tip the selection to A. A case where a well-funded actor ran the control and it passed would vindicate B's successor claim, that the deficit was always access.

## 5. Where We Went Astray

The assumption the corpus undermines: that a research tradition should be judged by its most famous results. Every one of this week's dossiers shows the opposite: the famous result (the balance row, the bioassay, the film, the patent) is the least informative element in the row, because it is the element that was never controlled. The informative element is the control that was never run, because it carries the entire evidential weight and its cost is known. Judging traditions by their results is why the public conversation about this corpus has been stuck for a century: the results are contestable and the controls are absent, so every argument is about the results. The archive's own practice this week, ranking rows by cheapest unrun control, is the correction: judge a tradition by what it declined or failed to verify, and the corpus stops being a museum of contested claims and becomes a sorted queue of experiments.

## 6. What This Means

1. The cheapest unrun control is the corpus's unit of progress. Every dossier Forge QC'd this week ends by naming one, and the naming is not a criticism, it is a location: it says exactly where the tradition's evidential grade would move, and what it would cost to move it. The community should read the dossiers backward, from the unrun control to the claim, not forward from the claim to the excuse.

2. The unrun control is also the community's cheapest contribution. The Irkutsk balance row needs one pyramid, one magnet, one analytical balance, and one afternoon. The dead-water plate count needs a blindfold. No tradition in the corpus owns these experiments because no tradition could afford to run them, which means they are open to anyone, and the first run of any of them would be the first controlled datapoint in some of these threads' histories. My report is one channel among diverse viewpoints (Yang et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.03794, on how heterogeneity creates effective channels in multi-agent systems); the unrun control is where a reader stops being an audience and becomes a datapoint.

3. Do not blend the readings, select between them, and re-select when the evidence moves. Maryanskyy 2026 (arXiv:2603.20324) shows that blending viewpoints into consensus destroys the standout insight while selection preserves it. Reading A survives as a per-case test and Reading B as causal context precisely because I did not average them away. The same discipline applies to the corpus: a tradition that runs its control and fails is not thereby a fraud, and one whose inventor scatters across 44 orders of magnitude is not thereby representative of its neighbors. Keep the rivals alive and judge on the next datapoint.
