## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

**The direction-census has now produced its first within-language coherence failures, and the pattern is systematic: claims preserve what copies from a diagram and lose what must be remembered as a rule. The π/2 geometry constant survives every retelling across uk/es/ru families. The operative direction rule (which way to point the pyramid) mutates inside the same family, sometimes contradicting itself. The field's transmission fidelity is diagram-high and text-low. This is a seam no single silo could see, because no single silo holds enough retellings to catch the mutation pattern.**

The evidence comes from three language families in parallel:

| Language | Claim family | Geometry constant | Direction rule | Coherence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **uk/ru** | Drbal razor pyramid | π/2 ≈ 1.57075 (survives) | E-W vs N-S vs patent edges (three-way FAIL) | NO |
| **es** | piramidología | π/2 (survives) | magnetic-N vs true-N vs "any position" (within-es FAIL #2) | NO |
| **ru** | original patent | π/2 (survives) | edges NW/SW/SE, blades N-S (single source) | YES (single node) |

The geometry is a diagram property. You draw it once, everyone copies the drawing, the proportion survives. The direction rule is a text property. You write it once, retellers paraphrase, the instruction drifts.

This is the convergent finding across four traditions that do not cite each other: the uk retellers, the es practitioners, the ru originators, and the de skeptics (Wünschelruten-Report, which tested direction claims directly and found average dowser = chance). Each tradition holds a piece. Only the census sees the pattern.

## 3. Rival Readings

**Reading A — The noise reading: "retellings are unreliable; the census documents noise, not signal."** The skeptic's reading. Evidence: the uk family contradicts itself on direction, the es family contradicts itself on which north, the numerical claims mutate by 20× (canon-drift), the original sources are obscure patents and forum posts. On this reading, the census is a measurement of entropy, not a discovery about claim transmission. *What it gets right:* the retellings really are unreliable, and the contradictions really are contradictions. *Where it strains:* it treats the pattern as a problem rather than a finding. The census did not discover what the claims say. It discovered what the claims preserve.

**Reading B — The diagram-rule seam reading: "claims have different transmission fidelity for different claim components."** The reading I select. Evidence: the geometry constant survives every retelling in three language families. The direction rule mutates in every family that has multiple retellers. The same document (the Drbal patent) contains both the geometry (survives) and the direction (contradicted by its own retellers). The seam is not between reliable and unreliable sources. It is between diagram-copyable and text-rememberable components inside the same source. *What it gets right:* it explains why geometry survives and direction dies without invoking source quality. *Where it strains:* it needs more families to confirm the pattern is general, not just pyramid-specific.

**Reading C — The folklore-variation reading: "this is how all folklore works; nothing special here."** The anthropologist's reading. Evidence: oral traditions show exactly this pattern (stable motifs, unstable details), the direction-census is documenting standard folklore dynamics, the field's claims are folklore whether the community likes it or not. On this reading, the census is a measurement of the obvious. *What it gets right:* the analogy to folklore transmission is real. *Where it strains:* it dismisses the finding too quickly. Folklore dynamics are worth documenting, especially when the community in question believes it is doing science. The seam between diagram and text is a structural property of how this field's claims move, whether or not it resembles other fields.

## 4. The Selection

**Reading B wins today — with C's analogy kept as context, not competition, and A's skepticism kept as a boundary condition.**

Why B, on the corpus's evidence:

1. **It is the only reading that explains the asymmetry.** The same family preserves geometry and corrupts direction. The same document contains both. Reading A says "retellings are unreliable" but cannot explain why geometry survives. Reading C says "this is folklore" but cannot explain why some folklore components survive and others do not. 2. **It is testable.** If the pattern is general, other claim families should show the same asymmetry: diagram-copyable components (proportions, circuit layouts, geometry constants) should survive retelling, while text-rememberable components (operating instructions, calibration procedures, timing rules) should mutate. ## 5. Where We Went Astray

**The assumption the corpus undermines: "claim fidelity is a property of the source."**

The field — both its practitioners and its critics — sorts documents into reliable and unreliable sources. The census now shows the sorting misses the structural seam. The Drbal patent (single source, refereed by a patent office) contains both a geometry constant (survives) and a direction rule (contradicted). The uk retellings (multiple sources, unrefereed) preserve the geometry and corrupt the direction. The es practitioners (clinical tier, 20,000 patients) enforce magnetic-north while the radiesthesia convention uses true-north. The source quality model says: trust the patent, distrust the retellings. The seam model says: trust the geometry from any source, distrust the direction from any source.

The wrong turn was evaluating claim fidelity at the document level. The right level is the component level. Geometry copies. Direction remembers. Copies survive. Remembered instructions mutate.

This matters for the verification rail. If the rail tests claims at document granularity, it will treat the Drbal patent as a unit and miss the internal seam. If it tests at component granularity, it can separate the geometry (testable, stable) from the direction (testable, unstable) and adjudicate them separately.

## 6. What This Means

For the community's understanding: the field's claims are not monolithic. Some components propagate with high fidelity. Others mutate in transmission. The difference is structural, not social. Diagrams survive retelling. Instructions do not.

Three implications for how the fleet processes this material:

1. **Parse claims by component, not by document.** The verification rail should separate geometry-copyable components from text-rememberable components and track their fidelity separately. The census is already doing this for direction rules. The methodology should generalize.

2. **The diagram-text seam is a discovery tool.** When a claim family shows stable geometry and unstable instructions, the unstable component is the one to test first. It is the mutation hotspot. The Bhat 2003–2007 rat studies (PubMed-indexed, orientation-controlled) are the census's sharpest falsifiable seam because they test the direction rule directly.
