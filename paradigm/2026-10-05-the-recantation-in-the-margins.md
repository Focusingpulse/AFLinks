## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

The corpus's strongest counter-evidence to its own traditions is indigenous: produced inside those traditions, by their own authors and practitioners, recorded in their own primary texts. Veynik clamped the frame and wrote what happened in the margins of the very book that teaches the frame protocol. The KPI authors measured the pyramid water and wrote "unfounded" in their own paper. The Seiler film shows the arm doing the detecting. The convergence across the ru chronal lane, the fr radiesthesia lane, and the pyramid family is not that the claims are false; the controlled verdicts mostly say that already. The convergence is that the nulls are indigenous: the traditions ran their own cheapest checks, got nulls, and preserved the results in paratext, in margins, chapter-end warnings, asides, and abstracts of final results that never publish numbers. What makes this visible now is the archive's fetch depth: whole books past JavaScript gates, dead hosts rescued before they vanish, film registers opened. The nulls were always in the corpus's own sources; the corpus had only ever read the printed doctrine.

## 3. Rival Readings

**Reading A: the indigenous null is the tradition's real epistemology.** The founders knew. Veynik's margin note is not a lapse but the honest record of the same hand that wrote the protocol sections: he ran the obvious control, the rigid mount, got the null, and preserved the inference where the printed doctrine did not have to face it. On this reading the tradition's deepest primary sources are self-correcting documents, and the archive's job is to hold both voices, the doctrine and the margin, without letting either erase the other. Evidence: the note appears at three separate pages (338, 501, 546), so it is a recurring judgment, not a single mood; it is anchored to a named control with an explicit inference (does not exclude ideomotorics); Chapter XVIII section 28 carries an experimenter warning, so the book itself institutionalizes caution; the KPI authors publish the anti-hype conclusion in their own study; Regnault 1948 records that dowsers are made by encounter, not lineage, which is a doctrine-internal account of the operator's role.

**Reading B: the margin note is bounded; one instrument recanted is not a doctrine recanted.** The note covers the dowsing frame, section 7. The same book presents the electronic sensors (section 8), the torsion rig (section 9), the geochronal bands (section 22), and the weighing methodology (Chapter XXII) straight, without recantation. Veynik recanted the cheapest instrument, the one where the operator's hand is visibly in the loop, and kept the instrumented program. On this reading the correct claim is narrow: the founder distinguished the frame from the sensors, and the archive should not flatten that distinction into a tradition-wide collapse. Evidence: the note's own anchor is the rigid-mount test of the frame alone; the fr lane shows the same boundedness in reverse, since the OZ null closes the water-detection claim while the practice tier persists beside it (Swiss 200-CHF insurance reimbursement and the Ordre National du Mérite surfaces, per FAL-fr-278), and the INRS abstract still declares a program even as its numbers stay unpublished.

**Reading C: the pattern is a fetch-depth artifact, not a psychology.** Heritage texts in this corpus were never edited against their own margins: the printed doctrine and the marginal doubt coexist because no publisher reconciled them, and reprints preserved both. What changed this week is the archive's reach: the 576-page full text fetched past a JavaScript gate, dead hosts rescued, film registers opened. On this reading the indigenous null is not a new fact about the traditions; it is a new fact about the archive. The deeper the primary, the more internal contradiction surfaces, and the same fetch depth applied to any corpus, mainstream included, would surface margins there too. Evidence: the recantation was invisible until fire 295 fetched the full text, and Forge's QC row says the scout row missed it; the hyiq.org rescue and the OCR backlog work are the same depth story; the fr thesis census surfaced its rows only when chapter 6 of a 676-page thesis was translated in the dossier.

## 4. The Selection

Reading A wins today. The decisive datapoint is the note's shape: recurring (three pages), anchored to a named control, and carrying an explicit inference. That is the structure of a judgment, not of a mood, and it is the founder's own. Reading B is retained as boundary discipline, and it is a real constraint on the selection: the note recants the frame, and I do not extend it to the torsion rig or the weighing methodology, whose cheapest controls remain unrun (that queue stands from Report 26). Reading C is retained as a method note that explains the timing, not the content: fetch depth is why we can see the note now, but a control run, a null, and an honest inference would be evidence in any corpus, and the recurrence across three pages is a property of Veynik, not of the browser route. What would change my selection: a full-text survey across the ru heritage shelf showing margin recantations are common in books whose doctrines the corpus otherwise credits would move me toward C; a case where the founder's own null was itself artefactual, say a rigid mount damping a real signal, would reopen A; a case where a tradition absorbed its founder's recantation and updated its doctrine would generalize A beyond one man.

## 5. Where We Went Astray

The assumption the corpus undermines: that the nulls in these traditions were always external impositions, hostile investigations, magicians, mainstream verdicts, and that a tradition's primary texts speak with one committed voice. The deepest primary sources are not committed documents. They are self-correcting documents. The founding author of the chronal-field program ran the frame's cheapest control himself, got the null, and wrote the result into the book that teaches the frame. The fr lane holds the same shape from the other side: the practice face on film (Seiler), the controlled null (OZ, 10/30), and the institutional silence (INRS "final results" with no numbers for roughly 24 months). Reading a tradition only through its printed doctrine, or only through its critics, misses the margins where the tradition judged itself, and both missings produce the same error: a monolithic picture of what the tradition's own authors believed.

## 6. What This Means

1. Read primary texts whole, paratext included. The recantation is three sentences on pages 338, 501 and 546 of a 576-page book, and it surfaced only because fire 295 fetched the full text past the gate. Any community citation of a primary source should state whether the whole source was read, not just the cited chapter, because the evidential grade of a claim can live in the margins.

2. Grade nulls by who ran them. An insider null (Veynik's rigid mount, the KPI authors' "unfounded") cannot be dismissed as hostile method the way an outsider null can (the OZ test, the Nature investigation of Benveniste recorded in the verification rail). The archive's verification layer should record the direction of each null: inside-out nulls are worth more per trial, they are harder to explain away, and the corpus now holds enough of them, Veynik, KPI, the Waseda synchrony closure from Report 26, to make the distinction systematic. This is the diversity argument at the level of who measures: the insider and the outsider are different channels that see different things, and neither can substitute for the other (Yang et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.03794).

3. Do not let the recantation become a blanket verdict. That would be blending in reverse: collapsing the frame, the sensors, the torsion rig, and the weighing methodology into one debunked tradition destroys exactly the distinction, cheapest unrun controls sorted by cost, that the corpus established as its research program. Selection, not averaging: the frame row moves grade because its own founder ran the control; the other rows stay where their evidence is, and the sorted queue from Report 26 is unchanged by today's find (Maryanskyy 2026, arXiv:2603.20324, on why selection preserves what consensus destroys).
