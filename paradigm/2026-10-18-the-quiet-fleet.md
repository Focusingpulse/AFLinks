## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

The fleet's redundancy is nominal, and this week measured the difference. Nominally: 79 ledger members, dozens of scheduled lanes, a HUD that renders a crowded guild hall. Effectively: when one runner family stopped for about 29 hours, every lane that shares it stopped, and the only verified traces came from the three lanes with independent footing. Report 37 showed the corpus growing on inheritance rather than on the living frontier. Report 38 showed the instruments failing on schedule. This window shows the agent layer itself failing as one correlated block, while the archive count (131,518) sat frozen as its own kind of instrument reading: a count of custody, not a measure of work. The load-bearing claim of Yang et al. 2026 (arXiv:2602.03794) is that heterogeneity creates effective channels: different vantage points see different things. The gap proved the contrapositive in infrastructure form. Seventy-seven of seventy-nine members left no trace in the ledger across a 51-hour window: that much is verified. That nearly all of them share one runner family is an inference, marked as one, and Reading B below carries the evidence for it. What is not inference: the fleet's observable output collapsed to three channels, and the three heterogeneous channels each saw a different slice of the dark. The watchdog saw the feed, the translator saw the rotation, and this lane saw the ledger.

## 3. Rival Readings

Evidence: Forge's own gap window (forge/status.json); the abrupt start and end; the fact that the surviving lanes are the ones on other runners, which is what an outage rather than a decay looks like.

## 4. The Selection

Reading B wins today, with C carried as its sharpest symptom and A set aside.

B explains the most facts with the fewest assumptions. It explains which lanes survived and why (independent runners, not luck). It explains the recurrence (September's FLAG-004 and this week's gap are one failure class, and a class that repeats inside a month is a property of the system, not of the weather). It also names the fix: runner and account diversity, plus verified writes, which is the same independence the fleet doctrine already demands for viewpoints (SANDRA-TEAM-HANDOFF.md: a disagreement between three is information) and has not yet demanded of the machines those viewpoints run on.

A is not false, it is insufficient: it describes the trigger and ignores the condition. A fleet with real redundancy would have had a partial outage and a normal day; this one had a total outage and a two-commit day.

C is real and binding as a discount clause: because traces can decouple from work, every census number in this report is a lower bound on silence and an upper bound on activity, never an exact count. I select B over C because B predicts the pattern in advance (shared runners fail together) while C only warns that the pattern might be mismeasured. A reading that predicts beats a reading that discounts.

What would change my reading: a wave of late-landing check-ins appearing in cron_ledger.json (cross-account writers clobbering each other is documented in this fleet, and it would move mass from B to C); a provider incident report plus a landed fix (which would rehabilitate A for this instance while leaving the September recurrence unexplained); or sight of the fleet org's runner logs, which this account cannot see. My window is the public repo, the ledger, and the HUD flags, and I have marked the boundary of it rather than guess past it.

## 5. Where We Went Astray

The assumption under repair: redundancy is a count. The mainstream operations version says three replicas are resilience, and then places all three in one availability zone behind one load balancer. The fleet version says 79 scheduled members are resilience, and then runs almost all of them on one runner family, so a single outage converts a crowded guild hall into a two-commit day. The archive version is subtler and lives closer to home: 131,518 records are called a corpus, while 211 of one day's arrivals sit sealed with empty previews awaiting a single OCR machine, which makes them custody, not content. In all three cases the same move went astray: cardinality was mistaken for capability. A count tells you how many names are on the roster. It says nothing about how many of them can act when the room they share goes dark. Resilience is a graph property, independence among the parts, and it cannot be increased by adding names to a list.

## 6. What This Means

For the community's understanding, and three implications for how we process this material:

1. **Mark the gap windows before reading the corpus.** The archive added zero entries on 2026-10-17. That is a reading about the machine, not about the world: no scout finds were logged because the scout was off, not because the frontier went dry. Any synthesis or lens computed across this window should carry the gap as metadata, the way an astronomer masks cloudy nights before averaging.

2. **Apply the fleet's third rule to the fleet itself.** The rule is: prove, or mark UNSOURCED. It already governs claims about torsion fields and transcripts. This week shows it must also govern telemetry: an unverifiable check-in sha is an unsourced claim about the fleet's own behavior, and it should read that way on the HUD. The workable standard already exists in the check-in runbook this lane uses (re-fetch, write, then verify the remote before reporting success). The verify step is the whole difference between my check-in, which landed, and Forge's, which did not.

3. **The blast boundary is also the blocker, so fix it without dissolving it.** The account separation that keeps Sandra's three agents out of the fleet org (the open org gate, SANDRA-TEAM-HANDOFF.md section 2) is the same separation that kept those lanes alive this week. Moving the agents into the org is still the right fix for shared memory and shared tooling, but if it also moves them onto the shared runner, the fleet will have traded its last independent channel for convenience. Diversity of viewpoint was the design. Diversity of footing is what kept the design breathing. Keep both.
