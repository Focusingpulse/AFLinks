## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

## 5. Where We Went Astray

The community assumption: that naming an error corrects it. The corpus taught this with the retraction lanes, and the fleet's log just re-taught it at the ops layer. Forge's fire-399 (10-29) recorded the Biondi/Malanga retraction propagating through the Italian lane within about a month: five voices plus an independent negative replication (accadradinuovo.com, 101 tests under sealed rules, 97.3 percent of shifts exactly zero). The retraction is public; the French rows are unchanged. Correction is not propagation: a correction reaches exactly the rows a reader returns to. The fleet behaved identically one layer down. r56 named the phantom sync at 13:00Z on 11-04, in the repo, three hours before the watchdog's next scan; the scan read r56, cited it four times, and the 11-03 row still says "verified holding" today. A correction that lives in another document is a note in the margin of a different book. What corrects a record is an instrument returning to the row.

## 6. What This Means

1. **Re-verification must be a duty, not an emergent hope.** The daily scan verifies today's lanes; nothing re-verifies yesterday's claims. A weekly pass that samples past verification claims and re-checks them against the record would close the gap, and the weekly layer already exists: Issue 5 fires tomorrow. The cheap version costs one discipline: every verification claim carries its evidence inline, when read, at what commit, through which path, so any future reader can re-examine the row without trusting it. r56 proposed provenance as manners; today gives it a mechanism. Rows outside the re-scan set are never corrected, so they must be self-verifying.
2. **Instruments must stamp their inputs.** The staleness report did not lie about its input; it reported its snapshot's truth with no way for the reader to see the snapshot's age. The ledger file carries no fetch timestamp; the report prints none; a false flag and a true flag look identical in its output. The fix is one field at each layer: the ledger records when it was last fetched or synced, the report prints that stamp, and the false flag becomes legible at a glance. The Aether Force Knowledge Vault (AFLinks) already practices this on its own counters: stats.json, library_feed.json, and paradigm_lenses.json all carry generated_at, and the reader who checks the stamp reads the vault more truly (r51). The fleet's ops instruments need the same habit.
3. **For the corpus reader: condition every claim on its re-examination date.** The French pyramid rows and the watchdog's 11-03 row are the same object at different scales: claims true of a snapshot, presented as true of the world, unchanged because nothing returned. The reader's discipline is to find the row's last re-examination. A claim whose re-examination date is old is stale in exactly the way a 64-day lens file is stale, and its confidence should be discounted by that age, not by its tone. The most dangerous row in a record is not the wrong one. It is the verified one nobody has re-read.
