## 3. Rival Readings

**Reading A: transmission noise, real effects.** On this reading the pattern is what any century-old amateur literature looks like after archival QC. Citations decay as they travel. The pyramid evaporation effect replicates, and Ivanitskii and Narimanov confirmed the folk observation themselves. The Torsind's ten-year eclipse record with correlations of 0.892 to 0.975 is a real quantitative anomaly regardless of who reads it. The miscites are copy errors, and copy errors are not evidence about the effects. Evidence for this reading: the 2002 paper does replicate the observation; the Torsind record is long, dated, and numeric; the French instrument family documented in Report 21 (FAL-fr-215-3) shows the same era had a genuine instrument culture, so the instruments are not props.

**Reading B: a self-undermining literature.** On this reading the pattern is diagnostic. Where a controlled test exists, the mundane mechanism wins: pyramid evaporation is thermal geometry. Where the cheap control is named but unrun, the silk versus nylon A/B, the planeto-meter bake-off, the blind ion counter at claimed vein crossings, the tradition has had 20 to 96 years to run it and has not. And the miscite is not noise but advocacy filtering: the advocates cited a paper without reading it, in their own favor, for about two decades. Evidence for this reading: FAL-ru-232-3's finding that the miscited paper runs against the citing program; the Desbuquoit lane's 73-year demand curve with zero new experiments (FAL-fr-215-1, Report 21); the four-title reissue trail from 1939 to 2012 with the founder's own concession that no neutralizer works in all cases.

**Reading C: the archive is the experiment.** On this reading the pattern is neither noise nor a diagnosis of the tradition; it is the archive's own product. The unit of progress is the named unrun test, and the QC layer is manufacturing those tests at a rate the tradition never did. Evidence for this reading: every dossier since 09-27 ends by naming one cheap unrun test; the register went multilingual in days; the miscite was found by the archive, not by the tradition, which had 20 years and never opened the journal.

## 5. Where We Went Astray

The assumption the corpus undermines: that a citation is validation. The Adamenko program's only mainstream-journal citation was treated as support for planetary-radiation pyramid effects for about two decades. The cited paper, opened and read, is a controlled study that explains the effect as heat flow. Citation counting without reading the cited paper is where this community went astray, and the same failure shape appears in the Cody ionisation family (Report 21, FAL-fr-215-3): one citation chain, the primary unfindable, the effect-direction garbled in every French carrier. The mainstream habit of weighting bibliographic pedigree, imported into this corpus, produces exactly the wrong verdict here.
