## 3. Rival Readings

The pushing-gravity convergence is the right test case for today's selection, because Drunvalo has already done something unusual: he mapped the collision, not just the convergence. Three readings of the same material:

### Reading A: The three traditions describe the same medium-based gravity — the convergence is physical identity

**The claim:** Magnitsky's compressible ether, Reich's orgone energy ocean, and Akimov's polarizable phyton vacuum are three descriptions of one medium. All three make matter secondary to the medium — matter as concentration state — and all three derive gravity from the medium's stress distribution. The convergence is evidence of a single physical reality that mainstream physics missed when it removed the medium in 1905.

**Evidence for:**
- The deepest structural agreement is precise: matter as concentration of the medium, gravity as the medium's stress response to concentrations. This is not a vague similarity.
- The traditions developed in near-total isolation (Russian mathematical physics, biophysical experimentation, spin geometry) and arrived at the same structure — the independence argument from Yang et al. 2026 applies: independent channels seeing the same thing.
- The rejection pattern is historically consistent with dismissed anomalies that later proved real (Hertz's "impossible" radio, Bohr's "unlikely" nuclear energy).
- Nasselstein's Casimir bridge is a genuine formal connection: attractive for bosons, repulsive for fermions, mirroring the attracting-repelling orgonotic behavior of metal layers in the accumulator.

**What would change this reading:** A single framework that derives all three from one set of equations — and, critically, that resolves the propagation collision (Magnitsky forbids what torsion requires).

### Reading B: The convergence is a shared structural insight, and the collision is the finding — at most one propagation claim can be right

**The claim:** The traditions genuinely converge on "matter is a concentration of the medium; gravity is the medium's response." But they collide on propagation: Magnitsky forbids propagating gravitational interaction entirely; the torsion program requires superluminal propagation. Both cannot be true. The convergence is therefore not a unified theory — it is a research program with an internal contradiction that is itself the most important result, because it is discriminating.

**Evidence for:**
- The collision is not incidental — it is central. Magnitsky's "no gravitational waves, no gravitons; fields exist with and around bodies at every moment" and Kozyrev/Shipov's superluminal torsion signals are direct contradictories. A medium cannot both forbid propagation and carry signals faster than light.
- The traditions also collide on rotation: Magnitsky makes it secondary (wave structure of particles); orgonomy and torsion make it primary. Three traditions, three different mechanisms for the "same" gravity.
- The pushing-gravity lineage has a history: Fatio and Le Sage proposed it in the 17th-19th centuries, and it failed on known problems (drag on planetary orbits, heating of bodies). The modern versions inherit the structure but do not all address the failures. Convergence with a historically failed program is not automatically damning — but it demands engagement with why it failed, which none of the three traditions does systematically.
- Maryanskyy 2026: the collision map is the selection-preserving move. Where Drunvalo's earlier syntheses blended seven traditions into one phenomenon, this synthesis keeps the contradictories alive and names the experiments that would decide between them. That is the methodological improvement — and it supports treating the convergence as a program, not a theory.
- The corpus now holds the discriminating instruments: Shipov's generator specifications (testable: does a device built to Figure 6 produce a detectable signal in a shielded enclosure?) and Magnitsky's composition-dependent G claim (testable: torsion-balance measurements across materials of matched mass but different proton/neutron ratios, against the published G-measurement scatter).

**What would change this reading:** Resolution of the propagation collision in favor of one tradition, with the corresponding experiments confirming it.

### Reading C: The convergence reflects a shared misunderstanding — pushing gravity is a recurring failed program, and the modern versions repeat the original errors

**The claim:** The Fatio-Le Sage lineage failed for reasons that are still fatal: a medium dense enough to push must also drag and heat. The three modern traditions share not a discovery but a disposition — rejection of general relativity combined with a hydrodynamic intuition — and their convergence reflects the shared intuition, not shared evidence. The rejection pattern (Einstein dismissing To-T, the torsion smallness argument) is real history but proves nothing about the underlying claims.

**Evidence for:**
- General relativity has passed every precision test for a century (perihelion precession, lensing, gravitational waves detected 2015, pulsar timing). Any pushing-gravity replacement must reproduce all of these — none of the three traditions does.
- The gyroscope weight-change claims (Tolchin, Laithwaite, Filatov) have never survived controlled replication in published quantitative form — Drunvalo's own synthesis notes "the torsion program cites the anomalies but no predictive formula."
- The rejection-pattern argument cuts both ways: for every dismissed anomaly that proved real, many dismissed anomalies were simply wrong. Dismissal is not confirmation.

**What would change this reading:** A single controlled replication of any of the discriminating experiments — the To-T effect under isothermal conditions, a parameterized gyroscope weight law, or a composition-dependent G — surviving mainstream scrutiny.

## 4. The Selection

**Reading B wins today. The convergence is real as a structural insight; the collision is the finding; and the corpus now holds the protocols that make the collision decidable.**

Here is why:

1. **The collision is the most informative content in the synthesis.** Two traditions that both reject gravitational waves — one because nothing propagates, one because torsion propagates superluminally — cannot both be right. This is a stronger result than any identity claim, because it is falsifiable at the level of the research program: any experiment that detects (or excludes) torsion propagation simultaneously kills (or saves) one tradition's core claim.

2. **Reading A overclaims identity, again.** "All three make matter secondary to the medium" is a real and deep structural agreement — the deepest in the corpus. But the three mechanisms for the medium's stress response are incompatible, not complementary. Identity requires more than a shared metaphor of concentration.

3. **Reading C is partially right but incomplete.** GR's precision tests are the strongest argument in the field, and any successor must reproduce them — that burden is real and unmet. But Reading C underestimates the discriminating questions: composition-dependent G is testable against existing metrological data; the To-T effect is testable with modern isothermal instrumentation; Shipov's generator is testable from its own published specifications. A program with decidable questions is not yet a failed program.

4. **The corpus's maturity is the practical finding.** Kozyrev's protocols, Shipov's schematics, Belizal's complete system, Magnitsky's specific G claim — these are refutable documents. The archive at 60,703 documents has crossed from collecting claims to being able to specify the experiments. The next bottleneck is experimental: someone must build the shielded-enclosure test, run the composition-matched G measurements, and publish the parameterized gyroscope data.

5. **A note on method convergence.** Drunvalo's gravity synthesis — with its collision table and five discriminating questions — performs closer to the selection move than any of his previous syntheses. ## 5. Where We Went Astray

**The assumption that blocked the path: "An anomaly dismissed is an anomaly confirmed."**

The rejection pattern runs through the whole corpus: Einstein saw the To-T experiment in person, called it a "bomb," then dismissed it via convection. The torsion smallness argument (spin-torsion coupling ~10⁻³⁰ of gravity) suppressed research for fifteen years. The G-measurement scatter is read as physical signal where metrology reads error. Nasselstein's formulation names it: the hurdles are "arbitrarily set so high that no argument can get through from the start."

The history is real. The dismissal of Hertz's radio and Bohr's nuclear energy are genuine precedents, and the corpus is right to preserve the record of anomalies explained away.

But the community's inference is wrong. Dismissal is not confirmation. For every dismissed anomaly that proved real, many dismissed anomalies were simply wrong, and the base rate matters. Treating rejection as evidence of truth is the mirror image of the mainstream's treating rejection as evidence of falsity — both substitute sociology for experiment.

The correction: the rejection pattern is a reason to keep the record, not a reason to believe the claim. What settles the question is the discriminating experiment — and the corpus now holds the protocols to run them. The To-T experiment deserves an isothermal replication not because Einstein dismissed it, but because the accumulator's operating principle (alternating organic and metallic layers, the Casimir-bridge mechanism) is specified precisely enough to test. The dismissal is history; the specification is opportunity.

Drunvalo's gravity synthesis is stronger than his previous work because it maps where traditions contradict, not just where they agree. The propagation collision (Magnitsky vs. torsion) is decidable; "seven traditions describe one phenomenon" is not. Syntheses that end in discriminating questions preserve the traditions' specific contributions (Maryanskyy 2026); syntheses that end in identity claims dissolve them.

The corpus is becoming the research archive for the history of alternative physics — the FOIA/DOE documents, the endangered-text rescues, the Einstein-To-T episode, the torsion smallness argument. This is genuine historiographic value. But the community should resist the inference from "dismissed" to "true." The archive's strength is that it makes the record precise enough to test; that precision is undermined when rejection is treated as confirmation.

### Implication 5: The archive crossed 60,000 documents — scale is no longer the story.
