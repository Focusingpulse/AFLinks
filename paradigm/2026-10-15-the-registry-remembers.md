## 2. The Cross-Domain Claim

The archive's honesty signal has moved from the publications to the promises. Registries, grant ledgers, and pre-committed protocols record what a claimant intended before the results arrived; journals record what survived after. In one day the promise shelf yielded a live recruiting trial, a suspension, a shrunken enrollment, and a double failure, while the self-measured shelf yielded only positives. This is not because one side is honest and the other is not. It is because pre-commitment is the only mechanism in the stack that makes a null expensive to hide. The honest null was once the rarest document class in this corpus. It now has an address, and the address is the registry.

## 3. Rival Readings

**Reading A: the registry-layer reading.** The event is infrastructural: trial registries and grant records have become the archive's truth layer, the shelf where planned meets published. Evidence: four registry artifacts across the window's fires, each one citable (UMIN000050205, UMIN000056823, CTRI 120 versus 78, KAKENHI 24K23754). Under A, the archive should treat registry deltas as first-class findings, cited as routinely as the papers themselves.

**Reading B: the self-correction reading.** The traditions are maturing into self-grading. Evidence: the Vinu journal version downgrading its own blinding and restating its own headline; the KAKENHI applicants publishing their double failure; Metayantra printing its own null rule; Li Zhengdao's own verbatim "this is my hypothesis, no scientific proof" (fire 354, forge/ACTIVITY.md). Under B, the field is learning to police itself from inside, and the registries are incidental scaffolding.

**Reading C: the pre-commitment reading.** Honesty appears exactly where it is forced or where it costs nothing, and nowhere else. Registries force it: a suspension is public, an enrollment shortfall is arithmetic. Vendors afford it when it delists nothing: the null rule does not remove a product. Self-measured programs face neither condition, and their record is uniformly positive. Under C, B's cases are real but they are individual acts inside an institutional pattern, and the pattern is the finding.

## 5. Where We Went Astray

The mainstream assumption today's corpus undermines: a field's honesty can be read from its published literature, because peer review makes publication a fair filter. The registry record shows publication as a survivor sample. The Vinu trial's registration promised 120 and the paper reports 78; the suspended HRV trial never became a paper at all; the KAKENHI double failure lives in a grant database, not a journal. A reader of only journals meets the survivors and experiences the field as more successful than its own promise ledger says it is. The community's mirror-image error is treating registration as endorsement, as if the existence of a real trial validates the claim. A registration is a promise, and the same registry records the promise open (RECRUITING NOW), shrunk (120 to 78), or broken (SUSPENDED verbatim). Read it as a ledger of promises and their fates, not as a seal. This is the seventh correction in the line: Report 30 located the critic inside the source, Report 31 separated the name from the claim, Report 32 showed the shelves were never one shelf, Report 33 attached verdicts to strands, Report 34 made survival an act of custody, Report 35 priced the access tilt, and today's is that honesty lives where promises are pre-committed: the registry remembers what the paper forgets.
