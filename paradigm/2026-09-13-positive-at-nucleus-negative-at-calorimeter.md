## 3. Rival Readings

### Reading A: Callisto closes the case — the positive results are real but irrelevant, and LENR as an energy program is dead

**The claim:** The 9-year, >100-contributor investigation is the most systematic test the field has ever had, and it found nothing at the calorimeter. The positive nuclear-product results are real but physically trivial: tiny fusion-rate enhancements that produce detectable particles and no useful energy. The 1989 dream — excess heat, new energy source — is falsified. What remains is an interesting nuclear-physics curiosity.

**Evidence for:**
- Callisto's scale: 9 years, >100 contributors, >40 replications, wide parameter space, and — critically — prosaic explanations for most prior positive calorimetry (power-accounting errors, hydriding entropy, stored chemical energy from recombination). Each proposed mechanism was tested.
- The Nature 2025 paper's own framing: "no net energy gain yet" — the authors themselves position the result as tabletop fusion *research*, not energy production.
- The magnitude gap: 15% enhancement of a rate that is itself negligible for power purposes is orders of magnitude away from the kilowatt-scale claims of the 1989-2009 era.
- The archive's own history documents the failure pattern: 37 years of excess-heat claims without a single independently replicated calorimetric result. Callisto explains why.

**What would change this reading:** A calorimetric result that survives Callisto's prosaic-explanation checklist — isothermal controls, recombination accounting, sensor validation — under independent replication.

### Reading B: The two result classes are compatible and jointly redefine the field — LENR is a nuclear-physics phenomenon, not an energy phenomenon

**The claim:** Callisto tested heat and found none. The positive papers tested nuclear products and found them. Both are correct. The field's error for 37 years was running the calorimeter when the interesting signal was in the particle detector. The redefinition: metal-hydrogen systems can host enhanced nuclear reactions under eV-scale stimulation — that is new nuclear physics. Whether it can ever yield net energy is a separate, currently unanswered question.

**Evidence for:**
- The observables genuinely differ. Neutron counting (Nature 2025, Sci Rep 2024) and isotopic analysis (JJAP 2025) are not subject to the calorimetric error modes Callisto cataloged. The positive results do not depend on any heat measurement.
- Three independent systems — electrochemical Pd/D, gas-phase Ni-Cu/H, acoustic Ti/D — all show nuclear products under stimulation. The convergence across systems is more convincing than any single result.
- The mainstream papers themselves perform this redefinition: the Nature paper is titled "Electrochemical loading enhances deuterium fusion rates" — a rate claim, not an energy claim. The UBC group's open-hardware benchtop design invites replication of the *rate* measurement.
- Maryanskyy 2026: the field's old framing ("excess heat = free energy") was a blending move that collapsed a heterogeneous set of claims into one banner. Selection preserves the distinction: nuclear products confirmed, excess heat not confirmed. Callisto and Nature 2025 both survive as documents; only the banner dies.

**What would change this reading:** A demonstrated contradiction — e.g., a Callisto-style systematic investigation that also fails to find nuclear products under the Nature 2025 conditions, or a calorimetric excess that survives the prosaic-explanation checklist.

### Reading C: Callisto is limited to its parameter space — the positive tradition's best configurations were not tested

**The claim:** Callisto's >40 replications covered prolonged electrolysis and pressure loading, but the positive results use different conditions: plasma immersion ion implantation (Nature 2025), gas-phase nanocomposites at ~450°C (JJAP 2025), acoustic cavitation (Sci Rep 2024). **Evidence for:**

A systematic investigation of electrolysis does not adjudicate gas-loading or cavitation claims.

**What would change this reading:** An extension of the Callisto methodology to the positive papers' configurations — PIII loading, gas-phase nanocomposites, cavitation — with the same rigor. If those also show no heat (while neutrons persist), Reading C collapses into Reading B.

## 4. The Selection

**Reading B wins today. The two result classes are compatible and jointly redefine the field: LENR is a nuclear-physics phenomenon, not an energy phenomenon. The archive should hold both Callisto and the positive papers as first-class documents, and stop treating either as a refutation of the other.**

Here is why:

1. **The observables are different, and the difference is decisive.** Callisto's null is at the calorimeter; the positives are at the particle detector. A claim of incompatibility requires showing that the positive papers predict heat that Callisto should have seen — they do not; a 15% rate enhancement is thermally invisible. Reading A's "case closed" and Reading C's "not tested" both miss that the field has split into two different questions.

2. **Reading A is right about energy and wrong about the field.** The excess-heat program is, on current evidence, dead — Callisto's prosaic-explanation checklist is the most systematic autopsy the field has received. But "no useful energy" does not make enhanced fusion rates irrelevant. The Nature result — eV-scale electrochemistry influencing MeV-scale nuclear reactions — is a genuine physics finding regardless of its energy budget. The field is not dead; it is redefined.

3. **Reading C is right about scope and wrong about what follows.** Callisto did not test PIII, gas-phase nanocomposites, or cavitation — true. But the correct response is not to keep the excess-heat banner alive behind untested configurations; it is to extend the falsification methodology to the new configurations. If the neutron results replicate and the heat never appears even there, Reading C collapses into Reading B.

4. **The convergence across three systems strengthens the nuclear-product claim.** Electrochemical, gas-phase, and acoustic stimulation; Pd, Ni-Cu, and Ti; neutrons and He-3. Three independent positive results with standard detection methods are harder to dismiss than any single calorimetric claim ever was. The archive's preservation of Fleischmann-Pons history was vindicated (Report 11) — but what was vindicated is the *nuclear* claim, not the *energy* claim.

5. **The archive's job now is to hold the tension.** Callisto belongs in the archive with the same care as the Nature paper — as a first-class document, not as an enemy. A corpus that only collects positive results is advocacy; a corpus that holds the strongest falsification document alongside the strongest positive results is a research archive. This is the Maryanskyy selection move applied to the corpus itself: do not blend Callisto and Nature into a mushy "the debate continues"; select the reading that keeps both documents intact and names what each shows.

## 5. Where We Went Astray

**The assumption that blocked the path: "The calorimeter is the arbiter."**

For 37 years the field's central instrument was the calorimeter: measure heat in, measure heat out, take the difference. Every controversy — Fleischmann-Pons, the DOE reviews, the replication wars — was fought over calorimetry. The assumption beneath it: if nuclear reactions occur, heat must appear; therefore heat is the test.

Callisto's investigation is the most rigorous execution of that assumption ever attempted — and it returned null, with prosaic explanations for most prior positives. The assumption's defenders will read Callisto as the final verdict. But the assumption itself was the error. Nuclear reactions announce themselves in products — neutrons, isotopes — before they announce themselves in heat. A 15% enhancement of a tiny rate is a particle-detector event, not a thermometer event.

The field ran the wrong instrument for 37 years. The calorimeter was answering a question ("is there net energy?") that was downstream of the question the particle detector answers ("are nuclear reactions occurring?"). The Nature 2025, JJAP 2025, and Sci Rep 2024 papers show what happens when you run the right instrument: the signal was there, small but real, and measurable with standard equipment all along.

The correction: match the observable to the claim. Heat is the observable for energy claims; products are the observable for nuclear claims. The corpus's quality gates should enforce this distinction — a heat claim needs calorimetry that survives the Callisto checklist; a nuclear claim needs products (neutrons, isotopes) detected by standard methods. Conflating the two is how the field spent four decades confusing itself.
