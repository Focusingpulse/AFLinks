# The Turenne Wave Taxonomy: Ten Varieties and the Saturation Phenomenon in French Scientific Radiesthesia

## Abstract

Louis Turenne (1872–1954), an École Centrale engineer and wireless telegraphy instructor under General Ferrié, developed the most systematic wave classification in French radiesthesia. Working from the principle that "similar bodies give rise between them to a wave phenomenon," Turenne identified ten distinct wave varieties: three horizontal (positive, negative, inverse), three vertical (positive, negative, inverse), radioactive waves, infra-waves, ultra-waves, and complex "Turenne waves." His most striking claim: the pendulum turns a number of times above an element equal to its atomic number, then stops — a phenomenon he called **saturation**. This synthesis traces Turenne's taxonomy through his biographical context, connects his wave varieties to Russian torsion field theory and form wave research, and proposes experimental tests of the saturation phenomenon.

## 2. Louis Turenne: Engineer of Waves

### 2.1 Formation

Louis Turenne was born in Paris on March 21, 1872, into a family of industrialists dealing in pumping equipment and water installations. He graduated from the École Centrale des Arts et Manufactures at age 19 — an engineering credential that grounded his later radiesthesia work in electromagnetic theory.

During World War I, he served as artillery battalion chief at Verdun, was wounded, and received the Croix de guerre and Légion d'Honneur. After his wound in 1917, he was appointed Professor of T.S.F. (wireless telegraphy) at the Artillery School of Fontainebleau under General Ferrié — a pioneer of French radio science.

This military-academic context is crucial: Turenne approached radiesthesia as an electrical engineer, not an occultist.

### 2.2 Research Program

Turenne's family business brought him into contact with water-diviners (sourciers). Observing their empirical success, he applied his electromagnetic training:

> "Water emits a characteristic electromagnetic wave which, once perceived by the muscular structure of the organism and recognized by a trained sympathetic nervous system, produces an induction of low-intensity current within the neuro-muscular system."

His thesis: the diviner's rod or pendulum amplifies microscopic muscle oscillations triggered by detected waves. The human body is the detector; the instrument merely makes visible.

This matches Rocard's later findings on magnetite receptors in human tissue.

### 2.3 The Neptunium Prediction

Turenne was the first to announce a "93rd body" in Mendeleev's classification and give its characteristics. Enrico Fermi later isolated it and named it Neptunium. Turenne's prediction came from radiesthetic detection, not nuclear physics — demonstrating that his methods could access information unavailable through conventional science.

### 2.4 The 1952 Trial

In 1952, Turenne was prosecuted by the Order of Physicians of Nice for practicing medicine without a license. The trial ended with purely symbolic damages of one franc — essentially an acquittal. Paul Reboux's funeral eulogy compared Turenne to Claude Bernard and Louis Pasteur:

> "He brought back to experiment, reason, and discipline a practice that minds of good will but too-rich imagination confused with magical practices."

Turenne died in Nice on June 30, 1954, at age 82.

## 3. The Ten Wave Varieties

### 3.1 The Taxonomy

Turenne's systematic investigation of elements, minerals, and natural bodies revealed **ten wave varieties**:

| Category | Varieties | Characteristics |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Horizontal waves | +, −, inverse | Detected with compass needles horizontal |
| Vertical waves | +, −, inverse | Detected with compass needles vertical |
| Radioactive waves | Four movements + stop | Radio-disintegration signature |
| Infra-waves | Below normal spectrum | Long wavelength domain |
| Ultra-waves | Above normal spectrum | Short wavelength domain |
| Turenne waves | Complex emission | Infra + ultra + neutrons + void |

The key insight: **elements emit waves in specific orientations and polarities**. Copper (atomic number 29) emits different waves than phosphorus (15) or uranium (92).

### 3.2 Orientation Matters

Turenne discovered that mounting compass needles at different angles revealed different wave types:

- **Horizontal mounting**: detected horizontal waves (+, −, inverse)
- **Vertical mounting**: detected vertical waves (+, −, inverse)
- **Oblique mounting**: revealed hybrid responses

This is analogous to polarization in optics. The wave field has structure; the detector's orientation determines what is detected.

### 3.3 The Radioactive Signature

Radioactive elements produce a distinctive pattern: four consecutive pendulum movements followed by a stop. Turenne called this the "radio-disintegration signature." It differs qualitatively from non-radioactive elements.

This provides a radiesthetic method for detecting radioactivity without Geiger counters — and potentially distinguishing different decay chains.

## 4. The Saturation Phenomenon

### 4.1 The Discovery

Working with metals and simple bodies, Turenne noticed a pattern:

> "His detector turned a certain number of times above each body, then stopped and refused any movement until it was temporarily moved away."

He recorded the number of turns:

- **Copper**: 29 turns
- **Phosphorus**: 15 turns
- **Uranium**: 92 turns

The number of turns equals the **atomic number**.

### 4.2 Interpretation

Turenne called this **saturation**. The detector "fills up" with the element's characteristic wave, reaches a threshold proportional to atomic number, then stops.

This is extraordinary if true. It means:

1. Radiesthesia can determine atomic number directly
2. Atomic number encodes wave emission properties
3. The pendulum responds to wave "quantity" in a countable way

### 4.3 Connection to Quantum Mechanics

The atomic number Z is:

- Number of protons in nucleus
- Number of electrons in neutral atom
- Position in periodic table

In de Broglie-Bohm theory, Z determines the quantum potential's structure. In torsion theory, Z relates to spin density (protons have spin). Turenne's saturation phenomenon may be detecting the same structural property that determines chemical behavior.

## 5. The Law of Similitude

### 5.1 Statement

**Two similar bodies, whatever their material, oriented north–south, give rise between them to a wave phenomenon — a reflected wave that goes from one to the other without passing beyond them.**

This is Turenne's fundamental law. It applies to:

- A map and the terrain it represents
- A photograph and the person depicted
- A witness sample and the substance it represents

The wave channel only exists when bodies are similar and properly oriented.

### 5.2 Newton's Law Connection

Turenne realized this connects to Newton's law of universal gravitation:

- Two bodies of equal shape and mass generate a reflected wave between them
- The meeting point is at mid-distance if bodies are equal
- The point moves toward the smaller body if they differ

But Turenne found: **surface area matters more than mass**. A hollow body (lighter but larger surface) shifts the meeting point toward the solid body.

This suggests the wave phenomenon responds to geometric structure, not just mass — matching form wave theory.

### 5.3 The Témoin Problem

A témoin (witness) is a reference sample representing what is sought. Turenne developed techniques for producing eternal, unalterable témoins:

- Elements of the Mendeleev table
- Colors of the spectrum
- Microbial strains
- Diseases
- Human organs

The challenge: avoid "parasitic waves" from handling. A photograph carries the subject's waves but also carries fingerprints, writing, and other contaminants. Turenne's blood-drop technique produces pure individual witnesses.
