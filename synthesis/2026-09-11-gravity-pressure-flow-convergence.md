# Gravity as Pressure and Flow: The Pushing-Gravity Convergence

## Abstract

Three research traditions that developed in near-total isolation from one another — Nikolay Magnitsky's Russian school of compressible oscillating ether, Wilhelm Reich's orgone biophysics (here via Peter Nasselstein's *Oranur-Physics* and David Boadella's 1955 orgone-gravitation lecture), and the Russian-Ukrainian torsion field program of Akimov, Shipov, and their German popularizers — converge on a single radical claim: **gravity is not a force of attraction acting at a distance, but a manifestation of pressure, compression, and flow within a material medium that fills space.** Where Newton posited attraction and Einstein posited curved spacetime, these traditions describe pushing forces, ether-density gradients, and superimposing currents. This synthesis traces the convergent claims through the three traditions, identifies the points where they contradict each other as sharply as they contradict the mainstream, and proposes the discriminating questions.

---

## 1. The Convergent Claim

Across the three traditions, four claims recur:

1. **Gravity pushes; it does not pull.** Magnitsky: "In gravitation there are no forces of attraction, but there are forces of pressing" [1]. Reich (via Boadella): gravitation is "a function of two superimposing orgone currents" — bodies move along their own energy streams that converge and separate, not because inert mass reaches across space [2]. Shipov: inertial forces themselves are generated by torsion fields — the geometry of rotation, not attraction [3].

2. **Matter is a concentration of the medium.** Magnitsky derives electron, proton, and neutron as wave solutions of the ether equations, with matter as stable compressions of ether density [1]. Reich: "Matter and the secondary energy bound to it are nothing other than highly concentrated orgone energy" — the "orgone energy ocean" is explicitly named as the substrate, the "ether," the "zero-point energy" [2].

3. **The medium is not empty space.** All three reject the vacuum of conventional physics. Magnitsky's ether is a "dense compressible inviscid oscillating medium" whose unperturbed density he claims to compute from the measured value of G [1]. Nasselstein's survey shows Reich's orgone concept anticipated the return of the ether through quantum physics itself — Dirac's sea, vacuum fluctuations, the Casimir effect — quoting Puthoff: quantum electrodynamics "has in a certain sense come full circle, furnishing us with the model of an energetic vacuum that is once again a completely filled space rather than an emptiness" [2].

4. **Rotation couples to gravity.** Shipov cites the anomalous weight changes of precessing gyroscopes (Tolchin's inertoid, Laithwaite's experiments, Filatov's gyroscope collisions) as evidence that spin-generated torsion fields modify inertial — and therefore gravitational — behavior [3]. Boadella documents Reich's finding that orgone moves in spirals at every scale, from muscular pulsation to galaxies, and that the spiral motion of natural phenomena is the visible signature of the orgone current that also carries gravitation [2].

This is the old kinematic "pushing gravity" lineage (Fatio, Le Sage, Newton's own correspondence-era doubts) reborn in twentieth-century language — but the three modern traditions arrived at it independently, through hydrodynamic field equations, biophysical experimentation, and spin geometry respectively.

## 2. The Three Traditions

### 2.1 Magnitsky: Gravity as Ether Compression

From the system of equations of compressible oscillating ether (continuity plus momentum conservation, following from Newtonian mechanics), Magnitsky claims to have previously derived a generalized nonlinear Maxwell-Lorentz system, the Biot-Savart, Ampère, and Coulomb laws, representations for the Planck and fine-structure constants, and wave-solution formulas for the electron, proton, and neutron whose computed energies, masses, and magnetic moments match experiment to fractions of a percent [1].

The gravity paper (Complex Systems, No. 4 (33), 2019) adds:

- **Gravity is pressing force.** The gravitational fields of protons and neutrons point toward particle centers (compression); electron fields point away but are negligible — hence gravity acts on all bodies, always "attractively," without any attractive force existing.
- **G is not constant.** It "weakly depends on the chemical composition of the interacting bodies" — consistent, he argues, with the non-overlapping precision intervals of G measurements (Quinn et al. 2013).
- **No gravitational waves, no gravitons.** Fields exist *with and around* bodies at every moment; interaction does not propagate from body to body. The r² law itself signals flat three-dimensional Euclidean space, not curved spacetime.
- **Gravitational collapse** is reinterpreted: increasing mass raises internal pressure, growing stresses in the compressed ether that gave rise to the body, ending in collapse with release of the ether's stored compression energy — an ether-dynamic account of supernovae-scale events.

### 2.2 Orgonomy: Gravity as Superimposed Orgone Currents

Nasselstein's *Oranur-Physics* (2025) is the most systematic recent statement of orgone biophysics, and its fourth chapter ("Space and Orgone") carries the gravitational content:

- **The substrate.** Reich's "orgone energy ocean" is explicitly equated with the ether and zero-point energy. Matter condenses from it via the "orgonomic potential" — a spontaneous concentration mechanism, not unlike Magnitsky's wave-solution condensations [2].
- **The Casimir bridge.** Nasselstein notes The Journal of Orgonomy's observation that the Casimir effect — attractive for bosons, repulsive for fermions — mirrors the simultaneously attracting-and-repelling orgonotic behavior of metal, which is the operating principle of the orgone accumulator's alternating organic and metallic layers. Puthoff's vacuum-energy program is read as late confirmation of what Reich practiced fifty years earlier [2].
- **The Einstein episode.** The To-T experiment (accumulator interior warmer than a thermodynamically identical control box) was demonstrated to Einstein on 13 January 1941; Einstein called it a "bomb" for physics, then dismissed it five weeks later via a floor-versus-ceiling convection argument. The orgonomy literature treats this as the paradigm case of a decisive anomaly explained away [2].
- **Boadella's gravitation lecture (1955).** Reich's theory: gravitation is a function of two superimposing orgone currents — earth and sun each move along their own energy streams, which "find each other, superimpose, and separate again." Orbits are not attractions but the braided paths of converging flows. Boadella contrasts this with Leonard Cramp's more worked-out "space aether" theory of gravitation (Cramp 1954, *Spacecraft in the Making*), finding Cramp ingenious but abstract: "In place of a purely theoretical and insubstantial aether, we have a practically utilizable and demonstrable orgone energy" [2].
- **The engineering claim.** The cloudbuster — long steel tubes drawing energy from the atmosphere — is presented as directed manipulation of the orgone envelope, including the documented 1953 diversion of a hurricane from the New York coast. Orgone work is framed as the first practical tapping of the same reservoir Cramp described: "Even in the aether itself there is a power that lies beyond our wildest dreams" [2].

### 2.3 Torsion: Gravity as Spin Geometry

The Akimov-Shipov program and its German expositors (Grachev's *Study on Torsion Fields*, Schadach's extended-electromagnetism essays) supply the third face of the convergence:

- **Inertial forces are torsion.** In Shipov's Theory of the Physical Vacuum, all inertial forces — Coriolis, centrifugal, Euler — are generated by torsion fields in a space of absolute parallelism with rotational coordinates [3]. Gravity and inertia share a geometric origin in rotation.
- **Gyroscope anomalies.** Weight changes in precessing gyroscopes (Tolchin's inertoid, built at the Perm Machine-Building Plant; Laithwaite's demonstrations; Filatov's collision experiments) are cited as the experimental signature that rotation modifies the inertial field [3].
- **The vacuum is structured.** Akimov's phyton model — nested electron-positron ring packets, polarizable into E-, G-, and S-field states — makes gravity a longitudinal spin polarization of the same medium whose transverse polarization is torsion [4].
- **The German reception.** Grachev's study, circulating in German, extends the program to consciousness and information (Kozyrev's star-position experiments reading the "actual" position of stars via torsion radiation), while Schadach's Barrett-based extended electromagnetism places non-Hertzian longitudinal waves at the center of vacuum energy extraction [5].

## 3. Where the Traditions Agree — and Where They Collide

| Question | Magnitsky (ether) | Orgonomy | Torsion program |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature of gravity | Pressing force of ether compression | Superimposition of orgone currents | Inertial/torsion field of spin geometry |
| Medium | Compressible oscillating ether (computable density) | Orgone energy ocean (= ether = ZPE) | Physical vacuum, phyton structure |
| Matter | Wave solutions of ether equations | Concentrated orgone energy | Condensations of vacuum polarization |
| Rotation's role | Secondary (wave structure of particles) | Primary (orgone moves in spirals at all scales) | Primary (torsion generates inertia) |
| Gravity waves | Do not exist | Not addressed | Torsion waves exist, superluminal |
| G constant | Not constant (composition-dependent) | Not addressed | Not addressed |
| Technology | Energy release at collapse | Cloudbuster, accumulator, ORUR | Torsion generators, inertoid propulsion |

The sharpest internal contradiction: **Magnitsky denies any propagating gravitational interaction (fields exist with bodies, nothing travels), while the torsion program's central experimental claim is superluminal propagation of spin-generated signals** (Kozyrev, Lavrentiev, Pugach star observations). Both reject gravitational waves as conventionally conceived, but for opposite reasons — one forbids propagation entirely, the other claims propagation far faster than light. Orgonomy sits between: orgone streams flow, but the orbit is the braided flow itself, not a signal passed between bodies.

The deepest agreement: all three make **matter secondary to the medium**. If matter is a concentration state of ether/orgone/vacuum, then gravity between concentrations is a property of the medium's own stress distribution — attraction becomes unnecessary as a concept. This is the structural convergence, deeper than any particular equation.

## 4. The Rejection Pattern

Each tradition documents the same sociological event: a decisive anomaly dismissed by a conventional mechanism.

- **Einstein and To-T (1941):** the accumulator's temperature excess, demonstrated in person, dismissed as floor-ceiling convection [2].
- **Torsion's smallness argument:** the G×ℏ estimate that spin-torsion coupling must be ~10⁻³⁰ of gravity suppressed research for fifteen years — until the dynamic-torsion results (Hehl, Kibble, Sciama) showed the static bound does not apply to radiating spin sources [4].
- **G's measurement scatter:** Magnitsky reads the non-overlapping precision intervals of G measurements as physical signal (composition dependence) where the metrology community reads experimental error [1].

Nasselstein's formulation is the shared diagnosis: the hurdles are "arbitrarily set so high that no argument can get through from the start" — the To-T experiment is required to work in a sealed sauna, i.e., in conditions where the claimed medium-exchange effect cannot occur by design [2]. Whether or not the underlying claims survive, the pattern itself is historically consistent with past anomalies (Hertz's "impossible" radio, Bohr's "unlikely" nuclear energy).

## 5. Discriminating Questions

1. **Is G composition-dependent?** Magnitsky's claim is testable with existing torsion-balance apparatus: systematic G measurements across materials of matched mass but different proton/neutron ratios. The metrology community's scatter already invites this analysis.
2. **Does the To-T effect survive a convection-controlled replication?** DeMeo's 2009 work claims it does; a floor-isothermal, humidity-controlled replication with modern instrumentation would either close the 1941 episode or reopen it.
3. **Do gyroscope weight changes scale with precession in any published quantitative law?** The torsion program cites the anomalies but no predictive formula; a parameterized replication (mass, spin rate, precession rate) would convert anecdote to data.
4. **Can one medium carry all three?** The synthesis question: Magnitsky's compressible ether, Akimov's polarizable phyton vacuum, and Reich's orgonomic potential are three descriptions of a medium that concentrates into matter. Are they formally equivalent, or do their equations make different predictions (e.g., Magnitsky forbids propagation; torsion requires it)?
5. **What did Cramp actually propose?** Cramp's gravitation theory (1954) is cited here only through Boadella's lecture; the original belongs in the collection as the missing British node of this convergence.
