# Validation — Mizuno's Processed-Metal (SUS304) Branch and Its First Independent Follow-Up (2025-2026)

**Domain:** LENR / condensed-matter nuclear science (hydrogen-metal lattice, anomalous heat + nuclear signatures) — Japan and the community-replication circuit
**Recorded:** 2026-10-16 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

**Tadahiko Mizuno** (Hydrogen Engineering Development & Application Company Ltd., Sapporo) reports a **material-driven** LENR claim that is distinct from his better-known palladium-on-nickel-mesh method already in this Yard (`2026-10-05-lenr-texas-am-nipd-lattice-mizuno-replication.md`). In this branch, the **reactor is the container itself** — stainless steel that has been **heavily cold-worked** (compressed/tensile processed, buffed), then filled with **hydrogen at ~1 atm and heated to roughly 300–800 °C**. The claims:

- **Excess heat:** total thermal output reported at **1.25× the maximum input**, an excess of **~200 W** by airflow calorimetry with a stated error of **±2 W**.
- **Electromotive force** from electrodes in the cell.
- **Signals interpreted as nuclear:** a **neutron peak near 0.7 MeV** (not the 2.45 MeV of D–D fusion) measured with an NE213 scintillator, plus gamma/isotopic surface changes. The report calls the reaction **"highly reproducible"** and states the effect **grows with temperature**.
- The authors' own framing is careful on one point and loose on another: they write that **heat measurement alone "does not prove the existence of a nuclear reaction,"** yet also that "nuclear emission and excess heat **cannot be explained by known physical phenomena.**"

## Who claims it, when, and where

**Tadahiko Mizuno**, with **Steve (Kuo-Hsing) Hwang** on the 2026 preprint. The central 2026 document is **"Analysis and Control Factors for Generation of Nuclear Reactions in Metals," Research Square preprint, 7 May 2026, DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-9273542/v1** — a **preprint, under review, not peer-reviewed**, reporting **>500 tests since 2020** with focus on 2024–2025 data. Its peer-reviewed predecessors are **Mizuno, T., "Neutrons Produced by Heating Processed Metals," *European Journal of Applied Physics* 7(4):13–18 (2025), DOI 10.24018/ejphysics.2025.7.4.383** and **"Analysis of Trigger Input for Nuclear Reactions Using SUS Alloys," *EJAP* 7(6):7–20 (2025), DOI 10.24018/ejphysics.2025.7.6.393**.

## Who attempted to replicate it, and what happened

- **An independent community replicator** posting as **"MagicSound"** (LENR-Forum / Martin Fleischmann Memorial Project circuit) ran an **interim test at Ed Storms's suggestion** and reported data **"consistent with"** the 304-alloy activation. The LENR-Forum's own **August 2026 newsletter** describes it, precisely, as **"an experimental follow-up, not a completed independent replication."** That is the honest label and the one this record carries.
- **The same programme's palladium arm failed a calibration test.** An interim report on **SR1 Storms replication experiments (27 February 2026, LENR-Forum)** describes two Pd samples showing apparent **10–20% excess over 100–275 °C**; a second sample roughly **three times larger produced the same absolute excess rather than a proportional one**, which pointed to drift; a **post-calibration with the Pd removed matched the runs within ±1 °C**, resolving the apparent excess as a **calibration artifact**. It also logged **marginal neutron counts** in both the fueled runs *and* the post-calibration.
- **A community critique of the proposed nuclear route** (LENR-Forum, 6 August 2026) argues the mechanism cannot be checked by the obvious method: at Mizuno's reported **0.7 MeV** the **Cr-50 neutron-capture cross-section collapses from ~17 barn to ~0.01–0.02 barn**, and Cr-51 emits its characteristic 320 keV line in only **~9.9%** of decays — so a **HPGe spectrometer would see no 320 keV peak** even after a long run. Correlation between cold-work, hydrogen and the signals is not evidence that chromium is the fuel.

## Result — the honest state

**One partial independent follow-up reporting consistency; no completed independent replication; a same-programme arm resolved as drift; and an unresolved verification problem for the nuclear interpretation.** The SUS304 branch looks more internally consistent than the older Pd branch — Mizuno's own note that his *calibration* container sometimes exceeded the *test* container is a real self-criticism — but the claim still rests on the originating group's calorimetry, with the community unable to independently confirm the nuclear signature.

## Confidence: low

**Why low.** The central document is a **preprint under review, not peer-reviewed**; the independent support is a **forum-sourced interim test explicitly labelled a follow-up, not a replication**; the **same programme's Pd arm was resolved as a calibration artifact** when a fresh calibration was run; the **neutron evidence is marginal** and appears in the post-calibration too; and the claim class carries a **standing nine-year null** — Project Callisto's 2015–2024 campaign (100+ contributors, 40+ Fleischmann–Pons replications, 200,000+ sample-hours) reported **no convincing anomalous heat** (this Yard, `2026-09-17-lenr-callisto-null-campaign.md`).

**Why not dismissed.** It is a **genuine independent replication attempt by a named community**, at a named scientist's suggestion, with a specific result reported — which is what the lane exists to catch. It is **not** a press release with no test behind it.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as reported:** Mizuno says a cold-worked stainless-steel container heated in hydrogen gives more heat out than in, plus signals read as neutrons and gamma, and his 2026 preprint reports more than 500 such tests since 2020. This run, that claim got its **first independent hearing** — one community replicator, testing at Ed Storms's suggestion, reported data consistent with it, and the community's own newsletter correctly calls that a **follow-up, not a replication.** Filed **low**, for specific reasons: the central paper is **not peer-reviewed**, the follow-up is **forum-sourced and incomplete**, the **same programme's palladium arm turned out to be calibration drift** when the calibration was re-done, the neutrons are **marginal and appear even in the post-calibration**, and the whole class sits on **Callisto's nine-year null.**

The value of the record is the *shape* it leaves for the reader: a self-described reproducible metal-hydrogen effect, in the field's most active claim, with **one partial independent test that leaned supportive and no completed one.** A negative is a complete result here too; what the rail still needs is a **single independent laboratory** willing to run the container against a matched control and publish the calorimetry — not another replication claim.
