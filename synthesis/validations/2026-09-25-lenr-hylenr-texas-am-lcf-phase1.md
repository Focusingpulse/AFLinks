# Validation — The Lattice-Confinement Reactor at a University Nuclear Engineering Department

**Domain:** energy (LENR / anomalous heat effect; lattice confinement fusion)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-25 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The standing claim, in two observables — this Yard's own split, from Dossier 021:

- **A. the energy claim** — a metal–hydrogen system driven out of equilibrium releases more thermal energy than is supplied to it; tested by **calorimetry**;
- **B. the nuclear claim** — the same systems host nuclear reactions at rates far above bare-nucleus expectations, evidenced by neutrons, helium or transmutation products; tested by **particle detection and isotopic analysis**.

**Hylenr Technologies** (Hyderabad, India) asserts a specific version: "Lattice Confinement Fusion" (LCF) — deuterium loaded into a palladium lattice at high density ratios, nanoscale active environments, coherent charge clusters, **no plasma and no containment vessel** — producing both sustained excess heat and transmutation products (the company's site names yttrium and uranium on post-reaction EDX).

**This line was already in the Yard, dated.** Dossier 021 (2026-09-17) carries Hylenr as **watch-only**: *"press and conference presentation, no peer-reviewed paper, no independent replication. A claim, not a validation."* What changed on 2026-09-21/22 is that the company announced a **completed Phase 1 study at a university**, and that a university-trained co-author appears on an ICCF-27 talk. That is the update this record exists to carry.

## Who ran it, when

**Hylenr Technologies** (Hyderabad; T-Hub incubated) with the **Department of Nuclear Engineering, Texas A&M University**, College Station, TX — in **Professor Lin Shao's** laboratory. The ICCF-27 program names **Anthony Cecchini** on the talk; Cecchini is a Nuclear Engineering PhD student at Texas A&M who began under Prof. Lin Shao in Fall 2025 and worked in that accelerator laboratory as an undergraduate from 2023. Hylenr's side includes **Agastya Rao** (Principal Research Lead), **Lavanya Upadhyaya**, **Siddhartha Durairajan** (CMD and co-founder) and **Shashank Siddhartha**.

- **Phase 1 completed:** announced 21–22 September 2026.
- **Presented at ICCF-27** — 27th International Conference on Condensed Matter Nuclear Science, Niagara Falls, Canada, **31 August – 4 September 2026**.

## Method

As described in the public record (company release, repeated by trade press):

- **Device:** the **BRT-NiUCS-2** reactor — a small modular system built on hydrogen-loaded **nickel–palladium** catalyst materials. **Multiple devices and catalyst samples** were prepared and tested under controlled laboratory conditions in the TAMU Nuclear Engineering Department.
- **Diagnostics reported:** **residual gas analysis (RGA)** on an **SRS RGA 100** under high vacuum; **thermocouples plus calibrated infrared imaging**; radiation-emission measurement; **post-reaction SEM/EDX** on catalyst samples.
- **Calorimetry: not quantitative in Phase 1.** The company states Phase 2 will apply quantitative calorimetry. Phase 1 compares operating temperature against a "calibration device" at "comparable input power" — that is a comparison, not an energy balance.
- **Controls:** a calibration device for the temperature comparison; background gas measurement for the RGA. **No blinding is described and no independent party is named as holding the analysis key.**

## Result — as reported

- **Gas.** Elevated **helium, argon and neon** in the active reactor versus background. Helium and argon reported at **two to three orders of magnitude above background**. **No corresponding nitrogen rise**, which the company reads as evidence against atmospheric leakage as the sole explanation.
- **Thermal.** Thermocouple and calibrated infrared measurements showed **higher operating temperature in the active reactor than in the calibration device at comparable input power**.
- **Materials.** SEM/EDX indicated changes in the catalyst after testing.
- **Not in the public record:** no COP, no excess-power figure, no energy balance, no isotopic-ratio data, no neutron or gamma count rate.

## Confidence: medium that a TAMU-run Phase 1 test happened; low that the reported signatures are anomalous

**What corroborates it (independently of the press release).** The **live ICCF-27 program** lists, in Session 3, **Monday 31 August 2026, 16:20–16:45**:

> *"Nuclear Signatures in a Hydrogen-Loaded Ni–Pd Lattice Confinement System"* — **Agastya Rao, Lavanya Upadhyaya, Anthony Cecchini**

Two of those three are Hylenr staff; the third is a Texas A&M nuclear-engineering PhD student in Lin Shao's group. So a **TAMU-co-authored Hylenr talk demonstrably existed on the ICCF-27 program**. That is external corroboration of the collaboration, and it is why this record is filed at all.

**Two notes against over-reading it.** (1) **The title differs.** The press release titles the paper *"Validation of Anomalous Heat and Nuclear Signatures in the BRT-NiUCS-2 Reactor: Phase 1 LCF Investigation"*; no talk of that name appears on the program. A conference abstract and a paper title can legitimately differ, so this is a discrepancy to note, not a finding. (2) **The calorimetry arm was company-only.** The program's second Hylenr talk — *"Dynamic Closed-Loop Calorimetry for Evaluation of Temperature-Dependent Thermal Performance in Hydrogen-Loaded Ni-Pd Systems,"* Tuesday 1 September, 09:45–10:10 — is by **Agastya Rao, Shashank Siddhartha, Siddhartha Durairajan**, all Hylenr. So the university co-author sits on the **nuclear-signatures** talk; the **heat** talk carries no university name.

**Why the anomaly label stays low.**

- **No paper.** The ICCF-27 proceedings page returned **HTTP 200 / 9,845 bytes / "Under Construction"** on 2026-09-25 — re-verified this run; it has been unpublished since the conference. No peer-reviewed version, no preprint, no deposited data.
- **The independence is partial by construction.** The paper is **co-authored by the vendor and the university**. This is a **vendor–university collaboration**, not a third-party replication. The Yard's "institutional" flag applies; the word "independent" does not, on this record.
- **The heat arm has no calorimetry.** A comparative operating temperature at "comparable" input power, against a "calibration device," is not an energy balance. On Dossier 021's pre-registered checklist this fails at items 1, 2, 5 and 7 before any number is read.
- **The nuclear arm has a live prosaic reading that the public record does not close.** Noble-gas excess at 10²–10³ × background with no nitrogen rise is a real measurement shape, and that is a genuine point in its favour. But chamber and catalyst outgassing, loading history, getter effects and prior gas exposure are all alternatives that the release does not exclude, and the discriminating instruments the company says it will bring — **SIMS, ICP-MS, isotopic ratios** — are named as *Phase 2*, i.e. not yet run.
- **Verdict on the Yard's own ladder: INCONCLUSIVE** — a positive or near-positive signal with checklist items open. That is the honest verdict for most of this field's history, and Dossier 021 exists precisely so it is not read as a pass.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Filed **loudly**, because a company putting its own reactor into a university nuclear-engineering department and reporting gas and thermal signatures is exactly the kind of event this Yard exists to catch, and because it moves a Yard watch-only line into a dated result. Filed **INCONCLUSIVE**, because the heat arm has no calorimetry, the nuclear arm has no readable paper, the control is a comparison device rather than a blinded blank, and the institution that would make it independent is also a co-author. Recorded as **claimed / attempted / measured / reported**: a Phase 1 collaboration happened and a program line proves it; the numbers arrive through the vendor's own press release; the discriminating instruments are named for Phase 2 and have not run. Phase 2 is the record to wait for, and Dossier 021 is the checklist it should be read against.
