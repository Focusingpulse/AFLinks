# Validation — High-Frequency Induction Excess Heat (Winzeler / ZHAW)

**Domain:** LENR / anomalous heat and transmutation
**Recorded:** 2026-09-23 (Replication Watch, run 4)

## What was claimed

The anomalous heat effect: that hydrogen-loaded metals, driven by an external field, can hold **identical output temperatures on substantially less electrical input** than the same apparatus requires in its inactive state — a coefficient of performance above 1 that no resistive or chemical process in the device accounts for. Alongside the heat, the paper claims **compositional change** in the metal surface, presented as consistent with nuclear transmutation.

The paper positions itself explicitly as a platform for LENR replication, not as a resolution of the mechanism.

## Who reported it, when

**Heinz B. Winzeler**, **ZHAW Zurich University of Applied Sciences**, Switzerland — single author.

- Presented at **ICCF-26**, the 26th International Conference on Condensed Matter Nuclear Science, **Morioka, Japan, 26–30 May 2025**.
- Published **3 June 2026** in the *Journal of Condensed Matter Nuclear Science* **41**:153–161.

**Volume note, worth recording because our own notes had it wrong:** JCMNS **41 (2026) is the ICCF-26 proceedings volume** (Morioka, May 2025). It is *not* the ICCF-27 volume. **ICCF-27** (Niagara Falls, Canada, 31 Aug – 4 Sep 2026) has **not published its proceedings** — iccf-27.org `/proceeding/` still reads "Under Construction" as of this run, ~9.5 weeks after the conference closed.

## Method

As reported in the abstract (see the scope limit below):

- A **compact quartz-glass reactor** combining **inductive heating** with **high magnetic-field excitation**.
- Test materials: **palladium-coated foils** and **pure nickel foils**.
- A pressure protocol: gradual **reduction of pressure** produced repeatable *activation*; short **hydrogen pulses** produced *deactivation*.
- Thermal outcome measured as the electrical input needed to hold a given output temperature, in the active versus inactive state.
- Post-run surface analysis by **scanning electron microscopy (SEM)**; composition by **energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX)**.

## Result

As reported by the author:

- **Coefficient of performance (COP) ranging from 1.4 to 2.4** in the active state — i.e. the same output temperature maintained at roughly 40–70 % of the electrical input the inactive state required.
- **SEM:** localised melting and pore formation on the foils.
- **EDX:** significant compositional change, with **carbon, oxygen, aluminium and silicon** appearing.
- Author's conclusion: "reproducible excess heat under well-defined conditions and surface changes consistent with nuclear processes," making the reactor "a versatile platform for systematic LENR research and scale-up."

## Confidence: low-to-medium

**What earns it more than a press release:**

- A **named university affiliation** (ZHAW) and a **peer-reviewed venue with a DOI and page range** — JCMNS 41:153–161.
- A **specific, gradeable, falsifiable thermal claim** (COP 1.4–2.4) rather than a qualitative "we saw something."
- The word the Yard cares about: the author reports the activation as **repeatable**, and describes a *deactivation* control (short hydrogen pulses). A device that can be turned off is a better claim than one that merely runs hot.

**What holds it down — and the first item is the whole problem:**

- **A heat signal is one observable, not two.** The Yard's **Dossier 021 (Anomalous Heat Effect, double-observable protocol)** was written for exactly this shape of paper: a thermal claim needs *either* checklist-gated calorimetry *or* a nuclear product signal. This paper offers COP numbers without any description of the calorimetric method visible in the abstract, and offers **EDX as the transmutation evidence**. EDX gives elemental composition, **not isotopes**, and cannot distinguish a transmutation product from contamination or from the chamber itself.
- **The reported elements are the tell.** Carbon, oxygen, aluminium and silicon are the four elements a **quartz-glass** chamber, its fittings, ordinary handling and the laboratory atmosphere are most likely to deposit. That does not make the result false; it makes the EDX evidence **uninterpretable** for the claim being made. **No SIMS, no ICP-MS, no isotopic ratios are reported** in the abstract. Without those, "transmutations" in the title is a stronger word than the data supports.
- **Single author, single laboratory, new positive claim.** This is not an independent replication of someone else's result — it is an *originating* positive from one group. On the Yard's ladder it sits at **"attempted, reported positive, awaiting replication,"** not at "replicated."
- **Scope limit of this record:** the full text (pp. 153–161) was not accessible in this run. **This record rests on the abstract.** Numbers were not verified against figures, and the calorimetric method was not inspected. Flagged as **abstract-only**.
- **A 2.4 COP claim, stated flatly, in a field whose central historical failure is irreproducibility.** The COP figure is the kind of number that has been claimed and retracted in this field for 37 years. It needs a method section and a second lab, in that order.

## Standing LENR ladder (updated)

The Yard has been accumulating a LENR ledger with *different kinds* of rung on it, and this adds a new kind. Kept side by side, not blended:

| Rung | What it is | Signal | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Callisto** (2015–2024, released 2026-09-08) | 9-year, >200,000-sample-hour campaign, >40 F–P replications | **null** | — |
| **Nature Comms 2026** | sub-keV D–D screening in PdD / TiD | positive | nuclear |
| **Kasagi et al., JCMNS 41** (2026) | NiCu thin films, ~1.1 W excess heat over 215 h | positive | reported |
| **Chen et al., *Nature* 644** (2025) | beam-driven D–D on Pd, +15(2) % rate, loading as modifier | positive | nuclear, with built-in electrolyte control |
| **Winzeler, JCMNS 41** (this record) | quartz reactor, inductive HF excitation, COP 1.4–2.4 | **positive** | **EDX only** |

Two of these five are nulls. The pattern to teach from them is **not** "LENR works" and **not** "LENR fails" — it is that the *observable class* decides what you can conclude. Callisto measured one observable rigorously and found nothing. Chen measured a rate with a control and found a small effect. Winzeler measured heat without an isotope measurement and found a large effect. Only one of those three has the internal control that would let you tell a real anomaly from an unmodelled loss term.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded as **attempted / measured / reported**: a university-affiliated researcher, in the field's own peer-reviewed journal, reports repeatable excess heat with a large coefficient of performance and surface changes he reads as nuclear. Both halves of that are true at once — **the thermal claim is specific enough to replicate, and the transmutation claim is not supported by the evidence offered for it.** EDX sees elements, not isotopes; carbon, oxygen, aluminium and silicon are what a quartz chamber and a laboratory contribute to any foil that has been heated to localised melting.

So the useful thing this record does for a home replicator is not to hand them a COP of 2.4. It is to show them **what a well-formed LENR claim looks like when it is still one observable short** — and to name the specific missing measurement: **isotopic ratios on the post-run foil.** That is the rung that would make the title honest.
