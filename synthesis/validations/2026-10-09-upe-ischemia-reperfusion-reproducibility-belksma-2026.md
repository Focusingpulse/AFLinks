# Validation — Ultra-Weak Photon Emission During Ischemia–Reperfusion: A Reproducibility Benchmark (Amsterdam, 2026)

**Domain:** biofield / bio-photonics (ultra-weak photon emission — measurement reliability)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-09 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The claim this record sits underneath is the **biophoton** one: that living tissue continuously emits light at extremely low intensity (roughly 10–10³ photons cm⁻² s⁻¹), that this emission is chemiluminescence from oxidative metabolism, and — in the stronger version used across the biofield literature — that it is a measurable window on a person's physiological *and* field state. The archive holds several biofield claims already (Reiki/biophoton, EEG cross-species, a placebo-collapse trial).

Every one of those claims presupposes a prior, plainer thing: **that the UPE measurement itself is stable and repeatable.** If the instrument gives a different number on a second visit, no claim built on it can be tested. This study tests exactly that prior — the reproducibility of the measurement — and is filed here as the noise floor the rest of the rail has to clear, not as evidence for a biofield mechanism.

## Who tested it, when, and where

**R. Belksma**, **T. L. Roos** and **A. J. Nederveen** (Department of Radiology and Nuclear Medicine, **Amsterdam University Medical Centers**, the Netherlands), with **E. J. A. van Wijk** (**Meluna Research**, Wageningen) — the van Wijk group being a long-standing centre of human UPE measurement. Published in the *Journal of Photochemistry and Photobiology B: Biology*, **11 March 2026**.

## Method — what they actually did

- **UPE recorded from the hands** of **30 participants** using a **custom-built photon-counting system** (photomultiplier-based), in a dark setup.
- **Two separate sessions per participant** (an intra-session and an inter-session comparison).
- **Transient ischemia** induced by a **two-minute upper-arm tourniquet**, then release (reperfusion) — a standard, reproducible oxidative-stress provocation.
- **Reliability quantified** with **Bland–Altman analysis**, **Pearson correlations**, and the **within-subject coefficient of variation (wsCV)**, plus a **normalized AUC** metric proposed as a more stable summary.

## Result — what was measured

- **The physiological trend reproduced:** UPE intensity followed a **multiphasic response** — a sharp **decline during ischemia to ~85% of baseline**, then **stabilization during reperfusion at ~95% of baseline**.
- **Baseline measurement reliability:** **high within a session** (r = 0.97, wsCV = 11.3%) but only **moderate between sessions** (r = 0.59, wsCV = 34.4%).
- **During the intervention:** **moderate** in the ischemic phase (r = 0.66, wsCV = 43.6%), and **poor in the reperfusion phase** (r = 0.32, wsCV = 99.9%).
- **A normalized AUC** quantification **improved the inter-session agreement** (baseline–ischemia r = 0.83, wsCV = 28.3%; reperfusion r = 0.80, wsCV = 35.1%).
- **Authors' reading:** UPE captures a **consistent temporal trend** through blood-flow restriction **despite significant individual variability**, and the study **establishes quantitative benchmarks** for UPE measurement stability.

## Confidence: medium–high (for the measurement finding), and it is a *null* on the stronger claim

- **What is strong:** a **peer-reviewed journal**, a **30-participant** sample with **two sessions**, a **pre-specified statistical toolkit** (Bland–Altman, Pearson, wsCV), and an **internal comparison that is the point** — within-session vs between-session.
- **What it actually establishes, and it cuts against over-readings:** the **between-session reliability is only moderate (r = 0.59)** and the **reperfusion phase is close to unmeasurable as a stable quantity (wsCV ~100%)**. Any biofield/biophoton claim resting on a *between-session* or *intervention-phase* difference has to clear that variability first.
- **What it does not do:** it does **not** test any biofield, consciousness, or practitioner claim. It is a **metrology** result. The consistent *direction* of the ischemia/reperfusion response is a real, reproducible physiological signal — which is why the record is worth keeping, and also why the low inter-session reliability is the honest headline.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured / reported:** an Amsterdam UMC team recorded biophoton emission from 30 pairs of hands across two visits and asked whether the number comes back the same. The *physiology* reproduced — a dip during a two-minute tourniquet, a return on release — but the *measurement* only partly did: excellent within a session (r = 0.97), moderate between sessions (r = 0.59), and near-noise in the reperfusion phase (wsCV ≈ 100%). Filed as the **reproducibility floor** for the biofield/biophoton rail — the first thing a between-session biofield claim has to beat — and explicitly **not** as a test of any biofield mechanism. It is a rare thing for this Yard to hold: a clean, peer-reviewed, negative-leaning *metrology* result in a domain that usually arrives as a positive claim.
