# Validation — The Observatoire Zététique Radiesthesia Protocol (Argenton-sur-Creuse, 2007)

**Domain:** radiesthesia / dowsing (Bovis-scale reading and buried-target discrimination)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-22 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

Two claims, nested:

1. **The specific claim under test.** A garage wrench belonging to Francis Gatti (president of the CASAR association) had, according to him, undergone a "poltergeist" event — dematerialisation, rematerialisation, spontaneous movement. He passed samples of it to several dowsers; **their own written reports stated the samples' "taux vibratoire" (vibratory rate) exceeded 20,000 Bovis units.** A soil sample from an alleged UFO landing site at Marcilly, supplied by one of the dowsers, carried a similarly exceptional reading. So the claim at stake was: **this object has a physical property that a radiesthesist can detect.**

2. **The general claim beneath it.** That a dowser can discriminate a target sample from physically matched distractors using rods or a pendulum — with the "vibratory rate" as the readout.

The Bovis scale is the French tradition's own numeric instrument. Its original anchor was physical: **1 UB ≈ 1 ångström, with 6,500 Å = red light**, and the standard reference for calibrating a reading is a **horseshoe magnet pole-flip** (a known, flippable physical difference). That anchor is why this test is clean: the tradition supplied both the claim and the readout.

## Who replicated it, when

**The Observatoire Zététique (OZ)**, a French skeptical organisation, at **Argenton-sur-Creuse on 17–18 March 2007**, with:

- **Francis Gatti** (CASAR) — supplier of the wrench samples and the origin of the 20,000-Bovis reports;
- **two named practitioners**: "M. R." and "M. B." (initials as published);
- **Nicolas Vivant**, who wrote the protocol and published it in French and in an English translation.

The critical design choice: the OZ **gave the practitioners methodological control over their own conditions** — including a run using a sample **M. B. supplied himself**, from a site he habitually worked with. This is the corpus's "étalonnage freedoms" — the tradition's own parameters, not the skeptics'.

## Method

Published in full on `zetetique.fr` before the results were discussed.

- **10 numbered boxes** (0–9), one sound signal as the trial marker.
- **10 metal samples of comparable size and mass**, one of which was the "poltergeist" wrench sample; **9 were matched non-target samples.**
- Boxes were placed **at a separation distance of the practitioner's own choosing** — the practitioner's own stated interference distance, ≥ 2.10 m in the protocol as published.
- **Three sealed envelopes**, lock-and-key custody; **five people organised into two teams that never met**, so the person coding the draw could not see the trials and the person scoring could not know the draw.
- The practitioner used **70 cm metal rods or a pendulum**, as they preferred.
- **The success criterion was fixed in advance and unilaterally: 1%.**

## Result

**Three independent experimental series. All three landed on chance.**

| # | Sample | Practitioner | Trials | Successes | Threshold for success at 1% | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wrench (garage Hubert) | M. R. | 7 | **1** | 4 | consistent with chance |
| 2 | Marcilly "UFO landing" soil (practitioner's own sample) | M. B. | 10 | **2** | 5 | consistent with chance |
| 3 | Wrench (garage Hubert) | M. B. | 32 | **4** | 9 | consistent with chance |

**The sample with the claimed >20,000 UB reading was not discriminated from nine matched distractors. Neither was the practitioner's own habitual sample.**

## Confidence: high

- **The protocol was published by the testing organisation, in advance, in two languages**, with the criterion fixed at 1% before any trial — so the threshold could not be moved after the fact.
- **The claimants are named and the claim is theirs**, including the specific >20,000 Bovis figure from the dowser reports (not from the skeptics).
- **The practitioners set their own conditions** — separation distance, instrument, and in one series the sample itself. The negative is therefore not "you tested it wrong."
- **The number of trials was small** (7, 10, 32). This is a real limit and it is named rather than hidden: an effect too weak to show up in 32 trials would not have been detected. What the result *does* settle is the **strong** claim on the table — that these samples carry an exceptional vibratory rate a dowser can identify. A strong signal should have been unmissable in 32 trials; it was not there.
- **A critique exists in the corpus's own tradition of critique, and cuts the OZ's way.** Fabrice Chapot (`pseudo-scepticisme.org`, 09 Nov 2007) faulted the OZ for **not documenting the randomization mechanism** and for reasoning as though the effect, if real, would present as a strong signal. The corpus's verification-rail synthesis records the same point (the practitioner's "liquefaction" as the run went on, and Henri Broch's *"Dura zetetica sed zetetica"* — the verdict held, harshly, inside their own camp). The randomization critique is fair; the conclusion — the target was not identified — survives it, because a failure to find a signal is not repaired by a loose randomizer.
- A later OZ sourcier test (14 July 2010, filmed by TF1) returned 10/30 successes, p = 0.197 — also non-significant, but underpowered, and Chapot correctly refused to call it either a success or a defeat. It is not folded into this record.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded as **attempted / measured / reported**: practitioners from the tradition's own network reported an exceptional numeric reading on a specific object; a third party built a protocol around that reading, let the practitioners set their own conditions, published the criterion first, and ran it three times; the object was not identified.

**Why this one belongs in the Yard's tip jar.** The Yard already holds the Munich 1986–88 campaign (>100 dowsers, 843 trials, false floor), Costerisant's 1937 field null, and the INRS 2026 study whose test is complete and whose verdict is not yet published. Argenton is the rung between them that the French rail was missing — and it is the one a home replicator can most directly imitate. It costs ten identical containers, ten matched samples, a sealed key, and a criterion written down before the first trial. **The whole protocol is a kitchen table and a promise not to move the goalposts.**

It also carries the lesson the corpus keeps rediscovering, stated here in its sharpest form: **the practitioner's own sample, supplied with confidence, was not found.** Confidence in a reading is not evidence for the reading — which is exactly what Dossier 005 (inter-rater agreement), Dossier 026 (buried-target hit rate) and Dossier 029 (psi-track) each test in a different way.
