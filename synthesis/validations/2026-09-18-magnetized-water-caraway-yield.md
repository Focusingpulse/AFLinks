# Validation — Magnetized Irrigation Water and Caraway Yield

**Domain:** magneto-culture / magnetized water in agriculture
**Recorded:** 2026-09-18 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

That passing irrigation water through a magnetic field ("magnetized water") changes its behaviour in soil and improves crop growth, water productivity and yield under saline or water-limited conditions — a widespread magneto-culture claim with a mixed literature.

## Who replicated it, when

N. S. Abd Elkareem, A. Amer, A. Shahin et al. Published in *Scientific Reports* 16, 16295, 26 May 2026. Field experiment at an agricultural research site, over two consecutive growing seasons (2023/2024 and 2024/2025).

## Method

Caraway (*Carum carvi* L.) grown in a split-plot design with three replications and 12 plots of 6 m² each (121 m² total, 1 m buffer zones). Main factor: irrigation water magnetized (W1) vs unmagnetized (W0). Sub-factor: surface drip (SDI) vs subsurface drip irrigation (SSDI). Soil electrical conductivity, irrigation water productivity, fruit yield, essential-oil yield and benefit–cost ratio were measured across both seasons.

## Result

Magnetized water reduced soil salinity buildup (non-magnetized water increased EC at all depths). Irrigation water productivity rose ~16% (SDI) and ~14% (SSDI). Under magnetized subsurface drip, fruit yield per hectare increased ~10.4% and essential-oil yield per hectare rose >24.6% (12.57 → 16.63 L/ha season 1; 13.38 → 17.11 L/ha season 2). Benefit–cost ratio improved modestly (e.g. 2.54 → 2.62 under SSDI). The authors report the effect held across two seasons but do not establish a mechanism, and call for future work on field strength, exposure time and magnet placement.

## Confidence: medium

Peer-reviewed in *Scientific Reports*; two seasons with three replications and a split-plot design with quantified yield endpoints — stronger than most magnetized-water reports. Limits: relatively small plots (12 × 6 m²); no blinding of harvest/analysis described; the physical mechanism by which a magnetic field alters water (or soil calcium-carbonate scaling, a common alternative explanation) is untested; and magnetized-water results are historically inconsistent across labs. Reported as a measured positive effect under these field conditions, not as a mechanism confirmation.

## Source

Abd Elkareem, N. S., Amer, A., Shahin, A. et al. "Physicochemical responses of soil and caraway crop to drip irrigation with magnetized saline irrigation." *Scientific Reports* 16, 16295 (2026). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-45468-3
