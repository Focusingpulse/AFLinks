# Validation — Radiant-Spectrum Calorimetry of Excess Heat in NiCu and Ni Thin Films

**Domain:** LENR / cold fusion (anomalous heat effect, metal–hydrogen)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-21 (Replication Watch, run 3)

## What was claimed

That nanostructured Ni-based thin films loaded with hydrogen (or deuterium) generate **anomalous excess heat** during hydrogen desorption — heat not attributable to chemistry, with an energy-per-absorbed-atom in the keV range, produced by the Iwamura/Kasagi/Takahashi line of work (permeation-induced transmutation; the NEDO "Metal–Hydrogen Energy" programme; the NiCu/CuNi-on-zirconia and multilayer-film families). This is the same family the Yard's Dossier 021 governs.

## Who replicated it, when

**J. Kasagi** (Research Center for Accelerator and Radioisotope Science, **Tohoku University**), **T. Itoh** (Tohoku / Yokohama City University), **Y. Shibasaki** (**Clean Planet Inc.**), and **Y. Iwamura** (Yokohama City University), Japan.

- First posted to arXiv as a conference presentation: **17 Sep 2025** (arXiv:2509.13847; the arXiv comment line reads "26th International Conference on Condensed Matter Nuclear Science").
- Published: ***JCMNS* 41 (June 2026): 62–72**, doi 10.70923/001c.163335.
- Earlier version of the same experiment: ***JCMNS* 39 (March 2025): 210–219**, doi 10.70923/001c.134004 (arXiv:2311.18347).

**This is a self-replication, not an independent one.** The authors are the lineage that made the original claim; two of the four are the same people. Treat it as the source verifying itself — which is exactly why the *direction* of the revision matters more than the headline number.

## Method

A **radiation calorimeter with a composite photon-detector system**. Two sample types: **pure Ni foil** and **NiCu composite thin films**. Samples were heated to **~1100 K in vacuum**; emitted electromagnetic radiation was recorded across **0.05–2.5 eV**, with detailed spectra taken below 0.5 eV. The total radiated energy was computed **directly from the measured radiation power, without fitting a gray-body emission model** — the point of the exercise, since a change in emissivity with hydrogen loading is the obvious way to fake or hide an excess. Long-term runs tracked the effect over hundreds of hours.

## Result

- **NiCu sample: excess heat ≈ 1.1 W.** **Pure Ni: a comparable but slightly smaller excess.**
- Sustained excess power for **at least 215 hours**, with a **decay time exceeding 2000 hours**.
- Energy released per absorbed hydrogen atom estimated at **>22 keV/H**.
- The thermal radiation spectrum peaked near **0.2 eV** and kept rising below 0.3 eV — a significant deviation from simple gray-body behaviour. Hydrogen **raised total radiation power by several percent**, with different spectral signatures for NiCu (little change below 0.3 eV, sharp rise above ~0.35 eV) and pure Ni (gradual rise from as low as 0.05 eV).
- **Against the earlier report of the same experiment**, the authors state two differences outright: **(i) the NiCu excess is much smaller than previously reported** (the 2023/2025 paper deduced **4–6 W**, 460 ± 120 kJ over 80 h, ≥410 ± 108 keV/H), and **(ii) a clear excess is now observed in pure Ni.**

The authors' own concluding caution is worth quoting in substance: the results "strongly support the occurrence of anomalous heat generation," but **excess energy alone is insufficient to establish a nuclear origin** — nuclear signatures (residual nuclei, γ-rays, charged particles) are required. Note that a companion paper in the same volume (Hioki, Iwamura, Itoh, Kasagi, Yamauchi, Takahashi, *JCMNS* 41:189–202, doi 10.70923/001c.163387) performs XAFS on the surface structure after the anomalous-heat run — i.e. the group is chasing the nuclear-signature half of the double observable, but this record has not read its numbers.

## Confidence: medium

- Peer-reviewed within the field's own journal (*JCMNS* is the condensed-matter-nuclear-science community's venue, not an independent physics journal), with careful, physically-motivated calorimetry — measuring radiation directly rather than modelling it is a real methodological step.
- **Self-replication, and a downward revision of the group's own number by roughly a factor of four.** That is the honest content of this paper.
- **The material-specificity weakened.** The original shape of the claim was that the *nanostructured NiCu composite* is what does it. Here plain Ni also shows an excess. Either Ni alone has the effect (which would move the claim toward the older Ni–H literature and away from the multilayer story) or the measurement floor/sample-to-sample variation has not been separated from the effect.
- No nuclear signatures reported here.
- *JCMNS* is a paywalled/limited-access venue; this record is built from the arXiv abstract and the publisher abstract page, not the full text. Full-text numbers (per-sample scatter, error budget, whether the pure-Ni excess was H-dependent) are unread.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded as **attempted / measured / reported**: a research group re-measured its own effect with a better-calibrated instrument, and the better instrument returned **a smaller excess, in more materials**. Read against the ladder the Yard already holds — Project Callisto's nine-year calorimeter null (2015–2024), the Nature Comms 2026 nucleus-signature positive, the Chen et al. *Nature* 644 (2025) beam-driven +15% rate with no heat — this pulls the LENR rail toward *"an anomalous heat signal that is real but small, material-unspecific, and still without a nuclear signature"*, not toward a confirmed new reaction. The next discriminating step is not another calorimeter: it is the same sample, measured for **both** observables at once, as Dossier 021 requires.
