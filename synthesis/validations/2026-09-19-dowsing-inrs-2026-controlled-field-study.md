# Validation — The INRS Controlled Dowsing Field Study, 2025–26

**Domain:** radiesthesia / dowsing (buried-target detection)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-19 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

The standing claim tested here is the practitioners' one: that a person holding wooden or metal rods can locate buried subsurface features — in this case pipes and the water in them — and that experience and instrument type make a difference to the ability.

## Who tested it, when

**Geneviève Bordeleau** (Institut national de la recherche scientifique, INRS, Québec), with **Manon Lévesque** (credited as "citizen scientist and dowsing practitioner"), **Erwan Gloaguen**, and **Bernard Giroux**.

- ACFAS 92nd Congress contribution accepted **2025-04-04** (session 207, natural resources).
- INRS news item **2025-04-21**.
- Field season at the experimental station by **baie Sainte-Marguerite, Sacré-Cœur (Côte-Nord, Québec)**, running to **autumn 2025**; ~50 participants by July 2025 and still recruiting.
- Final results presented at the **EGU General Assembly 2026, Vienna, 3–8 May 2026** (abstract EGU26-3985, DOI 10.5194/egusphere-egu26-3985, CC BY 4.0).
- **Peer-reviewed article: forthcoming.** The corpus records that the numeric tables were not public at the time of this record.

## Method

A **~100 m² plot was excavated** and objects buried in selected cells; the ground was backfilled and a **numbered grid of 25 cells** was laid out over it — "like a game of Battleship." Participants walked the grid and placed a token (in the field photographs, a plastic duck) on the cell where they believed an object lay.

Objects buried: **iron and plastic pipes**, either **empty or water-filled**; where water was present it varied as **saline or fresh**, **stagnant or flowing**. Instruments: **wooden or metal rods**, tracked as a variable in their own right. Participants: **54 — 27 with intermediate-to-extensive prior experience, 27 novices given basic training**; several participants ran **repeat trials** to assess reproducibility. The design was **co-designed with an experienced dowsing practitioner** "to ensure both methodological rigor and acceptance by the dowsing community."

This is the design feature that separates it from Munich 1986–88: the **cross-tab of object type × water content × instrument × experience** can detect a *sub-effect* (e.g. iron-only above chance, the magnetic hypothesis descending from Yves Rocard) even where a global null holds.

## Result

**Reported: the field experiment is complete and its results have been presented.** The abstract states "we present the final results," analysed as a function of object type, dowsing instrument, and prior experience level.

**What has not happened yet — and this record will not pretend otherwise — is publication of the numbers.** The per-row hit rates against the 1-in-25 cell baseline are behind the Vienna presentation and the forthcoming article. This card therefore records that **an independent controlled test was designed, run to completion, and reported**, not what its outcome was. It is a "someone tried, at scale, and here is where the answer will land" record, not a verdict.

## Confidence: low on the outcome — high on the fact of the test

- **The test happened**, with a documented design, a named team, a co-designing practitioner, and a public congress record. That is what makes it a validation rather than a proposal.
- **The direction is unknown.** Any assignment of a positive or negative result today, including a "no effect" default, would be an invention. The corpus should grade it whichever way it falls when the tables land.
- Limits worth stating in advance: single site; self-selected dowser pool (the experienced arm) though the novice arm partly answers this; buried pipes are admittedly a *proxy* for the practitioners' usual target of groundwater.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded here as **attempted / measured / reported**: designed, run with 54 participants, and reported at a major geoscience congress; outcome not yet readable. The three rows that will decide things when the article lands: **iron-vs-plastic** (the magnetic sub-effect — if iron separates from plastic, Rocard's mechanism gains its first instrumented trace; if nothing separates, the null strengthens in the one place a residual effect could plausibly have hidden), **experienced-vs-novice** (is any skill learnable or present at all), and **wood-vs-metal rods** (is the rod a passive pointer, as the ideomotor reading predicts, or does the instrument matter). This is the live item on the archive's verification rail; the honest record will grade it in either direction.
