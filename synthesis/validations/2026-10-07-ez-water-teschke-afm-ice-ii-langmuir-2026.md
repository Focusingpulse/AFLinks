# Validation — Interfacial Water Measured by AFM: Ice-like II Walls Around ~50 nm Clusters (Teschke et al., 2026)

**Domain:** water structure / exclusion-zone (EZ) and "fourth phase" water
**Recorded:** 2026-10-07 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The archive's central water claim — the one the Yard's water rail exists for — is that **water at a hydrophilic interface is not bulk water**: it forms a distinct, extended, ordered phase. Pollack's version calls it the **exclusion zone (EZ)**, reports it repelling microspheres out to hundreds of microns, and reads it as a fourth phase with a charge and a proton gradient. The claim has been *independently reproduced in existence* (Elton et al., *Int. J. Mol. Sci.* 2020, reviews many groups; the Yard's own 2024 EZ-in-xylem record) while its **interpretation** is contested — most sharply by Schurr's **diffusiophoresis** account, which explains the particle-free zone with no new water phase at all.

This record is a *convergent measurement of the existence claim by an unrelated instrument*, not a replication of Pollack's protocol.

## Who measured it, when, and where

**Omar Teschke, Wyllerson Evaristo Gomes, Paula Simões Casagrande, Jose Roberto deCastro** and **David Mendez Soares** — Teschke's group at the **Universidade Estadual de Campinas (UNICAMP), Brazil**, and the Rede de Química e Tecnologia. Funded by **CAPES** (Brazil). The group has been measuring interfacial-water dielectric profiles by this technique since at least **2001** (*Phys. Rev. E* 64:011605) and 2021 (*Chem. Phys. Lett.* 775:138655), so this is a mature method being applied, not a first attempt.

Published in **Langmuir** (American Chemical Society), **2026, 42(18): 12491–12497**; received 17 Dec 2025, accepted 15 Apr 2026, published online 30 Apr 2026. DOI **10.1021/acs.langmuir.5c06652**. **Not Pollack's laboratory; no shared protocol.**

## Method — what they actually did

- **Atomic force microscopy** (TopoMetrix TMX2000; Si₃N₄ tips, spring constant ~0.03 N/m, tip radius ~5 nm) scanned in liquid media at a few angstroms' spatial resolution.
- **Substrates:** cleaved **mica** (hydrophilic) and **air bubbles pinned to PTFE** (hydrophobic); also polycrystalline gold and silicon.
- **The measurement is not of molecules but of the electric field they produce:** the group's **"dielectric exchange force"** model converts measured **force-vs-separation** curves into a **spatially resolved dielectric-permittivity profile ε(z)** as the tip displaces a nanoscopic volume of the interfacial double layer. (The model is an inversion/fit, not a direct ε readout — see Confidence.)
- **Cross-check by a second, independent technique:** confocal **Raman** spectra taken at the **floating water bridge** interface (where an applied longitudinal field, ~10⁶ V/m, raises the interfacial proton population enough to detect) were compared to the known Raman lines of **ice II**.

## Result — what was measured

- **The interfacial region is spatially structured, not bulk-like.** At mica/water, ε falls monotonically from the bulk value (~80) to **~4 at the surface**; the force–separation profile shows attraction ≤3 nm and repulsion out to ~100 nm.
- **A repeating "cluster-in-wall" geometry.** At the air/water interface the ε profile oscillates: **central regions with ε ≈ 7.6 enclosed by walls with ε ≈ 3.6**, walls typically **~10 nm** thick, clusters reaching **~50 nm**. Structures of one, two and three stacked clusters were fit.
- **The wall matches ice II, by two routes.** The measured ε ≈ **3.6** corresponds to the tabulated value for **ice II**, and the interfacial-water Raman lines (**3190 / 1650 / 3320 cm⁻¹**, librational ~860, translational ~170) sit close to ice II's (**3194 / 1690 / 3314 cm⁻¹**) — hence "**ice-like II**."
- **Interpretation:** an interconnected hydrogen-bonded network of clusters ringed by ice-like walls, which the authors propose as a **proton-transport pathway** along the interface. They explicitly **reject the "water wires" conductive-strand picture** for what they measured, calling the profiles *surfaces*, not wires.

## Confidence: medium

- **Peer-reviewed, mainstream venue, independent of Pollack's lab, and a genuinely different instrument** (AFM dielectric profiling + Raman vs microsphere exclusion). It supports the *existence* claim by a route Pollack's critique (Schurr's diffusiophoresis) does not even apply to.
- **But it is the group's own long-running method** — the dielectric-exchange-force technique is Teschke's invention and this is a continuation of their 2001–2024 series, not an outside lab replicating a prior claim's numbers. So it is **independent of Pollack, not independent of Teschke**.
- **The ε profile is a fitted inversion**, not a direct measurement; the authors state the ±0.5 on ε ~3.5 "is associated with the fitting of curves," and they note AFM probes an electric-field pattern of the size of the tip diameter (~5 nm), substantially larger than atomic separation — so the atomic-scale structure is inferred, not imaged.
- **It does not adjudicate the mechanism contest.** The archive's water line contains at least two rival readings (Pollack's fourth phase vs Schurr's diffusiophoresis for the EZ particle effect); this paper is a third structural description (ice-like II walls) and does not test EZ exclusion, salt rejection, or the light-driven growth Pollack claims. It confirms "structure exists," not "which structure, or what it does."
- **Single group, no external replication of these specific numbers.**

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured:** an atomic-force-microscopy group in Brazil, working with an instrument and a model that share nothing with Pollack's microsphere test, reports that water at an interface is measurably **not** bulk water — nanoscale clusters ringed by ~10-nm walls whose dielectric constant (~3.6) and Raman spectrum resemble **ice II**, against bulk ε ≈ 80. That independently supports the archive's core water-structure claim at the *existence* rung, and does so with a competing explanation (Schurr's diffusiophoresis) made irrelevant because the measurement never involves a particle being excluded. It is filed **medium** because the technique is the reporting group's own, the structure is inferred through a fitted inversion rather than imaged atom-by-atom, and it settles *that* interfacial water is structured, not *why*, *which* structure, or whether Pollack's exclusion-zone mechanism is the right one. The rung it moves is "distinct phase exists"; the rungs above it — information storage, and structure-as-mechanism — are untouched.
