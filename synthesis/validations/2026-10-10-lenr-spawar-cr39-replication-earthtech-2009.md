# Validation — The SPAWAR CR-39 Pitting Replication and Its Control Campaign (EarthTech, Austin, 2009)

**Domain:** LENR / cold fusion (nuclear track detectors — the "extraordinary evidence" claim)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-10 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

In **2006**, *New Energy Times* (Krivit & Daviss) reported what it called the **"Extraordinary Evidence"**: **Pam Boss and Stan Szpak** of the US Navy's **Space and Naval Warfare Systems Command (SPAWAR)** laboratory, San Diego, had placed **CR-39 nuclear track detectors** in close proximity to the cathode of a **palladium/deuterium co-deposition electrolytic cell** and found **copious pitting** of the chips. They claimed the pitting **is evidence of nuclear activity in the cell** — CR-39 being, in most applications, an artifact-free detector of energetic particles.

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a cold-fusion cell really emit nuclear particles that pit CR-39 — or do those pits come from the chemistry in the beaker?

## Who replicated it, when, and where

**Marissa E. Little** and **Scott R. Little**, **EarthTech International, Inc.** at the **Institute for Advanced Studies at Austin**, Texas. This is an **independent laboratory** — not SPAWAR, and not the reporting outlet. Published in the **Journal of Scientific Exploration 23**(4): 411–417, **2009**, with the SPAWAR team's collaboration on protocol (via Steve Krivit's coordinated **Galileo Project**), and with SPAWAR's own **Szpak et al. (2005)** paper as the base for the first three runs.

## Method — what they actually did

**Phase 1 — replicate.** Four experiments. The protocol (their fourth run, supplied by the SPAWAR team through the Galileo Project): an **open electrolytic cell**, ~**25 ml** electrolyte, a **20 cm × 0.3 mm platinum-wire anode**, a **several-cm 0.25 mm silver-wire cathode wrapped around the CR-39 chip** to hold it in contact, electrolyte **0.3 M LiCl in heavy water plus 0.03 M PdCl₂**, current **raised from 0.1 mA to 100 mA** in steps over **almost 3 weeks**, by which time virtually all the palladium had plated onto the cathode.

**Phase 2 — control campaign (the part that matters).** They then attacked the origin of the pits along six independent lines:

1. **Alpha-track comparison.** CR-39 exposed to **Am-241** across a range of air paths (near-zero to ~5 MeV), plus **uranium ore** in contact (oblique incidence).
2. **Mechanical damage.** Deliberate scratches and ordinary handling, then etch.
3. **Protocol changes.** Runs shortened to **<48 h** and to **3 days** (100 mA to full Pd plating) — do the pits still form?
4. **Electrolyte substitution** at the same molarity: **HCl, LiCl, NaCl, KCl**.
5. **Light water substituted for heavy water**, and **metals other than Pd** co-deposited (including **CuSO₄** at two current regimes).
6. **Barriers and geometry.** **6 µm Mylar** sealed over half the chip; Mylar **bags** with the chip kept dry; and an **out-of-cell design** (cell wall machined open, Mylar window glued over it, chip held against the *outside* of the window, cathode against the inside) — the design Richard Oriani had reported positive results with.

## Result — what was measured

**The replication succeeded, and it is not in doubt:**

- All four initial experiments produced results **very similar to SPAWAR's**: a cloudy area under the cathode resolving after etching into **well-defined, copious pits, ~10⁶ pits/cm²**.

**The control campaign then separated the observation from the interpretation:**

| Control | What it showed |
|---|---|
| **Alpha comparison** | SPAWAR pits are **not like alpha tracks**: ~**25 µm** diameter against **7–8 µm** for 4 MeV alphas and **15 µm** for 1 MeV; **shallower, clumped**, with a "soap-bubble" look; **none elliptical** (oblique alpha incidence produces highly elongated tracks, and none appeared) |
| **Pre-etch visibility** | One can see the *"small pock marks"* **before etching** — an alpha particle's path **cannot** be seen optically before etching |
| **Light water vs heavy** | **"No discernible difference"** in pit quantity — significant, since deuterium's nuclear behaviour differs markedly from protium's |
| **Pd removed / other metals** | Pits formed **without palladium** (CuSO₄, and other metals) — the requirement for pits was a **dense dendritic** deposit; a **soft spongy** deposit gave none, and one CuSO₄ run gave none despite dendritic growth early on |
| **Electrolyte substitution** | **HCl gave no pits**; LiCl, NaCl, KCl all did |
| **6 µm Mylar barrier** | Pits **stopped underneath the Mylar** where Pd had not grown between film and chip — but where Pd grew in the gap, pits still formed |
| **Mylar bag (chip dry)** | **Orders of magnitude fewer tracks, and no spatial correlation with the cathode** |
| **Out-of-cell Mylar window** | Three scorable runs showed **only background track densities** |
| **Accelerated etching** | Chips soaked in the electrolyte for weeks then etched lost **80 µm in the first 1.5 hours** against a normal **1.5 µm/h** — direct proof of *"significant chemical interaction"* between CR-39 and the electrolyte (and far more material than a 5 MeV alpha's ~40 µm range) |

**The authors' conclusion, in their own words:** *"Our results do not provide a positive identification of the origin of SPAWAR pits. However, they do show that **chemical origin is a distinct possibility and therefore that nuclear origin is not a certainty.**"* They add that the Mylar result *"only proves that they cannot be due to nuclear particles which would penetrate such a barrier"* (e.g. alphas >1 MeV), and that the light-water and no-palladium results are *"less conclusive but are still consistent with chemical origin."*

## Confidence: medium–high that the observation replicates; high that the nuclear reading is unestablished

- **What is strong:** an **independent laboratory**, a **published protocol** followed, an explicit **four-experiment replication** reproducing the headline observation at the reported magnitude, and then a **multi-line control campaign** with the **decisive barrier experiment** (a 6 µm Mylar film stopping the pits) and a **direct chemical measurement** (the accelerated etch rate). The conclusion is **calibrated** — "distinct possibility," "not a certainty" — not a debunk.
- **What limits it, and the paper says so:** it *"does not provide a positive identification of the origin"* — it **narrows** the artefact space rather than naming the mechanism. The **HCl null is unexplained** (one salt gives no pits, three do). **n is small** (four initial experiments; three scorable window runs). The pit-count comparisons are **microscopic and qualitative**; the paper reports no blinded image scoring and no independent pit-count adjudication. The **outlier behaviour** — pits under Mylar where Pd grew into the gap — shows the barrier control depends on a deposition detail that is itself variable.
- **The claim it leaves standing, precisely:** the *pits are real and reproducible*, and the *nuclear interpretation is not established* because a **chemistry-only path produces the same pits**, including **without deuterium and without palladium** — the two ingredients the nuclear reading depends on.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted / measured / reported:** an independent Austin laboratory rebuilt the SPAWAR Pd/D co-deposition cell from the published protocol and reproduced the **"extraordinary evidence"** — **~10⁶ pits/cm²** of CR-39 under the cathode, four times over. Then it ran the campaign the claim needed and found that the pits **are not alpha tracks** (wrong size, wrong shape, no obliques, visible before etching), that they form **with light water instead of heavy**, **without palladium** and with **other metals**, that a **6 µm Mylar film stops them**, that they are **absent when the chip stays out of the electrolyte**, and that the electrolyte **actively attacks the plastic** (80 µm etched in 90 minutes against 1.5 µm/h). The authors' own verdict is exact and worth quoting in full: *chemical origin is a distinct possibility and therefore nuclear origin is not a certainty.* Filed **medium–high for the replication, high for the dismantling of the nuclear reading** — a rare record in this Yard, and the shape it should hold: **the observation replicated, the interpretation did not.**
