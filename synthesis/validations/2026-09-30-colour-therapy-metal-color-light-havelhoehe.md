# Validation — Metal Colour Light Therapy and Cancer-Related Fatigue (Havelhöhe, 2026)

**Domain:** alternative optics / colour therapy trials (anthroposophical metal-colour light; the coloured-glass lineage)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-30 (Replication Watch). **First record in this family in the Yard.**

## What was claimed

**Metal Colour Light Therapy (MCLT)** — German *Metallfarblichttherapie* — is an anthroposophical art therapy developed by **Marianne Altmaier** from 2001 onward (Lichtblick e.V., Germany), inspired by the coloured-glass windows of the **Goetheanum** in Dornach and of **Chartres Cathedral**. The instrument is a set of **twelve large glass panels, each infused with a different metal oxide** — cobalt, copper, gold, iron, silver and others — with patterns etched on the surface. The patient sits facing a panel lit by **natural daylight**; each panel weighs roughly 100 lb and is about 110 × 65 × 1.8 cm. The method is documented in Altmaier's book *Metallfarblichttherapie* (Mayer-Verlag, Stuttgart, 2010).

The claim is that the qualities of a metal, transposed into coloured light, act therapeutically — and specifically that a course of sessions produces lasting improvement in conditions including exhaustion, pain, sleep disturbance and stress-related symptoms. The corpus of evidence behind it has been qualitative and clinical-report level, and the **Goetheanum's own science institute has a three-year basic-research project running (from 2025, Matthias Rang, Torsten Arncken, Jan-Dirk Monden) precisely to characterise what metal-based coloured light actually is.** That is the honest status of the claim: an instrument in search of a measurement.

## Who replicated it, when

**Charlotte Streffer, Anja Thronicke, Michaela Ganz, Shiao Li Oei, Jessica Groß, Gerrit Grieb and Friedemann Schad** — **Gemeinschaftskrankenhaus Havelhöhe** (the anthroposophic hospital in Berlin) and the **Forschungsinstitut Havelhöhe**, at a DKG-certified breast cancer centre.

**Protocol published** as the LUCIA study (2023, protocol v1.0 dated 10 August 2023). **Results presented 18–20 June 2026** at the ISfAM conference *The Healing Arts — Forging Alliances of Arts & Medicine*, Berlin, as meeting abstract **26isfam067** (German Medical Science, published 12 June 2026, `https://doi.org/10.3205/26isfam067`).

**The randomized successor has already opened.** **LUCIA 2.0** (DRKS00040901) was registered with the German Clinical Trials Register on **13 July 2026**, last updated 10 September 2026, status **"Recruiting ongoing."** That is the live replication to watch on this rail.

## Method

**The 2026 series (LUCIA).** Prospective, **single-centre, single-arm**. **60 patients** with UICC stage 0–III breast cancer in the post-treatment phase, recruited up to May 2025. The intervention was **nine 45-minute MCLT sessions over 5–7 weeks**. Outcomes were **quality of life (EORTC QLQ-C30)**, the **Internal Coherence Scale**, and the **NCCN Distress Thermometer**, taken at baseline, post-intervention, and at three- and six-month follow-up.

The protocol paper already named its own limitation in plain terms: **no control group and no blinding**, with the authors proposing that future work use "a randomized controlled design with appropriate control conditions, such as sham light exposure or standard care," blinded outcome assessment and objective physiological measures.

**The 2026 successor (LUCIA 2.0).** Interventional, **randomized, parallel**, target 60, in the same hospital. Participants are assigned either to receive MCLT immediately or to a **waitlist group**; the **primary endpoint is change in cancer-related fatigue**, with quality of life, distress and internal coherence secondary, over six months of patient-reported follow-up.

## Result

Results reported for the single-arm series, at submission:

- **47 of 60 participants evaluable** up to the three-month follow-up.
- **Fatigue (EORTC QLQ-C30):** 61 ± 25.6 at baseline → **51 ± 24.2** post-intervention (Δ −10, **p = 0.001**), and **53.2 ± 26.6 at three months (p = 0.006)**.
- **Insomnia:** 69 ± 33.6 → **49 ± 33.4** (Δ −20, **p = 0.0003**), and **53.7 ± 33.2 at three months (p = 0.008)**.
- Data for the remaining participants, scales and follow-up marked **pending**.

The authors' own summary calls this "the first systematic evaluation" and says the preliminary results "indicate it could be a promising option" — language calibrated to a single-arm series, and correctly so.

## Confidence: low — reported, not established

**1. There is no comparison arm, and the authors said so before they ran it.** Every participant received the intervention. A fatigue and insomnia score in post-treatment breast cancer patients that falls during a nine-session, five-to-seven-week course of sitting quietly in front of coloured light with a therapist, and stays partially down three months later, is fully compatible with **regression to the mean, seasonal and natural recovery in survivorship, expectancy, and the attention of a therapist who spends 45 minutes with you.** With no control arm none of these can be separated from a light effect.

**2. The outcomes are all self-report.** The NCCN Distress Thermometer, QLQ-C30 fatigue and insomnia subscales and the ICS are questionnaires. There is no blinded rater and no objective physiological or sleep measure in the reported design.

**3. It is a meeting abstract, not a paper.** A DOI'd GMS conference abstract with 47/60 evaluable cases and no full methods section, no randomisation scheme to inspect, and no supplementary tables. It should be read as a **presented progress report**. Some of the six-month data were still pending at submission.

**4. And the follow-up trial's control tells you what it will and will not settle.** LUCIA 2.0 is randomized, which is real progress — but the comparator is a **waitlist**, not a sham light panel. A waitlist arm is a strong control for *time*, a weak one for *expectancy and attention*, and useless for the question the tradition actually poses, which is whether **the coloured light** does anything rather than the sitting-down. The instrument-versus-attention question therefore stays open even after LUCIA 2.0 reports.

**5. A neighbouring result that cuts the other way, on a different claim.** A separate 2026 exploratory trial in cataract-surgery patients (Frontiers in Psychology, 30 patients, six colours, randomized order, ambient light as control, HRV-measured) found that **blue light reduced anxiety and increased parasympathetic activity independently of colour preference**, with heart rate variability as an objective readout. That is about coloured light affecting autonomic state in a randomised design, not about metal-oxide panels in oncology — but it is the kind of objective instrument this rail lacks, and it is the direction a serious test would move.
