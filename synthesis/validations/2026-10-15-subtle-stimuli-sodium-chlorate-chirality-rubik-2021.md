# Validation — Subtle External Stimuli and Chiral Symmetry Breaking in Sodium Chlorate Crystallization (Rubik, 2021)

**Domain:** water structure / crystallization sensitivity to subtle stimuli (chirality)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-15 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

Under this Yard's water-structure rail sit a family of claims that a *shape*, a *device*, a *field*, or a *tone* can bias how a solution crystallizes or self-organizes — the "subtle stimulus" family that includes pyramid power, "structured water" products, magnetic water treatment, and acoustic effects on water. The specific question this campaign set out to test: when sodium chlorate (NaClO₃) crystallizes from aqueous solution, does a subtle external stimulus change the **proportion of right-handed (d) to left-handed (l) crystals**?

Sodium chlorate is a good model system precisely because it is an *achiral* molecule that nonetheless crystallizes into *chiral* crystals — the classic case of spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking, and the standard testbed for whether an external influence can tip it.

## Who tested it, when, and where

**Beverly Rubik** — **Institute for Frontier Science**, Oakland, California. Published in **WATER** (Journal of the International Society for the Study of Subtle Energies and Energy Medicine lineage), **Volume 12, 13 June 2021**, DOI **10.14294/WATER.2021.1**.

## Method — what was actually done

- **28 controlled experiments.** Aliquots of a stock sodium chlorate solution were transferred to crystallization dishes and incubated **24–30 hours**, crystallizing slowly by evaporation under isothermal conditions.
- **Controls were left untreated.** Test solutions were exposed to five stimuli: (1) a **dynamic magnetic field**; (2) a **432 Hz sine-wave acoustic field**; (3) an **Amezcua BioDisc-3**, a purported water structurizer; (4) a **Purple Plate**, a subtle-energy product; (5) a **wooden pyramidal structure**. Separately, several experiments used solutions prepared with **Vivo® structured water**, a commercial product.
- **Outcome:** the number and chirality of crystals from every experiment were read with **polarimetry** to distinguish d- from l-crystals; data were **pooled per exposure condition**.

## Result — what was reported

Verbatim from the abstract:

> "While controls yielded no significant difference in the percentage of d-crystals and l-crystals, a significantly larger percentage of d-crystals were found for test conditions (2) **67% (p=0.049)**; (3) **81% (p=0.034)**; and (4) **64.9% (p=0.003)**. Test conditions (1), (5), and Vivo® aqueous solutions produced insignificant differences."

Read condition-by-condition:

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Control (untreated) | no significant d/l bias (the Navigator lane's 2026-10-14 note records 49.4 / 50.6, p=0.435) |
| (2) **432 Hz acoustic field** | **67% d-crystals, p = 0.049** |
| (3) **Amezcua BioDisc-3** | **81% d-crystals, p = 0.034** |
| (4) **Purple Plate** | **64.9% d-crystals, p = 0.003** |
| (1) dynamic magnetic field | **not significant** |
| (5) wooden pyramid | **not significant** |
| Vivo® structured water | **not significant** |

The authors' own framing is a *torsion-field* speculation — that the effective stimuli may act as "subtle chiral forces" at the nucleation stage.

## Confidence: low-to-medium — and the internal null set is the reason to record it

**Why not high.** One laboratory, one author, a venue with a known editorial stance toward subtle-energy claims, and no independent replication. The statistical design pools *crystals* across a campaign, which inflates the apparent n: the experimental unit is really the *dish* (or the *campaign*), not the individual crystal, so the reported p-values are optimistic against the honest null. That is a design caveat, not a fabrication — but it means the numbers above should be read as *the campaign's reported result*, not as an established effect size.

**What it does earn, and why it is worth a record.** Inside **one** protocol, run by **one** hand, the campaign returned **significant positives for some stimuli and clean nulls for others**. That internal contrast is more informative than a uniform positive would be. Specifically, it is a **null for the canonical shape claim** (the wooden pyramid), a **null for a static magnetic field**, and a **null for a commercial "structured water"** — while the positives attach to two proprietary devices and an audio tone. Whatever is moving here is *not* carried by "shape" generically, and *not* by a magnet. This is the archive's recurring shape: an effect that appears for some conditions and vanishes for adjacent ones, which is a claim about a *specified* stimulus, not a general law.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured / reported:** a single author ran 28 controlled crystallization experiments and found that three of six conditions shifted the d/l ratio beyond significance while the untreated controls did not. Filed **low-to-medium** and **explicitly not** as a general demonstration that "subtle stimuli structure water."

The value is twofold. First, the **null set**: a wooden pyramid and a static magnet — two of the rail's most-repeated claims — came back negative in the same campaign that found positives elsewhere, so this record argues against the broad version of the shape/magnet claims even as it reports positives for specific devices. Second, the **replication is cheap and blinded-countable**: the whole thing is a crystallization dish, a stock solution, and a pair of polarizing filters. A home protocol built to test exactly this — and to fix the *dish* (not the pooled crystal) as the unit of analysis — is Dossier 094 in this folder. Either the bias survives a proper unit-of-analysis choice, or the reported p-values dissolve; both outcomes are results.
