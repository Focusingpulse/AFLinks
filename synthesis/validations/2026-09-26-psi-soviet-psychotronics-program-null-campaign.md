# Validation — The Soviet Psychotronics Program's Own Nulls

**Domain:** psi / biofield (telepathy, telekinesis, "skin vision," aura photography — the Russian psychotronika lane)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-26 (Replication Seeder, run 5)

## What was claimed

That mental suggestion, telepathy, telekinesis and fingertip "skin vision" are objective physical phenomena — and, in the program's later framing, that a **"bioplasma"** (Inyushin, Alma-Ata) underlies them.

## Who tested it, when

Not a single experimenter: a **state program**. It began with telepathy research in the **1920s** (Bekhterev; **Vasilyev's** mental-suggestion experiments), was accelerated by Cold War intelligence reports of American psi research into a "psi arms race," and by the **late 1960s** ran **twenty-plus special laboratories**:

- Leningrad University — the electromagnetic theory of suggestion
- the Popov Institute — mental-image transfer
- Alma-Ata — Inyushin's "bioplasma"
- Novosibirsk — ultra-long-distance reception

The flagship experiments named in the corpus:

- the **Moscow–Novosibirsk telepathic bridge** — **Kamensky** transmitting **tactile images** to **Nikolaev** across thousands of kilometres;
- **Kulagina's telekinesis**, demonstrated in front of academicians;
- **Kuleshova's** fingertip "skin vision";
- **Kirlian** photography treated as aura evidence;
- **Faraday cages**, built to rule out electromagnetic leakage.

## Result — the program's own records

The verdict is in the program's own documents, not in a skeptic's review:

- **Vasilyev's laboratory ultimately admitted it had never conclusively proven telepathy** as objective physical reality.
- Careful verification **dissolved each specific claim**:
  - ultra-long-distance telepathy → **subliminal sensory sensitivity and coincidence**;
  - Kulagina's telekinesis → **hypnosis of the observers, concealed devices, and electrostatics**;
  - skin vision → **blindfold gaps plus thermal sensitivity**;
  - the Kirlian "aura" → **gas discharge dependent on skin humidity**.

## The residue — why this is not simply a null

Two things survived, and both belong in the record:

1. **The instrumentation survived the claims.** The program produced **contactless health-monitoring systems, stress-assessment methods for pilots, cosmonauts and submariners, and early polygraph prototypes** — the instrumentation of the body's hidden reserves, born out of the wreckage of the psi claims. The pattern the Yard's bench notes keep finding at micro-scale (the claim dies, the instrument survives) ran here at **state scale**.
2. **The archive closed, and the nulls did not circulate.** The documents remain classified — officially for dual-use reasons (psychological manipulation, remote information extraction), practically through bureaucratic inertia. The Soviet psi literature is therefore **unfalsifiable by construction**: the primary evidence is sealed and the secondary literature is polemic on both sides.

## What is established, and what is not

**Established (process):** that a state applied substantial apparatus — Faraday cages, named laboratories, named investigators, thousands of kilometres of separation — to these claims, and that its **own** records register nulls and admit failure. That is a documented replication campaign with named actors and described methods, run at a scale no private researcher could match.

**Not established:** that any of the claimed phenomena exist. Nothing in the program's record, as this corpus holds it, supports them.

**Evidentiary floor:** this is **history, not live evidence.** The corpus's standing rule — a Soviet-sourced claim carries an evidentiary-floor label the way a translation carries a tier label — applies to the **nulls** exactly as it applies to the positives. The archive is sealed in both directions: we cannot check the successes because the failures are also in the box.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. The Soviet program is this corpus's control case, and the corpus states its lesson better than any verdict could: **verification that cannot publish is verification that never happened.** Twenty years, twenty laboratories, Faraday cages, millions of rubles — and the results went into a box, while the INRS dowsing study's results, whatever they turn out to be, will be public at EGU and in a journal. The two archives must therefore be read differently: the INRS row as live evidence, the Soviet row as history with a sealed floor.

This is the **psi rail's first record of any kind** in the Yard, and it is filed as a *campaign that ran and dissolved its own claims* — not as proof that psi is impossible, which nothing in this document establishes, and not as proof that it works, which the program's own records refuse. The transferable finding is the one the rail keeps rediscovering: an instrument that measures the body's hidden reserves can come out of a program whose central claims did not survive.
