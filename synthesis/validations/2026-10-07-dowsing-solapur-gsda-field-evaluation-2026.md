# Validation — Dowsing Under Audit: The Solapur District Field Evaluation (India, 2026)

**Domain:** radiesthesia / dowsing (buried-water location), India
**Recorded:** 2026-10-07 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The practitioners' claim, unchanged since Munich: a person holding rods or a pendulum can locate subsurface water, and the practice — cheap, quick, culturally embedded — is a cost-effective way to pick a well site. The Solapur dowsers add a local variant: they claim, in the authors' words, to detect "earth radiation" (forked stick, 52% of practitioners), a "vital energy field" (copper pendulum, 23%), a "body energy resonance" (barehand, 15%), or an electromagnetic signal (commercial "water finder," 10%), and 78% describe the ability as hereditary.

This is the same claim class already in the Yard from Munich 1986–88, Costérisant 1937, Chevreul 1854, and the still-unpublished INRS 2025–26 study. Solapur is a fourth independent national setting, and the first in India — a country where dowsing is not a fringe practice but a load-bearing part of rural water supply.

## Who tested it, when, and where

**Mustaq Ahmad Shaikh** and **Farjana Birajdar**, of the **Groundwater Surveys and Development Agency (GSDA), Solapur, Government of Maharashtra, India**. Field survey conducted **January 2021 – December 2022** across **45 villages in five talukas** (Solapur South, Mohol, Madha, Sangola, Malshiras) of a semi-arid Deccan-Trap basalt district (rainfall ~545 mm/yr, >85% of the district over-exploited).

Published **2026**, *International Journal of Science and Research Archive* (IJSRA) **20**(02): 042–052; received 26 April 2026, accepted 27 July 2026. DOI 10.30574/ijsra.2026.20.2.1275. No external funding; carried out as part of the agency's routine groundwater assessment work.

## Method — what they actually did

- **215 borewell sites documented**, each GPS-referenced, with pre-drilling predictions recorded for all methods: **146 sited by traditional dowsers**, **42 by Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT; Syscal Pro Switch 72, 5–10 m electrode spacing, 80–120 m depth)**, **27 by seismic refraction** (24-channel, 5 m geophones).
- **Validation protocol applied to every site regardless of selection method:** pre-drilling geophysics; structured interviews with dowsers recording predicted depth (±5 m) and yield (±100 L/h); drilling monitored with lithology and depth-to-water logging; **standard 72-hour pumping tests** to establish sustainable yield; water-quality chemical analysis; chi-square and regression analysis.
- **Dowsing methods catalogued by prevalence** (forked stick 52%, pendulum 23%, barehand 15%, electronic 10%), with dowsers charging **₹500–2,000 per site recommendation**; 78% claimed hereditary knowledge.
- Four techniques were documented and the four "claimed mechanisms" recorded verbatim, then tested against the same endpoint: **sustainable yield >500 L/h**.

## Result — what was measured

| Method | Sites | Success (>500 L/h) | Depth-prediction error |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dowsing** | 146 | **42%** (61/146) | **±18.5 m** (range 5–42 m) |
| **ERT (resistivity)** | 42 | **82%** (34/42) | ±3.2 m |
| **Seismic refraction** | 27 | **78%** (21/27) | ±4.7 m |

- **χ² = 42.7, p < 0.001** — the gap between dowsing and the geophysical methods is significant; geophysics also dominated at the high-yield end (>1,000 L/h).
- **Dowsing success tracked aquifer predictability, not the rod.** Success correlated **R² = 0.86** with how homogeneous and predictable the aquifer was — **68% in shallow alluvium**, 45% in weathered basalt, 32% in fractured basalt, and **12% in massive basalt** where surface indicators are absent. There was **no relationship between the dowser's response and actual yield (R² = 0.08)**, and no successful prediction of water quality. Success fell to **28% during severe drought**.
- **The authors' own attribution:** the 42% is explained by **subconscious terrain reading** (correlations of 0.68–0.71 with vegetation patterns and lineament intersections — the surface indicators of shallow water), **accumulated local geological knowledge** (a "mental database" of historical well sites), and the **ideomotor effect** moving the rod in line with the dowser's expectation. They conclude the performance "can be largely explained by the dowser's subconscious ability to read environmental cues and apply accumulated local knowledge, rather than any paranormal detection ability."
- **Cost framing (honest to include):** dowsing's ₹500–2,000/site is far below ERT (₹15,000–25,000), and cost-per-successful-well looks better (₹3,500 vs ₹21,000) — but the authors note this metric ignores the ₹80,000–1,50,000 cost of each dry borewell, which the higher dowsing failure rate multiplies.

## Confidence: medium

- **The direction is solid and converges with the rest of the rail.** A large documented sample (146 dowser-sited wells), a government hydrogeology agency, a physical endpoint (72-hour pumping-test yield, not a felt signal), and consistency with Munich 1986–88 and Costérisant 1937. The mechanism the authors land on — tacit local knowledge plus the ideomotor effect — is exactly the Chevreul reading already in this Yard.
- **But this is a field evaluation, not a blinded protocol — and that matters.** The dowsers **chose their own sites** (where farmers wanted wells), so the 42% and the 82% are measured on **different target populations**, and site-selection skill, not rod-sensing, is a live confound the design cannot separate. The comparison is between practices, not between a dowser and a chance baseline. It is **not** a Munich-style randomized blind test.
- **Venue is low-tier** (IJSRA, a general science archive, not a hydrogeology journal), the paper is descriptive, and the underlying field data (2021–22) predates publication by years — so it is a retrospective operational audit rather than a pre-registered experiment.
- **What it adds regardless:** an independent national replication of the *shape* of every controlled dowsing result so far — dowsing does not beat the instrument, and where it does better than chance, the success rides on how easy the target is, not on detection.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as reported:** a government hydrogeology agency put 146 dowser-sited borewells and 69 geophysically-sited borewells through the same pumping-test endpoint and found dowsing at 42% against 82% (resistivity) and 78% (seismic), with dowsers missing depth by an average of ±18.5 m. The authors — an agency whose own remit is water supply, not debunking — attribute the residual success to local knowledge and the ideomotor effect, and their own data show the rate rising and falling with how predictable the aquifer is, which is the tell that the rod is not the sensor. Read it as a **large-n field negative in a fourth country**, filed at **medium** because it is an operational audit with self-selected sites and a non-blinded design, not because its direction is in doubt. The three rows that would still separate a real sub-effect from a confound — a dowser against a randomly placed hole in the same formation, a blind protocol, and an iron-vs-plastic target — remain the ones Munich, INRS and this Yard's Dossier 026 were built to run.
