# Validation — The 1934 Lyon Blind Packet Test on Radiesthetic Mushroom Sorting

**Domain:** radiesthesia (food safety / mycological testing)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-22 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

That a radiesthesist, using a pendulum over a mushroom or over a carrier disc placed under it, can determine whether a species is **edible or toxic** — the "Mycophage" method, whose full protocol was published in the French radiesthesia literature (Mauguin's method, as relayed by Pouchet). This is the highest-stakes version of the radiesthesia claim: a false negative on a mushroom is a poisoning.

The method as described in the corpus record: **zinc–copper carrier discs**, a **horseshoe magnet**, a claimed **spectrum of "giration" points between 13 and 80 cm**, **north/south asymmetry**, and a claimed **radiation-field inversion** at **14 cm for *Amanita phalloides*** versus **1 cm for *Agaricus campestris***. Those are numbers, which is what makes the claim gradeable — and what makes its failure gradeable too.

## Who attempted the test, when

At the **Lyon Linnean Society's 1934 mushroom exhibition** in Lyon, France. Recorded the following year by **Pouchet** in *Radiesthésie et Mycologie* (**1935**) — a document now in the archive's French→English translation pipeline (Forge, FAL-fr-143-5).

The demonstrator is not named individually in the corpus record; he is identified by his role at the exhibition and by the method he used.

## Method

Two stages, and the order is the whole story:

1. **A demonstration on known material.** The demonstrator was given **two species whose identity he already knew** and classified them. He passed. This is the part of the episode that the tradition preserved as evidence of skill.
2. **A blind packet test.** Then **four coded packets** were set before him — the identities known to the organisers, not to the reader.

## Result

**0 of 4 correct.** Three of the four readings are itemised in the corpus record:

- ***Lactarius deliciosus*** (a well-known edible, the saffron milk-cap) — read as **indifferent**;
- ***Amanita phalloides*** (the death cap — the most lethal mushroom in the European flora) — read as **indifferent**;
- an **edible *Cortinarius duracinus*** — read as **toxic**.

Read as an instrument, the pendulum made two errors in the harmless direction and one in the dangerous direction. It did not separate the death cap from the milk-cap.

**The demonstrator's retreat, preserved verbatim in the record:** *"perhaps edible for you, bad for my stomach."*

## What the author did next — and why that is the part worth keeping

Pouchet did **not** suppress the result. He recorded the failure, then relayed **Mauguin's full Mycophage protocol verbatim** — carrier discs, magnet, the 13–80 cm giration spectrum, the north/south asymmetry, the 14 cm / 1 cm inversion — and **formally proposed a blinded commission of mycologists and radiesthésistes to adjudicate the method.**

**That commission never met.**

So the corpus now holds a document from inside the tradition that (a) states the method precisely enough to be tested, (b) records a blind test that failed, and (c) proposes the institutional test that would settle it — and records that the proposal was not taken up. This is the rarest kind of source in this archive: **a tradition recording its own miss, in real time, and naming the experiment it declined to run.**

## Confidence: medium — and the limits are specific

- **The account is a single secondary source** (Pouchet 1935), read here through the corpus's translation record rather than the French original. This run did **not** read the primary text.
- **n = 4 on the blind arm.** Four trials cannot establish a weak effect; they can refute a *strong* one. The strong claim here — that the method sorts edible from toxic mushrooms — is exactly what 0/4 bears on.
- **The demonstrator passed the known-species stage and failed the blind stage.** That is the pattern the corpus's verification-rail synthesis names as its first structural finding: **demonstration does not predict test outcome.** Whether the known-species pass was skill, the organisers' cueing, or the demonstrator's prior knowledge is not resolvable from this record — which is itself the point.
- **Not over-read:** a failed blind packet test in 1934 does not adjudicate every radiesthetic claim ever made. It adjudicates **this method, on this task, under blind conditions** — and it puts on the record that the tradition's own participants knew a blind test was the right instrument.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded as **attempted / measured / reported**: a practitioner demonstrated his method on mushrooms he already knew, submitted to a blind test of four packets, and read the death cap and a common edible both as "indifferent."

**The transferable thing here is not the failure — it is the shape of the day.** A demonstration was given, it looked convincing, a blind test was run beside it, and the two disagreed completely. That is the same asymmetry that the corpus has now documented four separate times across ninety-two years (Chevreul 1854, Pouchet 1934, Argenton 2007, INRS 2026) — and it is the reason every card in this Yard puts the blind condition in the design rather than in the write-up.

**What a home replicator takes from it:** the task was **sorting** — is this thing dangerous or not — which is a binary endpoint, a coded key, and a scoring sheet. You can run the 2026 version of the 1934 test with ten packets and a friend who writes the key down. The archive will hold the result either way.
