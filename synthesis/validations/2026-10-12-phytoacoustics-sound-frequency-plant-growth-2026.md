# Validation — Phytoacoustics: sound frequency against plant growth, with controls

**Domain:** acoustic and geometric resonance effects — applied to plants (the PAFT / "plant acoustic frequency technology" claim class)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-12 (Replication Watch, run 11)

## What was claimed

The claim class is old and wide: **exposing plants to sound — music, tone, or specific frequencies — increases growth and yield.** Its modern form is **PAFT** ("plant acoustic frequency technology"), promoted as a generator that raises crop yield. The positive literature is substantial and often large: cotton reportedly **+12.7 % average yield** under PAFT, with height, leaf area, boll count and boll weight all up; **rice +25 % in pots and +5.7 % in open field**; **wheat +17 %**. Effects are variously attributed to stomatal opening, membrane mechanosensing, calcium signalling, and phytohormone shifts.

The archive's own reason to care is not the mechanism but the **testability**: a claim that says "play sound at plants and they grow better" is one of the cheapest possible home tests, and one of the easiest to get wrong. The Yard's existing acoustic entries are the prime-ratio geometric-resonance null (`2026-10-02-geometric-resonance-prime-ratio-self-replication-null.md`) and a sound-frequency/meridian protocol study (`2026-09-24-sound-meridian-frequency-ladder-xu-jizong.md`) — neither is plant acoustics, and neither settles this rail.

## Who tested it, and when

Three independent 2026 results, all in the same year and none of them from the PAFT promotion side:

1. **Place, Kunz, Sager, Hentgen & Wigger** — *"Application of audible acoustic stimulation to investigate growth responses in three Pleurotus species."* **Journal of Microbiological Methods** 241:107393 (2026), doi **10.1016/j.mimet.2026.107393** (published 2026-01-10).
2. **Pagano, Mocarli, Belli, Boarino & Marra** — **CNR-IRET** (Istituto di Ricerca sugli Ecosistemi Terrestri, National Research Council of Italy, Florence) — *"Effects of low-frequency sound vibrations on growth and physiological parameters in Vicia faba."* Journal article, 2026; institutional record: https://iris.cnr.it/handle/20.500.14243/590342
3. **Minh & Bui** — *"Preharvest Sound Waves Enhance Yield and Nutritional Quality of Greenhouse Vegetables in a Frequency-Dependent Manner."* **Russian Journal of Plant Physiology** (2026-03-30).

And the field's own 2026 review: **Mos, Kassner & Robson**, *"Phytoacoustics: sound perception, mechanosensing, and biological responses to sound in plants,"* **Plant Signaling & Behavior** (2026), doi **10.1080/15592324.2026.2709223** — which states that plant responses to sound are *"highly context-dependent"* and calls for *"stronger methodological standardization."*

## Method, as reported

**The mushroom trial (Place et al.)** is the cleanest design of the three, because it is a **controlled frequency sweep with untreated controls across three species at once**. Substrate blocks of *Pleurotus eryngii*, *Pleurotus ostreatus*, and a commercial hybrid (*P. ostreatus* × *P. eryngii*, "Black Pearl") were grown under controlled production conditions and exposed to **50 Hz, 100 Hz, 225 Hz or 750 Hz at 80 dB for 300 s during the primordia stage**; untreated blocks served as controls; yields from **two consecutive harvests** were recorded; analysis by ANOVA or non-parametric equivalents at α = 0.05. The point of running three species and four frequencies is precisely that it can detect a sign flip that a single-species, single-frequency trial would hide.

**The broad-bean trial (Pagano et al., CNR-IRET)** is the institutional negative: *Vicia faba* under controlled climatic conditions exposed to **98 Hz at 74 dB**, with growth and physiological parameters measured against controls.

**The vegetable trial (Minh & Bui)** ran three crops with contrasting edible organs — carrot (root), tomato (fruit), Chinese cabbage (leaf) — under **four acoustic regimes for 20 days pre-harvest**: no sound (CK), low-frequency (~200 Hz), medium-frequency (~1 kHz), high-frequency (~8 kHz), each **continuous sine at 90 ± 2 dB for 2 h per day**.

## Result, as reported

**Place et al. — the sign flips with species and frequency, and one species is negative at every frequency tested:**

| Species | 50 Hz | 100 Hz | 225 Hz | 750 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *P. eryngii* | **decreased** | **decreased** | increased | increased |
| *P. ostreatus* | **decreased** | **decreased** | **decreased** | **decreased** |
| Black Pearl (hybrid) | increased | increased | increased | increased (strongest) |

*P. ostreatus* — the common oyster mushroom — yielded **less than control at all four frequencies**. Fruiting homogeneity also differed by species. The authors call for further trials to *"validate reproducibility."*

**Pagano et al. — a significant adverse effect, not a boost.** At **98 Hz / 74 dB** in *Vicia faba*: chlorophyll content **−14.2 %**, stomatal conductance **−26.7 %**, leaf fresh mass **−36.9 %**, leaf dry mass **−40.8 %**, all against controls. The paper frames this, reasonably, as a route to *inhibiting* growth selectively — which is the opposite of the claim class's promise.

**Minh & Bui — a frequency window, with the highest frequency worse than nothing.** Medium frequency (~1 kHz) gave the best marketable yield: **+6–7 % in cabbage** (≈7.1 → 7.5–7.6 kg/m²) and **+8–12 % in carrot and tomato**; vitamin C up 5–15 %; phenolics up in all three. But **high frequency (~8 kHz) failed to improve yield at all and reduced cabbage yield to 6.6 kg/m² — below control.** The beneficial effect is confined to a band, and leaving the band costs yield.

## Confidence: medium that the effect class is real; low that any specific frequency or dose generalises

**For the class being real.**

1. **Multiple independent groups, in different countries, with controls**, and effects large enough to survive ordinary statistics.
2. **Two of the three are institutional or peer-reviewed journals**, not promotion.
3. **The mushroom trial is a genuinely good design** — three species × four frequencies × untreated controls × two harvests — and it is the design that reveals the sign flip rather than hiding it.
4. **The frequency-window shape is internally consistent across studies** — a mid-band optimum (PAFT's own reports cluster around a few hundred Hz to ~1 kHz; the mushroom optimum is 225–750 Hz; the vegetable optimum is ~1 kHz), with the extremes doing harm. That convergence on a *shape* is the strongest thing in the rail.

**Against reading any of it as a recipe.**

1. **The sign flips with species.** *P. ostreatus* was harmed at every frequency while the hybrid was helped at every frequency — same apparatus, same protocol, same day. A claim that inverts between two congeners cannot be a single "sound makes plants grow" effect.
2. **The sign flips with frequency**, including within one species: *P. eryngii* was helped at 225/750 Hz and harmed at 50/100 Hz.
3. **The sign flips with organ and species in the vegetable trial** — and the highest frequency was worse than silence for cabbage.
4. **The institutional result is negative**, and not marginally: a quarter to two-fifths of leaf mass gone.
5. **The field's own 2026 review says the evidence is context-dependent and the methodology unstandardised** — which is the polite version of "these numbers are not comparable across studies."
6. **Mechanistic candidates are non-specific.** "Mechanosensing" and "stomatal response" would predict a dose response, not a sign flip; a sign flip usually means the effect is a **stress response**, not a growth stimulus — which is exactly what the CNR result (chlorophyll and stomatal conductance both down) looks like.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point.

**The honest content of this rail is not "sound helps plants" and not "sound does nothing." It is that the effect is real enough to measure and unstable enough that its sign cannot be predicted from the claim.** The same apparatus that helped a hybrid mushroom at 750 Hz harmed its parent species at every frequency it was offered. Ninety-eight hertz at conversational-loudness-plus reduced a broad bean's leaf mass by a third. A kilohertz helped three vegetables modestly; eight kilohertz hurt one of them below silence.

That pattern — a mid-band optimum with harm at the extremes, and sign depending on species and organ — is the same shape the Yard already teaches for magneto-culture and for biodynamic preparations: **an effect whose sign changes with the operating point can only be tested at more than one operating point.** A single-species, single-frequency home trial is therefore almost guaranteed to produce a result that does not replicate, in either direction.

**What would change this record:** a pre-registered multi-species, multi-frequency trial with a **sham-sound control** (speaker present, no signal — the control most of this literature omits, which would separate acoustic from thermal, vibrational and handling effects) and blinding of the harvesters. A trial that reports the *same* sign across two species and two frequencies would be the first real brick in the rail. **The celery result and the broad-bean result cannot both be a general law; whichever way the next controlled trial falls, file it.**
