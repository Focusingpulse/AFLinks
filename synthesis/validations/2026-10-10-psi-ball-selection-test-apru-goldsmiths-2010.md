# Validation — The Ball Selection Test in Chris French's Lab: A "Successful Replication" Whose Authors Disagreed (Goldsmiths, 2002; published 2010)

**Domain:** psi / ESP (forced-choice precognition — the Ball Selection Test)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-10 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

**Ertel's Ball Selection Test (BST)** is a low-tech forced-choice psi procedure. Fifty table-tennis balls sit in an opaque drawstring bag; each ball carries a number **1–5** (ten balls per number) and **red or green dots** (five of each number red, five green), giving **ten distinct ball types equally represented** and a **10% chance of a double hit** (correct number *and* colour). A participant jumbles the bag, guesses number and colour, then draws a ball blindly; the ball goes back. The claim is the ordinary psi claim: **hit rates above the 10% chance baseline**. Ertel had reported such a result at Göttingen (GEMI), and — this is the claim under test here — claimed the procedure is **more replicable** than conventional multiple-choice psi tests.

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** do unselected participants, tested under supervision by people who do not believe in psi, hit above the 10% chance baseline on the Ball Selection Test?

## Who tested it, when, and where

**Johanna Körting** and **Luke Hagstrom**, two **undergraduate psychology students** (then aged 23 and 26), each testing **20 participants**, working on their **BSc Final Year Projects** under **Professor Chris French**, head of the **Anomalistic Psychology Research Unit (APRU), Goldsmiths College, University of London** — the archive's own exemplar of the **skeptical lab**. Ertel had lectured at APRU and **left the test material there**; the students picked it up and ran it. Their theses (2002) supplied the data and the analyses; **Ertel re-analysed it and published it in the *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 24**(4): 581–598, **2010**, under the title *"Psi in a Skeptic's Lab: A Successful Replication of Ertel's Ball Selection Test."*

**Two arms of context travel in the same paper:** **GEMI I** — **47 unselected** participants completing the test **at home, unsupervised**, 480 trials each (**22,560 trials**); and **GEMI II** — **9 participants selected** from the 47 as good home scorers and **retested under supervision**, 480 trials each.

## Method — what they actually did

- **Participants:** **40** unselected APRU volunteers — **14 male, 26 female**, ages **19–56** (mean 28.6), mostly fellow students, plus some older family members; voluntary, with signed informed consent.
- **The decisive design deviation, stated by the paper:** the students and French **forwent the at-home phase entirely.** Every APRU trial was run **under supervision with unselected participants** — which is *not* how the GEMI protocol that produced the original positive was run, and it is the difference the paper's own analysis turns on.
- **Procedure:** **6 runs × 60 trials = 360 trials** per participant, distributed over **2–3 sessions** on different days, run under familiar conditions (college or student homes), a relaxed atmosphere, and standard GEMI instructions.
- **Supervision:** one experimenter (**Hagstrom**) stayed **inactive** except to watch that procedure was followed and **cheating did not occur**; the other (**Körting**) **filled out the record sheets herself.**
- **Materials deviation:** Körting used the **standard version-II material** (numbered balls with red/green dots); **Hagstrom substituted "air-flow" balls** (same size, small holes, **white vs orange** instead of number-and-dot markings) — for **"availability and time constraints,"** with no further explanation given.
- **Analysis, and this is where the record lives:** the students applied **one-sample t-tests** (and Hagstrom a **Bonferroni correction**). **Ertel re-analysed with a one-sided binomial test on summed hits and with a summed-Z² chi-square**, and argued the t-test lacked power and the Bonferroni correction was inadmissible because double hits were the pre-instructed success measure.

## Result — what was measured

| Arm | Participants | Trials | Observed hits/run | Expected | Test | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **APRU (this replication)** | 40 unselected, **supervised** | 14,400 | **6.45** (**10.75%**) | 6.00 | Zbin 2.99 | **.002** one-sided |
| APRU, summed-Z² | 40 | 14,400 | — | — | chi² 76.3 (df 40) | **.0003** |
| GEMI I | 47 unselected, **unsupervised (home)** | 22,560 | 6.97 (11.6%) | 6.00 | Zbin 8.07 | 10⁻¹⁴ |
| GEMI II | 9 **selected**, supervised | 4,320 | 10.39 (17.3%) | 6.00 | Zbin 16.00 | <10⁻⁵⁰ |

- **The APRU arm is above chance and it is the arm the skeptics ran:** **1,548 double hits against 1,440 expected**, p = .002 (one-sided binomial), p = .0003 (chi-square). Effect sizes are small — **ES1 = 0.025**, **ES2 = 0.029**.
- **Supervision cost hits, and the paper predicted it.** APRU's supervised 6.45/run is **significantly below** GEMI's unsupervised 6.97/run (**chi² = 6.55, df 1, p = .01**). The paper reads supervision as **psi-detrimental** ("increased emotional tension", plus the more skeptical social embedding).
- **The distribution is not a single lucky tail:** the kurtosis of the hit-score distribution is **flat** (5.95 against the expected 3.0; Z′ = 2.88, **p = .002**) — participants spread in **both** directions, with a top performer at **89 number / 224 colour / 67 double hits** (p ≈ 3×10⁻⁷ on double hits alone) and ranks 30–40 increasingly negative.
- **And the two students concluded the opposite.** **Körting:** *"The results obtained by Ertel (2002) were not replicated"*, having rendered p = .052 as not significant. **Hagstrom:** *"Hypothesis one [psi exists] was offered no significant statistical support after data had been subjected to bonferroni adjustments"* — while also writing that his own binomial analysis of the top participant *"does suggest that psi may exist"* and *"There may just be some gifted individuals."* The published **"successful replication"** is therefore **the test's designer's re-analysis of a replication attempt whose own authors reported failure**, on a statistical route they did not use, over data one of them collected.

## Confidence: medium–low — and the qualifier is structural, not fussy

- **What is genuinely strong:** an **independent lab**, a **named skeptical supervisor**, **40 unselected participants**, **14,400 supervised trials**, a **pre-instructed chance level**, **two statistical routes agreeing** (p = .002 and p = .0003), and a **predicted** effect (the supervised-vs-unsupervised drop). Very few psi records arrive with a **skeptic's name on the lab door**.
- **What makes it medium–low:** 1. **The positive is a re-analysis, not the study's own conclusion** — both authors of the underlying theses read their data as a failure, and one applied a correction the publisher calls inadmissible. This is a **disagreement about the analysis of one dataset**, not two independent confirmations. 2. **The paper is by the test's designer**, re-analysing his own instrument's replication — the experimenter-credibility structure the paper itself spends pages on, pointed the other way. 3. **The effect is small** (ES ≈ 0.025). 4. **Materials differed between the two experimenters** (standard balls vs air-flow balls), and **Hagstrom's trial-by-trial data was never provided**, so only Körting's half admits a full re-analysis. 5. **No blinding or sensory-leakage arm appears in this paper** — it is a blind-draw task, and the paper notes two earlier experiments had argued tactile and temperature cues could not explain BST performance, but the seal is asserted from those, not demonstrated here. (A companion Ertel paper, *JSE* 24(4) `jse_463.pdf`, does run the **blindfold-and-gloves** version and reports significant, near-identical hit rates on both — an argument that sensory leakage is not the source, made by the same author.)
- **The single most useful sentence in the record:** the paper's own **GEMI vs APRU** comparison — **the same test, run supervised, lost effect** — which is simultaneously the replication's strength (a predicted, dose-like structure) and the reason a **home-versus-lab** design matters for anyone trying to reproduce it.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted / measured / reported:** two psychology undergraduates in **Chris French's skeptics' lab** at Goldsmiths ran **Ertel's Ball Selection Test** on **40 unselected participants** under supervision — **14,400 trials** — and hit **10.75% against a 10% chance baseline** (p = .002 binomial; p = .0003 chi-square), with a **flat hit distribution** and a single participant at ~3×10⁻⁷. **Both students concluded the effect was not replicated;** the published *"successful replication"* is **Ertel's own re-analysis** using a statistical route they did not use. Filed as the **Yard's first positive psi record** and filed **medium–low** for exactly that reason: one dataset, two readings, and the friendly reading published by the instrument's designer. Its genuinely valuable structure is the paper's own **supervised-vs-unsupervised comparison** — the same test, run in a lab, gave back a measurable share of the effect — which is why this record belongs beside the Yard's null campaigns rather than above them: **a positive with a name, a threshold, a predicted moderator and an author-level disagreement attached is worth more than a positive with none, and this one carries all four.**
