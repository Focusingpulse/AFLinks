# Validation — EZ Water in Plant Xylem Vessels

**Domain:** water structure / EZ water
**Recorded:** 2026-09-18 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

That water adjacent to hydrophilic surfaces forms an ordered, solute-excluding "exclusion zone" (EZ), also called the "fourth phase" of water — first reported by Gerald Pollack's group at the University of Washington for synthetic surfaces (Nafion, gels, cellulose, metals). Pollack's programme further claims EZ water has distinct properties (higher density, viscosity and refractive index; light absorbance near 270 nm; negative charge) and can drive flow inside hydrophilic tubes.

## Who replicated it, when

Anqi Wang and Gerald H. Pollack, Department of Bioengineering, University of Washington, Seattle — the lab that originated the EZ claim. Published in *Scientific Reports* 14, 12071, 27 May 2024 (received Nov 2023; funded by the SAGST Foundation, Germany).

## Method

Fresh stems of napa cabbage, celery, asparagus and pumpkin were cut into clean sections and immersed in suspensions of 1 μm microspheres (carboxylate, polystyrene, amino, silica — deliberately varied in surface charge). Microsphere-free zones next to the cut xylem vessels were imaged by light microscopy and measured with ImageJ. Single pumpkin vessels (inner diameter ~80–230 μm) were dissected out and observed transversely in real time to follow EZ buildup inside the lumen. Variables tested: microsphere type, vessel length (0.2–4+ mm), vessel diameter, and NaCl concentration (0–500 μM). Cut samples; not intact transpiring plants.

## Result

EZ formed consistently against xylem vessels of all four species, excluding microspheres regardless of particle type or charge — evidence the exclusion is not an artifact of one tracer. EZ widths outside the vessels were 133 ± 22 to 142 ± 20 μm (cabbage, celery, asparagus, n = 9) and up to 240 ± 56 μm for pumpkin (n = 9), reaching 637 ± 82 μm for vessels ≥4 mm long. Inside single pumpkin vessels, EZ grew from the inner wall inward, filling 94 ± 3% of the cross-section within 4 min in an 80 μm vessel; 500 μM NaCl suppressed EZ fraction to <10%, while ≤100 μM allowed ≥40%. An upward water flow appeared inside vertically held vessels and persisted ~10 min; it was absent at high salt. The authors attribute the flow to a proton gradient generated as EZ nucleates.

## Confidence: medium

Peer-reviewed, quantitative, with particle-type controls. Limits: this is the originating lab extending its own claim into plant tissue — not an independent group — and the samples are cut, ex vivo vessels, so it does not show EZ behaviour in an intact transpiring plant. The wider EZ literature remains contested: a 2020 review (Elton et al., *Int. J. Mol. Sci.* 21, 5041) argues diffusiophoresis/chemotaxis (Schurr) explains the core phenomenon without a new water phase, reports neutron radiography incompatible with a higher-density phase, and flags that some metal-surface replication attempts have failed. Treat the existence of an exclusion phenomenon as well-supported; treat the "fourth phase" interpretation as unresolved.
