# Validation — Pre-Sowing Electroculture: DC vs AC on Soybean (Kuban State Agrarian University, 2026)

**Domain:** electro-culture (applied electric fields in agriculture)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-14 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The general electroculture claim is old — three centuries of it — and runs in two forms. The **passive** form (bury a copper dowel, harvest more vegetables) this Yard has already filed twice and both times as a **null**. The **active** form claims that a **defined electric field or current applied to seed or plant** improves germination, growth and yield. That is the claim this record tests, and it tests a sharper version of it: not *whether* an electric field helps, but **which kind of field, and how much** — comparing pulsed/continuous **DC** against **AC at power frequency** head to head.

## Who tested it, when, and where

**Igor V. Yudaev** and **Yuliia V. Daus** — **Kuban State Agrarian University**, Russia. Published in **AgriEngineering** (MDPI), **31 May 2026**.

## Method — what they actually did

- Seed: **soybean (*Glycine max* L.), cultivar Volgogradka 1.**
- A **full two-way matrix**: field intensities **0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 kV/cm** crossed with exposure durations **30, 60 and 120 s**, at a **fixed 10 cm electrode gap**.
- **DC treatment vs AC treatment vs an untreated control**, all at the same nominal field levels.
- **Laboratory germination** measured as **germination energy (day 3)** and **total germination (day 7)**, in **triplicate**.
- A **replicated field trial** followed, scoring **plant height, bean yield (mass), and disease incidence**.
- Statistics: **backward-elimination regression** to find which treatment factor actually predicted the outcome.

## Result — what was measured

- **DC beat both the control and AC.** Germination energy rose by **up to 60%**, and **total germination reached 100%, against 85% in the control.** The **optimal DC window** was **0.8–1.5 kV/cm at ~30 s** exposure (practical recommendation ≈1.0 kV/cm, 30–60 s).
- **AC at power frequency did the opposite.** It **failed to enhance germination and frequently suppressed it**, and **markedly increased susceptibility to fungal crown rot.**
- **The field trial agreed with the bench.** DC-treated seed gave the highest bean mass (**85 g per five plants** vs **80 g** control), while **AC-treated seed gave the lowest (72 g)** — i.e. AC did not merely fail, it was **worse than doing nothing**.
- **Dose, not time, was the lever.** Backward-elimination regression found **field intensity was the only significant predictor**; exposure time and interaction terms were **non-significant**.

## Confidence: medium (on the DC effect as reported); and it is explicitly NOT a general endorsement

**Why medium.** A **peer-reviewed journal (MDPI *AgriEngineering*)**, a **proper intensity × duration matrix**, **triplicate** lab runs, and a **replicated field trial** with a mechanistic side (the DC/AC split is a real, physically motivated contrast — the two currents differ in how they act at the membrane and in whether their effects reverse). That is a better-built experiment than most of the electroculture literature.

**Why not higher.** It is a **single laboratory and cultivar**, the yield margin in the field is **small (85 vs 80 g per five plants, ~6%)** even where germination gains were large, and the DC-optimal/AC-harmful **sign split is a mechanistic claim the paper asserts more than it isolates** — the AC harm and the DC benefit are consistent with a membrane-polarisation story, but the trial does not separate that from, e.g., electrode-driven ion effects in the seed layer. **Independence is unestablished:** I found no second group reproducing this exact DC/AC contrast.

**What it does earn.** It gives the electroculture rail something the passive nulls could not: **a structured dose response with a sign that flips with the type of current.** That is a testable shape, not a bare positive.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured / reported:** a Kuban State Agrarian University group ran soybean seed through a full grid of field strengths and durations, once with DC and once with AC, and let the field finish the argument. **DC raised germination hard (to 100% vs 85%) and lifted field yield modestly; AC at power frequency suppressed germination and invited fungal rot.** Filed **medium** — one lab, one cultivar, a small field margin — and **explicitly not** as a general thumbs-up for electrified agriculture.

The value here is the **shape**. The electroculture pavilion already holds two clean nulls — passive copper dowels and a constant weak field — and the honest synthesis is not "electroculture doesn't work," it is that **the dose and the type of current are the claim.** This trial is the first record in the rail with a **sign that flips**: the same nominal field, applied as DC, helped; applied as AC, it made things worse than the control. Read with the passive nulls, the rail's standing message is unchanged and sharper — **a field that is too weak does nothing, and a field of the wrong kind does harm, so only a specified, structured protocol can even be tested.** That is what a replication yard is for.
