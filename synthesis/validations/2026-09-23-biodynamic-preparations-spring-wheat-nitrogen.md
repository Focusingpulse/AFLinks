# Validation — Biodynamic Preparations, Nitrogen Availability and Soil Type in Spring Wheat

**Domain:** anthroposophical / biodynamic experimental method (Steiner's preparations; the Kolisko lineage)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-23 (Replication Watch, run 4)

## What was claimed

That the **eight biodynamic preparations** Rudolf Steiner specified in his 1924 Agriculture Course — two field sprays (500, horn-manure; 501, horn-silica) stirred into water in a prescribed rhythm, and six compost preparations inserted into manure and compost in animal sheaths — change how a crop **grows and yields beyond what their material nutrients can account for.** The doses are homeopathic in scale; the claim is that the effect is not nutritional.

This domain had **no record in the Yard before today** — no validation, no dossier, no quest card — despite the archive's own curriculum carrying Goethean and anthroposophical method as Section V. This is that gap's first entry.

## Who tested it, when

**Ellen Spoerk Gabriel** and **Walter A. Goldstein**. Goldstein's affiliation is listed as **Seva Mandir**; he has published on biodynamic preparations since the 1970s (including the 2019 *Open Agriculture* study linking preparations to root growth, root health and soil organic matter, and biodynamic maize breeding under biodynamic-organic conditions). Country is **not stated** in the retrieved metadata.

- **Preprint**, posted **7 March 2026**, listed 27 May 2026, currently marked **"Under Revision."** **Not peer reviewed.**
- doi **10.21203/rs.3.rs-9059525/v1** — Research Square.

## Method

Two **growth-chamber** experiments on a **conventionally bred spring wheat variety**, with the **same available nitrogen across all treatments — 60 kg/ha**. Two soils: a **pasture soil (Rygge)** and a **biodynamic soil (Alm Gaard)**.

- **Experiment 1** (Rygge soil): mineral N fertilizer vs. a **two-month-old manure compost** vs. an unfertilized control — each **with and without the biodynamic spray preparations**.
- **Experiment 2** (both soils): two **year-old composts** — one biodynamic, one non-inoculated — vs. an unfertilized control.

The design is a genuine multi-factor comparison, and — this is the part that matters — it tests the preparations **as an addition to an existing fertility treatment**, not against nothing. Held equal: N rate. Varied: N source, compost maturity, preparation presence, soil.

## Result

**Conditional and sign-switching — not triumphant.**

- **Mineral fertilizers supplied N better than the immature compost**; the compost caused **microbial immobilization of nitrate** (the compost tied up N rather than releasing it).
- **Mature compost supplied nitrogen more efficiently than the mineral fertilizers.**
- **Biodynamic compost had its strongest positive effect on wheat when applied to the biodynamic soil** — the effect was soil-dependent, and not in the direction of "biodynamic inputs always win."
- **In both experiments the biodynamic preparations enhanced growth and yield where N was not limiting, and had slightly inhibitory effects where N availability was lower or was immobilized by soil microbes.**
- Biodynamic compost **accelerated nutrient uptake** on both soils relative to the un-inoculated compost.
- Large increases in **total soil N** under the wheat, greater and accruing earlier on the biodynamic soil; the authors hypothesize **bacterial N₂ fixation coupled with rhizodeposition**, and note that early N accrual was enhanced by the biodynamic compost.

**The headline is the reversal:** the preparations helped *when the plant was already well fed* and slightly hurt *when it was short of nitrogen.* That is not what a nutrient effect looks like — a nutrient effect does not reverse sign — and it is also not what a straight "life force" claim predicts.

## Confidence: low-to-medium

**Why not higher:**

- **A preprint, explicitly "Under Revision," not peer reviewed.**
- **Growth chamber, not field.** Two soils, one wheat variety, no field season — so the result says nothing yet about farm-scale behaviour.
- **The retrieved abstract reports no effect sizes and no p-values.** Direction and conditionality are stated; magnitude is not quantified anywhere this run could read. The paper is behind a Research Square page whose abstract is all that was retrievable.
- **The authors are participants in the tradition.** Goldstein's programme is explicitly biodynamic and long-running. This is not a disinterested group testing a claim from outside.
- **No mechanism offered for the conditional switch.** "Preparations improve growth when N is not limiting" is a description. Why a homeopathic dose of fermented plant material should change sign with nitrate availability is not addressed.

**Why not lower:**

- The result is **conditional and partly unfavourable to the claim** (inhibitory under N limitation; no blanket advantage for biodynamic compost across soils). Trials that report a reversal are reporting a measurement, not a promotion.
- It **reproduces a shape already in the literature from an independent group** — see below. Convergent *structure* across labs is more interesting than a lone positive.

## The rail this sits on — the conditional finding has now been reached twice

| Year | Who | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **1996** | **Raupp & König**, *Biological Agriculture & Horticulture* **13**(2):175–188 — 28 field-plot and pot experiments near Marburg plus a **9-year spring wheat trial at Darmstadt**, Germany | Preparations **500** and **501** *increased* yields at low yield levels, had little effect at medium, and **lowered** yields at high levels: Y = 4.497 − 0.181X (r = −0.615). In the spring wheat trial the same sign-reversal appeared under high fertilization (Y = 28.930 − 0.87X, r = −0.767). The authors' reading: **normalisation of yields**, not stimulation. |
| **2000** | **Carpenter-Boggs, Reganold & Kennedy**, *Am. J. Alt. Agric.* **15** | Short-term trial, lentil and spring wheat: crop yield and quality similar across mineral NPK, biodynamic compost and non-biodynamic compost. **Few consistent short-term differences from the preparations.** |
| **2024** | **Dietrich, Dahn, Fritz, Berg, Köpke & Döring**, *Renewable Agriculture and Food Systems* — **27-year** organic rotation (the DOK trial, Therwil, Switzerland) | Biodynamic compost preparations had **no effect on yield or yield stability in any crop, including spring wheat**, in 27 years — *even though* they measurably changed the soil biological community. Not superior to untreated compost agronomically. |
| **2026** | **Spoerk Gabriel & Goldstein** (this record) | The preparations **enhance growth and yield where N is not limiting and are slightly inhibitory where it is**; strongest positive effect of biodynamic compost on the biodynamic soil. |

Read together, these four do not say "preparations work" or "preparations don't." They say something sharper and more useful: **the preparations do not raise yields on average, and when they do show up, they show up as a modifier whose sign depends on how well the plant is already supplied.** Raupp & König found it against yield level; the 2026 preprint finds it against nitrogen availability. **Two independent groups, two different variables, the same reversal.** That convergence is the finding worth carrying — and it is also a warning: a trial that reports only its mean will see nothing, and a trial run only at one fertility level will report the sign it happened to sit on.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded as **claimed / attempted / measured / reported**: the preparations were tested as an *addition* to controlled fertility, on two soils, in two experiments — which is more than most of what circulates about biodynamics — and the answer came back **conditional rather than categorical**. They helped where nitrogen was not limiting; they slightly hindered where it was. The same reversal, against a different variable, in a different country, from a different group, has been on the record since 1996.

The transferable lesson is the one this trail keeps teaching: **an effect with a changing sign is not a weak effect — it is a different kind of claim, and it can only be tested at more than one operating point.** A home replication of a biodynamic preparation that runs a single fertility level has roughly a coin-flip chance of reporting the sign that its fertility happened to select, and will have learned nothing either way. The honest first test is not "do preparations work"; it is "at how many fertility levels does the sign flip" — and its control is compost **without** preparations, not nothing.
