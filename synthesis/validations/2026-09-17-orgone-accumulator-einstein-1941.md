# Validation — Einstein Tests the Orgone Accumulator Temperature Difference (1941)

**Domain:** orgone accumulator / thermal anomaly
**Recorded:** 2026-09-17 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

Reich's claim: an orgone accumulator — a layered enclosure of alternating metallic and organic material, metal facing inward — maintains a higher interior temperature than the surrounding air, i.e. a temperature equilibrium never occurs inside it (at low humidity).

## Who replicated it, when

**Albert Einstein. Meeting of 1941-01-13, lasting five hours.** Einstein was, at the time, the most famous physicist alive and was not a member of Reich's circle. Laska's account reports that Einstein "was very impressed," and described the temperature difference as a "bomb" for physics *if it could be established flawlessly*. He then carried out measurements himself.

## Method

Einstein **measured the temperature difference between the accumulator interior and the surrounding air himself**, and "arrived at results similar to Reich's."

In a letter of **1941-02-07** he offered a conventional explanation instead of endorsing the phenomenon: in rooms the floor temperature is always lower than the ceiling; with a horizontal fixed plate present (the tabletop the accumulator stood on during the experiment), the underside of the plate communicates more with the floor through convection and the upper side more with the ceiling — "This circumstance has finally turned out to be the decisive one… In light of these experiments I consider the matter completely cleared up."

Reich replied on **1941-02-20** with a battery of control variants, each designed to break the convection path Einstein had proposed:

1. room thermometer placed *likewise above the tabletop* (with Einstein it hung freely in the room beside the tabletop);
2. a second wooden board fixed about 1 m above the tabletop, to interrupt convection from the ceiling;
3. the wooden tabletop replaced by a metal plate (a better heat conductor);
4. the accumulator **freely hanging in the room, without a table**.

And a separate arm:

5. **open air** — where, per Laska, "an even stronger temperature difference resulted, because the influences of walls, tables, etc., were absent."

## Result

In all four room variants "the temperature difference between the interior of the accumulator and the surrounding air resulted"; the open-air trial gave a stronger difference. Einstein never replied to the letter, did not run the further control experiments Reich expected, and in 1944 declined further involvement — asking that his oral and written utterances be treated discreetly and threatening legal steps if his name were connected with orgonomy. The exchange ended there. **The phenomenon was reproduced by both parties; the mechanism (convection vs. orgone) was never adjudicated by a neutral third party.**

Later, same lineage: **DeMeo, J. (2009), "Experimental confirmation of the Reich orgone accumulator thermal anomaly"** — cited in the corpus volume's bibliography as having addressed the objections raised since the 1940s and 1970s. DeMeo is a sympathetic investigator within the orgonomic tradition; the corpus does not contain a neutral replication.

## Confidence: medium

A primary-document-based account: Bernd Laska's 1976 essay is built on *The Einstein Affair* (Reich 1953c) and quotes Einstein's and Reich's letters directly, so Einstein's own position is visible in his own words. What is *not* in the corpus is the raw measurement data from either party, and no independent replication by a non-sympathizer has been located. The claim that the difference is a genuine thermal anomaly rather than convection therefore remains **attempted and reported, not established**.
