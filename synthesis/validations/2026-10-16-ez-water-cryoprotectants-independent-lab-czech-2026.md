# Validation — The Exclusion Zone Reproduced Independently and Linked to Cryoprotectants (Institute of Physics, Czech Academy of Sciences, 2026)

**Domain:** water structure / exclusion-zone (EZ) and "fourth phase" water
**Recorded:** 2026-10-16 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The archive's central water claim is that **water at a hydrophilic interface is not bulk water** — it forms a distinct, extended, ordered zone that excludes colloids and solutes out to tens to hundreds of micrometres. Pollack's version calls it the **exclusion zone (EZ)** and reads it as a fourth phase with a charge and a proton gradient.

The Yard already holds evidence that the *existence* of the zone is independently reproducible: the EZ has been demonstrated by several groups (Elton et al., *Int. J. Mol. Sci.* 2020), measured in plant xylem (2024), and independently re-measured by an unrelated AFM technique (Teschke et al., *Langmuir* 2026 — this Yard, 2026-10-07). But Teschke measured the *phenomenon* with a *different instrument*. What the rail has been missing is the plainest replication of all: **another laboratory running Pollack's own protocol** — Nafion plus microspheres — and getting the zone.

This record is that, and it goes one step further into a mainstream problem.

## Who tested it, when, and where

**Alexis Theodorou, Karel Pomeisl, Jan Pokorný and Irena Kratochvílová** — **Institute of Physics, Czech Academy of Sciences (FZU), Prague**. Published in ***Cryobiology*** (Elsevier), **March 2026** (published online 16 December 2025), DOI **10.1016/j.cryobiol.2025.105566**. The group works on cryopreservation and interfacial water; it has no affiliation with Pollack's laboratory.

## Method — what they actually did

- **The standard EZ protocol, not a novel instrument:** a **Nafion NT-030** membrane as the hydrophilic surface, with **1.00 μm carboxylate polystyrene microspheres** as the exclusion probe — i.e. the same microsphere-exclusion assay the EZ literature uses.
- **Temperature sweep:** EZ size measured **across a range of temperatures under cooling**, the regime that matters for cryopreservation.
- **Cryoprotectants:** **polyethylene glycols (PEG 100, PEG 1500, PEG 6000)** and **trehalose**, tested individually and in combination, at matched concentrations.
- **Fluorescent tracking:** PEG 6000 and trehalose were **dansyl-labelled** and followed by fluorescence, to see whether the cryoprotectant molecules actually distribute near the EZ.

## Result — what was measured

- **The EZ formed and was measurable** next to Nafion in this laboratory — the particle-free zone reproduces with the standard protocol, outside Pollack's group.
- **The EZ is temperature- and cryoprotectant-dependent.** Both classes of cryoprotectant changed EZ size; **trehalose suppressed the EZ less strongly than PEG at the same concentration** — a differential effect between two molecules used for the same purpose.
- **The dansyl-labelled cryoprotectants distributed near the EZ**, which the authors read as evidence of **long-range interaction between the cryoprotectant and interfacial water**.
- The authors describe it as the **first evidence linking cryoprotectant activity to EZ behaviour**, and suggest a role for interfacial water stabilisation under cooling, with implications for cryopreservation.

## Confidence: medium (a measurement reproduced, in a new lab and a mainstream venue; not a mechanism)

**Why medium, not higher.** Peer-reviewed (**Cryobiology**, Elsevier), and — the point — **independent of Pollack's laboratory, running Pollack's protocol.** A reproducible measurement in foreign hands is exactly what the rail needed, and it is now in a **mainstream cryobiology** journal rather than the water-structure niche.

**Why not higher.**
- It **reproduces the measurement, not the interpretation.** The 2020 Elton review's competing explanation — **Schurr's diffusiophoresis**, a particle-free zone that requires **no new water phase** — predicts a zone like this too. Nothing here separates "structured fourth phase" from "ordinary exclusion driven by surface chemistry and solute gradients."
- The **cryoprotection link is correlational and single-lab**; the authors themselves flag it as a first, not a confirmation.
- It is one laboratory and one surface (Nafion); metal-interface EZ results, which the review noted had **failed** in some replications, are not addressed.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured:** a Prague laboratory with no link to Pollack's used the ordinary exclusion-zone setup — Nafion and microspheres — reproduced the particle-free zone, watched it respond to temperature and to two cryoprotectants, and traced the labelled cryoprotectant molecules sitting near the zone. That is a **protocol-level reproduction of the measurement by foreign hands**, which is the water rail's oldest open question, and it now sits in a **mainstream cryobiology journal**.

Two honest limits. First, **a repeatable measurement is not a mechanism** — the diffusiophoresis account produces a particle-free zone without a new phase, and this study does not discriminate between them, so the archive should read it as *the zone is real and measurable in independent labs*, not as *the fourth phase is confirmed*. Second, the **cryoprotection link is the authors' own first claim, in one laboratory, and is correlational**. Read beside the Teschke AFM record and the same day's Max Planck ceiling on light-driven interfacial water, the rail's water picture is now consistent and narrow: **interfacial water is a real, independently measurable phenomenon — and every cheap explanation for it, light included, still has to be earned.**
