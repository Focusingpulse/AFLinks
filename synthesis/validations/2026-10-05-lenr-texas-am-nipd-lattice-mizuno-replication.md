# Validation — Hydrogen-Loaded Ni–Pd Lattice: A University Replication Attempt at ICCF-27

**Domain:** LENR / condensed-matter nuclear science (hydrogen-metal lattice, anomalous heat + nuclear signatures) — United States
**Recorded:** 2026-10-05 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

That a **hydrogen-loaded nickel–palladium lattice** — a metal foil/mesh loaded with H or D — releases **more heat than is put in**, and/or emits **nuclear signatures** (charged particles, transmuted elements, unusual radiation) that a chemical process cannot explain. The specific lineage being tested is **Tadahiko Mizuno's** method: **palladium applied to a nickel mesh**, then loaded with hydrogen/deuterium, reported to yield **5–20% excess heat at 10–30 W** via the newer electrode-preparation route (older glow-discharge versions claimed up to 2× input). Mizuno has publicly asked for replication for years and has said *"independent verification and replication are essential."*

This is the same claim class already in the Yard from the **positive** side (Ni–Cu nanopowder transmutation, glow-discharge excess heat, High-Frequency Induction COP 1.4–2.4) and the **negative** side (Project Callisto's 2015–2024 campaign: no anomalous heat across 40+ replications). A new **independent** Ni–Pd result is exactly the data point the class has been missing.

## Who replicated it, when, and where

- **Talk:** *"Nuclear Signatures in a Hydrogen-Loaded Ni–Pd Lattice Confinement System"* — **Agastya Rao, Lavanya Upadhyaya, Anthony Cecchini** — Session 3, **Monday 31 August 2026, 16:20–16:45**, **ICCF-27**, Niagara Falls, Canada (official program).
- **Companion talk (same group):** *"Dynamic Closed-Loop Calorimetry for Evaluation of Temperature-Dependent Thermal Performance in Hydrogen-Loaded Ni-Pd Systems"* — **Agastya Rao, Shashank Siddhartha, Siddhartha Durairajan** — Session 4, **Tuesday 1 September 2026**.
- **Institution (independently checked):** **Texas A&M University**, Department of Nuclear Engineering. Two of the three authors are identifiable as **TAMU nuclear-engineering PhD students under Dr. Lin Shao** — Upadhyaya (nuclear materials / molten-salt corrosion) and Cecchini (Extreme Materials & Accelerator Laboratory, started Fall 2025). Rao's affiliation was not independently confirmed this run. Mizuno's own history at **Texas A&M with John Bockris** makes the venue culturally continuous with the claim's lineage.

## Method — as reported, not as read

I did **not** read the paper or abstract; the description below is what the program lists plus what conference-floor reporting says. Reported as: a **hydrogen-loaded Ni–Pd lattice** tested for **nuclear signatures** (the title's observable) with a **dynamic closed-loop calorimetry** companion measuring temperature-dependent thermal performance. Conference-floor reporting (LENR-Forum, 31 Aug 2026) states the method is **"very close"** to Mizuno's palladium-on-nickel-mesh approach, that the work is **"being done at Texas A&M with excellent instruments,"** and that **"the results are clear."** The phrase **"replication of Mizuno's experiment"** is the commenter's framing, **not** the authors' abstract — treat that attribution as secondhand.

## Result — what was reported

- **Reported:** a positive Ni–Pd **nuclear-signature** result, characterised by conference-floor reporting as a **definitive replication** of the Mizuno method — the first claimed to land at a major laboratory after several earlier replication attempts failed.
- **Not established by this run:** the actual numbers, the controls, the calorimetry closure, and whether the signatures survive the standard null battery (atmospheric leakage, stored chemical energy, instrument artefacts). The talk title foregrounds **nuclear signatures**, not excess heat — so even a confirmed positive here would be a *nuclear-product* observation, which (as the Yard's double-observable record notes) is a different observable from the Callisto-null **heat** question and can be compatible with it.
- **Same-corridor context at ICCF-27 (2026):** **Astral Systems** presented IEC + lattice-confinement ("Multi-State Fusion") neutron-production work (Benyo, Bakr, Wallace-Smith), with conference reporting that **zirconium electrodes outperformed stainless steel**; the lattice-confinement design descends from **NASA** work on deuterated erbium/titanium. None of these is an independent replication of an outside lab's claim.

## Confidence

- **Medium** that the talk occurred and reported a Ni–Pd lattice nuclear-signature result at Texas A&M: the **authorship and session are confirmed on the official ICCF-27 program**, and the institutional affiliation of two authors is independently confirmed. That is a real, named, university-lab presentation.
- **Low-medium** that the effect is real. **No paper or abstract was read.** The strongest language ("definitive replication," "results are clear") is a **single conference-floor source**, not the authors. The class carries a **standing nine-year null** (Project Callisto, 2015–2024) and **prior failed Mizuno replications**; a conference talk does not retire either. And the record so far rests on the **program listing plus one forum post** — **single-source on the result, unconfirmed.**

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as reported:** a named Texas A&M nuclear-engineering group put a hydrogen-loaded Ni–Pd lattice in front of the ICCF-27 audience and reported nuclear signatures, with floor reporting calling it a close replication of Mizuno's method. That is a **real institutional replication attempt** in a claim class that badly needs one — and it is filed here at **medium/low-medium**, not high, for a specific reason: **the report is a program listing plus one conference-floor post.** We have not seen the paper, the calorimetry, or the controls.

**What would move this to high:** the Phase-2-style materials the class keeps promising — **a published paper with closed calorimetry, an inert (no-load) control arm, calibrated nuclear detectors, and post-run material analysis**, ideally with the *heat* and *nuclear-signature* observables reported **separately** so the record can be read against both the Callisto heat-null and the nuclear-product positives rather than collapsing them into one verdict.

**The transferable caution:** "definitive replication" and "results are clear" are **the loudest phrase in the room, and the loudest phrase is the one to hold at arm's length** until the numbers are in hand. The same conference-corridor enthusiasm has attended this claim class for four decades; the differentiator is never the announcement, it is the apparatus on paper.
