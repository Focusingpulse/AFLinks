# Validation — He-3 in Hydrogen-Loaded Cu–Ni/ZrO2

**Domain:** LENR / nuclear products
**Recorded:** 2026-09-16 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

That metal–hydrogen nanocomposites generating anomalous heat (the Cu–Ni/zirconia line developed under Japan's NEDO project, 2015–2017) do so through a nuclear process — specifically the "4H/TSC" (4-hydrogen / tetrahedral symmetric condensate) model of Akito Takahashi, which predicts helium-3 as a primary reaction product.

## Who replicated it, when

Tomoya Yamauchi, Yutaka Mori, Shuto Higashi, Hayato Seiichi, Masahiko Hasegawa, Akito Takahashi, Akira Taniike and Masato Kanasaki (Kobe University, Graduate School of Maritime Sciences; Osaka University). Published in *Japanese Journal of Applied Physics*, 3 February 2025 (preprint arXiv:2409.05382, September 2024).

## Method

Cu–Ni/zirconia nanocomposite samples that had shown anomalous heat generation in ~300 kPa hydrogen at ~450 °C were examined by two complementary techniques: (1) nuclear reaction analysis (NRA) using 1.4 MeV deuteron beams from a tandem accelerator; and (2) thermal desorption spectrometry (TDS) with a quadrupole mass spectrometer. The material was made by melt-spinning CuxNiyZrz ribbons (~10 μm), calcining in air at 450 °C for 60 h to form a ZrO2 matrix with isolated Cu–Ni nanostructured zones, then grinding to particles.

## Result

Both methods detected helium-3 in the samples. From proton etch-pit densities (with PHITS Monte Carlo), roughly 5.0 × 10^15 He-3 atoms were estimated in sample #3. The paper states a discrepancy of about one order of magnitude between the measured excess heat and the estimated He-3 production, and discusses possible reasons (alternative Li*-4 decay pathways, degradation of He-3 retention in ZrO2 at high temperature, additional fission heat channels).

## Confidence: medium

Peer-reviewed; two physically independent detection methods agree; samples come from a multi-institution programme (Tohoku, Nagoya, Kyushu, Nissan, Technova, Kobe). Limits: the authors belong to the same network that made the heat claim, so this is a self-follow-up rather than a fully independent replication; the ~10× heat-to-He-3 mismatch is unresolved; the result is a helium-3 detection, not a direct observation of the reaction.
