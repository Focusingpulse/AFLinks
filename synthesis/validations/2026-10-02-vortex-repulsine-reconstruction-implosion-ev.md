# Validation — The Implosion e.V. Repulsine Rebuilds: A Physical Reconstruction With Torque Numbers

**Domain:** vortex / implosion technology (Schauberger lineage)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-02 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

**Viktor Schauberger**'s **Repulsine** — the 1940s implosion turbine the tradition holds produced **both lift and energy** from high-speed inward (vortical) motion, by retaining and redirecting motion rather than expelling mass. The specific claims this rebuild is aimed at:

- that a **1941 precursor** was built and patented, and that the later **"Mauthausen" model (1944–45)** was already a *successor* — so the model said to have shown levitation is the **earlier** one;
- that the tradition's levitation reports attach to a **1942 test at the Kertl works in Vienna** (relayed by **Aloys Kokaly**, who supplied parts) — a test the Repulsine's own literature says was run **without Schauberger's presence or permission**;
- that the machines' corrugated "wave membranes" could be run at **over 12,000 rpm**;
- and that **catalysts** (substances Schauberger names only rarely) are an essential part of the "biomachines."

This is the vortex/implosion rail's second validation. Its first — `2026-09-28-vortex-schauberger-engine-cfd-test-negative.md` — was a **computational and model-boat** test. **This one is the physical reconstruction the CFD test could not substitute for**, and it answers the archive's own standing scout request for the results of the Implosion e.V. group's work.

## Who built and measured it, when

**A small working group of *Implosion e.V.*** (implosion-ev.de) — the German Schauberger-lineage association. The page is signed only "Super User" and is **undated**.

**Dating is the weak point, and it is stated plainly:** the page says the group has worked on a faithful rebuild "for some years" (*seit einigen Jahren*), references the Implosionshefte (no. 132 ff.) and the **PKS archive**, and states that the build of the 1941 Kämpfer/Berlin prototype is *currently* under way. **The page itself carries no dates, no measurement tables, and no named experimenters.** This record is therefore dated by fetch (2026-10-02) and marked accordingly.

Related reconstruction work (separate actor, also 2026): **Denny Okudinani** presented a CAD-and-3D-printed Repulsine reconstruction at **APEC on 2026-06-06**, studying slot and "gill" counts from historical photographs — explicitly reporting **no** measured thrust, power, torque, pressure, airflow or charge from a functioning build. Two independent parties are now moving the Repulsine from legend to hardware in the same year.

## Method, as reported — five models

The page describes five successive builds, and the method details are unusually specific, which is why the record is worth keeping:

1. **Model 1 — original tooling.** Copper **corrugated discs** (*Wellenscheiben*) made using **original steel dies recovered from the PKS archive**. The slots of the turbine blade wheel and the **gill slots in the wave membranes were reproduced from a gypsum cast of the original model**, which "fits the steel dies form-closed" and is offered as proof that the dies are still the original tooling. Purpose: test **material strength**. Torque–speed (Drehmoment-Drehzahl) characteristic curves were measured, showing a **direction-dependent effect** of the tangentially outflowing air — the curve differs clockwise vs. counter-clockwise (viewed from above).
2. **Model 2 — snail top.** At much lower speeds, a **snail-shaped top piece** — an original part still viewable in the Schauberger archive, which seats precisely into grooves 2 and 3 of the wave membrane — was tested; a **wave-shaped ring** was added around the Repulsine as a surface for the air stream to react against.
3. **Model 3 — catalysts.** Catalysts (**quartz flour, aluminium powder, silver and gold dust**, bound with water glass) were applied to a modified original model, which was then run inside a **closed aluminium housing**. The **torque curve showed a pronounced discontinuity — possibly a resonance point** — which the group flags for further investigation.
4. **Model 4 — oscillating membrane.** A **vibratingly suspended membrane** with **silica-gel inlet**, per Schauberger's remarks about oscillating membranes and cell-like functions.
5. **Model 5 — the 1941 precursor.** On the chronological reading that the Mauthausen Repulsine was a *successor*, and that only the precursor can have shown the Kokaly-relayed levitation effects, the group is now building from a **1941 sketch** (cooperation with Kämpfer, Berlin).

## Result, as reported

- **The material limit is the one hard number.** The corrugated membrane **cannot be run above ~7,000 rpm** without deforming and stretching — against original literature passages citing **over 12,000 rpm**. So a faithful rebuild of the *membrane* as specified **does not survive its own claimed operating regime.** This is a concrete, reproducible finding: it is a statement about the material and the geometry, not about vortices.
- **A direction-dependent torque effect** of the tangential outflow was measured (models 1 and, in curve form, 3).
- **A torque-curve discontinuity at one operating point** (model 3, closed housing) — described only as "possibly a resonance point."
- **No levitation is reported. No over-unity, no excess rotation, no power balance is reported.** The page's own forward look is "further experiments with various catalysts and their placement."
- The work is **explicitly ongoing**, not concluded.

## Confidence: medium that this measurement work was done and is reported this way; low that it bears on either headline claim

**For it.**

1. **It is a builder's own page, with photographs and named real objects** — the steel dies, the gypsum cast, the original snail part in the Schauberger archive, the Implosionshefte issues. These are checkable references, not assertions.
2. **The one quantified result is uncomfortable for the claim, not flattering to it.** A group sympathetic to Schauberger reports that the rebuild **fails its own design speed**. Self-reported negatives from a friendly party are worth more than positives from one.
3. **Direction-dependent torque is a real, repeatable kind of measurement** and the kind of thing that should be measured rather than argued about.
4. **It is a physical rig, not a simulation.** The CFD entry reached "unlikely" by modelling; this group got to a spinning object with a torque curve.

**Against it.**

1. **Undated, unnamed, unpublished.** No date, no investigators, no journal, no data table, no error bars. The curves exist only as page images the reader cannot read for numbers.
2. **No thrust or lift data at all.** The levitation claim — the load-bearing one — is **neither confirmed nor tested**: there is no statement of the speed at which lift was expected, and no vertical-force measurement. The record cannot say the levitation claim failed, only that it was not measured.
3. **~7,000 rpm was a material failure of their *reproduction*, not necessarily of the original.** A different copper, thickness or forming method may hold higher speed. The honest reading is "a faithful rebuild hit a material wall," not "Schauberger's claimed speed is impossible."
4. **The discontinuity is an observation in search of an explanation, and the group says so.** "Possibly a resonance point" is a hypothesis, not a finding, and it should not be read as a free-energy signature. Vibrational resonances in rotating machinery are ordinary.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted and partly measured:** a Schauberger-lineage association rebuilt the Repulsine five times, partly from original tooling, and published what amounts to a **negative with a number** — the wave membrane breaks its own claimed speed limit, and there is no lift or energy result behind it.

The value of this record is exactly the thing the Yard exists to teach: **the difference between a reconstruction and a replication.** Implosion e.V. have done the *reconstruction* honestly and well enough that their one hard measurement is a wall; what they have not done — and what neither the CFD entry nor Okudinani's printed model has done either — is **instrument the claim itself**. There is still no published rpm, input power, thrust, or static-pressure measurement from *any* Repulsine build, ours or theirs. That absence, now documented across three independent parties, is the finding.

**What would change this record:** a torque–rpm and vertical-force measurement from an actual run — even a null one — with the membrane material and forming method stated, and the speed at which levitation is predicted named in advance. A rebuild that reaches the literature's >12,000 rpm and measures no lift would be a stronger negative than this one; a rebuild that reaches it and measures lift the instruments can see would be a different kind of record entirely. Neither has been published.
