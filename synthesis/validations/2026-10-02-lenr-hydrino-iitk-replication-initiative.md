# Validation — An Independent Academic Attempt to Replicate the Hydrino Claim (IIT Kanpur, 2026)

**Domain:** LENR / water — the hydrino (fractional-quantum-number hydrogen) branch of the excess-heat claim
**Recorded:** 2026-10-02 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The claim under test is **Randell Mills' "hydrino" hypothesis** and the experimental programme built on it by **Brilliant Light Power (BLP)**.

- **The physics claim.** Hydrogen can exist in states **H(1/p)** in which the electron occupies a *fractional* quantum number, with a correspondingly reduced electron–nucleus distance, and in which the state energy is `En = −13.598/n²` eV with `n = 1/p`. Transition into a hydrino state is **non-radiative**: it requires a quantised amount of energy to be handed to a **catalyst** (nascent water, HOH, is the catalyst BLP names). The energy released per event is claimed to be enormous relative to chemistry — **204 eV**, set against the **1.48 eV** needed to produce H from water electrolysis.
- **The experimental claim.** Cells comprising a Ni anode, NiO cathode, a LiOH–LiBr eutectic electrolyte, and an MgO matrix were said to produce **net electrical output over the electrolysis input**, "typically multiples of the electrical input," in cases exceeding the input **by a factor greater than 10**. Solid-fuel calorimetry on the same catalyst was reported as releasing more than 10× the maximum possible conventional reaction energy. Molecular hydrino H₂(1/4) was claimed to be identified as a product by MAS ¹H NMR, ToF-SIMS, ESI-ToFMS, electron-beam excitation emission spectroscopy, Raman, photoluminescence, FTIR and XPS. BLP's commercial line (the "SunCell") is presented as a >100 kW optical power source, at a claimed cost of ~$20/kW, "validated by academic experts."
- **The light-water leg specifically.** BLP's own publications page cites **NASA Technical Memorandum 107167** (Niedra, Myers & Fralick, 1996), *"Replication of the Apparent Excess Heat Effect in a Light Water–Potassium Carbonate Nickel Electrolytic Cell"* — i.e. the claim that light-water/Ni-cathode cells, not just the Fleischmann–Pons heavy-water/Pd cells, show heat excess.
- **Status of the claim.** Hydrino electronic states are **not consistent with quantum mechanics**; BLP's own abstract concedes the point and rests instead on "non-radiation conditions of classical electromagnetic theory" (citing Goedecke, *Phys. Rev.* 135, B281, 1964). The claim has been commercially asserted since the 1990s and has never been independently reproduced under an open protocol.

## Who is testing it, when

**Raj Ganesh S. Pala** — Professor, Department of Chemical Engineering; associate faculty of the Materials Science Programme and the **Nuclear Engineering and Technology Programme**, **Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur, India** — and **Nandita Sharma**.

The published vehicle is a **presentation abstract** for the **ECS Meeting**, session *F01: Advances in Industrial Electrochemistry and Electrochemical Engineering: In Honor of Mark Orazem*: **ECS Meeting Abstracts MA2026-01, 1445** (published **7 July 2026**), doi **10.1149/MA2026-01281445mtgabs**. Pala's institutional record is that of a mainstream electrochemical engineer (electrocatalysis, photoelectrochemical water splitting, DFT of oxide surfaces; PhD Physical Chemistry, Utah 2005; postdoc UCSB), not a fringe author — which is what makes this a real development rather than a press release. He also gave a hydrino talk at **ICCF-26** (Japan, May 2025).

The abstract is candid about the sequence of reasoning that led here, and the candour is worth quoting because it is a proponent's reasoning, not a neutral one: Pala states that as a person "trained in computational quantum chemistry," his "first tests towards the validity of GUTCP" were to compare **Millsian**, Mills' own molecular-modelling suite, against "state-of-the-art density functional theoretical calculations, which I am very familiar with" — and that **"Millsian proved to be very accurate and astoundingly fast."** From that he concludes GUTCP is **computationally** accurate, "which led naturally to the deeper question: Is it also ontologically correct?"

## Method, as reported

Two legs, stated in the abstract:

1. **Computational (done, reported as favourable).** Millsian 2.0 (which *encodes* GUTCP) was compared against plane-wave DFT via **VASP** and against experimental results.
2. **Experimental (in progress, numbers not yet published).** The group is attempting to **replicate the experiments that claim to synthesise and characterise hydrino compounds**. The abstract distinguishes two sub-families of the claim: some experiments use **aqueous electrolysis**, others use **molten-salt electrolysis** — a distinction that matters, because molten-salt cells are the harder-to-contest version of the claim and the harder to build.

## Result, as reported

**There is no experimental result in the published abstract, and this record does not invent one.** The abstract's own phrasing is: *"In our presentation, we will discuss the results from our experimental replication initiatives."* The only reported finding is the computational one — that Millsian compared favourably with VASP in the authors' hands ("extraordinarily computationally efficient and accurate").

So the honest content of this entry is: **an independent, credentialled laboratory is putting the hydrino claim to experimental test, and the test is not finished.** That is the same shape as the Yard's INRS dowsing entry — record that the test happened, not a verdict that has not landed.

## Confidence: low that anything is settled — medium-high that the test is genuinely being run

**For it being a real test.**

1. **Named, verifiable, non-fringe institution and author.** IIT Kanpur is a national institute; Pala's publication record and group page are mainstream electrochemistry. This is not a company announcing its own product.
2. **A published, citable abstract** in a mainstream electrochemical-society meeting, with a DOI, naming the specific experiments to be replicated.
3. **The claim is stated honestly, including its fatal tension with quantum mechanics.** The abstract does not hide that hydrino states "are not consistent with quantum mechanics."

**Against reading anything into it.**

1. **No experimental data.** The abstract promises results, and promises are not measurements.
2. **The computational leg cannot support the physical claim, and should not be read as if it could.** That a modelling suite implementing GUTCP is fast, and reproduces some structures accurately, is a statement about **software**, not about whether fractional-quantum-number hydrogen exists. A fitted or empirically parameterised model can be accurate and a physical theory can still be false. The abstract itself concedes this line of reasoning is only "confidence-building."
3. **The authors' starting posture is sympathetic, not adversarial.** They came to the question already "convinced that GUTCP is computationally accurate." That is not disqualifying — but a replication by convinced researchers carries less weight against the claim than one by a neutral group, and the Yard should say so rather than pretend the difference away.
4. **Independent replication of the *effect* requires independent instrumentation on an independent apparatus.** The Yard's standing rule for LENR applies here: a heat signal is one observable, not two (companion Dossier 021). A positive must arrive either as checklist-gated calorimetry with the input-power metrology right, or as a nuclear product signal — not as a match between two software packages.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted / announced, with no measurement yet reported:** a mainstream electrochemical engineering group has taken the hydrino claim seriously enough to try to build the compounds, and its first public step was a software comparison rather than a calorimeter.

The teaching artefact here is the gap between the two legs. **"Our model matches the other model" is not evidence that the phenomenon is real,** and the archive should be able to say that plainly even while welcoming the attempt — the whole reason this record exists is that independent replication is the right response to a claim like this, and the right response is *not* to let the computational leg stand in for the experimental one. The load-bearing question the group has set itself is the correct one: synthesise and characterise the compound, or do not.

**What would change this record:** a published calorimetric or spectroscopic result from IIT Kanpur on the light-water/Ni or molten-salt cells — with the input-power metrology (a stated weak point across this whole field) made explicit, and either a nuclear product signature or an unambiguous characterisation of the putative hydrino. A clean **null** would be just as valuable and should be filed the day it appears; if the group finds nothing, the Yard wants that result as much as a positive.
