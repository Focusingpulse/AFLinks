# Validation — Biofield Therapy Under Double-Blind Conditions, Measured by Joint Human–Mouse EEG

**Domain:** orgone / biofield / bio-photonics — practitioner-mediated "energy healing," instrumented
**Recorded:** 2026-09-30 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The specific claim tested is **the Bengston Energy Healing Method**, and the specific lineage behind it is unusually concrete for this domain. Bengston's method centers on **"Image Cycling"** — the practitioner rapidly cycles a series of mental images of personal desires in a fixed mechanical sequence, with no belief content attached. Bengston and co-authors have reported since 2000 that this practice produced **anomalous remission in mice carrying transplanted mammary adenocarcinoma**, and the method has been offered publicly as a cancer therapy for humans on the strength of those animal results.

The general claim underneath it — the one every biofield modality shares — is that a practitioner's intent (not touch, not heat, not contact) produces a measurable physiological effect in a distant living organism.

## Who replicated it, when

**Arnaud Delorme, A. Cusimano, M. Tran, P. Nguyen, D. Deng, Chris Fields, L. Velíšek, R. Wagner, P. Yang and Lorenzo Cohen.**

Human EEG and the human side of the protocol ran at **The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center** (IRB protocol 2020-1210); the mouse EEG was collected at **Baylor College of Medicine** under a service agreement led by **Dr. Jianrong Tang** (animal protocol AN-5585). Data collection window: **28 November – 3 December 2022**. Published **28 May 2026** in *IBRO Neuroscience Reports* **21**, open access.

The protocol was **pre-registered** on the Open Science Framework (`https://osf.io/n2gwj/`).

This is the pattern worth naming: an academic integrative-oncology group and an EEG methodologist took the most famous controlled animal claim in the biofield lane and put real instrumentation on it.

## Method

**Participants — and the first thing that matters.** Two people, and only two.

- The **BT therapist**: a 70-year-old male with **13+ years** in the Bengston method and 40+ years of other modalities, and prior experience performing healing interventions **with mice** — explicitly noted so the reader can see he was not a novice in an unfamiliar setting.
- The **sham therapist**: a 52-year-old male with **no experience in any biofield modality**, instructed on where to sit and how to move in order to mimic the BT practitioner, and deliberately given **no instruction on mental imagery**.

**Sessions.** **24 sessions total, 12 per participant, four per day over six days.** Half the sessions used mice with cancer, half used control mice. Each session captured two files: a **10-minute baseline with the mice absent** (human EEG only, unsynchronised), then a synchronised block of **3-minute pre-baseline + ~30 minutes of treatment + 10-minute post-baseline**. During the pre- and post-baseline the mice were present but the human **turned his back on them**.

**Recording.** Human **64-channel EEG**; mouse **3-channel EEG plus 1-channel EMG**, simultaneously.

**Design.** Human analysis was a **2 × 2** contrasting mouse type (cancer vs control) against session type (BT vs non-treatment), **N = 8 in each of the four cells**. Mouse analysis was a **2 × 2 × 2**, adding which human participant was present.

**Blinding.** The experimenters interacting with the participant, the participants themselves, and the data analysts were **blind to mouse type**. The treatment and rest phases alternated within a session, so each session contains its own within-subject contrast.

## Result

**The measure closest to the claim came back null.**

- **No changes in spectral power were detected in the mice.** The straightforward reading of "the practitioner influences the animal's physiology" — a shift in the animal's EEG power spectrum — was not observed.
- A **significant increase in theta-band (4–8 Hz) coherence** was found for baseline vs treatment (cluster statistic 130.83, p < 0.01). The authors read this as the method possibly influencing **large-scale neural coordination rather than localised activity**.
- **All pairs of electrodes were coherent in all frequency bands at p < 0.002 after correction for multiple comparisons.** That is a large, broad effect — and it is the finding that cuts both ways, because the paper immediately notes that **both the BT and the sham condition showed widespread alterations relative to baseline**, "indicating that some of the observed synchrony changes may reflect nonspecific factors such as the mere presence of a human, or shared sensory cues rather than effects unique to BT."
- A **therapist-side signature** was reported: robust, reproducible EEG changes **across all frequency bands** during treatment regardless of which mice were present, which the authors say was **not seen in the sham participant** — a repeatable physiological state associated with the act of intentional treatment.
- An **interaction** was observed in which treatment differentially affected **human–mouse brain-to-brain coherence for cancer versus control mice**.
- Cross-frequency counts (baseline vs treatment / cancer vs control / interaction), by band — theta 4–8 Hz: **130.83 / 5.13 ns / 1.48 ns**; alpha 8–12 Hz: **179.99 / 6.57 ns / 1.44 ns**; beta 18–22 Hz: **86.90 / 6.07 ns / 0.60 ns**; gamma 30–45 Hz: **65.33 / 6.07 ns / 0.79 ns**. **Every significant result in that table is the baseline-versus-treatment contrast. The cancer-versus-control contrast and the interaction are not significant on any band.**

**The cancer endpoint is not in this paper.** The Methods state plainly that outcomes from the collection of **blood, tumour tissue and brain tissue** "will be reported in a separate publication." So the claim Bengston is actually known for — that the method affects tumour burden in mice — is **still untested in the published record of this study**.

## Confidence: low — as evidence for the claim; medium — as a protocol

**For the record:** this is one of the more rigorous published attempts in the biofield lane. Pre-registered, double-blind to mouse type, sham therapist as an active control against the "someone was there" explanation, alternating treatment/rest phases within each session, and genuinely bilateral instrumentation (64-channel human, 3-channel mouse + EMG).

**Against taking the positives as evidence:**

1. **One practitioner per arm.** The only BT practitioner and the only sham are each single individuals. The authors say this directly: any measured change "may reflect idiosyncratic traits, techniques, or neural responses unique to that individual rather than general properties of BT." The "therapeutic intent signature" rests on **one man**. It cannot be separated from any other thing that differs between a 70-year-old 13-year practitioner and a 52-year-old instructed mimic.
2. **The significant results are baseline-contrasts, and the sham moved too.** The theta-coherence and cross-species findings are significant against baseline, not against the sham condition; the paper states that **both** conditions altered relative to baseline. The authors conclude they "cannot conclusively attribute this to a specific biofield mechanism."
3. **The primary physiological measure was null.** No spectral-power change in mice.
4. **The cancer outcome is unpublished**, so the claim the domain is excited about is not answered here.
5. **A statistical caveat the authors raise themselves:** the false-discovery-rate correction was **sensitive to the amount of significance present** — the widespread coherence between *both* participants and the mice in treatment-vs-baseline **shifted the threshold** for other comparisons, including mouse type and interaction. They note no established correction method exists for this, and call the result "an observation rather than a demonstrated fact."
6. **The paper's own summary disclaims the use:** these are exploratory physiological findings with "no clinical implications," not to be read as justification for cancer treatment or as a substitute for evidence-based care.
