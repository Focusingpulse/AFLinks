# Validation — Polarized-Electrode Water Conductivity: An Independent Replication (Stuttgart, 2013)

**Domain:** water structure / electric double layer (claimed non-electromagnetic field detection via polarized electrodes in water)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-08 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

The claim is not about water memory in the Benveniste sense. It is about a **detector**. In the Russian line of work associated with **A. V. Bobrov** and **Muzalewskaya & Bobrov (1988)**, two **deeply polarized electrodes** placed in a small container of water are held to be sensitive to a **"non-electromagnetic" (non-EM) emission** from objects of different origin. The physical argument given is that the **electric double layer (EDL)** at the electrode surface — specifically the diffuse Gouy–Chapman layer — responds to a **spatial polarization of water dipoles**, so a change in the water's dipole organisation changes the current that flows through the water–electrode system at constant voltage. The specific experiment replicated here (Bobrov 2002, 2006, 2009) is stronger still: a **generator of 128 yellow super-bright LEDs** was said to produce a measurable effect on the detectors **not only directly but also indirectly, through water that had been irradiated by it** — a claimed imprint carried in the water.

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a set of polarized-electrode water cells, shielded from EM, heat and vibration, respond measurably differently when an LED generator is switched on, or when the water itself has been irradiated — and does it stay quiet under tap water and controls?

## Who tested it, when, and where

**Serge Kernbach**, Institute of Parallel and Distributed Systems, **University of Stuttgart**, Germany. Published in the *Journal of Scientific Exploration* **27**(1):73–109, 2013 (submitted 2013-03-12, accepted 2013-11-23). Kernbach came to it from a different field entirely — he was building **underwater electric-field communication** for small robotic devices (inspired by weakly electric fish), where low-frequency fields create exactly the deeply polarized electrodes in question. He reports that his own measurements were "highly reproducible for relative values within one experiment" but varied between experiments, with identified influences including **mechanical vibration and the emissions of blue-light LEDs** — which is what led him to Bobrov's work. **A. V. Bobrov is acknowledged in the paper for help designing the setup, running the experiments, and evaluating the results** — so this is an independent laboratory working *with*, not against, the original author.

## Method — what they actually did

- **Detectors:** polarized electrodes in a small water container; **several detectors placed inside a metal box**, shielded from EM fields and temperature changes. A constant voltage was applied and the **current through the water was recorded continuously** (~1–50 µA, resolution **0.01 µA**), about two hours before and after each intervention.
- **The "active" source:** an **LED generator of 128 yellow super-bright LEDs** (Bobrov's design).
- **The arms:** (a) an **active LED generator** present; (b) water **irradiated by the LED generator**; (c) **normal tap water**; (d) **control** runs (night hours, no objects nearby).
- **Series "A"** characterised the sensors' response to temperature, vibration and EM fields, to establish that those influences were excluded down to the sensitivity of the instruments. **Series "B"** (biological-origin non-EM fields) was run but excluded from the paper. **Series "C"** is the replication reported here.
- **Scoring:** a trial was scored positive if it showed a current change ≥ 0.01 µA; trials with an ambiguous response were coded T2/T3 and **excluded from the binary tests**; experiments where two or more sensors were simultaneously positive were counted positive. **Four datasets were excluded** where a sensor showed a significant change before or after the run, or the data were implausible due to environmental noise.
- **Statistics:** a chi-square goodness-of-fit test against the null of random data; a **Mann-Whitney U** test for "LED generator vs tap water" and "irradiated water vs tap water"; and a one-sample t-test of trial currents against an expected value of 0.

## Result — what was measured

**The replication was judged positive.**

- From **154 evaluated trials** in series C with the LED generator and irradiated water (**32 individual experiments recorded in parallel by several sensors**): **108 trials positive, 35 negative**.
- **Chi-square and Mann-Whitney U both rejected the null** at significance **α ≤ 0.005** (two-tailed). The **one-sample t-test rejected the null for the LED and irradiated-water arms** (α = 0.005, two-tailed) **but could not reject it for the tap water** (α = 0.240).
- **Between two and six sensors recorded a reaction simultaneously** in a given run — the paper's own emphasis, because a single drifting sensor is noise and several agreeing is not.
- **The LED generator and the irradiated water produced a similar impact** on the detectors; **tap water and the resting state produced mostly monotonic drift.** The author notes **"almost no reactions from non-irradiated water,"** and that **a longer exposure time produced a stronger reaction.**
- **No EM field or vibration change above the sensors' resolution** was observed during the runs.
- **The author's conclusion:** "Based on these results, we evaluate the main part of the replication attempt as successful."

## Confidence: medium

- **What is strong here:** an independent university laboratory, a **control arm that stayed quiet while the active arms did not**, a **pre-specified scoring rule**, a large trial count (154), **several sensors agreeing within a run**, and **the original author's collaboration** (which cuts both ways — it improves the setup's fidelity, and it means the replication is not blind to the original hypothesis).
- **What limits it, in the author's own words, and these are real:**
  - The measured currents are tiny (**1–50 µA**), so the setup is sensitive to cable resistance, amplifier input impedance, component temperature drift and electronic noise. The author states it is **"intended to detect changes," not for precise measurement** of ionic processes in the EDL.
  - The shielding is **"only up to the level allowed in a normal electronic laboratory"**; a stronger claim would need a specialised laboratory.
  - **No physico-chemical properties of the water** were measured before or after — so the *mechanism* (dipole polarisation, spin imprint, or something else) is untouched.
  - **The effect of sensor and source geometry was not fully explored**, and the author assumes it matters.
- **The interpretation is explicitly unresolved.** Kernbach writes that he **cannot say** whether the irradiated-water reaction is a **spin-based imprinting of water** as Bobrov proposed, and that it is **unclear whether the few responses from non-irradiated water were environmental noise or something else.**
- **It is not a home-trivially reproducible result** as published — 0.01 µA resolution and a shielded enclosure are laboratory requirements. But it is the kind of result that a patient home lab could *approach* with better-than-hobby instrumentation, which is why it is worth filing.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted / measured / reported:** a university laboratory in Stuttgart rebuilt a Russian subtle-energy detector claim from the ground up — polarized electrodes in water, shielded, with an active LED arm, an irradiated-water arm, and a tap-water control — and reported **108 positive trials out of 154 evaluated**, with the null rejected by chi-square, Mann-Whitney and (for the active arms only) a t-test, while the tap-water arm stayed statistically quiet. The author judged the replication successful and named its limits himself: currents of microamps, ordinary-laboratory shielding, no water chemistry measured, geometry unexplored, and the mechanism unresolved. Read it as a **rare positive independent replication in the water domain** — not proof of a non-EM field, but evidence that a specific, pre-specified detector response *did* reproduce outside the originating group, with a control that behaved as a control should. It is the kind of row the Yard exists to file: a claim someone actually tried, with the numbers shown and the caveats left in.
