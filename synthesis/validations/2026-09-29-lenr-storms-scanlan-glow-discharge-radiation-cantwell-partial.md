# Validation — Partial Replication of Storms/Scanlan Glow Discharge Radiation (Cantwell & McConnell, 2008)

**Domain:** LENR / glow-discharge radiation (not excess heat)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-29 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

E. K. Storms and B. Scanlan, *Radiation produced by glow discharge in deuterium* (8th International Workshop on Anomalies in Hydrogen/Deuterium Loaded Metals, Catania, Sicily, 2007), reported **two kinds of radiation** from a deuterium glow discharge: one thought to be **monoenergetic electrons in the 0.8 MeV range**, and a **second emission obtained when oxygen was added to the D2**.

## Who replicated it, when

**Rick Cantwell and Matt McConnell, Coolescence, LLC — March 2008.**

Independence: a different organisation and a different set of authors. Ed Storms is acknowledged for suggestions and extended discussion and Scott Little read an early draft, so there was informal contact with the original claimant — but **no shared authorship and no shared institution**. Treat as substantially independent on personnel, with contact declared.

## Method

**Discharge rig.** A 1.8 litre chamber evacuated by a turbomolecular pump, with gas pressure read on Baratron gauges. Anode: a 1 mm Pt rod with all but the last 2–3 mm glass-encased. The cathode is driven negative by a DC supply capable of **2000 V at 100 mA**; chamber and anode grounded; **no provision for cooling the cathode or the chamber**.

**Cathodes.** Copper, and palladium made by diffusion-welding a 100 µm Pd foil to a copper substrate.

**Detection — four independent channels.**

- Two Geiger–Müller tubes, **LND 712 (2 mg/cm² window)** and **LND 71221 (10 mg/cm² window)**.
- A silicon diode detector, 25 mm² × 1.5 mm (Ortec BA-016-025-1500), shielded from visible light by 1.2 µm Al foil and read by a multichannel analyser; its background noise limited the minimum detectable energy to about 50 keV.
- **CR-39** nuclear track detector, placed inside the chamber, later etched and microscoped for tracks.
- A **magnetic deflector**: a toroidal core cut to form a 0.4 in gap and wound with 100 turns, giving a measured 100 gauss per amp (up to 3 A), with a half-collimator so off-axis particles cannot scatter into the GM tube.
- Absorbers on a **rotary feedthrough** so transmission could be measured without breaking vacuum: 1 µm Mylar (0.14 mg/cm²), 1.2 µm Al (0.3 mg/cm²), 1.8 µm Al (0.5 mg/cm²), plastic (100 mg/cm²), CR-39 (100 mg/cm²).
- Count timing against the discharge waveform recorded on a digital storage oscilloscope.

**Runs.** D2+O2 glow: equal parts of deuterium and oxygen reacted with a glow lasting a few minutes, chamber pressure typically 1.5–3 Torr, **25 % duty cycle (typically 4 s on, 12 s off)** to hold the cathode below 60 °C, average glow power 2–10 W, glow voltage varied 500–1200 V. Comparison gases: H2+O2, He+D2, and single gases O2, D2, H2, He and N2 at 2 Torr. The deflector was calibrated against a **¹⁴C beta source (156 keV)** in vacuum and in 2 Torr O2 with magnet current varied 0.1–3 A.

## Result

- In **oxygen-containing** plasmas, counts from the 2 mg/cm² GM tube appear once glow voltage exceeds about **600 V**; in D2, H2, He and N2 they only start at **900–1000 V**. Count rates rise non-linearly with voltage.
- Counts are **stopped by a 100 mg/cm² plastic absorber** and are **not seen at all** with the 10 mg/cm² GM tube.
- For D2+O2 below 900 V: reduced **66 %** by 1 µm Mylar, **75 %** by 1.2 µm Al, **95 %** by 1.8 µm Al; stopped at a total areal density of **2.5–3.0 mg/cm²**.
- **Identical behaviour with copper or palladium cathodes, and with H2+O2 as with D2+O2.** The counts do not depend on deuterium or on palladium.
- **CR-39: no radiation above background. Silicon detector: no counts above background.** Both rule out heavy energetic particles (alphas, protons).
- **The electromagnet had no effect** on GM counts from a D2+O2 glow on a Pd cathode, or from an O2 glow on a Pd cathode — while the same field did deflect the ¹⁴C betas (50 % reduction at 1 A, 80 % at 2 A). This rules out electrons.
- Counts occur **only during the "on" periods** of the glow pulse, not above background during the "off" periods.
- Absorber transmission through 1.8 µm Al or 1 µm Mylar **increases with glow voltage above 900 V** and is **constant below 900 V** in oxygen-containing plasma.

**The authors' conclusion.** They are satisfied the counts are not electrical noise. They then attribute them conventionally: oxygen **K-alpha x-rays at 525 eV** below ~900 V (nitrogen's K-alpha at 392 eV is too soft to clear the 2 mg/cm² window, which is why N2 does not show the low-voltage counts), and **Bremsstrahlung** whose energy rises with glow voltage above 900 V. An order-of-magnitude estimate — 0.1 % x-ray generation efficiency, 0.1 % of electrons energetic enough, window attenuation and collection efficiency included — predicted roughly **100 counts/min/mA** against the **~50 counts/min/mA** they observe at a 700 V glow. Their verdict, in their words: *"If any anomalous radiation was produced, it was below the sensitivity threshold of our detection systems."* They name the open problem for anyone continuing: separating the numerous low-energy photons of the glow from any anomalous radiation.

## Confidence: medium-high

This is an unusually complete record — named replicators, dated, full apparatus, four orthogonal detector channels, a calibrated deflector, a declared gas-composition control matrix, and count-timing against the drive waveform. Its verdict is also the reason it belongs in the Yard:

- The **observation replicates** — radiation with similar absorptive characteristics to Storms/Scanlan's second emission is produced, repeatably, and it is not noise.
- The **anomaly does not** — the effect is indifferent to deuterium and to palladium, is not deflected by a magnetic field, produces no tracks and no silicon counts, and is quantitatively consistent with ordinary oxygen K-line and Bremsstrahlung x-rays.
- The original claim's headline feature, **monoenergetic ~0.8 MeV electrons, is not reproduced**; the deflector test is a direct negative.

Limits: a partial replication by design (one report, one apparatus, ~11 pages); the count-rate estimate rests on assumed efficiencies rather than calibration against a known photon source; and the paper is a 2008 workshop-style report, not a journal article.
