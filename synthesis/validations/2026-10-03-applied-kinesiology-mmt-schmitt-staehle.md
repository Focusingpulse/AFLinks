# Validation — Applied Kinesiology Muscle Testing: The Blinded Retest Does Not Reproduce the Open-Label Concordance

**Domain:** biofield / manual muscle testing (applied kinesiology) — diagnostic claim
**Recorded:** 2026-10-03 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

The applied-kinesiology tradition's core diagnostic claim: **manual muscle testing is a valid measurement channel** — that a limb held against the tester's hand **weakens** when the subject is exposed to a substance to which they are sensitive (allergen, food, toxin), and holds firm otherwise. On the strong form, one practitioner's hand-reading recovers information the subject's own physiology is not conscious of, and does so reliably enough to substitute for laboratory testing.

This is the **operator-channel claim in its diagnostic form**: the instrument is a trained person's proprioception, and the read-out is a binary strong/weak muscle response.

## Who tested it, when, and how

Three records, one question, in ascending order of control:

1. **Schmitt (1998) — open-label, high concordance.** A study correlating applied-kinesiology muscle-test findings with **serum immunoglobulin levels for food allergies** reported **~90.5% concordance**. But it was **open-label**, and the corpus's QC notes it carried **"KA-mediated subject selection."** The tester's knowledge of the sample was not controlled — i.e. the measurement channel and the expectation were the same person.
2. **Staehle (2005) — double-blind test-retest, below chance.** A **double-blind test-retest** of muscle testing returned **35% — below chance** — and discarded **28% of the sample pre-test through an adaptive exclusion** step. Two things then have to be read together: the raw hit rate fell *below* the 50% a coin would give, and the protocol's own exclusion rule removed more than a quarter of the participants before scoring.
3. **AQuAS / RedETS (2024) — a state health-technology assessment.** The Catalan agency for health quality and assessment ran a **9-database search** on applied-kinesiology allergy diagnosis and found **no systematic review or RCT of diagnostic accuracy** to build on. Its signed conclusion, per the corpus: **"no existe evidencia confiable"** — there is no reliable evidence. A companion 2024 Madrid **UETS** assessment of the adjacent *Seitai* technique reached a harder verdict on the underlying construct, concluding the "vital energy" it rests on "is not an anatomical, physiological or functional structure verifiable in a scientific setting."

## What happened

- The **blinded** attempt (Staehle 2005) **did not reproduce** the open-label concordance (Schmitt 1998). It did not merely fall short — it landed **below chance**, which is the signature of a channel carrying no signal plus a protocol whose exclusions removed the cases that did not fit.
- The **open-label** result is exactly what the operator-channel hypothesis predicts *without* the channel being real: the tester knows the sample, the test is the tester.
- The **state assessment** did not find a controlled diagnostic-accuracy study at all — which means the tradition's headline number has, for the relevant question, never been produced under blinding by anyone.

## What was measured vs. what was asserted

- **Measured:** an open-label concordance (~90.5%); a blinded below-chance retest (35%) with a 28% adaptive exclusion; a 9-database search returning no controlled diagnostic-accuracy evidence.
- **Asserted, not measured:** that muscle testing substitutes for laboratory allergy testing; that the 2005 exclusions were "invalid" rather than the protocol working as designed; that the 1998 concordance generalises beyond the open-label design.

## Confidence

- **Medium-high** that a double-blind test-retest of applied kinesiology returned ~35% with a ~28% adaptive pre-test exclusion, and that a 1998 open-label study reported ~90.5% concordance. These are two specific, named, dated studies the QC layer extracted from a state HTA.
- **Medium** that the AQuAS/RedETS 2024 HTA is as described (9-database search, no SR/RCT of diagnostic accuracy, "no existe evidencia confiable").
- **Low-medium** that this pair settles the general claim. Two studies are a rail's beginning, not its end. What they settle is narrower and firmer: **the high number came from an unblinded design; the blinded design produced the null.**

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted twice, with opposite results, and summarised by a state agency as having no reliable evidence.** The value of this record is the shape it shows, which the Yard keeps meeting: **the observation does not survive its own control.** An open-label practitioner test finds the thing; a blinded test of the same claim returns below chance; the exclusions do the rest of the work.

**What would change this record:** a **blinded manual-muscle-test reliability study with non-adaptive exclusions** — pre-register the sample, forbid the tester from dropping cases mid-run, blind the tester to the sample, fix the outcome in advance, and report the hit rate against chance. That is the QC layer's own named cheapest test, and it is a home-scale protocol (~$0, two people, a concealed key). Thirty years after the two studies above, it has not been published. **A clean positive under that design would reopen the claim; a null under it would close the operator-channel reading at its most favourable measurement site.**

**Do not blend these rows.** The 1998 open-label concordance and the 2005 blinded null are not two halves of an average. They are a design effect, and the design difference is the whole story.
