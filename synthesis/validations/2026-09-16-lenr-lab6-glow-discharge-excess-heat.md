# Validation — Proton–LaB6 Glow-Discharge Excess Heat

**Domain:** LENR / anomalous heat
**Recorded:** 2026-09-16 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The long-standing LENR claim that a metal–hydrogen system, driven out of equilibrium, can release more thermal energy than the electrical power put into it — the "anomalous heat effect" lineage that runs from Fleischmann–Pons (1989) through gas-loading and glow-discharge work. This record notes a *new* claim made in that lineage, not a reproduction of one specific prior result.

## Who replicated it, when

Chia-Yi Chen, Hung-Wei Liang, Chun-Yu Lin, Nai-Wei Liu, Hau-Kun Jhuang, Chih-Jui Hsieh, Ming-Cheng Jheng, Chien-Hsiu Ho, Rocan Hsing, Shang-En Wu, Lou-Chuang Lee, Hung-Hui Hsieh and Zhifei Li — Alpha Ring International Ltd. (Taipei, Taiwan; Monterey, CA, USA). Published in *Scientific Reports*, 17 July 2026.

## Method

A high-power proton–LaB6 glow discharge was run in low-pressure hydrogen. LaB6 serves as both a boron-containing and thermionically stable cathode material. Electrical input power and water-cooling thermal output were monitored continuously. The calorimeter was calibrated repeatedly against ISO/IEC 17025 standards. Control runs used tungsten cathodes under comparable calorimetric conditions. Used cathodes were examined by SEM-EDS.

## Result

Under higher-power discharge conditions, several runs showed integrated gain (thermal output ÷ electrical input) exceeding unity, reproducibly under those specific conditions. The effect was *not* observed in all discharge regimes and appeared more frequently near operating points where plasma instability also grew. Tungsten-cathode controls did not reproduce the elevated-gain phenomenon. SEM-EDS showed oxidation, erosion, redeposition and substantial local surface modification of the LaB6 cathodes. Thermochemical estimates based on cathode mass loss remained substantially below the observed excess-energy scale. The authors state the physical origin of the excess energy remains unresolved and present the work as calibrated experimental observations, not a demonstration of a mechanism. No nuclear signature (neutrons, gammas, transmutation products) is reported.

## Confidence: medium

Peer-reviewed and carefully instrumented, with an independent-material control (tungsten) and disclosed calibration practice. Limits: every author is an officer, employee or affiliate of the company that built and operates the system (competing interest disclosed in the paper); the effect is conditional and absent across many regimes; no nuclear product is reported; the mechanism is unidentified.
