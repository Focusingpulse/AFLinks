# Validation — Cress Biocrystallisation Differentiation: A Blinded Attempt That Came Back Inconclusive (GIRI 2026)

**Domain:** anthroposophical / Goethean experimental method (Kolisko–Pfeiffer lineage — cress seedling bioassay + copper-chloride "metabolomic fingerprint")
**Recorded:** 2026-10-09 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The **picture-forming** tradition (Lili Kolisko's rising pictures, Ehrenfried Pfeiffer's copper-chloride crystallisation) holds that a substance's *whole state* can be read as a self-organising crystalline pattern, and that the pattern is sensitive to things ordinary chemistry misses — growing conditions, preparation, and (in the strongest form) formative factors. The **usable** version tested here is narrower and sharper: that a **cress-seedling bioassay with a copper-chloride crystallisation "metabolomic fingerprint"** can reliably **differentiate** between potentised preparations that are chemically near-identical — specifically **Stannum metallicum 30x** (tin, 10⁻³⁰), **Silicea 30x** (silica), and **Lactose 30x** (the trituration control). This is the same lineage already in the Yard as the Hiscia copper-chloride pilot; the difference is that here the question is **discrimination between preparations**, not sensitivity to a known difference.

## Who replicated it, when, and where

**Anezka Marie Sokol** (Witten/Herdecke University), **Paul Doesburg** and **Claudia Scherr** (Cancer Research Society / the Hiscia-lineage crystallisation group), **Johanne Zeise** (University of Kassel), and **Stephan Baumgartner**. Published in the *International Journal of High Dilution Research* **27**(1), the **Proceedings of the 39th GIRI Meeting** (Warsaw, April 2026); issue published 2026-05-14. This is a **conference-proceedings contribution**, not a full paper — noted in the confidence below.

## Method — what they actually did

- **Preparations:** hand-succussed under sterile conditions from purchased **4x triturations** of each substance, producing **Stannum metallicum 30x**, **Silicea 30x**, and **Lactose 30x**.
- **Design:** **five experiments** with the preparations **plus five systematic negative control (SNC) experiments** run under identical conditions with **Water 1x**. Each experiment held **two repetitions of each potency**.
- **Bioassay:** cress (**Lepidium sativum**) seeds germinated in vitro for **96 h** in the preparations or controls.
- **Readout:** aqueous extract of the seedlings mixed with a thin copper(II) chloride dihydrate solution, evaporated to give **two-dimensional crystallisation patterns**; each experiment ran **6 groups of 7 crystal plates → n = 210 plates per condition** (SNC also n = 210).
- **Blinding and randomisation:** **fully randomised and blinded until data analysis**, both as to series (verum vs SNC) and as to individual treatments. Experiments were split between **two different researchers** (2 and 8 experiments respectively).
- **Statistics:** texture parameters of each image analysed by **2-way ANOVA**, followed by **LSD pairwise comparisons** where the preceding global F-test was significant (p < 0.05).

## Result — what was measured

- **Only two of the three pairwise comparisons** between the verum preparations showed a **significant differentiation** — i.e. the method did **not** cleanly separate all three.
- **The differentiation that did appear was researcher-dependent** — it tracked who ran the experiment, which is the signature of a procedural, not a substance, effect.
- **The systematic negative controls showed an unexplained, significant variance.** Because a stable method must give *nothing* on a null, this **renders the results inconclusive** until the source of that variance is found.
- **Authors' own verdict:** the SNC variance "renders the results inconclusive"; investigation into the sources of variance (e.g. cress germination anomalies) "is warranted before further conclusions can be drawn." They stress the **importance of SNC experiments** — the controls are what caught the problem.

## Confidence: medium (design) / low (result) — and it is a *null*

- **What is strong:** **blinded and randomised** design, **systematic negative controls**, a large per-condition n (**210 crystal plates**), **two researchers**, and **pre-specified statistics**. This is a better-controlled test than most of the tradition's output.
- **What limits it:** it is a **conference-proceedings abstract**, not a full peer-reviewed paper; the differentiation that survived was **researcher-dependent**; and the **SNC variance is unexplained** — the authors themselves will not draw a conclusion.
- **Context that matters:** this sits on top of a lineage that *did* reproduce once — Doesburg's earlier **15-experiment, two-laboratory** Stannum metallicum 30x vs water 30x replication reported p < 0.001 with SNCs holding flat. The 2026 study's inconclusiveness is therefore a caution about **method stability and differentiation**, not a refutation of the whole line. Read the pair together: the same method gave a clean positive on one question and an inconclusive answer on another.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted / measured / reported:** a blinded, randomised, systematically controlled attempt to get a cress-seedling biocrystallisation assay to tell three potentised preparations apart. It could not — only two of three comparisons separated, the separation followed the researcher, and the negative controls would not sit still. The authors call it **inconclusive** and name the reason themselves. Filed as a **first-class partial/null** in the anthroposophical-method domain: the method's own controls caught its instability, which is exactly what controls are for, and the honest headline is "not established here," not "shown." It is the counterpart to the Hiscia pilot already in the Yard — the same lineage answering a harder question and, this time, declining to answer it.
