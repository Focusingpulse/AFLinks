# Validation — Costérisant's 1937 Water-Dowsing Field Review

**Domain:** radiesthesia / dowsing (groundwater location)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-19 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

That a *sourcier* (water-dowser) holding a rod or pendulum can indicate where to dig for groundwater — and can indicate its **depth and yield** — reliably enough to guide the siting of a well.

## Who tested it, when

**Oscar Costérisant**, engineer, writing in the *Revue des sciences naturelles d'Auvergne*, vol. 3, fasc. 3–4, **1937**, pp. 145–147 (DOI 10.3406/rsna.1937.1644, Persée). The piece is an engineering review, not a polemic: the observations were gathered "in the course of the establishment of about 200 water-supply projects," studied especially in **Puy-de-Dôme and Haute-Loire**.

## Method

Two parts, both worth naming separately.

1. **Retrospective field review (n ≈ 200 projects).** Costérisant examined the water-supply projects he had been involved in and asked, case by case, whether a dowser's indication had been borne out by what the digging actually found.

2. **Prospective tracking test.** Following and marking the underground course of a **visible spring** is, he writes, "the ABC of the dowser's art." He posed that task to different dowsers at **Cizière (commune of Saint-Éble), Lorlanges, and Azérat**, then compared their marked paths against the water threads the workmen's picks actually uncovered.

## Result

**Reported: the number of observed successes is effectively zero.**

The reason is the finding that outlived the review. Costérisant refused to count indications given "very close to an apparent spring," because digging below a spring's emergence point will inevitably draw water through fissures in the rock — so a "success" there is produced by the digging, not by the detection. He also records that dowsers' **indicated depths and yields "have never been found accurate."**

In the prospective tracking test, all three sites were **negative**: the workmen's picks followed a different path from the one indicated. At Cizière, the dowsers' line was simply the straight-line extension of a real known segment; at Lorlanges and Azérat, they were following local tradition — which was itself wrong.

## Confidence: medium

- Strengths: an engineering-scale base (~200 projects), an author inside the profession with no stake in a verdict either way, and — unusually for 1937 — a **prospective** test with an observable ground truth (where the picks actually found water).
- Limits: it is a **retrospective review plus a small-n field test**, not a randomised trial; the counts of dowsers per site are not given; and the Persée scan of the article is **partially cut off** in the corpus record (the passage on Paulhac and Costecirgue trails into the page break). The article text was read as quoted excerpts in the corpus ledger, not in full.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Costérisant is the earliest engineering-scale null in this corpus's French radiesthesia rail, and its most durable contribution is not the verdict but the **confound he named in 1937**: a detection claim needs a *discrete* target, or "success" is manufactured by the digging itself. The 2026 INRS study says the same thing in its own abstract — in water-rich ground, "groundwater is often ubiquitous... This raises questions about what constitutes a genuine 'detection.'" Ninety years, one problem, and the same fix: bury something discrete and score it cell by cell.
