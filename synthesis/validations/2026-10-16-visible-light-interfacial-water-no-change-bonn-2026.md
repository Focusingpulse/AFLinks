# Validation — Visible Light Leaves Interfacial Water Unchanged (Max Planck Institute for Polymer Research, PNAS 2026)

**Domain:** water structure / interfacial (exclusion-zone) water
**Recorded:** 2026-10-16 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

Two claims are in play, and they share a single sentence — *light does something to water at a surface that heat alone does not.*

- **The mainstream version is the "photomolecular effect."** Tu, Zhou, Lin, Alshrah, Zhao & Chen (*PNAS* **120**(45):e2312751120, 2023) reported evaporation exceeding the thermal limit; Lv, Tu, Zhang & Chen (*PNAS* **121**(18):e2320844121, 2024) reported that **green light at the air–water interface cleaves "water clusters" off the surface**, read from UV-VIS-NIR and confocal Raman spectra. The mechanism was billed as nonthermal and nonresonant, with implications for climate modelling and solar desalination.
- **The archive-adjacent version is the structured-water claim.** The exclusion-zone (EZ) line holds that **radiant energy builds and orders interfacial water** — Henderson reported in 2021 that mid-IR and near-IR expand the EZ and an adjacent proton zone at Nafion. The 2020 Elton et al. review (*Int. J. Mol. Sci.* **21**(14):5041) explicitly named **"the growth of the EZ with laser irritation" as a Pollack-lab finding still awaiting independent replication.**

This record is that independent test — arriving from a molecular-spectroscopy institute rather than a water-structure lab.

## Who tested it, when, and where

**Yucong Chen, Joseph C. Shirley, Zi Xuan Ng, Yongkang Wang, Yuki Nagata, Arsh S. Hazrah and Mischa Bonn** — Department of Molecular Spectroscopy, **Max Planck Institute for Polymer Research, Mainz, Germany**. Published in ***PNAS***, **14 September 2026**, DOI **10.1073/pnas.2615377123**. The group shares no protocol and no personnel with the MIT originators of the photomolecular effect.

## Method — what they actually did

- **Macroscopic endpoint:** high-precision **confocal displacement** tracking of the water surface height → a direct **evaporation rate** measurement.
- **Molecular endpoint:** **surface-specific vibrational spectroscopy** (sum-frequency generation) reading the **OH-stretch** of interfacial water with **sub-nanometre (Ångström-scale) depth sensitivity** — resolving the hydrogen-bonded continuum, the free-OH peak, and molecular orientation in only the top few layers.
- **Continuous visible light:** **450, 532 and 635 nm** (blue, green, red), across **a wide range of humidities**.
- **Extreme-flux test:** **femtosecond visible and near-infrared pulses at 515, 800 and 1030 nm**, with **peak intensities raised by more than ten orders of magnitude** above the continuous case.

## Result — what was measured

- **Evaporation rate: no measurable change.** Continuous 450/532/635 nm illumination left the macroscopic evaporation rate unchanged across all humidities tested.
- **Interfacial structure: no detectable change.** The vibrational spectra showed **no modification of hydrogen bonding or molecular orientation** under identical conditions.
- **No response at 532 nm** — the wavelength of green light that produced the **strongest** response in the earlier studies.
- **Extreme femtosecond pulses: still nothing**, even at intensities >10¹⁰× the earlier work.
- **The authors' conclusion:** the neat air–water interface is **remarkably insensitive to visible irradiation**; any substantial light-enhanced evaporation is most consistent with **photothermal or geometric effects in complex, absorbing materials**, not a new photophysical pathway intrinsic to liquid water.
- **This is not a lone result.** It joins a prior independent critique, Ahmed & Lu (*PNAS* **122**:e2419957122, 2025, *"Absence of water clusters in visible light interactions with the air–water interface"*), which argued the earlier IR/Raman features are explained by **bulk liquid water plus residual gas-phase water passing the purge** — with no cluster signature at all.

## Confidence: high (that visible/NIR light does not order *pure* interfacial water, and that the photomolecular effect is not reproducible)

**Why high.** A peer-reviewed ***PNAS*** paper from an independent molecular-spectroscopy institute, with a **direct molecular probe** (not an inference from a spectral fit), a **macroscopic** independent endpoint (the evaporation rate), and an intensity range spanning **ten orders of magnitude**. It confirms and strengthens an **earlier independent critique**. Two independent groups now find the effect absent; the originators' own strongest wavelength (532 nm) is specifically negative here.

**What it does NOT say — the scope boundary, stated plainly.**
- It tests **neat, pure water at a flat air–water interface.** It does **not** test water at a **hydrophilic solid** (the Nafion interface where the EZ is *defined*), and it says nothing about **solutes**, **porous media**, or **hydrogels**.
- It tests **visible and near-IR**. It does **not** test **mid-IR at the water absorption bands (~3 μm)**, which is the band the EZ line actually uses to grow the zone.
- It therefore bounds **one sub-claim of the family** — *light orders pure interfacial water* — and **nothing more**. It is a ceiling on the photomolecular claim and on the "light alone restructures plain water" reading; it is **not** a verdict on the EZ measurement itself.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured:** an independent Max Planck group put a flat water surface under blue, green and red light, then under femtosecond pulses **ten billion times brighter**, and measured the evaporation with a precision height sensor and the surface water's hydrogen bonding with a sub-nanometre vibrational probe. **Nothing moved.** Not the evaporation rate, not the bonding, not at the wavelength the original authors called their strongest. Two independent groups have now failed to find the photomolecular effect, and one of them — this one — has the better instrument and the wider range. Filed **high-confidence as a dismantling of that specific claim.**

The honesty is in the boundary. This does **not** retire the exclusion zone; it retires the idea that **plain water at a plain surface rearranges under ordinary light.** The EZ lives at a *charged solid* interface and is grown with *mid-IR*, and neither was tested here — so the archive's core water claim is untouched by this result, and the "radiant energy structures water" intuition gets a hard, named limit rather than a verdict. Read with the Yard's Teschke and xylem records (the EZ *measured* by independent instruments), the water rail now says something precise: **interfacial water is real and independently measurable, and light is not the free explanation for it that the culture assumes.**
