# Validation — Pre-Sowing Magnetic-Field Priming of Anise Seed (ElDoliefy et al., 2026)

**Domain:** electro-culture / magneto-culture in agriculture (dry-seed exposure to a magnetic field before sowing)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-07 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

That exposing **dry seeds to a magnetic field before sowing** — a non-chemical "seed invigoration" — raises germination and early vigour, and that the effect is **frequency- and dose-dependent** rather than a simple "more field, more growth." The claim class is decades old and splits cleanly in this Yard: the **2026-09-28 record** sets the 2025 Belgrade cabbage field trial (~25% head-weight gain at 350 mT) against the **1975 Minnesota multi-crop field null** (five crops, four locations, nothing), and notes that most of the positive literature is **laboratory-only**. This is another laboratory positive — but one with a **mechanism readout**, which is what makes it worth a card.

## Who tested it, when, and where

**ElDoliefy, A. E.; Amer, A.; ElShahed, E. S.; Mohammed, H. S.; Shahin, A.; Kamel, F. M.; Sabry, M.; Yousef, M.; Essam, M.; Soliman, K.; Sobhy, M.; and Samir, B.** An **Egyptian** group; the certified anise seed (cv. "Baladi 1," a local landrace) came from the **Medicinal and Aromatic Plants Research Department, Horticultural Research Institute, Agricultural Research Center, Giza**.

Published in ***Scientific Reports*** **2026, 16(1): 19542**, 23 June 2026. DOI **10.1038/s41598-026-54832-2**.

**Lineage caveat, stated up front:** three of the authors (Mohammed, Shahin, Amer) published a **2024** paper, *"Frequency-Dependent Pre-Sowing Magneto-Priming of Anise Seeds Affecting Their Productivity"* (*Agricultural Research*, Springer). So this is a **follow-up by an overlapping group**, not an independent third-party replication of someone else's claim.

## Method — what they actually did

- **Seeds:** aniseed (*Pimpinella anisum* L.) cv. Baladi 1; **13 groups × 100 seeds, three replicates, complete randomized design**.
- **Field generation:** a **coil of ~1,000 turns of copper** (≈3 Ω) driven by a waveform generator and power amplifier; a **digital Gaussmeter** mapped homogeneity to **20 ± 0.5 Gauss (≈2 mT), spatial variation < ±2.5%**. (Note the scale: this is a **~2 mT** field, three orders of magnitude below the 350 mT static fields of the Belgrade cabbage trial — a different dose regime entirely.)
- **Treatment matrix:** **DC (0 Hz), 5 Hz, 10 Hz, and 15 Hz**, each at **30, 60 and 90 min**; untreated control of the same seed lot.
- **Endpoints:** germination percentage, speed and **vigour index** (VI = [mean root+shoot length] × G%) tracked weekly for **3 weeks**; then, at the 90-min dose, **enzyme assays** (α-amylase, protease, catalase) and **semi-quantitative RT-PCR** of the stress gene ***sod1*** and the cytoskeletal ***actin*** gene.

## Result — what was measured

- **Germination percentage rose up to ~25%** over control, and all treated arms reached ~100% by week 3 — so the *percentage* endpoint largely converges, and the **vigour index is the sensitive discriminator**.
- **Frequency optimum at 5–10 Hz.** VI peaked at ~**400** (5 Hz, 30 min) and ~**450** (10 Hz, 90 min) against a lower control; **5–10 Hz** is named the "biologically active" window. Longer exposures sometimes fell **below** control (a hormetic dose–response the authors invoke explicitly).
- **A frequency-specific enzyme signature at 90 min:** **catalase** rose most at **10 Hz** (≈28–33 vs ≈6.2 U/g FW control, a ~4.5× increase); **α-amylase and protease** were highest at **15 Hz**; amylase activity climbed **1.55 → 2.09** across treatments.
- **Gene level:** ***sod1* transcripts were down-regulated under every field treatment** (greatest at 5 Hz, 0.6-fold; 10 Hz, 0.5-fold), which the authors read as *lower* ROS and adjusted redox signalling rather than oxidative damage; ***actin*** fluctuated (up at DC and 15 Hz).
- **Authors' reading:** the magnetic field acts as a **mild abiotic stressor** that non-linearly tunes redox balance and reserve mobilization — H₂O₂ as a signalling molecule, catalase as the adaptive detoxifier — rather than a direct growth stimulus.

## Confidence: medium-low

- **Peer-reviewed in a mainstream journal** (*Sci Rep*), with a stated field homogeneity and a concrete dose matrix — better documented than most of the magneto-priming literature.
- **But it is a laboratory germination study, and the class it belongs to is the one this Yard already flagged as weak.** The 1975 Minnesota field null and the thin field evidence mean a lab germination/vigour gain is a long way from a yield claim. Note also that **final germination % converges to ~100% in all arms** — the effect lives in *speed and vigour*, not in whether seeds germinate.
- **Originating group with a 2024 precursor on the same crop and the same frequency hypothesis** — this is a within-programme extension, not an independent replication.
- **No blinding** is reported (the endpoints are physical — lengths and enzyme activities — which limits the bias risk, but the design is not blind and has n = 3 per cell).
- **The mechanism story is inferred** from expression/enzyme data at a single timepoint (90 min), and the *sod* change is a small fold-difference on a semi-quantitative RT-PCR; it is a plausible redox narrative, not a demonstrated causal chain.
- **Directional honesty:** the result is a **positive**, so it does not have the null's self-validating quality; a home replicator should treat the "5–10 Hz is the active window" claim as the thing to test, not to assume.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured:** an Egyptian group primed anise seed in a 2 mT field at DC and 5/10/15 Hz and found germination up ~25%, a vigour-index peak at 5–10 Hz, and a frequency-specific redox signature — catalase up ~4.5× at 10 Hz, hydrolytic enzymes up at 15 Hz, *sod* down throughout. It is filed **medium-low** for three stated reasons: it is a **laboratory germination** result in a class whose field record is a 1975 null and one thin 2026 positive; the group **published the same frequency hypothesis on the same crop in 2024**, so it extends rather than independently confirms; and the "active window" is exactly the kind of parameter claim that survives by never being tested outside its home rig. The useful move this card enables is the reverse of the usual one — not "does the field help?" (the lab says yes) but "does the 5–10 Hz window and its redox fingerprint survive a **blind, non-originating** reproduction on a different seed lot?" Until that runs, this is a mechanism-rich lab positive, not a yield claim.
