# Validation — Biodynamic Preparations 500/501 in an 18-Year Replicated Field Trial

**Domain:** anthroposophical / biodynamic experimental method (Steiner's preparations; the Kolisko lineage)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-28 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

That the **eight biodynamic preparations** Rudolf Steiner specified in his 1924 Agriculture Course — two field sprays (**500**, horn-manure; **501**, horn-silica) stirred into water in a prescribed rhythm, and six compost preparations (502–507) inserted into manure and compost — change how a crop grows and yields **beyond what their material nutrients can account for**. The doses are homeopathic in scale; the claim is that the effect is not nutritional.

## Who tested it, when

The **INBIODYN viticulture systems comparison** at **Geisenheim, Germany** (49° 59′ N, 7° 56′ E) — a long-term trial established in 1991, converted to the comparison systems in **2006**, and reported here across **2006–2023**. Published in *Agronomy for Sustainable Development* **46**, article 13 (2026) on **12 February 2026**, **open access, peer reviewed**. The design is a **complete block with four field replicates** per management system; each main plot is four rows of 32 vines with buffer rows. Crop: *Vitis vinifera* cv. **Riesling** on SO4 and Börner rootstocks.

## Method — what makes this the clean test of the preparations

Because the trial runs **three systems that share everything except the preparations**, it isolates them better than a biodynamic-vs-nothing comparison ever could:

- **Integrated** — Good Agricultural Practice; mineral N topped up to match the organic plots, biannual herbicide, chemical fungicides.
- **Organic** — Demeter-adjacent practices *without* the preparations; multi-species cover crop, mechanical weeding, copper/sulfur mildew control.
- **Biodynamic** — **identical soil and vine management to organic**, with the biodynamic preparations **added on top**: horn manure and horn silica applied several times a year (horn silica before full bloom, at veraison, and pre-harvest; horn manure around bud break and post-harvest), compost preparations 502–507 in the compost, cow-pat-pit in years without compost.

So the **organic-vs-biodynamic contrast is the preparation effect**, with soil, cover crop, weeding, rootstock and nitrogen supply held equal. Measured over 18 years: yield, pruning weight, Ravaz index, berry weight, juice pH and acidity, yeast-available nitrogen (NOPA), leaf macro/micronutrients, predawn water potential, and *Botrytis* incidence, analysed with linear mixed-effects models and change-point analysis.

## Result — as measured

- **Biodynamic ≈ organic.** "Generally, organic and biodynamic management **did not differ in most of the investigated parameters**, only for **phosphorus** in leaves significantly lower values for biodynamic management in comparison to organic were observed." Across 18 years, the biodynamic preparations produced **no detectable agronomic advantage over organic management**.
- **Both below integrated, on average.** Organic yields **−16.7%** and biodynamic **−14.3%** vs integrated; pruning weights **−16.4%** (org) and **−18.9%** (bd). Berry weight significantly higher under integrated. Ravaz index did **not** differ among systems (growth and yield fell in parallel). Total soluble solids showed **no significant differences**.
- **The gap narrowed with time.** Change-point analysis found a shift to higher relative yields for organic (2014) and biodynamic (2015) — i.e. **8–9 years after conversion** — with relative effect sizes improving from about −27% to −7% (bd) and −28% to −11% (org).
- **The one place biodynamic pulled ahead was climate.** In **hot, dry vintages** (group 1) both systems shifted to **positive** relative yields: organic **+2.3%**, biodynamic **+9.0%** vs integrated. In cool, wet vintages both were about −20%. The authors read this as **climate resilience**, not a preparation effect — the same shift appears (more weakly) for organic.
- **Nutrient status was adequate in all systems**; deficiency was not the cause of the yield gap. Leaf copper was ~295 ppm in the organic/biodynamic plots vs ~19 ppm in integrated — a residue of copper fungicides, not a preparation effect.

## Confidence: high that the preparations added nothing measurable over organic here

**Why high.**

- **18 years, four field replicates, complete block, peer reviewed, open access.** This is the strongest design form available for a perennial crop, and the trial's own claim is that it is "the only long-term study on perennial crops to systematically compare these systems."
- **The contrast is clean by construction.** Biodynamic and organic share soil, cover crop, weeding, nitrogen supply, rootstock and copper/sulfur regime. The delta is the preparations.
- **The result is a near-total null across a wide parameter set** — yield, vigour, juice quality, phenolics-adjacent measures, *Botrytis* — not the absence of a single pre-chosen endpoint.

**Limits to note.**

- **One site, one cultivar.** Riesling in Geisenheim is a temperate-oceanic site; the result is not a universal statement about all crops and climates.
- **The trial does not test 500/501 against nothing.** It tests them against organic practice. A preparation effect that is *substitutable* by good organic management would be invisible here — which is, in fact, the most parsimonious reading of the whole literature.
- **Vintage confound.** The authors themselves flag that growing degree days correlate strongly with time-after-conversion (p = 0.029, R² = 0.219), so part of the apparent "system maturation" is simply **warming**.
- **One preparation-adjacent signal survives:** leaf **phosphorus** was significantly lower under biodynamic. That is a difference, but in the direction of a **deficit**, not a benefit.

## The rail this sits on — long-term field trials keep returning null

| Year | Who | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **1996** | **Raupp & König**, Germany — 28 field/pot experiments plus a 9-year spring wheat trial | Preparations 500/501 *raised* yields at low yield levels and **lowered** them at high levels: sign reverses with yield level. |
| **2024** | **Dietrich, Dahn, Fritz, Berg, Köpke & Döring** — **27-year DOK trial**, Therwil, Switzerland | Biodynamic compost preparations had **no effect on yield or yield stability in any crop**, in 27 years — even though they measurably changed the soil biological community. |
| **2026** | **Spoerk Gabriel & Goldstein** — growth chamber, spring wheat (already in this Yard) | Preparations help where N is **not** limiting, slightly inhibit where it is — the reversal again, against a different variable. |
| **2023** | **Rienth et al.**, Mont-sur-Rolle, Switzerland — 5-year commercial vineyard, 500/501 vs organic control | Minimal differences between biodynamic and organic in vine performance and berry quality. |
| **2026** | **This record — INBIODYN, Geisenheim**, 18 years, four replicates | Biodynamic indistinguishable from organic across almost all parameters; the only significant difference is a **lower** leaf P. |

Read together, five independent long-term field trials across two countries and two crops say the same thing: **the preparations do not raise yields on average, and their only reliable appearance is as a modifier whose sign depends on conditions.** Geisenheim adds the longest and cleanest *field* comparison yet — and it adds the one genuinely new wrinkle: in **hot, dry years** the agroecological systems close the gap and can exceed integrated, with biodynamic highest of the three. Whether that is the preparations or simply the soil/cover-crop regime they share with organic is exactly what this trial's design cannot separate — and the authors do not claim otherwise.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded as **attempted / measured / reported**: eighteen years, four field replicates, and a clean design that puts organic and biodynamic side by side with **everything but the preparations held equal** — and the preparations do not show up. The system-level result is real (integrated out-yields both, then the gap closes after about nine years), and the climate result is real (both agroecological systems reach parity in hot, dry vintages), but neither is attributable to 500/501 on this evidence.

The value of the record is not that it settles biodynamics. It is that it **closes the most common loophole in the argument** — "the preparations work, it's just that the trials were badly run or too short." This one was neither. And it **sharpens the rail**: a home replicator should not ask "do the preparations work?" and read a mean. They should ask **"at how many fertility levels, and under how many climate conditions, does the sign flip?"** — and their control is **compost without preparations**, not nothing. The honest first result in this line is a reversal, and Geisenheim is the strongest field evidence yet that when you control for management, the reversal is the whole story.
