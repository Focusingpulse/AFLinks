# Validation — Bricage's 2025 AFSCET Double-Blind Dowsing Protocol

**Domain:** radiesthesia / dowsing (practitioner-built controlled test)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-26 (Replication Seeder, run 5)

## What was claimed

That the pendulum response is a real measurement — and, in the author's own methodological form, that the instrument is not the pendulum:

> "Ce n'est pas le pendule qui est l'instrument de mesure. L'instrument de mesure c'est l'ensemble individu-pendule."

("The pendulum is not the measuring instrument. The measuring instrument is the human–pendulum assembly.")

On that doctrine the operator must be **calibrated** before each series, questions must be yes/no and precisely worded, operator fatigue must be answered by stopping and recharging on a *planche de radiesthésie*, and the cascade of yes/no questions carries its own coherence checks.

## Who did it, when

**Pierre Bricage**, vice-president of the **AFSCET** (Association Française de Science des Systèmes Cybernétiques, Cognitifs et Techniques), presented *"Protocole double-aveugle radiesthésie — Testing Dowsing Practice: A Meta-Analysis Using Artificial Intelligences and an Experimental Testing Methodology"* at the **Journées de l'AFSCET, Andé, 16–18 May 2025**.

Two named practitioners ran the pendulum — the corpus records them as **RI** (experienced elder, volunteer register) and **RE** (gift-economy operator) — against a **simultaneous random-predictor arm (RH)**.

## Method, as recorded

- **62 independent tests** — **31 after-draw** and **31 before-draw**.
- The random-prediction arm ran **simultaneously**, not reconstructed afterwards.
- Practitioner-set conditions: individu–pendule calibration, yes/no question form, cascade with coherence checks, and a stop-and-recharge rule on fatigue.

## Result, as recorded — and as read here

| Condition | RI (experienced) | RE (gift-economy) | Random (RH) |
|---|---|---|---|
| AFTER-draw | 100% | 60–71% | 0–40% |
| BEFORE-draw | 33–43% | 20–40% | 20–33% |

The paper's recorded verdict is **"not-by-chance"**, **flagged** in the corpus for *après-condition fragility* and **cascade stopping-rule circularity**.

**This lane's reading of that table — not the paper's conclusion:** the two rows are not the same kind of test. An **after-draw** "prediction" is a reading of an event that has already happened; it does not discriminate a detector from a reader. The **before-draw** rows are the only prospective arm, and there RI (33–43%), RE (20–40%) and the random predictor (20–33%) **overlap**. The separation the verdict rests on is carried by the after-draw column. And the cascade rule lets the operator declare an answer incoherent and restart, which **discards failures by design** — the exact failure mode a pre-registered trial count exists to prevent (the Munich lesson of 2026-09-19).

**What this lane did NOT find:** cell counts. The corpus records percentages per condition and the 31/31 split; it does not record how many trials sat under each percentage. **No n is stated here, because none was read.**

## A second, separate finding: the meta-analysis layer is not a citation base

The same paper carries an **LLM-assisted meta-analysis of 328 papers**. The corpus's QC finding, recorded independently of the experiment: the reference list shows hallucination hallmarks — **duplicate titles under different authors, patterned page numbers, and journals that do not exist**. The pre-2020 classic anchors it cites (Sollas 1884 → Schmidt & Walach 2000) are real.

**Consequence, stated for the Yard's benefit:** the *experiment* is a primary source that can be argued with; the *meta-analysis* attached to it is not evidence about dowsing and must not be cited as though it were.

## What is established, and what is not

**Established (process):** a named designer holding an institutional office; a dated venue; two named practitioners plus a random arm; a written doctrine of operation; and — the operative fact — **a controlled test run by the tradition's own practitioners**, which is a rarer object in this rail than a skeptic's null. The French verification chain in the corpus runs Chevreul 1854 → Costérisant 1937 → Munich 1986–88 → Argenton 2007 → **Bricage 2025** → INRS EGU26, and this is the rung the tradition built itself.

**Not established:** above-chance detection. The prospective arm does not separate from its random predictor on the recorded ranges, and the stopping rule prevents the after-draw arm from being scored as a run.

## Confidence: low-to-medium

- **That this test existed, with this author, date and method:** **medium-high** — attested in two corpus syntheses drawn from the same Forge translation and dossier.
- **That dowsing was detected:** **no assessment.** The after-draw arm cannot support one, and the before-draw arm does not produce one.
- **Numbers:** **medium-low** — read through the corpus's own synthesis tables. The primary translation is **not carried by this repo's tree** (`translations/` was retired from the public repo on 2026-09-20, Berne Art. 8), so the paper itself was **cited, not read** in this run.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Bricage 2025 is the **third rung** of the French rail and the only one built by the tradition itself: a practitioner-designed double-blind rig, run by named operators, with a random arm seated beside them and the operator's own calibration doctrine written down and published. The archive's own synthesis reads it as *signal present, protocol issues flagged*, and this record keeps that ambiguity rather than resolving it: the row that separates the practitioners from the random predictor is the row that cannot distinguish detecting from reading, and the arm that could have carried the decision — before the draw — does not separate. Both sentences belong in the record, because the reason to keep a Yard is to show the difference between a demonstration and a test.
