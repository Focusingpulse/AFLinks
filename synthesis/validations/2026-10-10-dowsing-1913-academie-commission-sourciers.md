# Validation — The Académie des Sciences Commission des Sourciers (France, 1913–1921)

**Domain:** radiesthesia / dowsing (buried-target location — the state-science verification)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-10 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

The claim is the one the Yard already holds from four later independent settings: a person holding rods or a pendulum can locate what is underground — water, mineral, voids, buried structures — without instruments and without seeing the target. By 1913 the Académie des Sciences had already pronounced once on it: its 1853–54 inquiry concluded that the movement was an **unconscious muscular movement produced by the imagination** — the reading the Yard files today under Chevreul and the ideomotor effect. The 1913 question was narrower and better posed than "does dowsing work": *when a dowser states a depth and a target, does the ground bear him out?*

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a dowser's stated prediction about a hidden target — its existence, its position, its depth — survive being dug up?

## Who tested it, when, and where

The **Commission des Sourciers** of the **Académie des Sciences**, Paris. The 1913 phase was anchored by **Armand Viré** (1869–1951), director of the underground-biology laboratory at the Muséum, whose report was read to the Academy on **22 December 1913** by **Edmond Perrier**; the file opens earlier — a note by **Regnault** (*A propos des Sourciers*) was received and referred to the commission at the séance of **7 July 1913** (CRAS t.157, p.87), 5½ months before Viré's paper. The war interrupted the work; the commission **resumed in 1920–21** with new members including **Édouard Branly**, **Charles Richet** and **Perrier**, at first without public, and ran fresh field tests at **Meudon (May 1921)** and **Versailles (21 July 1921)**.

## Method — what they actually did

Four distinct designs, in ascending rigour:

1. **Dug verification against stated depth.** Operators predicted a target and a depth; the ground was then opened and the prediction checked against the find. Recorded rows include: **Luzech (Impernal)** — iron slag, arrowheads and bronze rings at **0.41 m**; an iron pin at **0.65 m**; a **Baume** nail at **0.45 m**; **Puy d'Issolud** burials at **1 m and 2 m** plus a scramasax skeleton; **Limogne** at **1.50 m** beneath the church wall. A printed **miss**: an **ossuary** predicted as such, which at **2.40 m** proved to be a pottery and animal-bone layer.
2. **Cross-validation by independent operators on separate days.** At **Padirac**, two operators (Pelaprat and Mermet) worked **days apart**, **before** Martel's exploration, and their readings were then set against Martel's survey plans — including a chain of galleries reported at Gintrac (10 km) and Granou (12 km) reached via eight branches. The Academy also records **its own negative control**: **no fluorescein** was found where a water connection had been predicted.
3. **The Lacave blind protocol.** This is the design worth the record. The commission worked from a **secret precision plan** of the **Brunet tunnel** — a 350 m *coudé* passage, 2.50 × 2 m, at 50–100 m depth — while **only a truncated tourist reduction was public** (deliberate fraud-proofing). Independent operators predicted the route blind. The post-hoc comparison of their plan to the survey, **at the same scale**, was recorded as *"coincida dans toutes ses parties, à 1 millimètre près"* — it agreed in every part, to within a millimetre. The same test recorded **21 m of unknown cavities** and a **1,200 m river with four temporary resurgences**. One caveat the corpus itself raises: **Viré owned the grottes de Lacave and Brunet dug the tunnel** — the era's best blind control ran on the proponent's home ground, which is context a modern reader needs before quoting the millimetre.
4. **Instrumented depth work and the 1920–21 resumption.** A separate machine-checked line (the **Igue Cantarel** at **18.50 m**, **Bourrât** at 584 m, and a **Cournoux** two-gallery row correct to **0.50 m**) plus the resumed commission's tests: **Meudon, May 1921** — a mineral-discrimination test that **attracted no professional dowser** (they preferred natural veins to small metal samples); **Versailles, 21 July 1921** — water-conduit location where **the amateurs failed** and a named operator succeeded with precision, after which the commission wrote that *"cette coïncidence des prévisions avec la réalité nous paraît très remarquable"* (the corpus record names that operator **"Prodel"**; a public French account of the same experiments gives **"Probst"** — **spelling unverified**, flagged not resolved).

## Result — what was measured

- **The dug rows largely held, at the stated depths** (0.41 m, 0.45 m, 0.65 m, 1 m, 2 m, 1.50 m) — with **one printed miss** (ossuary → pottery/bone at 2.40 m).
- **The blind Lacave test produced the strongest positive claim in the corpus's dowsing record** — a same-scale plan agreement *"à 1 millimètre près"* — **and it is compromised by the proponent's ownership of the site**, which the record states alongside the result rather than instead of it.
- **Padirac cross-validated** by two operators on separate days, ahead of the survey, with the fluorescein control negative.
- **The Academy printed its own floor**, and this is the headline the tradition's retellings usually drop: *"il y a sourciers et sourciers"* — there are dowsers and dowsers; **two "très entraînés" (highly trained) operators were incapable**; there was an **18 m dry well**; and at Meudon the **professionals declined to compete at all**.

## Confidence: medium — the record is real and partial, and it grades itself

- **What is strong:** a **national academy's own commission**, a **published primary** (CRAS t.157), **dug ground truth** rather than a felt signal, **two-operator cross-validation on separate days**, and — for 1913 — an unusually deliberate **blind design with a secret control plan**. The failures are **printed by the Academy itself**, in the same note as the successes.
- **What limits it, and both sides of today's argument should hold this:** the verification rows are **not a randomized blind trial** — the operators often chose or knew the ground; the Lacave blind ran on the **proponent's own property**; the report is a **commission's narrative**, not a data table, and the corpus holds no raw readings, no error bars, and no independent re-measurement of the dug rows. **n is small and selection is uncontrolled.** The "1 millimetre" figure is a **plan-overlay coincidence at map scale**, and the corpus's own read flags that the OCR of the map scale is garbled — quote it as a reported comparison, not as a survey-grade accuracy.
- **The corpus's own additional caveat:** the 1913 line was **not blind to the hypothesis** in the modern sense — Viré was a participant-observer reporting to the body that had commissioned him, and the 1920–21 resumption was explicitly run **"sans public"** (without an audience), which is a discretion choice, not a blinding one.
- **Why it is filed at medium and not higher or lower:** it is the **strongest documented positive** in the dowsing rail — and it is a **1913 commission report on a proponent's home ground**, filed beside Munich 1986 (null), Costérisant 1937 (null), Argenton 2007 (chance three times), the 2010 OZ sealed-envelope test (10/30, p = 0.197) and Solapur 2026 (42% vs 82%). The rail's later, better-controlled results all point the other way. This record's job is to hold the one row that does not, **with its flaws attached**.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted / measured / reported:** in 1913 the French Academy of Sciences put dowsers through dug ground truth, two-operator cross-validation, and — at Lacave — a genuine blind test with the control plan kept secret from the operators, and its own report contains both the successes and the floor: rows that hit at the stated depth, a plan-overlay agreement *"à 1 millimetre près"* on ground the proponent owned, and, in the same document, a miss, two trained operators who could not perform, an 18 m dry well, and professionals who declined to try. It is filed as a **historical replication attempt, partial and self-graded**, and it is the single record the dowsing rail's positive side can point to. It should be read with its own limitations — commission narrative, uncontrolled selection, proponent's home ground, no raw data — which the Academy's own text supplies and which the modern retellings on both sides usually omit.
