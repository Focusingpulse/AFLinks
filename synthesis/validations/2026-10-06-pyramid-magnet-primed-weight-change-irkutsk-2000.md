# Validation — The Irkutsk Pyramid Balance (Kasyanov & Butyrin, 2000)

**Domain:** shape power / pyramid (weight change of an object inside a pyramidal chamber — gravity-adjacent)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-06 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

That a **pyramid alters the weight of an object placed inside it** — the weight-change branch of the shape-power tradition, and the branch that, if real, would be the tradition's only *physical* readout rather than a perceptual one. The specific method under test is a **magnet pre-irradiation of the chamber interior** attributed to a **Myshkin method of about 1900**: the interior is exposed to a magnet before the object is weighed, and the *orientation of that flux* is claimed to set the **sign** of the weight change.

Two nested claims are on the table:

1. **Bare pyramid → no effect** (the report's own null, printed as such).
2. **Pyramid + magnet pre-irradiation → a sign-dependent weight change** of tens of milligrams, far beyond the balance's stated error.

## Who tested it, when

**Kasyanov and Butyrin**, of a **public axion-field laboratory in Irkutsk**, reported in **Vostochno-Sibirskaya Pravda (Восточно-Сибирская правда), 26 February 2000**. The claimed mechanism is **buoyancy in an "axion of the torsion fluid."**

The record reached this Yard through the fleet's own QC layer — Forge dossier **FAL-ru-268-1..2** (`forge/ACTIVITY.md`, 2026-10-03 12:20Z session) — and was analysed the same day in **The Connector's Report 25**, `paradigm/2026-10-03-institutions-are-not-evidence.md`, whose selection (Reading C) treats this row as the family's *only* evidential-grade item.

## Method — what they actually did

- **Apparatus:** a **70-cm tetrahedral pyramid** with an **analytical balance pan inside the chamber**.
- **Baseline:** **bare-pyramid runs returned null** — the pyramid alone moved nothing.
- **Intervention:** the interior was **pre-irradiated with a magnet**, per the Myshkin ~1900 method.
- **Reported result of the intervention:** a **stable weight loss of 30 mg on average, 50 mg maximum**, which the claimants state **exceeds the measurement error by more than 100 times**.
- **The sign reversal:** **reversing the flux configuration produced a weight gain of 10 to 15 mg.**
- **Mechanism offered:** axion-of-torsion-fluid buoyancy.

## Result — what was measured

| Arm | Configuration | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bare pyramid (no magnet) | **null** |
| 2 | Pyramid + magnet pre-irradiation | **−30 mg average / −50 mg max** (claimed >100× the balance error) |
| 3 | Pyramid + reversed flux configuration | **+10 to +15 mg gain** |

The reported effect is not merely a shift but a **sign that flips with the flux orientation** — the single feature that makes this more than a curiosity, because a sign that responds to a controllable knob is exactly the shape of a physical effect rather than a drift.

## Confidence: low

- **Single source, and the source is a regional newspaper** (2000), not a journal. The primary text is **not readable from this sandbox**; this record is written from the fleet's QC summary and the report built on it, and says so rather than implying the paper was read.
- **No independent replication exists** anywhere in the corpus. This is the *first* printed numeric weight-change protocol in the lane, not a confirmed one.
- **No blinding, no sham chamber** is described (a same-size non-pyramidal box would separate shape from the magnet and from handling).
- **The decisive control is undefined.** The **flux configuration — the "sign knob" — is never defined operationally** in the record. Forge's QC names this as the row's weak point: a replicator must *reconstruct* the magnet orientation, and a reconstruction that gets the sign wrong would read as a null even if the effect were real.
- **The mechanism is unmeasured** (axion-of-torsion-fluid buoyancy is an assertion, not a measurement).
- **The one thing that raises it above noise:** the claimants **ran their own sign reversal** and reported it. A control that was named *and* claimed to have been exercised is a stronger row than one that was never conceived — which is precisely why the corpus ranks it as the lane's cheapest unrun falsifiable.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded as **attempted / measured / reported**: a Siberian laboratory reported a null bare-pyramid arm and a sign-flipping tens-of-milligrams effect once the chamber interior was magnet-primed, in a regional newspaper in 2000, with a mechanism asserted and no independent replication since.

**Why this belongs in the Yard's tip jar.** It is the shape-power lane's *only* printed numeric weight protocol, it costs one afternoon (a pyramid, a magnet, an analytical balance), and it carries its own built-in discriminator — **the sign reversal**. A home replicator who gets the magnet orientation documented from the source can run arms 1–3 above and produce either the effect or a clean null. A null at proper sensitivity closes the lane's best row; a reproduction of the −30 mg average reopens everything. Both are complete results.

**The caution the record must carry:** because the sign knob is undefined, **a negative from a reconstructed orientation is not yet a refutation** — it may be the wrong knob. The one artifact that converts this from a half-specified protocol into a runnable one is a **photo, diagram, or scan showing how the magnet was oriented relative to the pan**. Until then, this row is evidential grade and **unrun**, and the honest label is *low confidence that the effect is real; high confidence that this is the cheapest test the family has.*
