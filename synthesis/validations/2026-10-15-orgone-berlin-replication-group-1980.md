# Validation — The 1980–82 West Berlin Replication of Reich's Bench Biology

**Domain:** orgone / life-field (Wilhelm Reich's bion and cancer-cell phenomenology, and the orgone accumulator)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-15 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

Wilhelm Reich's program made a chain of bench claims, in ascending order of ambition: (1) that inorganic and organic material, when sterilised and allowed to swell, disintegrates into vesicles that "clump together, begin autonomous movements, and are spontaneously 'born' as unicellular organisms" (the *bion* observations); (2) that a characteristic blood-test / cancer-cell phenomenology is visible in the microscope; (3) that a physical "orgone" energy concentrates in layered organic-metal structures (the orgone accumulator, ORAC); and (4) that ORAC exposure produces measurable biological — and later clinical — effects. This record concerns a group that set out to test (1) and (2) on the bench, and then (3)–(4) by self-experiment.

## Who tested it, when, and where

A **self-organised group of physicians and medical students in West Berlin**, beginning **early 1980**. The documentary record is **Heiko Lassek** (late director of the **Wilhelm Reich Institute, Berlin**), writing in the German source collection **orgon-archiv.de**, *"Texts from 60 years."* Lassek describes the group presenting its work at universities across the German-speaking and Scandinavian region after three years.

## Method — what they actually did

**Phase 1 (from 1980, ~3 years).** In Lassek's own words:

> "In early 1980, interested physicians and medical students in West Berlin began, on their own initiative, to experimentally reproduce and critically check Reich's central experiments in cell investigation and cancer diagnostics. […] It turned out that Reich had discovered and described several phenomena still unknown today, which can be reproduced and documented with the most modern methods such as **post-contrast videomicroscopy**."

The methodological point is real and specific: they re-ran the bench preparations with an instrument class — **post-contrast videomicroscopy** — that **did not exist in Reich's Oslo laboratory** of the 1930s. The posture is: replicate the biology first, with better instruments than the original, before arguing about interpretation.

**Phase 2 (1982).** Self-experiment with **accumulator-type apparatus**, which produced the observation that anchors the school's later clinical line:

> "It turned out that subjective and objective effects were experienced the more clearly, the more pronounced a pain or disease state already was before the orgone irradiation began — and that many healthy test persons without any symptoms needed markedly longer and more frequent irradiation times to feel strong vegetative reactions of the organism."

## Reported result

- **Phase 1 (the group's own claim):** several of Reich's described cell-investigation and cancer-diagnostics phenomena could be **reproduced and documented** with modern videomicroscopy.
- **Phase 2 (the group's own claim):** a **dose-response by pathology** — the detector that shows the effect most clearly is a pre-existing disease state; healthy volunteers are the least responsive and required longer/more frequent exposure.

## Confidence: LOW on the result; MEDIUM on the fact and quality of the attempt; HIGH that nothing blinded followed

**Why low on the result.** The outcome is reported by the group itself, in a venue maintained by the school, with **no independent witness laboratory** and **no blinded or pre-registered protocol** described. "Could be reproduced and documented" is a report of seeing phenomena through a microscope, not a controlled comparison. A bion/vesicle preparation is exactly the kind of object where preparation artefacts and observer expectation are the default explanation until excluded.

**Why the attempt still counts, and is worth a record.** This is the clearest case in the archive of a heterodox line applying the **correct experimental posture** to its own founder's bench biology: reproduce it, upgrade the instrument, and take it to universities — rather than to a sympathetic audience. It is also a *historical replication attempt* with named actors, dates, a described method, and a stated result, which is what this Yard records.

**The counter-ledger, stated plainly.** The archive's own synthesis of this line is explicit: *"the clinical record is practice reports, not trials. No blinded, controlled replication of the ORAC palliative effect exists in the archive."* Phase 2 was uncontrolled self-experiment; its "dose-response by pathology" is precisely the shape that **expectancy and selection** also produce (sick volunteers are motivated observers), and nothing in the described design separates the two. The German school's own internal skeptic — **Bernhard Harrer**, *Critical Evaluation of Life-Energy Research by Wilhelm Reich* — concentrates his fire on exactly the third link (the atmospheric-energy hypothesis that the accumulator presupposes).

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted / reported:** a West Berlin group re-ran Reich's bench biology with a better microscope and reported that the described phenomena were reproducible, then ran an uncontrolled self-experiment on the accumulator and reported an effect that scales with pre-existing pathology. This is filed as a **historical replication attempt**, **not** as a confirmation.

The narrow claim that survives is checkable in principle: that Reich's **bion/vesicle preparations and blood-test phenomenology are real as microscope phenomena**, whatever their interpretation. That is a claim a modern laboratory could test with blinding and a preparation-artefact control — and per the archive's own ledger, nobody has. The accumulator's clinical claim remains, as of this record, the least-tested large claim in the orgone rail: forty years on, its strongest evidence is a self-experiment and a set of practice reports.
