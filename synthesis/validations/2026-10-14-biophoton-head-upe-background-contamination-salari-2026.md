# Validation — Extracranial "Brain UPE": A Background-Light Artifact (Calgary, 2026)

**Domain:** biofield / bio-photonics (ultra-weak photon emission — claim dismantling)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-14 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

Over the last several years a specific, strong claim entered the biofield literature: that **ultra-weak photon emission (UPE — the "biophoton" signal)** recorded **extracranially, at the human head**, is a **non-invasive biomarker of brain activity** — a photonic window on the brain that needs no electrodes. The claim is the "photonic" sibling of the EEG-based biofield claims this Yard already carries, and it presumes the same thing: that the light is coming from where the study says it is.

## Who tested it, when, and where

**Vahid Salari**, **Vishnu Seshan**, **Rishabh Rishabh**, **Daniel Oblak** and **Christoph Simon** — Department of Physics and Astronomy, Institute for Quantum Science and Technology, and Hotchkiss Brain Institute, **University of Calgary**, Canada (Simon is a well-established quantum-optics researcher). Published as a *Letter* in the **Journal of Physical Chemistry Letters**, **16 July 2026**, **17**(28): 7811–7816; a preprint also circulated (bioRxiv / arXiv:2603.26630). The group's earlier work includes direct UPE imaging of mice and plants (*J. Phys. Chem. Lett.* 2025).

## Method — what they actually did

- They **re-measured head UPE under properly dark (background-free) conditions** and compared the result with the magnitudes reported in the "brain UPE" literature.
- They **quantified the background**: how much of a reported head signal can be accounted for by ambient light leaking into the detector.
- They worked the **optical budget of the measurement chain**: the **attenuation of light by scalp and skull** as a function of wavelength, set against the **spectral sensitivity of the photomultiplier tubes (PMTs)** used in those studies.
- The result is a **critical re-analysis plus measurement**, not a new biomarker trial — the question is whether the reported signal can originate where it is claimed to.

## Result — what was found

- **Under proper dark conditions, the UPE from the head is far weaker than the "brain UPE" papers report.**
- **The large reported signals are explained by background light contamination** — in at least one case a "head" signal was lower than the background, i.e. consistent with the head simply **shadowing ambient light** rather than emitting its own.
- **The optics close the door further.** Photons **below ~600 nm are strongly attenuated by scalp and skull**, while **longer wavelengths fall largely outside the effective spectral sensitivity of the PMTs** used. The authors estimate the photon flux that would need to originate in the brain to be detectable through the skull at **five to seven orders of magnitude above plausible biological levels**.
- **Their conclusion, in plain terms:** even if UPE from the head is recorded under genuinely background-free conditions, it is **likely to be dominated by emission from the scalp rather than from the brain** — certainly as long as PMTs are the detector. The claim as stated is not supported.

## Confidence: high (as a negative on the strong claim)

**Why high.** A **peer-reviewed Letter in a mainstream physical-chemistry venue**, from a **quantum-optics group** with the right instruments and the right expertise, attacking the claim with **two independent lines** — a direct dark-condition measurement, and a first-principles optical budget (tissue attenuation vs detector sensitivity) that does not depend on anyone's measurement at all. The two lines agree, and the failure mode identified (**background light leaking past an incomplete blackout**) is mundane, specific, and checkable.

**What it does not do.** It does **not** say biophoton emission is unreal — UPE is well documented from cells, plants and skin. It says the **brain-specific, extracranial** version fails: the signal is not from the brain, and the reported magnitudes are an artifact. That is a **narrow, decisive null** on one strand, not a verdict on the whole rail.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured / reported:** a Calgary quantum-optics group asked the plainest possible question about the "brain biophoton" claim — *is the light coming from the brain?* — and answered it twice over. Measured under a proper blackout, the head signal is far smaller than the literature reports; the big numbers that literature found are **background light**; and the optical budget says the brain's own light would be attenuated by skull and scalp below detectability while the detectors are blind where the transmission is best. Filed **high-confidence as a negative on that specific strand.**

It is worth saying what this is *not*. It is not a claim that biophotons do not exist — they do, and this Yard's own UPE records are about measuring them properly. It is not a swipe at the biofield rail as a whole. It is a **dismantling control** of exactly the kind the pavilion exists to hold: a confident, biomarker-flavoured claim, tested on its own terms, and found to be an instrument artifact. The companion record from Amsterdam put a **reproducibility floor** under UPE measurement; this one puts a **ceiling** on what a head-mounted PMT can see at all. Together they say the honest thing — **the measurement has to be able to show the light is from the brain before any claim can rest on it, and here it cannot.**
