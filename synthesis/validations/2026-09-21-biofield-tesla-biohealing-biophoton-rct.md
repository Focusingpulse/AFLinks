# Validation — The Tesla BioHealing Biophoton Generator Stem-Cell Trial

**Domain:** biofield / bio-photonics (device-applied "life-force" exposure, human endpoint)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-21 (Replication Watch, run 3)

## What was claimed

That a **"strong biophoton generator"** — a passive commercial device placed near the body — measurably increases a person's own (endogenous) circulating **stem/progenitor cells**. On the archive's side this is the bio-photonics tradition's most aggressive operational claim: that an inert device can drive a stem-cell response in a healthy adult. This card records the test, not the truth of the claim.

## Who tested it, when

Authors: **Mariola A. Smotrys, Helen Y. Gu, Laura J. Poirier, Audrey Ager, Kari Miller, Seth D. Robinson** and others. Lead sponsor: the **First Institute of All Medicines (FIAM)**. Two clinical centres, in **Pennsylvania and Florida, USA**.

Device: **Tesla BioHealing® Biophotonizer-A** — an over-the-counter commercial product.

- Protocol and registration: **ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06855459** (also "FIAM-SC255"). First patient enrolled **14 Jan 2025**; **status COMPLETED**; primary completion **30 Apr 2026**.
- Results published **19 Dec 2025** in *Journal of Nanotechnology Research & Reports* (SRC Publishers), **doi 10.47363/jnrrr/2025(7)242**. The same paper also appears under a second SRC title, *Journal of Clinical Case Studies Reviews & Reports*, **doi 10.47363/jccs/icctmd2026/2026(8)1**.

## Method

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled. **71 adults** (registered plan was ~46; a prior **15-participant open-label pilot** had reported a 336% rise in CD34+ cells, *p* = 0.0106, with 14/15 responders and individual increases up to 1348%). Participants slept with **four devices at the corners of the bed, ≥8 hours nightly for 14 days**, active or visually identical placebo. Endpoints: flow-cytometric **CD34+, CD133+, and CD34+CD133+** circulating cells at baseline and follow-up; SF-36 quality of life and Pain Disability Index concurrently. After the blinded phase, placebo participants crossed over to open-label active treatment.

## Result

As reported: active exposure produced **~2.7× CD34+, ~3.5× CD133+ (leukocyte population), and ~3.1× CD34+CD133+** cells, all *p* < 0.01, versus baseline and placebo; **no significant change during placebo**; **SF-36 improved 16–30%** (*p* < 0.05); **PDI fell by 7.35 points** (*p* < 0.00001); improvements "replicated in the crossover cohort"; no adverse events. Within-subject paired tests and ANOVA are reported for the three cell types.

## Confidence: low

The result, if it held, would be among the largest device-driven biological effects ever reported in a healthy population. Several things have to be true at once for that, and the study does not clear the bar:

- **The design pools an unblinded pilot into the treatment arm.** The first 21 participants received the active device with no blinding, and their data are included in the treatment group. Those are not randomised observations.
- **The sponsor is not independent of the product.** The trial was run by FIAM around a named commercial device, and the same group had already published the pilot that supplied the effect size used to justify the trial. There is no replication by a disinterested academic group.
- **The venue is a low-credibility publisher.** SRC Publishers' metrics are unverifiable and it is not indexed in Scopus/Web of Science; the article's own landing page, fetched for this record, was **injected with Indonesian gambling spam** ("slot gacor") — a signature of a hijacked or vanity outlet, not a functioning editorial system. The same paper being published under **two different SRC journal titles** is a further red flag.
- **The endpoint is noisy and the effect is unphysiologically large.** Circulating CD34+ counts vary substantially day-to-day; claimed 3.5-fold shifts in a healthy adult from a passive bedside device far exceed what any known stimulation produces.
- **No pre-registered analysis plan is visible, and the analysed N (71) exceeds the registered target (~46),** with no published explanation of the increase.
- This record has **not** read the full text. It rests on the abstract, the registry entry, and the registration's own description — i.e. on the authors' reported numbers.

**What would move it:** an independent group, blinded, with a pre-registered primary endpoint and a randomised (not pooled) sample, reproducing a CD34+ change. Absent that, this is a claim to grade, not a result to cite.

## Related live test (watch item, not a result)

A genuinely independent academic biofield trial is running in an adjacent space: **UC Irvine, NCT07581080** — a randomised, single-masked crossover pilot of standardised Usui Reiki for fatigue in adults on cancer immunotherapy (n ≈ 12, six 30-minute weekly sessions, with EEG and blood biomarkers at weeks 1 and 6; start 2026-04-20, primary completion expected 2026-12). Small, but institutionally independent and pre-registered.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded as **claimed / attempted / reported**: a registered, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with a real randomised arm reports an extraordinary device effect, and two things are true at once — **the trial exists and is registered** (so it is not merely a press release), and **its venue, its sponsor relationship, and its pooled unblinded pilot make it unable to carry the weight of its own result**. This is the loud-flag class the Yard exists to catch: when a commercial device claims a 3-fold stem-cell response, the record should be *highly visible and highly qualified*, not absent and not endorsed. Set beside this run's rock-crystal card, the biofield rail now has both halves of the honest picture: one study whose **control dissolved the effect**, and one whose effect **survived the control only as far as the design can be trusted**.
