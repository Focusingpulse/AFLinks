# Validation — A Precision Null on Solenoid Anomalous Forces (Modified Kaluza–Klein)

**Domain:** torsion / anomalous force / gravity–electromagnetism coupling (scalar-field claims) — Poland + Germany
**Recorded:** 2026-10-05 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

That the **modified Kaluza–Klein theory of Mbelek and Lachièze-Rey** — a 5D model adding an external scalar field ψ that couples gravity and electromagnetism — predicts a **new, non-shieldable force from solenoids** that ordinary Maxwell electromagnetism does not, with a strength that depends on **ambient matter density**. Mbelek reported **an anomalous torque on a nonmagnetic torsion pendulum** consistent with the prediction. This sits in the same claim family as the **Saxl effect** and the "electro-gravity / charged capacitor" forces that periodically resurface in the torsion-and-scalar-waves rail — claims the archive treats alongside its torsion-field and non-Hertzian-electrodynamics threads.

## Who tested it, when

**Martin Tajmar and M. Olek** (**AGH University of Krakow**) with **O. Neunzig** (**Technische Universität Dresden**) — published in the **International Journal of Modern Physics A**, online **25 July 2026** (DOI below). This is the group's **third** swing at the claim, after a **vacuum pendulum test** (Monette, Jentzsch, Kößling & Tajmar, IJMPA 2024) and an **in-depth steady-field EM-gravity coupling search** (Tajmar, Kößling & Neunzig). It is an independent replication of **Mbelek's** result by a mainstream precision-measurement lab.

## Method — what they actually did

- **Instrument:** a **laboratory precision balance**, resolution **0.1 mg**, used to look for anomalous *weight* or *force* changes rather than pendulum torque.
- **Field source:** **toroidal solenoids** — driven with **DC up to 3 A** — with both **ferromagnetic and nonmagnetic** samples, because the theory's sign/magnitude depends on the solenoid material.
- **The key design move:** the earlier pendulum test was run in **vacuum**, but the theory is claimed to depend on **ambient matter density**, so this experiment tested at **ambient pressure *and* with the apparatus immersed in a water environment** — directly targeting the escape hatch the theory uses to explain away previous nulls.
- **Controls:** thermal drifts and electromagnetic effects were **eliminated** before the reported limit was set.

## Result — what was measured

- **No measurable mass change or anomalous force** appeared, within the balance's **0.1 mg** resolution, for either ferromagnetic or nonmagnetic solenoids.
- Using **Mbelek's own theory and coupling constant**, the null **lowers the limit on any anomalous coupling by at least 6–10 orders of magnitude** — depending on solenoid type.
- **Consistent with the group's priors:** the 2024 **vacuum pendulum test** found **no** such force and attributed Mbelek's original ambient-pressure observation to **convection effects** (suppressed under higher vacuum / by a separating wall) and, at higher fields, to ordinary **diamagnetic** interaction. The 2024 in-depth search likewise found **no anomalous forces or torques** down to nano-newton scale across capacitors, solenoids and tunnelling currents.

## Confidence

- **High** that this is a **clean, peer-reviewed, institutional null.** The paper is in a mainstream physics journal; the authors are career precision-measurement researchers (Tajmar's group built nano-newton torsion balances and high-vacuum shielded rigs expressly for this class of claim); the experiment **targets the theory's own escape hatch** (ambient matter density) instead of ignoring it; and the result is **triply consistent** with the group's two earlier nulls. The claim tested is fringe; the test is not.
- **Scope limits, stated plainly:** the null constrains **Mbelek's specific scalar-field prediction** for **static** solenoid fields. It does **not** adjudicate unrelated "torsion field" or "scalar wave" claims that take other forms, nor **high-frequency** couplings — which the authors explicitly name as the next, harder regime. A single negative does not close a family of claims; it closes one instrumentable member of it.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as attempted / measured / reported:** a mainstream precision-measurement lab took Mbelek's scalar-field force claim — the kind of claim usually argued about rather than *measured* — and gave it a 0.1 mg balance, the solenoid types it specifies, and the ambient-matter-density condition it leans on, and **found nothing**, tightening the bound by six to ten orders of magnitude. It is the **third null** in the same group's line, each aimed at the last one's escape hatch.

This is a **negative result and it is first-class content**, not a dismissal: it sharpens the question rather than closing the field. Two things stay true at once. **One:** a claim that can only survive by relocating its effect each time a control closes one door (vacuum → ambient density → high frequency) is telling you something about the claim, not just about the experiments. **Two:** the null is scoped to a **specific, static, instrumentable** prediction — it is not a verdict on every torsion or scalar claim in the rail, and the authors themselves name the **high-frequency** regime as the open door. The honest state is therefore: **one clearly stated member of the family tested and failed, at high sensitivity, by people who build their own instruments for exactly this — and one door left explicitly ajar.**
