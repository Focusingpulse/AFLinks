# Validation — The Pyramid Water-Evaporation Test (Ivanitskii & Narimanov, 2002)

**Domain:** shape power / built-enclosure geometry (the pyramid form effect) — water and storage
**Recorded:** 2026-10-01 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

The shape-power claim the Yard already holds in three dossiers: that a hollow pyramid of the Egyptian proportion does something to matter held inside it. This record covers the **water end** of that claim — the widely repeated observation that water, food and biological samples kept under a pyramid dry faster and keep longer than the same material outside it.

The specific claim under test is narrower, and it is the one the popular literature converts into evidence for a non-physical "pyramid field": **the rate at which water evaporates depends on the shape of the enclosure it sits in.**

## Who ran it, when

**G. R. Ivanitskii** (Genrikh R. Ivanitskii) and **A. A. Narimanov**, of the Russian Academy of Sciences' biophysics institute (rendered as the *Institute of Theoretical and Applied Biophysics* in the Russian science press and as the *Institute of Theoretical and Experimental Biophysics* in the indexing record; the first name of Narimanov appears variously as Abdujalil and Arif in the secondary press).

- **The paper under this record:** Ivanitskii, G. R. & Narimanov, A. A. *"Explanation of the pyramid effect"* (Объяснение эффекта пирамид), **Biophysics (Биофизика) 47**(5): 943–952 (2002). Indexed as PubMed **"Explanation for the pyramid effect"**, 10 December 2002.
- **The companion paper:** Narimanov, A. A. *"On the pyramid form effects"* (Biophysics 2001, no. 5, pp. 951–957; indexed as *"Pyramid effect"*, 1 November 2001) — the wider set of observations listed under *Result* below.
- The 2002 result was reported to a general readership in *Nauka i Zhizn* (*Наука и жизнь*), "Термодинамика пирамид" — the popular account through which the apparatus and the arithmetic below reach the public record.

**Why this paper is in this record at all.** The archive found it while auditing a citation chain, not while looking for pyramid research: the Russian shape-power lane's **only genuine mainstream-journal citation** turned out to be this one, cited by its own advocates as *support* for a claimed non-physical pyramid effect — a use that runs the other way once the paper is opened. See *Source* below.

## Method, as reported

A controlled **comparison of two enclosures**, not of a pyramid against an open room:

- **Chamber A — pyramid:** cardboard, base **40 × 40 cm**, height **24.5 cm**.
- **Chamber B — cube:** cardboard, edge **26 cm** (a comparable internal volume).
- **Material:** cardboard, chosen deliberately because it **transmits thermal radiation well** — i.e. the chamber is meant to be a thermal envelope, not a shield.
- **Sample:** water in a vessel inside each chamber; also biological and mineral samples, and seeds.
- **Measurement:** the rate at which water (and the samples) lost volatiles, plus the samples' comparative drying and germination.
- **Analysis:** the evaporation process was modelled against **six chamber parameters** — chamber height, chamber volume, how volume changes with height, the height at which the sample sits, the chamber's cross-sectional area at that height, and the rate at which the internal volume heats.

## Result, as reported

- **Water inside the pyramid evaporated about 1.6× faster than inside the cube.** This is the headline number, and it is a *positive*: the folk observation was reproduced.
- Biological and mineral samples dried more intensively; seeds germinated sooner.
- The six-parameter thermal-geometry equation's **calculated values agreed well with the measured ones** — which is the authors' actual conclusion: the rate of evaporation depends substantially on the shape and size of the chamber, and on where inside it the sample sits.
- The indexed abstract states the same verdict in the field's own language: the dynamics of volatiles from samples inside the chambers are affected by chamber shape; the previously obtained experimental results are **consistent with the theory**; all phenomena observed in constructions of various shapes are **described in terms of known physical conceptions**; and chambers with outside pulsating heating are variations of the known **"Brown motor devices."**

**The authors' reading, in the archive's own summary of it: hollow pyramids are simple heat engines.** An enclosure heats by day and cools by night; the heating drives evaporation; the temperature and humidity inside the cavity are not uniform; the shape and height of the cavity set where the gradients fall. Nothing in the result requires a field.

**The companion paper (Narimanov 2001) reported a wider and more mixed set**, carried here for completeness and *not* as confirmation: presowing dry barley seeds held in the pyramid **stimulated** growth, while "pyramid water" used for sowing **suppressed** early growth; the pH of pyramid-held water changed; the decomposition rate of hydrogen peroxide in a pyramid-held aqueous solution rose about **threefold**; and the masses of both biological objects (seeds) and inorganic solids (quartz) were reported to **change** in the intrapyramidal space. The last two are extraordinary claims that the 2002 thermal analysis does not address.

## Confidence: high that this study was done and reports this; high that it settles the evaporation claim and not the field claim

**For it.**

1. **It is a controlled comparison with a shape-matched enclosure**, which is more than most of the tradition's own reports offer: pyramid against cube of comparable volume, same material, same room.
2. **It is peer-reviewed and PubMed-indexed**, and the popular account of the apparatus and the arithmetic exists independently in the Russian science press. The 1.6× figure appears in both.
3. **It is a positive result taken seriously by the archive** — the observation replicated. The Yard is not filing a null here; it is filing a claim that *worked* and then got a mundane account.
4. **The mechanism is quantitative and checkable.** A six-parameter equation whose predictions match the measurements is a stronger explanation than an appeal to a field, and it makes a prediction a home replicator can falsify (see *What would change it*).

**Against it.**

1. **One centre, one geometry pair.** Two chambers, one pyramid proportion, one cube. The equation is fitted to this arrangement; the paper does not sweep a range of shapes.
2. **The authors explain rather than replicate.** Ivanitskii and Narimanov were testing the *mechanism*, not auditing the claim's promoters; the archive is the party doing the citation audit.
3. **The companion paper's extraordinary rows (quartz mass change, H₂O₂ decomposition) are not covered by the thermal account.** A heat engine explains faster evaporation. It does not explain a mass change in quartz. Those rows are filed here as **unresolved**, not as refuted.
4. **This record was written from the paper's indexed abstract, the Russian popular account of it, and the archive's own QC dossier — not from the full paper**, which was not readable from this sandbox. The 1.6× figure and the six-parameter account are corroborated across three independent carriers; the paper's internal tables are not in hand.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured and reported:** two researchers at a Russian academy institute compared a cardboard pyramid against a comparable cardboard cube, found water evaporating **about 1.6× faster inside the pyramid**, and accounted for it with ordinary thermal geometry. The folk observation replicates. The field does not follow from it.

The useful part for the Yard is what this record does to the rail it lands on. The Yard has held three pyramid dossiers and **no pyramid validation** — a reader could reasonably conclude nobody had ever tested the thing. They have. Twice, with controls, in two different countries and two different disciplines: here on **water**, where the effect is real and mundane; and in the companion record on **rat stress physiology**, where the effect is reported real with a shape control and no mundane account offered. Those two results do not agree about the mechanism, and the Yard should not blend them.

**What would change this record:** a published run in which the treated and control enclosures are held at the same internal temperature and humidity — if the 1.6× survives that, the thermal account is incomplete. That is a cheap test and it is now a quest-shaped hole in the rail rather than a rhetorical one.
