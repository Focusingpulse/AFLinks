# Validation — Active Electro-Culture, Polarity-Resolved (HVEF on Tomato Seedlings)

**Domain:** electro-culture / magneto-culture in agriculture
**Recorded:** 2026-09-25 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The three-century electro-culture claim: that an applied electric field or current improves plant growth and yield. Two ends of it matter here, and this Yard now holds a record at each:

- **the passive/home end** — copper-wrapped dowels and constant magnetic fields. Both are **null** in this Yard: the copper-dowel container trial of 2026-09-18 (a rod transmits ~2 mV into soil) and the constant-field wheat/mustard trial of 2026-09-16.
- **the active end** — a real, applied, kilovolt-class field, at institutional scale. That is the claim tested here: that a **high-voltage electrostatic field (HVEF)** regulates plant growth and development non-chemically, and that **the polarity of the field is part of the effect**.

The specific claim under test is the field's mechanism, not its yield: that an exogenous field crosses the cell membrane and re-organises **intracellular ion homeostasis** — in particular the spatiotemporal transport of **magnesium**, the metal at the centre of chlorophyll — and that this is what couples to photosynthesis. The paper also names the practical objection it is answering: that HVEF shows a **"biphasic regulatory effect,"** with negative fields producing *"promotion first, inhibition later"* and late-stage growth arrest, mechanism unclear.

## Who ran it, when

The team led by **Zhenyu LIU**, **Shanxi Agricultural University**, China.

- **Paper:** *"Coupling mechanisms of electrophysiology and photosynthesis mediated by ion dynamics in high-voltage electrostatic field-regulated tomato seedling growth,"* **Engineering Agriculture** **13**(4): 26668 (2026). DOI **10.15302/J-FASE-2026668** (Higher Education Press).
- **Journal press release:** EurekAlert, **18 September 2026** — the source through which every number below reaches the public record.
- Author list as indexed for the DOI: Ruijie Xie, Yanbo Song, Xiaojing Shi, Liyan Jia, Zhenyu Liu. (Crossref does not expose authors for this record — checked this run.)

## Method, as reported

- **Comparative experiment**, tomato seedlings, **positive (+HVEF) vs negative (−HVEF) field**, against control. **25-day treatment.**
- **Phenotype:** plant mechanical strength, biomass.
- **Ion mapping:** inductively coupled plasma spectroscopy and energy-dispersive spectroscopy mapping, profiling ion distribution separately in **roots, stems and leaves**.
- **Electrophysiology:** plant **impedance spectroscopy** and **membrane potential**.
- **Photosynthesis:** leaf chlorophyll content; **stomatal conductance and stomatal aperture**; activity of key photosynthetic enzymes including **Rubisco**; intercellular CO₂ concentration.

Not in the public record: field strength, electrode geometry, dose–response curve, sample size, replication count, or the field-off control's description. Those are the parameters a replicator would need first.

## Result, as reported

- **Preferential magnesium transport.** After 25 days of **+HVEF**, magnesium was directionally enriched in leaves, reaching **1.58× the control concentration** — the strongest single number in the record.
- **Electrophysiology.** +HVEF induced **cell-membrane hyperpolarisation and reduced impedance**, described as "opening" high-speed ion-transport channels.
- **Photosynthesis.** Leaf **chlorophyll content increased substantially**; **stomatal conductance and aperture increased**; **Rubisco and related enzyme activities were synergistically activated**. Because stomatal conductance rose while carbon assimilation also improved, **intercellular CO₂ stayed stable** — the authors read this as a genuine assimilation change rather than a CO₂ artefact.
- **Growth.** Mechanical strength and biomass increased under +HVEF over the 25 days.
- **Polarity.** The release states that **negative** fields in practice show *"promotion first, inhibition later,"* leading to growth arrest or plant death at later stages — so the reported effect is **polarity-dependent**, not merely field-present.

## Confidence: medium that the study was done and says this; low-medium that the effect generalises

**For it.** Peer-reviewed journal; a university group; a named mechanism with a **quantitative ion measurement** (the 1.58× Mg figure) and **both polarities compared against a control**. In a domain where most entries are single-treatment anecdotes, that is a better design than average, and the polarity arm is the reason it is interesting: a two-sign result is harder to produce by accident than a one-sign one.

**Against it.**

1. **No independent replication.** A single centre, a single species, a single duration, a single group.
2. **The record is press-release deep.** Every number above arrives through the publisher's own media contact (Higher Education Press). The DOI landing page was not readable from this sandbox; **the paper itself was not read.** A press release is a claim about a paper, not the paper.
3. **The framing is promotional** — "empower," and a headline asking *"why does a positive electric field better 'understand' the plant's heart?"* That is not an argument against the data, but it is a reason to hold the data at press-release weight.
4. **The parameters a replicator needs are absent** from the public record (field strength, geometry, n, field-off description).
5. **It is a mechanism study, not a yield replication.** It supports the *plausibility* of the electro-culture claim at tissue level. It does not measure yield under field conditions, and it makes no attempt to reconcile itself with the two nulls this Yard already holds.

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. Recorded as **reported**: a university group compared two field polarities against a control and measured **where the magnesium went**. That is the useful shape — a mechanism with a number — and it is the **first positive entry on the Yard's electro-culture rail after two nulls**.

The three records do not contradict each other, and it would be dishonest to read them as a score. They are different experiments: passive millivolt dowels in container pots; a constant magnetic field on wheat and mustard; and an active high-voltage electrostatic field on seedlings. The composite statement the Yard can now make honestly is that this claim is **dose-, polarity- and delivery-dependent** — which is exactly what a replicator should test, and exactly what a three-hundred-year argument has mostly failed to hold constant. What is still missing on this rail is the same thing missing everywhere else: **nobody has repeated anyone.** The two nulls are independent of their claim's promoters; this positive is not independent of its own.
