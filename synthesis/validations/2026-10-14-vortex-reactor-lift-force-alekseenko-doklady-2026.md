# Validation — A Measured Lift Force in a Vortex Reactor (Novosibirsk, 2026)

**Domain:** vortex / implosion technology (Schauberger lineage)
**Recorded:** 2026-10-14 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

The family of claims this record sits under is the **"implosion" claim**: that a swiftly rotating fluid or air vortex — Schauberger's lineage, and the branch that runs through this Yard's Repulsine and vortex dossiers — can generate a **lift force** and, in the strong version, usable extra energy, from vortical motion alone. Until now this Yard held that claim only as an **unvalidated dossier family** plus one **negative** record (a 2026 CFD and model-boat test that traced an apparent forward thrust to water simply being ejected under the vortex, and found no extra rotation from heat-driven vortices).

This record is the other half, and it is a cleaner one: a **mainstream thermophysics institute has measured a genuine lift force in a vortex reactor** — and named an ordinary mechanism for it.

## Who tested it, when, and where

**S. V. Alekseenko**, **I. V. Naumov**, **S. G. Skripkin** and **B. R. Sharifullin** (Novosibirsk State University; **Kutateladze Institute of Thermophysics, Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences**) with **V. N. Shtern** (Shtern Research and Consulting, Houston, TX). Published in **Doklady Physics / Доклады РАН** (Proceedings of the Russian Academy of Sciences), **Vol. 527 (2026), pp. 66–73**, Technical Sciences section — i.e. a peer-reviewed academy-proceedings venue, not a preprint server. The English-language title is *"About the lift force of a vortex reactor."* The same group's method paper appeared in *Experiments in Fluids* in late 2025.

## Method — what they actually did

- A **cylindrical reactor** closed by a **rotating upper end disk**, with an **adaptive (freely rotating) sidewall** rather than a fixed one.
- The disk imparts swirl; **viscous friction of the fluid against the sidewall induces self-sustained rotation of the wall itself**, which intensifies the swirl.
- They measured the **axial load on the support bearing** while varying the **disk rotation frequency**, and tracked where the mechanical power delivered to the disk went.
- The experiment is a single-body mechanical measurement: the instrument is the **load cell on the bearing**, and the effect under test is whether a vortex can pull the vessel upward against its own weight.

## Result — what was measured

- **A net lift force was detected on the rotating reactor vessel.** The authors attribute it to a **stable toroidal vortex** whose **central region is at reduced pressure**; the disk rotation forms a low-pressure zone that draws fluid in and organizes it into the torus, whose pressure deficit produces a resultant **upward force on the vessel walls**.
- **Above a critical disk rotation frequency** — the point at which self-induced rotation of the vessel begins — the **measured axial load on the support bearing drops sharply**, and with further increase of rotation frequency **it can be reduced to zero**, so that the apparatus **separates from its support** (the authors' word: a *levitation effect*).
- A **second, bookkeeping finding:** a substantial part of the mechanical power fed to the rotating disk is **transferred through the vortex flow to the sidewall and spent driving its rotation**, rather than being dissipated as frictional heat. This is an efficiency claim about where the input power goes — **not** a claim of power *gain*.

## Confidence: high (that the lift is real and mainstream-measurable); NONE that it is anomalous

**Why high on the measurement.** A peer-reviewed Russian Academy of Sciences proceedings paper, from a long-established thermophysics institute and a group with a deep published record on rotating-disk vortex flows (*Experiments in Fluids* 2025; *Physics of Fluids*; *Chemical Engineering & Processing*). The endpoint is a **load on a bearing** — a hard mechanical quantity, not a field reading — and the effect has a **named conventional mechanism**: a low-pressure core in a toroidal vortex.

**Why it is nonetheless not a win for "implosion."** Vortex-generated lift is **ordinary, understood fluid dynamics**, and industry already uses it — the pneumatic **"vortex gripper"** (a swirling-air device that lifts a workpiece without contact) has been modelled and measured since at least 2010. Nothing here requires a non-classical force, a scalar field, or an energy anomaly; the authors' own account is a pressure deficit. **A vortex that lifts is not a vortex that gives energy away**, and this record must not be read as the latter. The paper reports **no excess energy** — if anything, it details where the input power goes.

**What it does earn.** It converts "vortex lift" from a legend into a **measured number from a mainstream bench**, and it shows the *mechanism* is mundane — which is exactly the discriminating result the pavilion needs. Read alongside the existing negative CFD record, the two together say: **vortical motion can lift; it does not thereby become free energy.**

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. **Recorded as measured / reported:** a Russian Academy of Sciences thermophysics group closed a cylinder with a spinning top disk and an adaptive wall, and measured the load on the bearing. A **real lift force appeared**, with a vortex-shaped low-pressure core named as its cause; at high enough rotation the vessel's load went to zero and it came off its support. That is a **hard mechanical measurement from a peer-reviewed academy venue**, and it is filed **high-confidence**.

The honesty is in what it does and does not say. This is the **ordinary half** of the implosion claim: vortices can lift things, by ordinary pressure mechanics, and industry already exploits that. It is **not** evidence for free energy, not evidence for a non-hertzian field, and not evidence for Schauberger's stronger assertions — the paper itself explains the lift with a pressure deficit and, separately, accounts for where the input power goes. It sits **beside, not against**, the Yard's existing negative that a vortex engine cannot give extra rotation: both results describe the same ordinary physics working in the same direction. **A vortex that lifts is not a vortex that gives energy away**, and this record is the clearest measurement the pavilion has of that distinction.
