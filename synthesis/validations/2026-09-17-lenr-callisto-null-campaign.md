# Validation — Project Callisto: A Nine-Year Null on the Anomalous Heat Effect

**Domain:** LENR / anomalous heat effect (calorimetry)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-17 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

That a metal–hydrogen system driven by electrolysis or gas loading can release more thermal energy than is supplied to it — the "anomalous heat effect" (AHE), the claim lineage running from Fleischmann–Pons (1989) through four decades of excess-heat reports.

## Who replicated it, when

**Project Callisto** — an international program running **2015–2024**, with **more than 100 contributors**, whose final report was released **2026-09-08** at `callisto.report`. The corpus's scout upgrade ledger credits the report to **"Ainslie et al."**; that attribution appears only in the scout record and was not independently verified here.

## Method

More than **40 formal and modified Fleischmann–Pons-style replications** — prolonged electrolysis, cutting-fluid additives, oxygen bubbling, and high-input-power variants — across a wide loading / stimulation / material parameter space. The program additionally cataloged the conditions under which AHE had been reported, and examined the calorimetry methods used in the prior positive reports. The report is published as chaptered PDFs; one chapter is titled `07-replications.pdf`.

## Result

**No evidence of the AHE or of any cold-fusion phenomenon** in the tested space. Most prior "excess heat" reports were traced to prosaic explanations: power-accounting errors, calorimeter response changes, hydriding entropy, sensor faults, stored chemical energy from recombination, gas-cooling differences, and contamination. The scout recommends archiving the report as a historically significant falsification document.

## Confidence: medium

This is the largest and most systematic negative campaign in the corpus's LENR record, with a documented method and a prosaic-explanation checklist attached to each prior positive mode. Limits, stated plainly: **in this corpus the record is a scout summary plus a chapter catalog — not the report text.** The ">40 replications" and "200,000+ sample-hours" figures come from the scout ledgers, not from a fetched primary passage; the archive has not independently audited the protocol; and the work covers a defined parameter space, so it does not by itself adjudicate gas-loading or cavitation configurations it did not test.

## Why this record matters in the Yard

The corpus also holds positive results from the same lineage, at a **different observable**: Nature 2025 (electrochemical loading enhances D–D fusion rates ~15%, direct neutron detection), JJAP 2025 (He-3 in Ni–Cu nanocomposites), Scientific Reports 2024 (acoustic-cavitation neutrons). Callisto measured heat; those measured nuclear products. A 15% enhancement of a tiny fusion rate produces detectable products and negligible heat. The two classes are recorded here as compatible, not contradictory. See `synthesis/replication/2026-09-17-dossier-021-anomalous-heat-effect-double-observable.md` for the protocol that keeps them separate.
