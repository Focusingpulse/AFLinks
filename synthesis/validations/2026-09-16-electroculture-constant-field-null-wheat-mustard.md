# Validation — Constant Magnetic Field, No Effect on Germination

**Domain:** electro-culture / magneto-culture
**Recorded:** 2026-09-16 (Replication Watch)

## What was claimed

That pre-sowing magnetic-field treatment of seeds improves germination and early growth — a widespread claim in the electro-culture / magneto-culture lineage, where many studies report positive effects at various field strengths and exposures.

## Who replicated it, when

I. V. Ivanov and Lyudmila Sokolova. Published in *BIO Web of Conferences* (2025), vol. 160, art. 01014 — conference proceedings, not a journal article.

## Method

Spring-wheat and white-mustard seeds were germinated in a constant magnetic field of induction ≤14 mT, with magnets oriented to expose the seeds to either the north or the south pole. Two experiments were run: (1) three variants (control / north / south) with fourfold repetition; (2) two variants (control / north) with eightfold repetition. Dry seedling mass was the primary measure; moisture absorption and residual seed magnetization were also checked.

## Result

Variance analysis at the 0.95 probability level found no increase in seedling dry mass from the magnetic field, in either the monocot or the dicot crop, and no dependence on field direction. No elevated moisture absorption and no residual magnetization were detected. The authors concluded that magnetic treatment of germinating seed of these crops is impractical under these conditions.

## Confidence: medium

A clear, pre-stated null with replicated arms and a high repetition count — the kind of negative result that is usually under-published. Limits: modest field strength (≤14 mT, below the ~45–500 mT used in many positive reports), only two species, only dry-mass and moisture endpoints, and conference-proceedings rather than journal peer review.

## Source

Ivanov, I. V., Sokolova, L. "Seeds germination in constant magnetic field." *BIO Web of Conferences* 160, 01014 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1051/bioconf/202516001014
