# Validation — The 1988 Nature Investigation of High-Dilution Basophil Degranulation

**Domain:** water memory / high dilution (homeopathy-adjacent)
**Recorded:** 2026-09-22 (Replication Seeder)

## What was claimed

That an antiserum against human IgE, diluted far past the point at which any molecule of it could remain, still triggers **degranulation of human basophils** — i.e. that water retains a biologically active imprint of a solute it no longer contains. The protocol required violent shaking (*succussion*) at each dilution step, the signature move of the homeopathic preparation method.

The paper: Davenas, Beauvais, Amara, Oberbaum, Robinzon, Miadonnai et al., "Human basophil degranulation triggered by very dilute antiserum against IgE," ***Nature* 333** (30 June 1988): 816–818, doi 10.1038/333816a0. Nature published it **with an editorial disclaimer** — the editors stated the conclusions "have no physical basis" — and as an explicit condition of publication asked that independent laboratories replicate the result.

The Yard's water-memory dossier (028) cites this episode in one sentence as history. This record sets down what actually happened: who, when, what they ran, what they got.

## Who replicated it, when

A three-person investigative team sent by *Nature*, arriving at the INSERM laboratory (U200, Clamart, Paris) about a week after publication:

- **Sir John Maddox** — *Nature*'s editor and a physicist
- **Walter W. Stewart** — chemist, NIH; a fraud investigator who had previously raised questions about a Nobel laureate's work
- **James Randi** — stage magician and professional skeptic

With the cooperation of Benveniste and his team. Report published as Maddox, Randi & Stewart, "'High-dilution' experiments a delusion," ***Nature* 334** (28 July 1988): 287, doi 10.1038/334287a0, with Maddox's follow-up editorial at *Nature* 334:367 (4 Aug 1988).

Benveniste's reply ran at *Nature* 335:759 (Oct 1988).

## Method

**Seven attempts in total, in two stages.**

*Stage 1 — the laboratory's own protocol (four attempts).* The team watched the lab run its existing assay. **Three of the first four attempts came out somewhat favourable to Benveniste.** The investigators were explicit that they did not accept these: the methodology was not rigorous enough for the readings to mean anything.

*Stage 2 — the team's own design (three attempts).* **Benveniste invited the team to write a stricter procedure.** They did:

- samples **randomized**;
- tubes **coded**, with the code key wrapped in tinfoil by Randi and taped to the ceiling;
- the readings taken with the operators blind to the codes;
- and — the detail that makes this a clean record — **before the codes were broken the team asked whether anyone present had any objection to the procedure. None was raised.**

## Result

**The three blinded attempts were negative.**

*Nature*'s published conclusion: the laboratory had "fostered and then cherished a delusion about the interpretation of its data." **No fraud was alleged.** The team's stated mechanism was **unintentional observer bias** — they reported that multiple readings of the same samples in the *non-blind* runs agreed more closely with each other than statistics would predict, which is the signature of an operator (consciously or not) closing the gap.

**Both parties' own conflicts were printed in the same report**, which is worth keeping: *Nature* noted that the salaries of two of Benveniste's colleagues were paid by a French manufacturer of homeopathic medicines — and that the same firm had paid the investigators' hotel bill.

**Benveniste's objection, on the record:** the protocol used by the investigators was not identical to his own; his paper's Table 1 legend stated that the **dilutions** were double-coded but the **reading** was not. He refused to withdraw the claim.

## The rest of the replication record (so this is not read as one bad week)

The 1988 rung is not the end of the story, and the Yard should hold the whole shape of it:

| Year | Who | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **1988** | Maddox / Stewart / Randi (*Nature*) | Three blind randomized attempts at the basophil assay — **negative** (this record) |
| **1993** | Hirst, Hayes, Burridge, Pearce & Foreman, ***Nature* 366**:525–527 | "Human basophil degranulation is not triggered by very dilute antiserum against human IgE" — independent attempt, **negative** |
| **1999** | Belon, Cumps, Ennis, Mannaioni, Sainte-Laudy, Roberfroid et al., ***Inflammation Research* 48**:17–18 | A European multi-centre trial reports *inhibition* of basophil degranulation by successive histamine dilutions — a **positive** claim, from a different assay |
| **2002** | BBC *Horizon*, with Martin Bland (St George's Hospital Medical School) | Tested that claim directly; announced "absolutely no evidence at all" of a difference between the solution that started as pure water and the one that started with histamine — **null** |
| **2001–2006** | Wayne Jonas et al. for **DARPA**; US DoD-funded team, published *FASEB Journal* 20(1):23–28 (2006), doi 10.1096/fj.05-3815hyp | Independently tested Benveniste's later **"digital biology"** claim (that the activity can be recorded as an electromagnetic signal and played back into untreated water). They used **Benveniste's own apparatus**. Result: "**Our team found no replicable effects from digital signals.**" Positive readings appeared **only when one particular Benveniste researcher was running the equipment** — an experimenter effect Benveniste acknowledged having noticed himself. |

The FASEB study is notable for the method as much as the result: it deliberately built a **conflict-management process** into the experiment so that the parties could work together at all — an explicit attempt to avoid a repeat of 1988. There was no fraud allegation in it either.

## Confidence: high for the 1988 rung; high for 1993 and 2006; medium for 1999/2002

- **1988 — high.** Reported in the primary literature and confirmed across independent accounts of the affair. The design is described in enough detail to be gradeable: named investigators, named protocol, randomized coded samples, blind reading, objections solicited before unblinding, and a published mechanism (unintentional bias) rather than an accusation.
- **1993 and 2006 — high.** Both are named, cited, and independently verifiable. **2006 is the strongest single rung in the whole chain** because the replication used the claimant's own hardware and the claimant participated as a consultant — the design removes almost every "you did it wrong" escape.
- **1999 and 2002 — medium here.** These two rungs come from the corpus's own water-trajectory note (`navigator/trajectory/2026-09-16-water-as-information-medium.md`) and general accounts; this run verified their existence and direction but not the per-laboratory numbers.
- **What none of this establishes:** that water cannot hold structure. It establishes that **this claim — a persistent biologically active imprint at high dilution — was tested under blind conditions by named parties, at least four times over eighteen years, and did not replicate.** The corpus's own reading of the residue is worth keeping: Beauvais (2024) reads the positive fragments as **experimenter-dependence**, not as water (per the water-trajectory note).

## Honest framing

We hold no brief — the test is the point. This is recorded as **attempted / measured / reported**: a claim was published, the publishing journal sent its own investigators, the claimant himself designed the blind protocol they used, and the blind runs came back negative — followed over the next eighteen years by an independent *Nature* negative, a positive claim from a different assay that failed its own televised re-test, and a DARPA-requested attempt using the claimant's own machine that found the effect only when one particular person operated it.

That last detail is the transferable lesson, and it is the reason this record matters to the Yard rather than only to historians: **an effect that follows the operator and not the apparatus is the failure mode no home replication can skip.** It is why Dossier 028's protocol pre-registers its endpoints and scores its crystal arm blind, and why every card in the water rail has to state who held the thermometer.
