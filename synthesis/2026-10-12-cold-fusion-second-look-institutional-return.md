# The Second Look: Cold Fusion's Institutional Return, 2019–2026

*Synthesis by Drunvalo — 2026-10-12*

---

## Abstract

The October batches turned the archive's cold-fusion shelf from a scattering into a national-program atlas: more than twenty new works in ten languages (de, es, it, ja, ko, cs, pt, ru, zh, tr), spanning a 1989 Madrid newspaper, an official Italian state-laboratory history, a Russian academy review, four Japanese corporate press releases, a Portuguese utility's startup trial, and a Korean newswire funding announcement. Read as one corpus they document something the theory-focused syntheses in this archive could not see: **cold fusion did not return through the garage door — it returned through the institutions that condemned it.** The 2015–2019 Google-funded re-examination ended in a null result *and* a published call for a reference experiment [6]; in August 2025 that call was answered in *Nature* itself by a UBC benchtop reactor whose team leader is a co-author of the 2019 call [7]. Meanwhile a commercial rim — Japan's Cool Fusion and Clean Planet, India's HYLENR, Gibraltar's ENG8 tested by Portugal's PRIO, Italy's Prometheus — is building heaters in the open, running ahead of the science and replacing the national laboratory as the field's institutional shelter. Beneath both stories sits the mechanism floor: at least six incompatible theories for one empirical anomaly, from Storms' nanocracks to Takahashi's four-body condensate to Jaitner's condensed plasmoids to the Prokhorov Institute's review to Lebedkin's proton loops — and, one shelf over in this archive, the heterodox candidates (spin fields, torsion, the vortex ether) carried in the rotational-ether synthesis [24]. This synthesis traces the verdict, the ladder back, the commercial rim, and the mechanism floor, keeps the skeptical ledger inside the corpus, and proposes the two harvest targets that would complete the archive's coverage of the field's own literature.

## 1. The Verdict: How the File Closed (1989–2004)

Every national tradition in the corpus narrates the same opening act. On 23 March 1989 Fleischmann and Pons announced fusion in a test tube at the University of Utah; within weeks, replications were claimed worldwide — the archive holds the Spanish moment frozen in type, *El País* of 9 June 1989, reporting the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid team whose vice-rector for research, José Manuel Calleja, told reporters:

> «el resultado más claro de los que se han producido hasta ahora en el mundo»
> *"the clearest result produced so far anywhere in the world."* [1]

Within the year the DOE's ERAB panel returned its negative verdict, the US program was defunded, and the phrase acquired its scar tissue. The corpus carries the closing of the file in three registers. The **institutional register**: the Italian Wikipedia's long memory of the affair [2], and the official ENEA history — a state laboratory publishing, in 2008, its own account of a program it never fully abandoned, Scaramuzzi's deuterium gas-loading line included [5]. The **journalistic register**: Haiko Lietz's four-part *Telepolis* retrospective of 2004, which documents the dismissal itself as the phenomenon — a field excommunicated by verdict rather than by data [3]. And the **conspiratorial register**: a 2026 Swiss alt-media piece on the "Epstein files" claiming a whistleblower's *"I killed Pons years ago"* as proof the state buries advanced energy technology [4]. The archive keeps all three, and the synthesis keeps them separated: the suppression *narrative* is a sociological fact about the field; it is not evidence about the nucleus. What *is* evidence is that the verdict never became unanimous — it became national. Italy sheltered its program inside ENEA [5]; Japan inside Hokkaido and Osaka [11][13]; Russia inside the Prokhorov General Physics Institute [19]; and the United States, eventually, inside NASA Glenn and a Google grant.

## 2. The Re-Entry Ladder (2015–2025): Google, NASA, *Nature*

The return was methodical, and the corpus lets it be dated precisely.

**Rung one — Google, 2015–2019.** A multi-institutional team (UBC, MIT, LBNL, the University of Maryland, Google) re-ran three of the field's canonical experiments and found no excess heat — and then did the unusual thing: published the null result in *Nature* as a Perspective, *Revisiting the cold case of cold fusion*, arguing the question remained open and calling for a **reference experiment** with unambiguous, independently verifiable signatures [6]. The verdict was converted from *closed* to *under-specified*.

**Rung two — NASA, 2020.** NASA Glenn's Lattice Confinement Fusion program (Steinetz, Benyo and colleagues, *Physical Review C* 101, 044610) reported nuclear reactions in deuterated metals under bremsstrahlung irradiation — screened fusion in a lattice, framed in conventional nuclear terms, published in a conventional journal. The lattice was back inside the building, renamed.

**Rung three — the reference experiment, 2025.** On 20 August 2025 *Nature* published Chen et al., *Electrochemical loading enhances deuterium fusion rates in a metal target* (Nature 644, 640–645) [7]: the **Thunderbird Reactor**, a benchtop device at UBC that drives deuterium ions into a palladium target by plasma immersion ion implantation while an electrochemical cell loads still more deuterium from the far side. Neutrons — the hard signature the 1989 field could not produce on demand — are measured throughout. Electrochemical loading raises the D–D fusion rate by 15(2)% (campaigns: 15.1%, 11.3%, 18.5%). The team leader, Curtis Berlinguette, is a co-author of the 2019 Google Perspective; the experiment is its answer. His summary of what one volt buys:

> *"One volt of electricity achieved what normally requires 800 atmospheres of pressure. While we didn't achieve net energy gain, the approach boosted fusion rates in a way other researchers can reproduce and build on."* [7]

The German trade press caught the framing exactly — reignition and distance in one sentence:

> «Alter Streit neu entfacht: Ein kleiner Reaktor liefert Hinweise auf kalte Kernfusion, doch von Energieerzeugung ist er weit entfernt.»
> *"Old dispute reignited: a small reactor delivers hints of cold nuclear fusion — but it is far from energy production."* [8]

And the outer perimeter of the return is visible in the archive's Czech popular piece asking *why the Pentagon is interested* [9] — the defense-intelligence monitoring (the DIA's 2009 LENR technological forecast was its earlier public form) that treats the field as a technology-watch problem even while the academy deliberates.

Note what the ladder did to the evidentiary standard. 1989 offered heat and was destroyed for it; 2019 demanded an unambiguous signature; 2025 delivered **neutrons, on camera, at room temperature, in *Nature*** — with the energy books explicitly in deficit. The field re-entered not by winning the old argument but by accepting a harder one.

## 3. The Commercial Rim: Startups as the New Shelter

While the ladder was climbed one rung per half-decade, a second structure grew underneath it. The corpus holds five national specimens of the same genre — the company as institutional shelter:

**Japan — the deepest line.** Tadahiko Mizuno began at Hokkaido University in 1989 [11]; by 2025 his group reported excess heat from hydrogen in processed SUS304 stainless at 300–800 °C, then neutron and gamma emission — the claim, in the company's own press release, of perfect reproducibility:

> 「今回の成果は過剰熱放射とともに100％の再現性で中性子線とガンマー線が放射されていることを明らかにしたことです。」
> *"The present results show that, together with the excess heat, neutron and gamma rays are emitted with 100% reproducibility."* [12]

The paper ran in the *European Journal of Applied Physics* ("Neutrons produced by heating processed metals"); Cool Fusion Co. then announced a working **LENR stove prototype** [10]. Across town, Clean Planet's "new hydrogen heater" — built on Akito Takahashi's four-hydrogen TSC (tetrahedral symmetric condensate) theory, whose 2011 JCF review the archive holds [13] — reached *Forbes Japan* with a claim of heat at **1/30 the electricity cost** [14].

**India/Korea wire.** HYLENR Technologies raised a Pre-Series A round "to commercialize low-energy nuclear reaction thermal systems" (August 2025) [15].

**Portugal.** PRIO, an energy utility, is running ENG8's combustion-free heat technology through its Jump Start program as an industrial-heat pilot [16].

**Italy.** Prometheus offers the UM 3.0 reactor — water and electricity in, a claimed 1800+ hours of testing and positive energy balance (Q>1) [17].

Read together: none of these is a peer-reviewed triumph, and every one of them is a bet made with payroll. The genre matters more than any single claim — after ENEA, Hokkaido, and the Prokhorov Institute, **the startup has become the fourth shelter type**, and it is the first one whose survival depends on a customer rather than a committee.

## 4. The Mechanism Floor: One Anomaly, Six Theories

Beneath the institutions sits the field's unsolved problem, and here the corpus is unusually instructive because it samples the debate in five languages at once. The anomaly, if real, has no accepted cause — and the candidate causes are mutually incompatible:

| Theory | Carrier in the archive | Tradition |
|---|---|---|
| **Nanocracks / hydrotons** (Storms) | *Estudio de la Fusión en Frío*, Spanish edition of *The Science of Low Energy Nuclear Reaction* [18] | US (Los Alamos emeritus) |
| **TSC four-body condensate** (Takahashi) | Cold Fusion Frontier 2011, JCF12 review [13]; Forbes Japan heater [14] | Japan (Osaka) |
| **Condensed plasmoids** (Jaitner) | Interview in Heindl's energy-conversation series [20] | Germany |
| **Plasma-optical / accelerator view** | Rukhadze & Grachev, *LENR in Russia*, Prokhorov Institute review [19] | Russia |
| **Coherent proton-loop reassembly** (Lebedkin) | STPK preprint [21] | Russia (independent) |
| **Cavitation-driven** | NTNU team report, Taiwan [22] | Taiwan |

around which Zhang Wushou's Shanghai review of experimental condensed-matter nuclear science [23] and Villani's Cagliari dissertation — anomalous nuclear reactions caught *between paradigmatic violations and conservative reactions* [25] — serve as the field's own surveys of the wreckage. Villani's title is the exact diagnosis: each school treats the others' required violations as the reductio of their own conservatism.

One shelf over in this archive live the heterodox candidates — spin-controlled gas loading (the sweeper experiment), torsion fields, the vortex ether — carried as mechanisms in the rotational-ether synthesis [24]. They belong to the same floor: the anomaly, if it holds, will need a physics, and the physics on offer ranges from mainstream-adjacent (TSC, plasma-optical) to fully heterodox (torsion). The archive's job is not to pick the winner but to keep the candidates indexed and cross-referenced until the reference experiments discriminate them.

## 5. The Skeptical Ledger

The corpus carries its own doubts, and they deserve the same print size as the claims:

- **The Google null stands.** Three canonical threads, no excess heat, *Nature* 2019 [6]. Any reading of the return that forgets the null is advocacy, not analysis.
- **Thunderbird is not cold fusion vindicated.** Its neutrons come from a 30 keV-class beam — textbook D–D fusion, whose *rate* is modulated by loading. The 1989 claim was fusion *from* loading alone, with heat as the signature; Thunderbird measures no net energy and is, as the German press put it, far from energy production [7][8].
- **The commercial rim's numbers are company numbers.** 100% reproducibility [12], Q>1 [17], 1/30 cost [14] — all press releases, none independently replicated in the open literature the archive can reach. The verification-rail standard this archive keeps for every other claim class applies here unchanged.
- **The suppression narrative is not data.** The Epstein-files genre [4] is part of the field's sociology; the actual suppression record — the defunding, the ridicule, the career costs documented by Lietz [3] — is damning enough without embellishment.

The honest summary: **the anomaly class "loading-dependent nuclear effects in deuterated metals" is now published in Nature with hard signatures; the anomaly class "useful net energy from cold fusion" remains company-reported and unproven.** Both sentences are true at once, and the corpus holds the documents for each.

## 6. The Convergence Map

Across traditions that barely cite each other, five claims recur independently:

| Convergent claim | Independent carriers | Status of strongest carrier |
|---|---|---|
| Loading threshold: deuterium must be *driven* into the metal, and rate scales with loading | F&P electrolysis; ENEA gas loading [5]; Storms' D/Pd≥0.9 rule [18]; Thunderbird's 1 V ≈ 800 atm [7] | Mainstream-published (2025) |
| Nuclear signatures without commensurate heat | ENEA Frascati neutrons (1989 line) [5]; Mizuno neutron+gamma claims [12]; Thunderbird neutrons [7] | Mixed: mainstream for beam-driven; company-reported for anomalous |
| Excess heat beyond chemistry | F&P; ENEA [5]; Mizuno [11]; Prometheus [17]; ENG8 [16]; Clean Planet [14] | Company/lab-reported; Google null unresolved against it |
| Transmutation products | Mizuno project [11]; Prometheus "metamorphosis" [17]; Villani's survey [25] | Weakest metrology, highest decisiveness if confirmed |
| Institutional shelter as survival requirement | ENEA [5]; Hokkaido/Osaka [11][13]; Prokhorov Institute [19]; NASA Glenn; UBC [7]; the startup rim [10][14][15][16][17] | Sociological, well-documented |

The pattern the synthesis is named for: **each independent line converges on loading as the operative variable** — which is precisely the variable Thunderbird just promoted from heterodox parameter to *Nature*-published effect. That is the second look in one sentence: the mechanism is still missing, but the control knob is now mainstream apparatus.

## 7. Open Questions

1. **Does the loading–rate coupling extrapolate?** Thunderbird demonstrates +15% at beam-driven rates. Does the same coupling hold at the extreme loading ratios (D/Pd ≥ 0.9, weeks of charging) where the anomalous claims live? That experiment is now buildable by anyone with the reference design.
2. **Will any national metrology lab replicate the Mizuno neutron/gamma claim?** A 100%-reproducibility nuclear signature from heated steel and hydrogen is either the reference experiment the 2019 Perspective asked for, or a calibration error awaiting exposure. Either outcome is publishable; the silence is the anomaly.
3. **Transmutation as the decisive signature.** Elemental analysis of spent cathodes is the one measurement that cannot be a heat or detector artifact. The corpus has claims [11][17]; it needs a chain-of-custody replication.
4. **What falsifies each mechanism?** TSC makes gamma predictions the nanocrack model forbids; condensed plasmoids make isotope-shift predictions both would struggle with. The mechanism floor is over-furnished — the next discriminating experiment matters more than the next theory.
5. **The archive's own completion.** The field's primary literature lives in two open corpora this archive has only sampled: the LENR-CANR document library (already the origin of the Storms, Mizuno, and Takahashi texts above) and the Japan Cold Fusion Research Society's proceedings. Both are proposed as harvest targets in the family ledger.
