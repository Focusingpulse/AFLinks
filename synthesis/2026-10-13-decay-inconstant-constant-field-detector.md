# The Inconstant Constant: Radioactive Decay as a Field Detector

*Synthesis by Drunvalo — 2026-10-13*

---

## Abstract

The decay constant is the quietest quantity in physics — the one parameter the whole measurement system of radioactivity is built to treat as beyond influence. Over the last two weeks the archive's translation batches assembled, almost accidentally, a complete dossier of the claim that it is not: that radioactive decay rates are structured by the environment in the widest sense — by the anisotropy of space (Shnoll's sixty-year cosmophysical program at Moscow and Pushchino) [1][2], by the Earth–Sun distance and by a single solar flare in December 2006 (Jenkins and Fischbach at Purdue, fought out in *Astroparticle Physics*) [3][4], by rotating masses at 8,000 rpm (Melnik and Mokeev at the Siberian Physical-Technical Institute) [7], and by directed human intention (Krasnikov's "information induction"; the Chinese institutional qigong program's americium-241 experiments at Tsinghua) [8][14]. What makes this lane different from most of the archive is that **the counter-ledger exists inside the same journals**: Norman et al.'s 22-nuclide reanalysis answering Jenkins–Fischbach in *Astroparticle Physics* itself [5], and Pommé et al.'s twelve-dataset metrology verdict "Is decay constant?" — no, beyond instrumental instabilities, it is not inconstant [6]. The anomaly is real as a documented, unresolved disagreement between high-quality instruments. And one detail keeps the pattern: across five independent traditions that share almost no citations, **the instrument chosen is the same** — a counted alpha or beta source — and twice over, the witness isotope is the same one: americium-241, with plutonium-239 beside it. Decay counting is where every program in this archive meets the same wall: an effect that lives permanently at the edge of Poisson statistics, large enough to structure a histogram, too small to survive a metrology institute's ionization chamber. This document traces the claim chain, carries both ledgers in full, and asks what would settle it.

## 1. The Constant That Reports

Radioactive decay is the metrologist's sacred cow. Pommé and his co-authors at the European Commission's JRC Geel laboratory state exactly what is at stake when anyone proposes to disturb it:

> "Some authors have raised doubt about the invariability of decay constants, which would invalidate the exponential-decay law and the foundation on which the common measurement system for radioactivity is based." [6]

This is why the claim chain in this document matters more than its individual results. Half-lives are not merely nuclear data; they are the clock against which radiometric dating, nuclear medicine dosimetry, reactor engineering, and the entire radionuclide traceability chain are calibrated. A modulation of the decay constant — even at the permille level — is not a curiosity. It is a report from a sensor nobody knew they had.

And decay counting is, on paper, the ideal field transducer. The source is a sealed physical object, decoupled from the laboratory environment by every mechanism standard physics recognizes. The signal is a stream of discrete, timestamped events with known statistics — Poisson — so deviations have an exact null model to be tested against. The instrument needs no calibration against the influence being sought. If there is a subtle field anywhere in this archive that couples to matter, the decay counter is the cleanest microphone one could point at it.

Five independent research programs, in four countries, across seventy years, reached for this microphone. Almost none of them cite each other. That is what makes the convergence worth tracing.

## 2. Shnoll: The Fine Structure of Noise

Simon El'evich Shnoll did not set out to disturb nuclear physics. A biochemist at Moscow State University, he spent the 1950s measuring the fine structure of fluctuation in biochemical reaction rates — and found that the shapes of his histograms were not random. The same shapes recurred: in different experiments, at the same local time; in different laboratories, simultaneously; with periodicities locked to the sidereal day and the sidereal year — the Earth's rotation against the fixed stars, not against the Sun.

The program that grew out of this observation ran for over six decades and eventually settled on the most controlled random process available: alpha decay of plutonium-239, measured second by second in a purpose-built apparatus at the Pushchino Institute — a databank running continuously since 7 July 2000 [1][2]. Its peer-reviewed flagship, published in *Uspekhi Fizicheskikh Nauk* in 2000, states the claim precisely [1]:

- The fine structure of statistical distributions — the *shape* of the histogram of a random process — is not itself random. It changes regularly with time.
- Histograms of *independent, simultaneous* processes (decay at Pushchino, noise in a resistor elsewhere, biochemical kinetics elsewhere still) are similar to each other at the same local time, and the similarity recurs after a sidereal day, a sidereal month, a sidereal year.
- During solar eclipses, a characteristic histogram shape appears at the moment of maximum eclipse, repeatedly.

Shnoll's interpretation, stated cautiously across hundreds of papers: the histogram shape is a record of the anisotropy of the space through which the laboratory moves — a "cosmophysical factor," gravitationally or cosmologically sourced, that biases the fine detail of every stochastic process on Earth. The noise has a geography.

The mainstream reception is the honest center of this story: the effect was never reproduced outside the Pushchino methodological chain. The histogram-shape metric depends on choices — binning, smoothing, the shape-similarity algorithm — that resist blinded transfer to other laboratories, and no independent metrology group has confirmed the sidereal periodicities under pre-registered conditions. Shnoll's own framing of the result as an anomaly of the *form* of noise rather than its magnitude made it nearly unfalsifiable by conventional replication: you cannot disprove that two histograms "resemble" each other without agreeing on what resemblance means.

And yet the claim refuses to die, because every few years a different instrument, in a different country, in a different metaphysics, reports something with the same silhouette. The Tomsk group states their debt directly:

> «В работе С.Э.Шноля и др. [1] причина образования тонкой структуры форм гистограмм не определена. Хотя в одной из публикаций исследуется воздействие быстро вращающегося массивного тела на форму функций распределения флуктуаций скорости альфа-распада.»

> *"In the work of S.E. Shnoll et al. [1], the cause of the fine structure of histogram shapes was not determined — although one of the publications does investigate the effect of a rapidly rotating massive body on the shape of the distribution functions of alpha-decay rate fluctuations."* [7]

The Tomsk authors take Shnoll's open question — what structures the noise? — and answer it with their own candidate field. We will meet their experiment in section 5. First, the episode that made decay variability a mainstream-journal controversy.

## 3. Purdue and the Sun

In August 2008, Jere Jenkins and Ephraim Fischbach of Purdue University, with four co-authors, posted an analysis that detonated quietly across nuclear metrology. Two archival datasets of long-term decay counting — silicon-32 (and its daughter chlorine-36) measured at Brookhaven National Laboratory, and radium-226 measured at the Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt in Germany — each showed small, unexplained periodic fluctuations. The Purdue group compared them:

> "We show from an analysis of the raw data in these experiments that the observed fluctuations are strongly correlated in time, not only with each other, but also with the distance between the Earth and the Sun. Some implications of these results are also discussed, including the suggestion that discrepancies in published half-life determinations for these and other nuclides may be attributable in part to differences in solar activity during the course of the various experiments, or to seasonal variations in fundamental constants." [3]

The modulation is permille-scale — a fractional change of order 3×10⁻³ in the ²²⁶Ra counting rate between perihelion and aphelion — and phased to the 1/R² Earth–Sun distance law. Their candidate carrier: the solar neutrino flux, which falls off with the same 1/R². The paper appeared in *Astroparticle Physics* in 2009 and has accumulated over 170 citations — the largest mainstream beachhead any decay-variability claim has ever held [3].

Then came the flare. Reviewing their own laboratory's counting data, Jenkins and Fischbach found that a 1 μCi manganese-54 source at Purdue had registered a dip in its decay rate coincident with the solar flare of 13 December 2006:

> "We report the detection of a significant decrease in the decay of ⁵⁴Mn during the solar flare of 13 December 2006, whose x-rays were first recorded at 02:37 UT. … A secondary peak in the x-ray and proton fluxes on 17 December at 12:40 EST was also accompanied by a coincident dip in the ⁵⁴Mn decay rate." [4]

The dip tracked the GOES satellite X-ray and proton records twice in four days. In the body of the paper the authors make the extraordinary interpretation explicit: if neutrinos from the flare caused the dip, those neutrinos "would have traversed approximately 9,270 km of Earth" — the chord through the planet to the Purdue basement at 02:37 UT — before registering in the decay of the sample [4]. A decay counter as a neutrino detector of a type no one designed.

Peter Sturrock's group at Stanford added statistical weight through the early 2010s: power-spectrum analyses of the BNL and PTB series reporting lines near 11 yr⁻¹ and 12.7 yr⁻¹, the latter associated speculatively with rotation dynamics of the solar interior (the Rieger periodicity known from solar-flare statistics). The decay constant, in this reading, is a seismograph of the Sun's core.

## 4. The Counter-Ledger, in the Same Journal

This archive is full of claims whose skeptics live outside the literature. This lane is different — and the difference is its greatest documentary value. The refutations were published in the *same* journal, by groups with the best instruments on Earth.

First, Eric Norman's group at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory reanalyzed their own long-running counting programs — 22 nuclides, including some of the same isotopes:

> "Evidence *against* correlations between nuclear decay rates and Earth–Sun distance" — *Astroparticle Physics* 31 (2009) 135–137. [5]

No annual modulation, in datasets at least as good as BNL's and PTB's. Then, across a decade, Stefaan Pommé and collaborators at JRC Geel — one of the world's reference metrology laboratories for radioactivity — assembled the systematic verdict. Twelve independent long-term decay datasets, spanning alpha, beta-minus, beta-plus, and electron-capture nuclides, examined for oscillations at every frequency from 0.08 to 20 yr⁻¹:

> "Besides small annual effects, no common oscillations could be observed among α, β−, β+ or EC decaying nuclides. The amplitudes of fitted oscillations to residuals from exponential decay do not exceed 3 times their standard uncertainty, which varies from 0.00023% to 0.023%. … There is also no significant modulation at 12.70 yr⁻¹, contrary to claims by Sturrock et al. … This contradicts the assertion that 'neutrino-induced' beta decay provides information about the deep solar interior." [6]

The small annual effects that *do* appear are tracked to the instruments themselves — environmental sensitivity of ionization chambers and electronics, varying from one apparatus to the next. The paper's keyword list, unusually for a metrology journal, includes the phrase "Cognitive bias" [6]. The skeptical ledger here names its own diagnosis: the modulation lives in the analysis pipeline, not in the nucleus.

Where this leaves the Purdue claim: unresolved in the strict sense. The BNL and PTB datasets really do contain the reported periodicities — nobody disputes the Fourier peaks' presence in those two series. What is disputed is their origin: solar neutrinos (Jenkins, Fischbach, Sturrock), instrument and environment (Norman, Pommé), or analysis-selection effects. No pre-registered, blinded, multi-laboratory dataset has ever been collected to settle it. The situation is the archive's familiar one — but at the highest instrumental level it has ever reached: **a genuine, documented disagreement between excellent instruments, carried in full in the peer-reviewed record.**

## 5. Tomsk: Rotation Touches the Nucleus

The Russian torsion school approaches the same instrument from the opposite direction. If decay rates report the state of the vacuum, then *engineering* the vacuum locally — with a rotating mass — should move the decay rate on command. The archive holds the full claim chain of the Tomsk group's test of exactly this.

The experiment, reported by I.A. Melnik and D.Yu. Mokeev at the Siberian Physical-Technical Institute and framed explicitly as an independent-expert replication of earlier rotation experiments, is disarmingly simple [7]. The source: americium-241 with a plutonium-239 admixture, counted by a GaAs semiconductor detector resolving the 5485.6 keV (Am) and 5155 keV (Pu) alpha peaks simultaneously — the two isotopes serving as each other's internal control. On the same vertical axis as source and detector, an asynchronous motor spins a steel vessel of liquid at 8,000 rpm. Then the motor is switched off, and counting continues.

Their report of the result:

> «Построение гистограммы совокупности всех относительных выборок … убедительно показало, что вращение воздействует на альфа-распад атомного ядра причём, распределение альфа-частиц становится мультиплетным и интенсивности америция падает, а изотопа плутония возрастает… Однофакторный дисперсионный анализ этих распределений определил коэффициент Фишера F=98…»

> *"Constructing the histogram of the full set of relative samples showed convincingly that rotation acts on the alpha decay of the atomic nucleus; the alpha-particle distribution becomes multiplet-structured, the americium intensity falls while the plutonium isotope's rises… One-way analysis of variance of these distributions gave a Fisher coefficient F=98 (critical value 3.85)."* [7]

And on the character of the field they believe they are switching:

> «Экспериментальные результаты последних лет определили свойства неэлектромагнитной компоненты физического поля, генерируемого вращающимися объектами, такие как хиральная поляризованность (т.е. отличие правого от левого), память (метастабильное состояние) и зависимость свойств поля от внутреннего состояния вращающегося объекта.»

> *"Experimental results of recent years have established the properties of the non-electromagnetic component of the physical field generated by rotating objects: chiral polarization (the difference of right from left), memory (a metastable state), and dependence of the field's properties on the internal state of the rotating object — including the defect structure of its crystal lattice, which points to an information-bearing character of the influence."* [7]

Three properties in that sentence deserve slow reading. *Chiral polarization*: the effect distinguishes clockwise from counterclockwise — the signature the torsion program predicts for a spin-coupled field. *Memory*: after the motor stops, the altered distribution relaxes only slowly — a metastable vacuum state, not a force that vanishes with its source. *Mirror symmetry between the two isotopes*: americium down, plutonium up, in the same spectrum, on the same detector — an internal control that rules out every drift that would move the whole spectrum together, and the detail that makes this report more interesting than most of its genre.

The theory spine holding up the interpretation is in the archive alongside the experiment: Melnik's companion papers on registering non-electromagnetic fields and the field of rotating objects [13][15]; Kholodov and Goryachev's comparison of neutron beta-decay models [10]; their synoptic paper mapping Terletsky's particle-quadruple vacuum onto the Shipov, Akimov, and Okhatrin–Tatur vacuum models [12]; and Tatur's "Conservation Laws as a Norm," which re-examines the status of the conservation laws themselves from the discovery of parity violation onward — the theoretical license for expecting that what we call constants are statistical norms of a structured vacuum, holding exactly only on average [11].

The newest entry extends the claim from one instrument to two at once: Tatur, Lukyanitsa and Shishkin (Вестник МАСИ, 2025) report *simultaneous* correlated deviations in radioactive decay statistics and in corona-discharge statistics — the Shnoll signature of cross-instrument synchronism, in a 2025 journal, twenty-five years after the UFN flagship [9].

The skeptical ledger on this leg must be stated plainly: no metrology laboratory has replicated the rotation effect; the Fisher F=98 statistic speaks to the difference between two sample sets but cannot by itself exclude systematic coupling (vibration, EMI, temperature) between motor state and detector; the "metastable after-effect" is precisely the shape an instrumental thermal relaxation would take; and the multiplet structure of the alpha spectrum under rotation has no proposed quantitative mechanism. The Tomsk reports are internally careful — noise exclusion, constant temperature, paired t-tests — but they remain single-laboratory results from a self-described Institute of Vacuum Physics of the Academy of Trinitarianism, outside the interlaboratory traceability chain that Pommé's verdict represents.

## 6. The Intentionality Leg

Two of the archive's traditions do not merely *detect* the supposed field with decay counters — they claim to *steer* it.

I.I. Krasnikov's report, published through the same trinitas channel, is blunt:

> «В опубликованных ранее работах … было продемонстрировано не только искусственное ускорение процесса радиоактивного распада (снижение уровня радиоактивности в результате одноразового воздействия почти на 20%), но также и его замедление (повышение радиоактивного излучения образца на 3%). … был сделан вывод о проявившемся новом виде взаимодействий – информационном.»

> *"In previously published works … not only artificial acceleration of the radioactive decay process was demonstrated (reduction of the radioactivity level by almost 20% as a result of a single exposure), but also its deceleration (increase of the sample's radioactive emission by 3%). … The conclusion was drawn of a new type of interaction manifested — informational — proposed to be assigned to a new field, the 'physics of information'."* [8]

A ±20%/3% bidirectional swing from a single "information induction" exposure is an enormous effect size by this field's standards — three orders of magnitude above the permille modulations of sections 3–5 — and for exactly that reason it belongs in the ledger with the heaviest caveats the archive can attach: single-laboratory, no independent replication, no published raw time series, mechanism undefined. It is recorded here because the archive holds it, and because of what stands beside it.

What stands beside it is the strongest-institutionalized decay-modulation claim on record. The Chinese national external-qi measurement program — traced in this archive's qi–torsion synthesis — took its most famous measurement to the nucleus: experiments on Yan Xin's external qi at Tsinghua University in 1987, published in *Atomic Energy Science and Technology* in 1988, reporting alteration of the americium-241 decay rate under emitted qi, with Lu Zuyin's account in *Qigong Exploration* carrying the program's documentary record [14]. State laboratories, institutional funding, a nuclear-engineering journal — and the same witness isotope as the Tomsk rotation experiment.

Hold the two legs against each other and the pattern sharpens: **the Chinese qi program and the Tomsk torsion program, with no evidence of contact between them, both chose americium-241 as the load-bearing isotope of their central claim.** Am-241 is the standard calibration source of alpha spectrometry worldwide — chosen, in both cases, for the most boring and credible reason there is. The shared instrument is not evidence of the field; it is evidence that five traditions converged on the one detector whose readings no one can dismiss as noise in a homemade apparatus. Every one of these programs understood the same thing: if you claim the constant is inconstant, your counter had better be the metrology community's own.

Krasnikov and the Tsinghua program vs. their absence from every independent dataset. The anomaly is not "proven" or "disproven" — it is *held open* by a symmetry of good instruments, and has been for seventy years.

4. **The interpretation fork is ontological, not evidential.** Given the same silhouette of data — structured deviation at the statistical edge — Shnoll sees the anisotropy of space, Purdue sees the Sun's neutrinos, Tomsk sees spin fields, Krasnikov and Beijing see information and qi, and Geel sees cognitive bias in the analysis pipeline. The data does not choose between these. What chooses is the research program's prior commitment — which is precisely what the verification-rail methodology warns about, and why this dossier carries both ledgers in full rather than resolving them by editorial vote.

## 8. Open Questions

1. **The experiment that would settle it.** A pre-registered, blinded, multi-laboratory decay dataset: one source type (Am-241 for continuity with both claimant traditions), counted simultaneously by metrology-chain ionization chambers and semiconductor spectrometers at three or more sites, with the full environmental telemetry published alongside the counts, and the frequency list fixed in advance. Everyone in this dossier claims to want exactly this experiment. It has never been run. The estimated cost is trivial by Big-Science standards; the obstacle is that no program's survival requires the answer.

2. **The Shnoll databank.** Twenty-six years of second-by-second Pu-239 counts from Pushchino are the most under-analyzed public asset in this lane. A digitized, versioned, openly licensed release would let the histogram-shape claim be tested by people with no stake in either ledger — and would test the palindrome and eclipse effects against modern time-series methods. Proposed to the family ledger as an acquisition and digitization target.

3. **Is decay the transducer or the target?** The Tomsk mirror-symmetry detail (Am down, Pu up, same spectrum) and the Purdue flare dip push in opposite directions: one suggests the field modulates the *decay process* differently per nuclear species; the other, that a solar agent momentarily changes *detection or decay* indifferently. A rotation experiment run on a mixed-isotope source by a laboratory with no torsion commitments would discriminate. Until then the instrument-as-interface question stands: these counters may be measuring the field, the nucleus, or the observer's analysis — and the five traditions disagree on which.

4. **The metrology wall, from the other side.** Pommé's residuals *are* non-zero at the 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ level; his verdict is that they track instruments, not nuclei. A systematic cross-laboratory study of the *instrumental* annual residuals themselves — the accepted, boring, published ones — has never been checked against the cosmophysical hypothesis. It is the one dataset where the skeptical ledger and the anomaly ledger would be analyzing the same numbers.
