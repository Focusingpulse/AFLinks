# The Instrumentation of the Invisible: How the French Form-Wave School Built Detectors for a Physics It Could Not Measure

## Abstract

Every research program faces the gap between what it claims and what it can measure. The French form-wave school — Chaumery, de Belizal, Enel, La Foye, Morel, and their living legatee Guy Thieux — is unusual in that they named the gap themselves and then built an entire apparatus culture inside it. Their own treatise concedes that form waves sit "on the order of millionths and billionths of electron-volts" and that no instrument of their era could record them: "the day such a measurement system is found, we would need radiesthesia less... But we are not there yet." What they did instead — patented pendulums, corrected protective bars, selective emitters, and a safety protocol written in the blood of their own founder — constitutes a coherent response to a missing-instrument problem. This synthesis reads the school's apparatus lineage as an instrumentation program: the Universal Pendulum as a spectrum analyzer, the Escargot and C.30 Bomb as frequency selectors and emitters, the Barre Atlante/Louksor as a corrected shield, the Reciprocal Circles Board as a tunable source, and the Multiple-Wave Oscillator as the one instrument with a contested but real electrobiology pedigree. It then sets the school's own boundary against three convergent measurement claims from independent traditions — Russian torsion detectors (Akimov/Shipov), Enel's Omega as a spiritual marker, and the modern geobiology mainstream (Hartmann grids, EMF dosimetry) — and asks the question the school itself left open: what would it take to close the gap they named?

## 1. The Missing Instrument, Stated by the School Itself

The most under-cited passage in *Physique Micro-Vibratoire et Forces Invisibles* (de Belizal & Morel, Desforges 1965/1976) is the authors' own measurement confession. They situate form waves, in energy terms, near the magnetic waves accompanying cosmic rays — "de l'ordre de millionièmes et milliardièmes d'électrons-volts" — and concede that no physical instrument then existed sensitive enough to record them. Their sentence is a research program in miniature:

> "Le jour où l'on pourrait trouver un tel système de mesures, nous aurions moins besoin de la radiesthésie pour les ondes de forme qui sont davantage du domaine de la physique pure, et deviendraient ainsi partie d'une science exacte. Mais nous n'en sommes pas encore là."
>
> *"The day such a measurement system is found, we would need radiesthesia less, for form waves belong more to pure physics, and would thus become part of an exact science. But we are not there yet."*

Two claims are packed in here. First: form waves are, in the school's own view, *physics* — weak but real, in principle measurable. Second: the pendulum is a *stand-in* for the missing instrument, not a replacement for one. This is a different epistemic posture than either true-belief occultism or orthodox dismissal, and it explains the school's most distinctive product: an apparatus culture built around a detector that is a trained human nervous system.

The 1936 patent (FR 816,132, Chaumery & de Belizal) is the legal embodiment of this posture: they patented an *apparatus* — the Universal Pendulum and cosmo-magnetic batteries — because apparatus was what they could define, even while the field it detected remained below every instrument threshold of the era.

## 2. The Apparatus Chain as an Instrumentation Program

Read in sequence, the school's instruments map onto the standard components of any experimental field: source, detector, selector, shield, and safety protocol.

### 2.1 The Universal Pendulum — spectrum analyzer

The Pendule Universel (1930s, patented 1936) is a spherical wooden pendulum graduated with an equator of "wave heights" (H.O.), magnetic and electric meridians, and both visible and invisible color positions. The operator tunes thread length (octave) and cursor position (color/height); rotation above the corresponding substance indicates resonance. Functionally this is a *swept spectrum analyzer* with a human operator as the null detector — the same role the galvanometer played in early radio. The sphere itself was the school's founding discovery: the (−) pole emitted a vibration corresponding to no known color, named **Negative Green (V−)** — destructive, dehydrating, the "connecting link between life and death," and (in their reading) the mummifying agent of Egyptian funerary practice.

### 2.2 The Escargot and the C.30 Bomb — selector and emitter

Chaumery's initial Escargot ("snail"), perfected by de Belizal and Morel, was a *separator of vibratory frequencies by selection* — a tunable filter for the emitted spectrum. The C.30 Bomb (serialized in *La Radiesthésie pour Tous*, August 1963) was its active counterpart: a radionics emitter that, per the journal's editorial, rested on "the minute study and the implementation of energies exactly calculated for precise effects." The pair also marketed a dual-capacity claim — testing the "physical value" and the "moral value" of personnel — a revealing window into how far the 1963 framing exceeded any measurement warrant.

### 2.3 The Barre Atlante / Louksor — the corrected shield

The Atlante Bar descends from a ring found in 1860 by the Marquis d'Agrain, passed to de Belizal through his wife, the marquis's granddaughter. **Morel's correction is the school's most instructive engineering moment:** he found that the ring's form, flattened and enlarged, *saturated to the point of producing negative green* — the very ray that had killed Chaumery. He modified the ends until the bar no longer had "this drawback," named it the Louksor (after a model from an Egyptian sarcophagus), and defined its effective protective zone (15–20 m practical radius, per the book). The corrected design is still sold by Éditions Servranx today, and reached the geobiology mainstream through Jacques La Maya's *Médecine de l'habitat*. A shield redesigned because it was generating the hazard it claimed to block is a genuine negative result inside the tradition — evidence that the school's internal feedback loop, whatever its detector, was not pure rationalization.

### 2.4 The Reciprocal Circles Board — tunable source

Jean de La Foye's Tablette des cercles réciproques (1960s–1970s, described in *Ondes de vie, ondes de mort*) is an emitter of emissions due to forms: 12/24 color emissions, directing axes drawn from the Hebrew Tetragrammaton, a law of compensation, and north/centre/south circles. Its builder's own framing is the school's epistemic honesty in miniature: the Geepi presentation (Bellovaque, 2017) opens by stating that no autonomous measuring instrument has ever detected these emissions, and that only the radiesthesist's pendulum currently can — *working hypotheses, not recognized truth*.

### 2.5 The safety protocol — written in the founder's death

Chaumery died 27 February 1957, recorded by de Belizal as "victim of his experiments, completely dehydrated by our negative green ray (V−)." The V+ antidote was found only afterwards, with Morel. The tradition thus carries what almost no other subtle-energy lineage has: a casualty, a named cause, a post-hoc countermeasure, and a standing correction culture (Morel's Louksor redesign) built around it. Whatever one makes of the causal claim, the *institutional* response is what experimental safety cultures look like.

## 3. The One Instrument with a Pedigree: the Lakhovsky Thread

The school's apparatus culture has exactly one branch that touches institutional science: Georges Lakhovsky's Multiple-Wave Oscillator (MWO, "Olom" in the French literature). The chain is three-generational and documented in Thieux's own author notes: Georges Lakhovsky (1870–1942, cellular oscillation theory, MWO for cancer therapy) → his son Serge Lakhovsky (1913–2003, director of Colysa France laboratories) → Guy Thieux, who worked with Serge Lakhovsky and Prof. Étienne Guillé at the NYNAPSE molecular-biology association (Orsay, 1975–1985) studying Hertzian dipoles, the radio-cellulo-oscillator, and the MWO's action on cells. Enel used the MWO therapeutically from 1953 until his death in 1963, and Thieux's edition of the *Carnets inédits* (Tome III, *Radiesthésie thérapeutique*, 2019) devotes substantial space to it.

This matters for the synthesis because the MWO is *not* below the measurement threshold — it is a high-voltage broadband RF emitter, and its biological effects were contested in exactly the way institutional science contests things. The French school's embrace of it shows the tradition was not categorically opposed to instrumented physics; it adopted instrumented apparatus where instrumented apparatus existed, and built pendulum-actuated apparatus where it did not.

## 4. Convergent Measurement Claims Across Independent Traditions

The school's gap — a claimed field below every instrument threshold, detected by trained humans — recurs across traditions that did not borrow from each other:

- **Russian torsion physics (Akimov, Shipov):** torsion fields claimed to be extremely weak (the standard comparison was ~10⁻⁰⁹ or weaker relative to electromagnetism), generated by spin and by *shape* (cones, cylinders), and detected by an apparatus culture of torsion detectors — some electronic (EGS-concept), some again human-operated. The shape-generation claim is the direct structural parallel to ondes de forme: both traditions assert that geometry alone emits a subtle field.
- **Enel's Omega:** within the French school itself, Enel's discovery that saints' tombs emanate the *horizontal component* of negative green — named Omega, a marker of spiritual energy — is a measurement claim of a different kind: the same detector, a *semantically different* reading, distinguishing sacred from noxious by a directional component of the same "color." The tradition thus claims its detector has resolving power fine enough to split a ray into components with opposite valence.
- **Modern geobiology (Hartmann, Curry, and the EMF literature):** the contemporary Belgian/French geobiology scene — documented in the Être Plus survey (Jouvet, Sep 2026) — measures what instruments *can* measure (EMF, radioactivity, radon, geological faults, underground water) and then extends to what they cannot (telluric networks, place memory), explicitly treating "felt perception as an instrument, just like a measuring device, provided it is exercised with rigor." The WHO's recognition of domestic EMF pollution as an aggravating factor in stress and sleep disorders gives the discipline a legitimate instrumented core; the telluric extension rides on that credibility.

The convergent pattern across all three: **a two-tier epistemology** — an instrumented tier that anchors credibility, and a human-detector tier that extends beyond it, with the boundary between tiers policed by rigor language ("working hypotheses," "provided it is exercised with rigor") rather than by instruments themselves.

## 5. Western vs. Russian-French Intersections

The Russian and French traditions intersect at exactly one point: **shape as a field source.** The Akimov/Shipov program's torsion generators and the Chaumery/de Belizal sphere experiments arrive at the same claim — geometry emits — from completely different lineages (spin-field theory out of Soviet physics vs. pendulum radiesthesia out of French dowsing societies). Where they diverge is instructive: the Russian program predicted *electronic* detectability and built detector hardware; the French school predicted sub-instrument energies and built human-actuated hardware. Thieux is the hinge where the two meet the institutional tier: a petroleum geophysicist (magnetic, gravimetric, seismic methods — the instrumented Earth sciences) who spent a decade at Orsay on oscillator biology and then carried the Enel manuscripts forward. His biography is the two-tier epistemology lived in one person.

## 6. Open Questions the School Itself Set Up

1. **The named gap, revisited.** The school predicted that form waves would join "an exact science" when measurement caught up. Eight decades later, what has actually caught up: SQUID magnetometers (femtotesla), quantum sensors, the EMF-biology literature (melatonin/sleep effects at µT-level exposure). None of these has detected a form wave — but none was aimed at the claim with the school's own specificity. A falsification-grade experiment would take the school's own operational definitions (V− at sphere point 0–400, the Louksor's 15–20 m protective radius, the Reciprocal Circles Board's color emissions) and test them against instrumented dosimetry in blinded conditions. The tradition's apparatus culture makes this unusually concrete: the emitters, shields, and spectra are specified in enough detail to replicate.
2. **The operator as instrument.** If the pendulum is a stand-in detector, the operator is the sensor element — which makes operator training, blinding, and inter-operator agreement the tradition's real psychophysics frontier. The 1963 "moral value" claims show what happens when this sensor is unpoliced; the school's own rigor language shows they knew it.
3. **The Omega split.** If one detector reads the same ray's horizontal component as sanctity and its vertical component as lethal, the tradition claims an angular resolution in a field no instrument sees. That is the sharpest single testable claim in the lineage — and Enel's tomb data (many tombs, one claimed invariant) is the natural dataset.
