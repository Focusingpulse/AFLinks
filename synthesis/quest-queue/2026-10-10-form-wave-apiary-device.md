## The honest framing (the spine of the card)

The card tests a claim, not a tradition. The form-wave lineage is not being asked to prove a mechanism — it is being asked whether a hive with the device on its roof counts fewer mites than a hive with a plain ball of the same weight on its roof.

Three things the card keeps in front of the family:

1. **The source ran no control, and says so.** Both hives got the device; the author changed its settings while he watched; and he tells the reader plainly that this is not a scientific experiment. The card's whole addition is the second cluster with the inert object.
2. **The source's own earlier version failed.** The 1991 radionic treatment was abandoned as "of a very relative effectiveness" on heavily infested hives — and the author's own verdict on the whole line is that "the effect of the medication on the varroa populations is not mastered." A family should know that before they build anything.
3. **The count is the experiment, and the count is easy to get wrong.** The source's own warnings are the card's method: use a screened bottom board, keep the cloth clear of the mesh, count every ten days, and **clear the ants** — they carry the mites off the cloth and ruin the count. A season of sloppy counts proves nothing either way.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that the **two clusters track each other** — that a hive's mite count is driven by its colony strength, its queen and the weather, and that a passive object on the roof changes none of it. That is the honest verdict, and the card is designed to reach it cleanly. The one thing the card can genuinely establish, whatever the result, is the family's own baseline: a season of ten-day mite counts on their own hives, which is worth having whether or not the form does anything.
