## The honest framing (the spine of the card)

Nobody is being asked to prove that electrogravitics works or that it does not. They are asked one narrow question: **is the Honda weight change a field effect or a fluid effect?**

Four things the card keeps in front of the family:

1. **The mechanism is dead, and this card is not a revival.** Cady's 1952 ONR investigation attributed Brown's effects to ionisation of the surrounding air or oil and measured the tests that discriminate (covering the outboard wire cut thrust from ~7 g to ~1 g; reversing polarity cut it 7.5 → 3 g). The modern vacuum tests agree. Nothing here reopens that.
2. **The effect is real, and this card is not a debunk either.** A charged asymmetric capacitor pushes — that is measured, replicated and now flown. What died was the *explanation*, not the observation. The card's question is about a **weight** measurement, which is a different claim from the thrust.
3. **The revival's number is weak on its own terms — and that is an argument, not a measurement.** 0.29 ± 0.17 g is ~1.7σ from zero; the AC/DC ratio runs opposite to the field-squared law its own rescue theory requires; and the effect sits twenty orders of magnitude below the field scale the mechanism would need. Every one of those is arithmetic *about* the confound. **Nobody has removed the confound.** That is the gap, and it is a one-afternoon gap.
4. **The expected result is the mundane one, and the mundane one is a complete result.** If the change appears in oil and vanishes in the density-matched bath, the anomaly becomes a flow measurement — which is precisely what the archive's own assessor predicts, and which retires the revival's only quantitative result. If it survives the bath and scales as V², that is the first controlled confirmation in thirty years and a major result. **Both are worth having, and the card is written so that either one is a pass.**
