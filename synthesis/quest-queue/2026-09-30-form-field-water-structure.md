## The honest framing (the spine of the card)

**The source's own numbers converge, and the source reads convergence as proof.** Stage I falls 20555 → 13310. Stage II rises 12514 → 17546. Stage III rises 10700 → 15750. **Every arm lands near 15,000.** The source's language gives it away: stage I's change is *"the approach of the structure to stage III"*, and stage III's is *"brought the structure … closer to stage I."* That is a description of **convergence toward a middle state** — and convergence toward a middle state is also precisely what happens when nothing is acting at all: water settles, temperature equilibrates, aggregates dissociate, and a noisy measurement regresses to the mean. **The source's data cannot tell those apart**, because of the next point.

**The percentages are measured against the wrong baseline.** Stage I's "65 %" is the treated sample's *final* value over the *control's initial* value. If the control water also drifted during the same 30 minutes — and the source's own text says its water changes over weeks — then the figure contains the form's action **and** the water's own settling, with no way to separate them. **The card's primary metric is therefore R = treated(final) ÷ control(final), both at the same moment**, which cancels whatever the two cuvettes share. The card also reports the source's own metric, so the two can be compared directly and the difference is visible.

**A sign-flip is the claim and a sign-flip is also the signature of noise.** The source's sharpest prediction is that the *direction* of the change depends on the water's initial state. That is genuinely falsifiable — **and it is exactly what a noisy instrument does.** So the card pre-classifies the sample and **writes the predicted direction before the run**. Without that pre-registration the sign-flip can be explained after the fact either way, and the card would be unfalsifiable in the way the engine's honesty rule forbids.

**The source's control is weak, and the card says so rather than inheriting it.** The control cuvette sat "several metres away" — in the same room, in the same air, and **possibly inside the claimed field**, since the source's own claim is that the form acts "inside and outside" itself. The card adds **arm C**: a cuvette beside the cylinder at the same height, with the paper present but the water not enclosed. If arm B and arm C agree, "the paper is in the room" is ruled out; if they disagree, the room is doing something.

**A scattering method that has not been shown to resolve a known difference is not a measurement.** Water scatters very weakly, and a cheap detector in a bright room will read noise. **The instrument check is mandatory:** if the method cannot separate distilled from tap water — or tap water from tap water with a trace of milk — by more than its own noise floor, then a no-change result is **void, not a null**. "No difference" and "the instrument could not have seen a difference" are different results and get filed identically if the check is skipped.

**Under-promise:** the most likely outcome is **R ≈ 1** — the treated and control cuvettes drift together, and the source's headline table turns out to be the water's own convergence read against a control that was drifting too. That is a complete, useful result: it files the first home test of a 2012 claim the archive has held untested, and it retries a torsion-field lineage the archive already marks `suppressed`. A repeatable R ≠ 1 in the pre-registered direction would be genuinely surprising and would justify the taller follow-up — the layer and position series the source reports as non-uniform.
