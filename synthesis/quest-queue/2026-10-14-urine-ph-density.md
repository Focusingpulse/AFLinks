## The honest framing (the spine of the card)

Nobody is being asked to prove that Methodology 101 works, and nobody is being asked to follow its diet. They are asked one narrow question: **in your own body, do urine pH and urine specific gravity move together the way the source says?**

Four things the card keeps in front of the family:

1. **The source gives a direction and no number.** *"High alkalinity of the urine (high pH) is practically always accompanied by low density."* No threshold, no range, no correlation figure, no cohort, no paper. A claim with a direction and no criterion cannot be falsified as written — so the card writes the criterion, and states it before day 1.
2. **The source refuses to say what a result means.** *"The criteria 'bad/good' are not applied here."* That refusal is the tell: the card's whole point is to produce the number the source declines to publish.
3. **The ordinary explanation is the one to beat.** Diet, hydration and time of day move both numbers. A correlation in the pooled data may be nothing but breakfast — which is exactly why the morning-void subset is the primary control, and why the diet and fluid log is not optional.
4. **This is a measurement, not a treatment.** The card does not test the diet, the supplements, or the "refrain from medications" rule. It does not ask anyone to change what they eat or take. It asks for 42 readings and a plot.
