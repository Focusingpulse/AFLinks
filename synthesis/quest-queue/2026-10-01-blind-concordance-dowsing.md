## What makes it the queue's first of its kind

- **The first card whose endpoint is inter-observer concordance.** Every prior dowsing card (005, 029, 034, 035, 044, 048, 057) scores **one operator against a hidden target**. This one scores the **group against itself**, then against the feature — the question the 1946 ETH study was built to ask.
- **The first card built on a published controlled NULL result.** The source is a negative study, so the expected outcome is a null and a positive is the surprise. (Card 041 is the queue's first *already-refuted claim*, but that is a physics claim overturned by later literature; this is a null-result *study* being replicated.)
- **The first card whose source is the founding controlled dowsing experiment** — the first time a geophysics institute tested dowsers under blind conditions (ETH Zürich, 1944/46).
- **The first within-operator test–retest measurement** — and it runs the exact follow-up the source named and never performed.
- **The design choice that is the whole card:** the 1946 report had **no chance baseline for concordance** — it stated that no concordant areas "stand out from an undoubtedly reaction-free environment," but never computed how much concordance sixteen dowsers marking large areas would produce *by chance*. The card's fix is the arm the source lacked: **a shuffled-map baseline** (each plan rotated by a random offset, the concordance statistic recomputed 1,000 times), so "they agree" is scored against "how much would they agree if their marks were random." The source's own false positives — several dowsers marking **non-existent** mains on a field with **visible shaft covers** — are designed out by removing every surface clue and photographing the field to prove it.

## Honest Framing

This is a test, not an endorsement. The 1946 ETH trial is the earliest controlled
dowsing experiment in the corpus and it found **no** concordant reaction zones and a
55 cm water main detected by **no** dowser — but its author explicitly refused to
conclude that the rod deflection has no physical cause, writing that the experiments
were "evidently laid out too primitively and too summarily." The card keeps that
refusal: a negative result here retires *this protocol's* claim, not the tradition.
The likely outcome is that concordance sits at the shuffled baseline and no agreed
zone lands on the buried hose — the 1946 null reproduced at home scale, which is a
complete and useful result and earns the Skeptic's Star. A concordant zone on the
hidden feature, repeatably, would be genuinely surprising and would justify the
taller follow-up (more fields, more operators, a pre-registered geophysical
measurement on the agreed zone). The card is a physics-and-method test of a cited
claim; it is **never** a substitute for a real utility locate before digging.
