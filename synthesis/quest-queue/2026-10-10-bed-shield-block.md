## What makes it the queue's first of its kind

- **The first card whose independent variable is a BLOCKER — a material placed between a claimed source and a person.** Every prior dowsing card varies the *target* (where the water is, whether the flow is running, whether the feature is hidden); this one holds the target fixed and varies **what is put on top of it**.
- **The first card whose primary endpoint is CROSS-OPERATOR TRANSFER** — not whether one operator can find something, and not whether two operators agree on where it is (that is dossier-059's concordance question), but whether a state **created by one person** changes a **second, blind person's** reading. The source ran this test exactly once, by accident, and reported the negative himself.
- **The first card that tests a rule the source stated as a rule.** *"If someone has placed a block over a flow of radiation, that block is retained until the one who placed it removes it."* The removal arm (A places, A removes; C places, A removes) tests that sentence directly — a differential prediction the source supplied, which the card can falsify.
- **The first card whose blocker set includes a material chosen to be absurd** — a single strand of human hair, and a bare hand with nothing placed at all. The source's own experiments put them on the same footing as an iron rod; the card takes that seriously and tests it, rather than quietly dropping the parts that sound silly.
- **The first card in the queue whose expected null is a statement about the OPERATOR rather than about the world.** A null here does not say "there is no radiation"; it says the *block* is a property of the person reading, not of the ground — which is the most useful thing a family can learn before buying a lead plate.

## The honest framing (the spine of the card)

The card tests a claim, not a tradition. Nobody is being asked to prove that geopathic radiation exists. They are being asked one narrow question: **when one person puts something on the spot, does a second person's reading of that spot change?**

Three things the card keeps in front of the family:

1. **The source already ran the decisive test and reported the answer.** His visiting colleague, who knew nothing of the block, found the flow while his own rod stayed silent. He wrote it down and called it a dilemma. The card is that observation, given N trials and a blind setter.
2. **The source's own material list is a market, not a result.** Lead plates, barite plaster, Wastra foil, Sirius foil, RET plates, straw — named and sold, none of them tested against a control by the author. And his own experiments say the material does not matter. A family should know that before they spend money on any of it.
3. **The reading is easy to get wrong, and the source's own rule is why.** A block that only its placer can remove, and that a glance can create, cannot be distinguished from expectation unless someone else takes the reading. That is the whole design.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that **neither reader beats chance** — that a rod or pendulum held over a taped spot reads what the holder expects, and that a hair and an iron rod are indistinguishable because neither is doing anything. That is the honest verdict and the card is built to reach it cleanly. The one thing the card establishes whatever the result is the family's own baseline: a blind, chance-scored reading series on their own floor, which is worth having whether or not anything blocks anything.
