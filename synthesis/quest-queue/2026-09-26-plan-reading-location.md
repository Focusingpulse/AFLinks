## The honest framing (the spine of the card)

**The representation claim is the one worth testing.** Every dowsing card in the queue so far tests the physical field — a person (011), a room (005), a water line (035), a substance (034). None tests whether a **representation** of a place carries the information. The plan-reading claim is the tradition's most consequential and most fragile: if true, location information is available without proximity; if false, it is the cleanest possible demonstration of the ideomotor account, because the operator is in a different room holding a sheet of paper and the only thing that could move the rods is expectation.

**The design choice that is the whole card is the cross-room baseline.** An operator will *always* mark zones on a plan — the rods always move. So "did the operator mark something?" measures nothing. The discriminating quantity is **A − B**: how much better the plan predicts its *own* room than a different room. That is the number that separates a real signal from expectation, and it is why the control plan is not an extra but the test itself.

**A PASS would need independent replication before any claim beyond "in this family's hands."** A FAIL is a complete and valuable result — the archive most needs it, because the tradition's defenders cite map reading as its strongest evidence.
