## What makes it the queue's first of its kind

- **The first card whose target is ABSENT** — a residual trace of a removed object rather than a present one. Every other dowsing card in the queue points the operator at something that is still there (a buried line, a water vein, a chemical, a hidden zone) or at a representation of it (a plan).
- **The first card whose ground truth the tester creates themselves**, so the operator genuinely cannot infer it. In the water-line and geopathic cards the operator could in principle have prior knowledge of the site; here the "imprinted" spot is one the tester chose and marked, and the object was removed before the test.
- **The first card to test whether an imprint FADES** — the source's own claim is about *duration* ("remanence lasts the longer…"), so the card measures the same spots at +1, +7, +30 and +90 days.
- **The first card carrying a differential prediction from the source itself** (remanence ∝ atomic number). The card is built so the **differential**, not the mere hit, is the strong endpoint.
- **The second Nest card to treat the dwelling as more than its geometry** — the first (083, Plan-Reading Location) treats a *plan* as carrying information; this one treats the *place* as carrying a trace of what was in it.

## Honest Framing

This is a test, not an endorsement. The remanence doctrine is a claim of the Turenne radiesthesia lineage, quoted here in the source's own words: *"it is possible that in that place there was once gold or precious metals, but that they were subsequently removed, and that what the detectors picked up was nothing other than the residual waves of those bodies. They are called precisely 'remanences'… Such waves do dissolve, yes, but over times that may be very long."* The companion article on the memory of places is candid that nothing here is established: it states that **no recognised scientific instrument currently measures a morphic field**, that Sheldrake *"has not demonstrated that haunted houses possess a morphic field, nor that human emotions remain suspended in buildings,"* and that the real question is *"whether the person is content to interpret an atmosphere or whether they truly obtain specific and verifiable information."* The card turns that question into a scored test. The likely outcome is that the operator cannot beat chance, and that is a **complete and useful result** (Skeptic's Star), not a failure. The card makes **no health claim**, and it is not a test of anything a family should act on before the result is in. It is a method test of a cited claim.
