## Rubric Justification

- **Practical:** a named apparatus (a copper or bronze garden tool, a matched steel tool, ≥3 matched plots, a kitchen scale) with a measurable outcome (**total harvest mass per plot, copper vs matched steel**, plus gravimetric soil water content). Not pure theory.
- **Replicable:** home garden, **~$30–100** (copper/bronze tool $25–80, steel tool $15–40, seed $5–15, scale $0–20). No lab, no mains wiring, no chemistry.
- **Relevant:** maps to the Village **food** survival domain (crop production — the tool that works the soil) and to the **Gardening** mirror (soil-vitality / cultivation complement) — the guild whose seed candidate is "Schauberger vortex irrigation; structured-water on garden."
- **Honest:** the card tests the **yield claim in the source's own paired-strip shape**, and states plainly that every figure is Schauberger's own or his biographers', that the 1948/49 Salzburg test is a paired design with **no raw numbers, no statistics and no independent replication**, and that the mechanism (ferroelectric currents breaking down soil water) is not accepted physics. It names the two confounds the source does not separate — **copper as a nutrient/fungicide**, and **tool mass/shape** — and requires the tools matched so only the metal differs.
- **Linked:** a source doc id (the translation slug), a replication dossier, a pre-registered pass/fail, and an evidence list.

**What makes it the queue's first of its kind:** it is the first card whose **independent variable is the METAL OF THE TOOL that works the soil** (copper vs steel) — the implement itself, not the water, the field, the timing, the device or the seed; the first on the **Schauberger "Golden Plough" / copper-tool lineage**; the first whose **endpoint is a yield comparison between two matched plots worked with tools of different metal**; the first whose **mechanism claim is electrochemical — the tool as an electrode acting on soil water** (galvanic/electrolytic, in the source's own words); and the first that tests a claim about **soil-contact materials as a class** — the material that touches the earth.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the expected result is a **null** — the two plots within garden noise of each other. A home garden has large variance, so a 15–20% difference is at the edge of what one season can resolve; that is why the card requires **≥3 matched pairs** and a **second season** before it calls a positive. A null **bounds the claim at garden scale** — it does not disprove an effect that requires a full-size plough and a whole field. But the source's own test was already a paired strip design, so the card is a scaled-down replication of a stated protocol, not an invention — and the outcome is a hard number.
