## What makes it the queue's first of its kind

- **The first card measuring WEIGHT CHANGE in a pyramidal chamber.** The Yard's other pyramid cards measure desiccation, blade retention, charge accumulation, water evaporation, and rat stress. **None measures mass.** A weight change is the one pyramid readout that would be *physical* rather than perceptual or biological.
- **The first card whose discriminator is a SIGN FLIP under a controllable knob.** The source reports the effect reversing when the flux reverses — that is a built-in control, and the card makes it the primary discriminator rather than a footnote.
- **The first card that adds a shape control the source omitted.** Arm D (the same magnet in a size-matched non-pyramid box) separates *shape* from *magnet* from *handling* — the confound the tradition has never ruled out.
- **The design choice that is the whole card: two knobs, two controls.** The **sign flip** (B vs C) tests the source's own claim; the **shape control** (B vs D) tests whether any effect is the pyramid at all. A run that moves the mass in both the pyramid and the plain box has found a magnet effect, not a shape effect — and the card says so before the first weighing.

## Honest Framing

This is a test, not an endorsement. The source is a **single 2000 newspaper report** from a self-described "public axion-field laboratory," with a reported mechanism (buoyancy in an axion-of-torsion fluid) that **no instrument has measured**, **no blinding**, and **no shape control**. Its decisive control — the **flux orientation** — is **never defined operationally**, so this card reconstructs it and says plainly that a null from a reconstructed orientation is **not yet a refutation**; it may be the wrong knob. That caveat is the whole reason the card exists: it converts a half-specified protocol into a runnable four-arm test, and it tells the family what would change the answer.

The expected outcome is a **null in all four arms** — the pyramid and the magnet-primed chamber do not move the mass beyond balance noise — and that is a **complete, useful result** that earns the Skeptic's Star and closes the lane's best row at home scale. A replicated **sign-flipping** weight change would be the surprise and would require a second independent family before it is believed. The card makes **no claim that pyramids alter gravity** — it measures whether one specific printed effect is reproducible with a balance, and it teaches the measurement discipline the entire shape-power tradition has never built.
