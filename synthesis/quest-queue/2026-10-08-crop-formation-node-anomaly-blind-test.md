## The honest framing (the spine of the card)

The card tests a claim, not a tradition. Nobody is being asked to prove that balls of light make crop circles; the family is asked whether the **node-length structure** of a formation is reproducible by ordinary flattening. That is a question a garden can answer.

Three things the card keeps in front of the family:

1. **Elongation is not the claim.** A man-made circle produced a 144% node peak — higher than the ~120% that Levengood's own earlier work had already attributed to flattening. So "the nodes grew" is a known, mundane effect and proves nothing. **Only the structure — the smooth centric radial gradient — carries the claim.** The card measures the structure.
2. **The expected result is a null on the mundane explanation, and that is a complete result.** The 2014 test predicts no centric gradient from a hand-made circle; reproducing that under blind conditions is a genuine finding, and it is what makes the remaining anomaly *interesting* rather than *settled*.
3. **A positive (PASS-B) would be the surprising result and would need to repeat.** If a hand-made circle *does* come out with a clean centric gradient, the anomaly is dissolved by giving flattening a mundane mechanism — and precisely because that is a strong claim, the card requires a second circle before belief.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that a hand-made circle shows elongation without the centric gradient — reproducing 2014 — which leaves the 1999 anomaly unexplained and unreproduced. That is the honest verdict this card is designed to reach cleanly, and it is worth filing.
