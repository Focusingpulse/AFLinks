## The honest framing (the spine of the card)

**The claim is a claim.** The source says that burning a weed's own seeds and spreading the ash "shuts down or breaks its connection with the in-streaming energies that sustain it," so that the weed "dries up and struggle[s] to grow or reproduce." **No conventional account recognises such a mechanism.** This card tests the claim; it does not endorse it.

**The source calls its own record spotty, and the card says so first.** *"Some peppers work brilliantly … in other cases there is no noticeable effect."* That is not the card's scepticism — it is the source's own sentence, and it is the reason the card is worth running: the practitioner community has left the question open, and a family with four quadrats and six weeks can add a data point.

**The design that makes it a measurement.** Three things do the work:

1. **The inert control (arm C) is the whole test.** Char and ash are never inert — wood ash raises pH and adds potassium and calcium; char changes surface area and soil biology. **A card that spread only pepper ash and saw fewer weeds would be measuring a fertility effect.** Arm C gets the same *mass* of plain wood char from non-target material, so the only difference between A and C is the pepper's identity. **Without arm C, a positive result means nothing.**
2. **The two pepper forms answer the source's own controversy.** The source reports that practitioners who burn to white ash "don't seem to have the best record of success," and suspects the carbon framework is the active part. **Running a pyrolized pepper and an ashed pepper side by side** turns an inherited opinion into a measured result — and it is the first card in the queue that tests a controversy *inside* its own source rather than testing the source against a conventional baseline.
3. **The baseline count is the second control.** Weeds grow in patches; four adjacent quadrats can differ by more than the effect. **Counting before applying, and moving the quadrats if they differ by more than 30%,** is what stops a patch from being read as a result.

**The likely result is "the pepper did nothing beyond what any char would do," and that is a complete result.** It tells a family not to spend a season on the method, and it is exactly what the source's own "spotty record" language predicts. A pepper that beats the inert control would be genuinely interesting and would need a second season and a second weed before any conclusion.

**The central limitation, stated up front: one season, one weed, one site.** The source says well-established weeds may take four years, and that peppering works better where biodynamic management is already in place. **A null here does not refute the method** — it is a null at home scale, in one season, on one fast annual. And the moon-timing claim is a stated refinement, not a controlled variable: a null does not refute it either.

**Safety and honesty:** the one real hazard is the **fire** — burn outdoors where smoke can dissipate, in a hole or on a fire-safe surface, away from dry vegetation, with water at hand, and never in a confined space. The pepper is a **fine dust** — wear a mask when sieving and spreading. Nothing here is caustic, hot at the point of use, or pressurised. **The card's own honesty note:** the source records Steiner's remark that *"the enthusiasm one has for making the remedy is a factor in its success."* The card treats that as an untestable claim and does not build it in — but it is worth stating, because it is exactly the kind of claim that makes a null result easy to explain away.
