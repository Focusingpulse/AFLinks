## Read this first — what this card can and cannot test, and the one rule that overrides it

**What it tests.** The Hartmann-grid tradition makes one claim about **matter** rather than about a field: that a dowsed grid crossing — a **node** — carries an increased concentration of **radon**, the radioactive gas that comes up out of the subsoil. Radon is a gas. Gases can be counted. This card counts it.

**What it does not test.** It does not test whether the Hartmann grid exists, whether torsion fields are real, or whether any protective device works. It tests one sentence: *does a dowser's mark coincide with a place where a gas has accumulated?*

**The rule that overrides the whole card.** If your room's radon is at or above your country's action level, **fix it — regardless of what the rods said.** A null on the node claim says nothing about whether the gas is there. A high reading is a real finding about your house, not a failed experiment. Radon measurement is passive and safe; radon itself is not.

**Do not let the dowsing result decide whether you mitigate.** That is the one way this card could do harm, and it is written into the pass/fail.

## Pass/Fail Criteria
- **PASS (the claim survives)**: node mean ≥ 1.5× the neutral-zone mean in **≥ 2 of 3** deployments, paired difference exceeding the instrument's own repeat-measurement spread, paired detectors within 1.5 m of each other in the same room.
- **PASS (refutes the claim — the expected result)**: node and neutral-zone means agree within the instrument's spread in all three deployments.
- **FAIL**: the pairs differ but the **neutral zones read higher**.
- **INCONCLUSIVE — and this is NOT a null**: the room's radon is below the instrument's detection floor or below the level at which it can resolve the effect sought (**the test was unattempted**); fewer than 3 deployments; the marks moved; the paired detectors had different exposure windows; the calibration is unknown.
- **ARTIFACT**: the difference tracks the date, the weather, the ventilation, the barometric trend, or the detector's height/distance from the wall rather than the mark.
