## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A broadband RF power-density meter** (~$50–150) — reads µW/m² across the common bands (the "Cornet"-style handheld is the common home instrument). **Not a phone app.**
- **A timer plug** (~$10) — to switch the router off at night.
- **A longer Ethernet and/or power cable** (~$10) — to move the router out of the bedroom wall.
- **A notebook / log sheet** — the five spots, three readings each, peak and average, and the outdoor reference.
- **Optional:** a second phone or a stopwatch for the two-week sleep log (labelled an anecdote).

## Protocol (pre-registered)

1. **Pre-register, before measuring.** Write down: the five spots (bed head where the head rests, pillow, router, nearest mains-powered device or baby monitor, and an outdoor reference point away from the house), the meter and its calibration status, the scoring rule (three readings per spot at a fixed time of day; record peak and average), the mitigation plan, and the thresholds. Photograph the sheet.
2. **Baseline survey.** Measure the RF power density in µW/m² at each of the five spots, three readings each, at the same time of day. Record peak and average. Record which devices are on.
3. **Mitigate.** Put the router on a timer so it is off while you sleep (or move it at least 3 m from the bedroom wall, or to another room). Wire the devices that can be wired. Charge the phone outside the bedroom. Move any baby monitor at least 1 m from the crib.
4. **Re-survey.** Re-measure at the same five spots, at the same time of day, with the same meter, with the mitigation in place. Record peak and average.
5. **Compute.** For each spot, the ratio of the after-reading to the before-reading. For the bed head, the ratio to the outdoor reference, before and after.
6. **Optional, and labelled an anecdote:** log sleep latency (minutes to fall asleep) and wake quality (1–5) for two weeks before and two weeks after the mitigation. Report the direction. Do not treat it as a result.
7. **Post.** Photo of the pre-registration sheet, the before/after numbers, the meter at the bed head and at the router, the mitigation itself, and a one-paragraph verdict.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (field reduced — the physical result):** the RF power density at the bed head falls by a **factor of 10 or more** after mitigation, measured at the same spot, at the same time of day, with the same meter, while the outdoor reference reading is unchanged.
- **PASS (no field — the honest null):** the bed head already reads at or near the outdoor ambient before any change, so the bedroom was not a significant RF source and there is nothing to reduce — a complete result (Skeptic's Star).
- **NOTE (the sleep log):** a change in the logged sleep latency or wake quality is reported as an **n=1 anecdote**, never as a result. The card does not claim the field caused the sleep, only that the field changed.
- **FAIL:** the meter was not zeroed or calibrated; the reading was taken at a single instant; the mitigation was not verified by re-measurement; or the before and after were taken under different conditions (different time of day, different devices on).
- **VOID:** the meter was faulty, or the reading was taken within the meter's own near-field — a VOID is not a NULL.

## Independent-variable discipline

The one variable is **the sources on/off and their distance.** Everything else — the spot, the time of day, the meter, the orientation — must be held fixed, or the comparison measures the conditions, not the field. The outdoor reference is the control that catches a meter that has drifted or a reading taken next to a neighbour's source.

## What a result would mean

- **Field falls, sleep log flat** → the field was yours and you turned it down; the sleep you logged did not move. The honest conclusion is that removing the field changed the field, and nothing else you could measure. That is a complete, useful result.
- **Field falls, sleep log moves** → interesting, and still an anecdote. The honest follow-up is a blinded crossover (the family does not know which fortnight the router was on), which is the only design that could separate the field from expectation.
- **Field already at ambient** → the bedroom was not a source. The concern, whatever it is, is not about your bedroom.
