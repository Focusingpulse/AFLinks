## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The form-wave / geobiology tradition holds that the shape of a building is not decoration but an active agent. The source states it twice:

> "In the construction of a building, the proportion of its spatial dimensions, internal and external, is very important. **Any space with a height, width and length in the proportion of 3, 5, 8 will always have a greater resonance and harmony.**"

> "It is evident that the forms of any building are not indifferent; **every form has an energetic action that acts on our reactions**, as can happen in the different sensations received by any of us on entering a square, triangular or elongated space, with an excessively high or low ceiling, etc."

The source's surrounding rules for the habitat (all the same family — form as an agent):

> "In a very general way we will say that the O.F. [form emissions] emitted by symmetrical geometric forms are usually beneficial, and that those emitted by polygons and irregular, uncompensated geometric forms are usually harmful."

> "All regular polygons with an even number of sides are polarised forms and therefore, in principle, beneficial. All polygons with an odd number of sides are depolarised forms and therefore liable to cause problems."

> "convex forms would be healthy for the human habitat and, in contrast, concave designs would be dangerous."

> "the circle and the sphere are the most perfect forms... semicircles and hemispheres emit 'negatively' from their flat sides, so we should avoid objects with these forms."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does the proportion of a space — specifically the 3:5:8 proportion the tradition singles out — measurably change how the space feels to the people in it, compared with a space of matched volume and a different proportion?

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the claim and no test of it.** A grep for `proportion`, `3:5:8`, `3, 5, 8`, `room mode`, `room resonance`, `reverberat`, `RT60` and `standing wave` across `living-library/synthesis/quest-queue/` and `synthesis/replication/` returns **no card and no dossier** on room proportion. The queue's nearest shelter cards test earth energies (005, 021, 035, 057), form waves on a person's field (011), a material's resonance (016), a pyramid's shape-force (050) and a plan's information (044) — **none tests the proportion of a space.**

Four things the card must say out loud, because they are what a family is actually testing:

1. **The claim as stated is unfalsifiable, so the card must operationalize it.** "Greater resonance and harmony" is a quality, not a number. The card fixes the operationalization in advance — a **forced-choice preference** and a **0–10 harmony/comfort rating** from blinded occupants, plus a simple **heart-rate-variability** reading — and pre-registers it. A test that lets the operator decide afterward what "harmony" meant is not a test.

2. **The tradition's "resonance" is explicitly NOT acoustic.** The source is careful: the emissions "have no wave character... nothing to do with electromagnetism or with any other known type of energy." **So the card does not test acoustics** — a room's acoustic modes are a real and separate thing, and measuring them would answer a different question. The card tests the claim's *occupant-facing* content: the source says the form "acts on our reactions," and that is what a blinded occupant can report.

3. **The effect, if real, is small and the endpoint is subjective.** Room proportion is one variable among many (light, colour, contents, smell, expectation, the participant's mood). A home test with a dozen participants is **underpowered**, and a null is the likely and complete result.

4. **The expected result is a null, and that is a complete result.** The most likely outcome is that the 3:5:8 space and the matched control space score the same, within the noise. That is a clean, complete refutation of the *home-scale* claim, scored as such — not a failure.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that blinded occupants cannot tell the 3:5:8 space from a matched control, and the honest verdict is *"a room's proportion is not detectable by occupants at home scale."* A clear, replicating preference (≥70 % of participants choosing the 3:5:8 space, with a matching rating gap) would be genuinely surprising and would justify the taller follow-up (a larger N, a physiological arm, and a blind room-swap).

## Why it matters

- It is the queue's **first card on the proportion of a space** — the dwelling itself, as a variable. Every prior shelter card tests a field (earth energies), a material (wood), a shape-object (pyramid) or a representation (a plan); **none tests the geometry of the room a family actually lives in.**
- It is the queue's **first card whose endpoint is a blinded human preference/rating** — the transferable lesson being *how to operationalize a vague quality claim into a pre-registered, blinded measurement*, which is the move every "this feels better" product claim needs.
- It is the queue's **first card on the 3:5:8 proportion** — the one proportion the form-wave tradition singles out as "always" harmonious, and the one the queue has never tested.
- The design choice that is the whole card: **matched volume.** The control space has the *same internal volume* as the 3:5:8 space, so the test isolates *proportion* from *size*. A bigger room feels different for reasons that have nothing to do with 3:5:8.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$100–300** — cardboard, plywood, curtain/tarp, or a pair of large boxes to make two enclosures of matched internal volume, each big enough for one seated person; a tape measure; a phone (for the timer and, optionally, an HRV app using the camera); a printed rating sheet.
- Safety: **low.** No high voltage, no chemicals. The only hazards are the enclosure materials (sharp edges) and claustrophobia — participants must be free to leave at any time.
- Accessibility: **immediate** — no season, no waiting. The whole test can run in an afternoon with family and friends.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Two enclosures of matched internal volume** (≥ ~7 m³ each, enough for one seated person), one with dimensions in the **3:5:8** proportion (e.g. 1.2 m high × 2.0 m wide × 3.2 m deep) and one a **1:1:1 cube** of the same volume (≈ 1.97 m per side). Cardboard, plywood, curtain or tarp; the material must be the same for both.
- **A tape measure** — to set and record the dimensions.
- **A phone** — timer; optionally an HRV app (camera-based) for the physiological arm.
- **A printed rating sheet** — the pre-registered forced-choice and 0–10 scale.
- **A blindfold or a curtain** — to keep participants from seeing the enclosure before entering (and to keep the operator from cueing them).
- **A log sheet** — for pre-registration and the per-participant record.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — pre-register (mandatory).**
1. Write the **pre-registration sheet** before any participant runs: the two proportions, the matched volume, the enclosure material, the participant count (target ≥ 12), the order randomization, the exact question wording, the 0–10 scale anchors, the HRV method, and the thresholds. Photograph it.

**Phase 1 — build and verify.**
2. Build the two enclosures. **Measure and record** the internal dimensions of each; confirm the volumes match within ±5 %.
3. Confirm the enclosures are **identical in every other way** — same material, same light, same temperature, same contents (empty), same smell.

**Phase 2 — run.**
4. Each participant enters **one enclosure at a time**, alone, for a fixed interval (e.g. 5 minutes), seated, doing nothing in particular. The **order is randomized** per participant (coin flip), and the participant does **not know** the hypothesis or which enclosure is which.
5. After each enclosure, the participant answers, **without discussion**: (a) the forced-choice question ("Which of the two spaces felt more harmonious / welcoming?" — asked after both, or a per-space rating); (b) a **0–10 harmony/comfort rating** for that space; (c) optionally, the HRV reading taken during the last 2 minutes.
6. The operator **does not speak** during the run and does not reveal the hypothesis until all participants are done.

**Phase 3 — score.**
7. Compute, per participant, the preference (3:5:8 or control) and the rating difference. Compute the group's **preference proportion** and the **mean rating difference** (with a sign test or a simple binomial check against 50 %).
8. Repeat with a **fresh set of participants** (or a second session) to check the result replicates.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** **≥ 70 %** of participants choose the 3:5:8 space as more harmonious, **and** the mean rating gap is **≥ 1.5 points** in its favour, **and** the result replicates in a second set — the proportion claim is detectable by occupants at home scale and needs the taller follow-up (larger N, a physiological arm, a blind room-swap).
- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** the preference is **at chance** (roughly 50/50, within the binomial noise band) and the mean rating gap is **< 1 point** in two sets — a room's proportion is not detectable by occupants at home scale. A complete result; Skeptic's Star.
- **FAIL (void, not a null):** the two enclosures do **not** match in volume (±5 %) or in material/light/temperature — the comparison is not controlled and the run is void.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** fewer than 12 participants; one set only; the order was not randomized; participants discussed the hypothesis beforehand; the enclosures differed in any way other than proportion; or the operator cued the participants.
- **ARTIFACT:** the preference tracks **enclosure order** (first-always-wins) rather than proportion; the preference tracks **which enclosure the participant saw first** or the operator's expectation; or the result disappears when the enclosures are swapped in position.

## Evidence

Photo of both enclosures with the tape measure showing the internal dimensions + the **pre-registration sheet** (proportions, volume, material, N, order randomization, question wording, scale anchors, thresholds) + the per-participant record (order, preference, 0–10 ratings, HRV if taken) + the group preference proportion and mean rating difference with the chance calculation + the second-set repeat + the void/artifact checks reported whether or not they void the run.

## Confounds (named in advance)

- **Volume vs proportion — the central confound.** A larger room feels different for reasons unrelated to 3:5:8. **Matching the internal volume is the whole experiment.**
- **Order and expectation.** The first space visited may feel different simply because it is first. **Randomize the order and record it.**
- **Everything else in the room.** Light, colour, temperature, smell, sound and contents all affect how a space feels. Hold them constant and record them.
- **The participant's prior beliefs.** Someone who has heard of sacred geometry will expect the "special" room to feel better. **Do not tell participants the hypothesis until the end.**
- **The operator.** The person running the test can cue participants without meaning to. **The operator does not speak during the run.**
- **Sample size.** A dozen participants is underpowered; a small real effect will not show. Report the N and the noise band.
- **Claustrophobia and comfort.** A small enclosure may simply feel worse for reasons unrelated to proportion. Let participants leave at any time and record any discomfort.

## Verdict rules

≥ 70 % preference for the 3:5:8 space with a ≥ 1.5-point rating gap, replicating in a second set → **the claim survives** (surprising; needs the taller follow-up). Preference at chance with a < 1-point rating gap in two sets → **the home-scale claim is refuted** (the expected, complete result). Enclosures not matched in volume or material → **void, not a null**. Fewer than 12 participants, one set, unrandomized order, or operator cueing → **inconclusive, not a refutation**.
