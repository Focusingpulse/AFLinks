## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source proposes a **System of Objective Testing (SOT)** — an analytical system for reading a person's metabolic state from objective measurements rather than from complaints. Its own description of the two data sets, quoted:

> "The SOT is an analytical system based on two sets of objective clinical data:
>
> 1. The principal indicators of the organism's condition (pulse, respiration, temperature, blood pressure, etc.) and neuro-endocrine reflexes.
>
> 2. Analyses of urine and saliva."

The template claim — that deviations cluster rather than appear independently — quoted:

> "It also became clear that many deviations from the norm have a tendency not only to appear simultaneously but also to group themselves in a quite definite way, forming a sort of template of disturbances. **For example, high alkalinity of the urine (high pH) is practically always accompanied by low density**, and so on and so forth."

The count of templates, quoted:

> "There are five of these systems, and each of them, like a seesaw, may leave the state of equilibrium in two possible, opposite directions. Thus we have in total ten templates of metabolic disturbance, each of which can be identified without difficulty with the help of the SOT."

The source's reason for preferring urine and saliva over blood, quoted:

> "But what about blood analyses, someone will ask? The fact is that blood is a medium in the organism in which serious changes set in only when it is already time to think about resuscitation. Therefore one should not wait for that hour — changes in the secretory fluids 'speak' no less, and moreover do so in good time."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** in one person, across many samples, **does urine pH co-vary with urine specific gravity (density) in the direction the source states — high pH with low density** — and is that relationship stable enough to be called a "template"?

## The ordinary explanation the card must carry

**Urine pH and urine specific gravity are each driven by ordinary, well-understood factors — and several of those factors move BOTH.** This is the confound the card is built to see:

- **Diet.** A high-protein (meat) load acidifies urine (pH falls, commonly into the 5.0–6.0 range); a vegetable/fruit load alkalinises it (pH rises). The source's own recommended diet is meat + raw vegetables — i.e. a diet that swings both directions.
- **Hydration.** Dilution lowers specific gravity toward 1.000 and also blunts pH excursions. Drinking a litre of water moves density down and can move pH either way.
- **Time of day.** First-morning urine is the most concentrated (highest specific gravity) and the most acidic; later samples are more dilute.
- **Exercise, illness, and the previous meal's timing** all move both numbers.

**So a negative pH↔density correlation is the ordinary expectation for a person whose diet and hydration vary** — and that is precisely why the card must log diet and fluid intake, and must test the relationship **within the first-morning-void subset alone**. If the correlation only appears in the pooled data and vanishes within the morning subset, the "template" is diet timing, not metabolism.

**The source's own rule predicts more than a correlation, and the card says so.** A "template" implies the deviations **cluster** — that a person's readings occupy a stable region rather than scattering. The card therefore also measures **test–retest stability**: does the same person land in the same pH/density region on repeat mornings?

## The protocol (pre-registered)

**Apparatus (home, ~$25–45):**
- Urine pH test strips, 0.5–1.0 pH resolution, with a colour chart (≈$10–15).
- Urine specific-gravity reagent strips (1.000–1.030) **or** a handheld urine refractometer (1.000–1.060) (≈$15–30). **The refractometer is preferred** — it removes the colour-chart judgement from the reading.
- A notebook or a spreadsheet. Optional: a home blood-pressure cuff and a thermometer for the vital-signs half.

**Entry conditions (hard):**
- Write and photograph the pre-registration BEFORE day 1: the two measurements, the two daily sampling times, the diet/fluid log fields, the primary endpoint, the sample-count floor, and the pass/fail thresholds.
- Check the strips' expiry date and record it. Expired strips void the run.
- **This is a measurement exercise, not a treatment.** Do not change any medication, and do not adopt the source's diet for this card. If you are already on a stable diet, keep it stable and log it.

**Block:** 21 consecutive days. **Two samples per day:** (a) the first-morning void, within 15 minutes of waking, before any fluid; (b) one afternoon/evening sample at a fixed time you choose. Measure **both quantities on each sample within 30 seconds of collection** (dip pH strip, dip SG strip or refractometer drop), read under the same light, and record both.

**Log every sample:** date, time, sample type (morning/evening), pH, specific gravity, hours since last meal, whether the previous meal was meat-heavy / vegetable-heavy / mixed, and rough fluid intake since the previous sample.

**Primary endpoint:** the **within-person Spearman rank correlation (ρ) between urine pH and specific gravity** across all complete samples (≥ 40 samples = 21 days × 2). Spearman, not Pearson — pH from a colour chart is ordinal.

**Secondary endpoints:**
1. The same correlation computed **within the first-morning-void subset alone** (≥ 20 samples) — the confound control.
2. **Test–retest stability:** the standard deviation of the morning-void pH and of the morning-void specific gravity across the block — i.e. is there a stable "region" at all?
3. The correlation **within a fixed diet band** (samples whose previous meal was logged as the same category) — the tightest available diet control.

**Pass/fail (pre-registered):**

| Outcome | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **PASS — source's rule supported** | ρ ≤ **−0.50** across ≥ 40 samples, **AND** ρ ≤ −0.30 within the morning-void subset alone, **AND** the morning-void pH and density each show a stable central region (SD within the published normal bands) |
| **PASS — source's rule refuted (the expected, ordinary result)** | ρ between **−0.30 and +0.30** across ≥ 40 samples — the two numbers move independently in this person, and the stated "template" is not visible in them (Skeptic's Star) |
| **FAIL** | fewer than 30 complete samples; the two measurements not taken in the same session; expired strips; the refractometer/strips not calibrated or read under changing light; the diet or fluid log not kept |
| **VOID** | illness, a medication change, or a deliberate diet change during the block — **a VOID is not a NULL** |

**Reproduction:** the source claims the template is general. The card is runnable in any household; **≥ 2 independent households reporting the same direction** is what would move this from one person's chemistry to a claim about people.

## What a result would mean

- **ρ ≈ 0 (the likely result):** the source's worked example does not reproduce as a within-person relationship. The "template" language is then a claim about a clinician's pattern-reading, not a property of two numbers anyone can measure at home. **This is a complete and useful result** — it retires a specific, checkable assertion while leaving the source's broader theory untouched and untested.
- **ρ strongly negative, and it survives the morning-void control:** the source's worked example reproduces. That would be a genuine, cheap, home-reproducible finding — and the next question is immediately the interesting one: **is it metabolism, or is it diet and hydration?** The card's diet log is designed to answer exactly that.
