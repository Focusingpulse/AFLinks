## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source asserts that **misogi** — the Shinto purification by cold water — has a real, measurable effect on the body, and that the effect is **not** water memory. Its own thesis, quoted:

> "The effect of misogi does not lie in water remembering some 'information' and 'transferring' it to the human body. Such a claim has not been scientifically established at present, and it fundamentally misreads the essence of misogi."

> "The true worth of misogi lies in the mechanism by which, through the medium of water, it brings about a 'conversion' in the human body and mind."

The mechanism it names, quoted:

> "Cold-water stimulation activates the sympathetic nervous system, and as a subsequent reflex brings about a state of parasympathetic dominance. Blood flow is promoted, stress hormones fall, immune function improves."

> "In research at Tokyo City University, it was confirmed that alternating hot-and-cold bathing (such as a cold plunge after a sauna) balances the autonomic nervous system. The rapid temperature change from high heat to cold water strongly stimulates the sympathetic nervous system, and the subsequent reflex heightens the parasympathetic nervous system. The so-called 'totonou' (refreshed, in-tune) sensation is brought about by this change in autonomic nervous activity."

The numbers it cites, quoted:

> "In a large-scale study conducted in the Netherlands, it was reported that subjects who took a cold shower of at least 30 seconds for one month had 29% fewer sick days than the control group. When combined with regular exercise, the difference widened to 54%."

> "Even more interesting is the research finding that cold-water stimulation lowers the value of the stress hormone cortisol. In a 2000 study, a significant decrease in cortisol values was confirmed in subjects who stayed in 14°C water for one hour."

The historical claim it leans on (Takaki Kanehiro, Meiji-era naval medical officer, founder of Jikei Medical University), quoted:

> "In resisting the cold, red blood cells increase, oxygen (caloric value) increases, and one becomes resistant to cold · Activation of metabolism through the promotion of blood circulation · Strengthening of immune function"

The practice as the source describes it, quoted:

> "By combining breathing technique, vocalisation and cold-water bathing, it is an elaborate programme that converts body and mind from the 'everyday' to the 'non-everyday,' and then again to a 'new everyday.'"

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a **30-second cold-water finish** at the end of an ordinary daily shower produce a **measurable autonomic conversion** — a pulse response that a **matched warm shower of the same length does not** — when the two are randomised day by day within the same person?

## Honest status of the claim

**This is a mechanism claim, stated as settled, by a source that ran no experiment of its own — and the source is unusually honest about what it does and does not claim.**

1. **The source is a synthesis, not a study.** It reports no experiment of its own. Every number in it is second-hand: the Netherlands cold-shower trial, the Tokyo City University bathing work, and a 2000 cortisol study are named but **not cited** — no author, no journal, no year for the first two. The card cannot verify them and does not pretend to.
2. **The mechanism it names is ordinary physiology, and the source says so.** Cold-water shock → sympathetic activation → a subsequent parasympathetic rebound is the mainstream account. **The source is not claiming a hidden force; it is claiming that an ordinary mechanism is what a traditional practice has been doing all along.** That is why this card can be run at all.
3. **The source explicitly rejects the water-memory explanation** — and carries the refutation itself, quoted:

   > "According to a joint Canadian-German study published in *Nature* in 2005, the redistribution of energy through the hydrogen bonds between water molecules is extremely fast, and it became clear that the 'memory' of a particular structure is lost within 50 femtoseconds (50 trillionths of a second)."

   > "This scientific fact is a clear refutation of commercial claims such as 'water with smaller clusters' or 'water with a special structure.'"

   **So this card is NOT a water-memory card and must not be read as one.** The queue already carries three cards in that family (`2026-09-20` Water Memory Imprint, `dossier-067` Water-to-Water Transfer, `dossier-079` Coil-Geometry Water). This one tests the *opposite* proposition: that the effect is the **cold**, not the water.
4. **The source has an interest, disclosed here.** It is a promotional article by a spiritual teacher (Yamana Reishū, of the Waki-ryū Kaimeigakukan study school) whose closing lines sell a LINE channel and study meetings. **Grade the claims, note the interest, do not treat the interest as the refutation.**
5. **The source's own framing is honest about its limits** — it says plainly that "not being reproducible" and "not existing" are not synonymous, and that it does not claim water memory has been proven. It applies the same standard to itself, which is why the card can.
6. **The archive holds no claim-status record for cold-water exposure, misogi, or the cold-shock response.** Recorded as a gap, not a backlog item.

**What the source DOES give, and why the card exists:**

- A **named action** with a duration the source itself specifies — "a cold shower of at least 30 seconds" — which is the smallest possible intervention.
- A **stated mechanism** with a **stated direction** — a sympathetic surge followed by a parasympathetic rebound, i.e. a *recovery* signature, not merely a pulse rise.
- A **stated effect size** on a household-measurable outcome (29% fewer sick days over a month).
- **No apparatus at all.** Every other card in this queue needs an instrument. This one needs a shower and a clock.
- A claim that **contradicts the archive's own dominant position** on water — which makes it a genuinely informative test rather than a confirmation.

**The ordinary mechanisms that must be excluded before the claim is interesting:**

| Ordinary mechanism | What it predicts | The control that separates it |
|---|---|---|
| **A warm shower also moves the pulse** | any shower raises then lowers heart rate, so "a change after a shower" proves nothing | **the control arm is a matched warm shower of the same length**, randomised day by day — the claim is the *difference between the arms*, not the change itself |
| **Time of day, ambient temperature, sleep, caffeine** | the pulse response drifts with everything else in a day | **fix the time of day**, log room temperature, sleep and caffeine, and use a **within-person crossover** so each participant is their own control |
| **Habituation to cold** | the cold-shock response weakens with repetition, so a fixed cold-then-warm order would show a trend that is not the mechanism | **randomise the arm daily** (coin flip) so habituation cannot masquerade as an arm effect |
| **Season and illness** | sick days cluster in winter regardless of showers | **run the whole 42-day block inside one season**, and log symptoms daily rather than recalling them |
| **Measurement sloppiness** | a pulse taken "after a shower" at varying delays is noise | **a fixed protocol**: seated, at 60 s and 180 s after the water stops, same chair, same clock |
| **The participant knows which arm they are in** | expectation could move a subjective rating | the **primary endpoint is a pulse count, not a feeling** — the subjective rating is logged separately and never used as the primary result |

**So the honest position is:** the source's mechanism is ordinary and its numbers are uncited, but the claim is **specific, directional and cheap** — and it is the one claim in the archive that says the water-memory corpus is looking at the wrong variable. The card's job is to run the comparison the source never ran: **cold finish against matched warm finish, in the same person, randomised.** The likely outcome is that the arms differ in the acute pulse response (the mainstream prediction) and that the sick-day difference is too small to see in one household — which is a complete result either way.

| | Ordinary (warm shower, drift, habituation) | Source's claim supported |
|---|---|---|
| **Post-shower pulse recovery, cold vs warm** | the arms do not differ beyond the day-to-day scatter | cold days show a consistently **larger** recovery than warm days |
| **Randomised daily arm** | no trend with time in the block | the arm effect is present from the first week |
| **Symptom-day count, block vs baseline** | no difference distinguishable in one household | fewer symptom-days in the block than in the baseline |
| **A second household** | no relationship | the arm effect reproduces |

## Why it matters

The archive is full of the claim that water carries information. **This source says the effect everyone attributes to the water is the cold** — and it is the only document in the queue's inputs that makes that argument from inside a tradition that practises the thing. If a family runs the crossover and finds no difference between a cold finish and a warm finish, they have shown that in their own house the "conversion" is not distinguishable from an ordinary shower. If they find a consistent difference, they have the first within-person randomised result of its kind in the archive — and it is a result about **temperature**, which is a variable the water-memory cards never varied.

It is also the queue's first card a family can run **today, for nothing, with no instrument at all**.

## Protocol

**Pre-registration (write and photograph before the first day):** the participant, the guardian if the participant is a minor, the two arms and how the arm is chosen, the water temperature band, the duration, the time of day, the two pulse timings, the primary endpoint, the baseline length, the block length, the pass/fail thresholds, and the exclusion list.

**Entry conditions (hard):**
- **Adults, and teenagers with a guardian present and consenting. This card is not for young children.** Cold-water shock is a genuine physiological stressor.
- **No ice baths, no cold-water immersion, no breath-holding in water, and never alone.** The intervention is a **30-second cold finish at the end of a normal warm shower** — nothing more. The source's own "cold plunge after a sauna" is described, not prescribed.
- **Stop immediately** if shivering becomes uncontrollable, the skin goes numb or white, or the participant feels faint. Log the stop; the day counts as an incomplete day.
- **Exclusions:** a heart condition, Raynaud's phenomenon, cold urticaria, pregnancy, or any condition for which a clinician has advised against cold exposure. **If in doubt, do not run the card.**
- **A 14-day no-cold baseline is required** before the block. Without it there is no comparison for the sick-day endpoint.
- **The arm must be randomised daily** (a coin flip, logged before the shower). A fixed order is a FAIL, not a result.

**Materials:** an ordinary shower · a timer or a phone clock · a thermometer for the water temperature · a notebook or a sheet · a coin.

**The sessions:**
1. **Baseline — 14 days.** Normal showers, no cold finish. Log each day: date, time, water temperature, sleep rating, caffeine, any symptom (and its severity), and the two post-shower pulses.
2. **Block — 28 days.** Before each shower, flip the coin and write the arm down. **Cold day:** shower normally, then 30 seconds of cold water at the end (record the temperature). **Warm day:** the same shower, no cold finish (record the temperature). Keep the total shower length as close to constant as you can.
3. **Measure the same way every day.** When the water stops, sit in the same chair and take the pulse for 60 seconds starting at **1 minute** after the water stops, then again for 60 seconds starting at **3 minutes**. Record both counts.
4. **Log the symptom day** — any day with a symptom (cold, sore throat, headache, feeling unwell) counts as a symptom day, with a one-line description.
5. **Analyse.** Primary: the **recovery** (pulse at 1 min minus pulse at 3 min) on cold days versus warm days, against the day-to-day scatter of the warm arm. Secondary: symptom-days in the 28-day block versus the 14-day baseline rate.

**Measurable outcome (primary):** the **difference in mean post-shower pulse recovery (1 min minus 3 min) between cold-finish days and matched warm-finish days**, within the same person, against the scatter of the warm arm. Secondary: the **symptom-day rate** in the block versus the baseline; and whether the arm effect is present in the first week (habituation check).

**Noise floor:** the standard deviation of the warm-arm recovery values. **An arm difference smaller than this is not measurable in this household.**

## Pass / fail

- **PASS (source's mechanism supported):** cold-finish days show a **consistently larger** pulse recovery than warm-finish days, the difference **exceeds the warm-arm scatter**, it is **present in the first week** (so it is not habituation), and it **reproduces in a second household**. The sick-day endpoint is reported separately and is **not** required for this pass.
- **PASS (source's mechanism refuted — the expected, ordinary result):** cold-finish and warm-finish days **do not differ beyond the warm-arm scatter** — in this house, a cold finish is not distinguishable from an ordinary warm shower on this measure. The family has tested the claim in their own body, for free, and retired it. *Skeptic's Star.*
- **FAIL:** no 14-day baseline; the arm was not randomised daily; the cold duration or the water temperature varied outside the pre-registered band; the pulses were taken at inconsistent times or from a standing start; the shower length changed systematically between arms; or a result was read from fewer than 20 complete days.
- **VOID:** the participant was ill, injured, or on medication that changes heart rate for a substantial part of the block; the water temperature was not recorded; or the participant knew the hypothesis well enough to be unable to keep to the protocol. **A VOID is not a NULL.**
