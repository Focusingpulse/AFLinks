## The claim (as asserted by the source)

**Primary source.** The SRCAA "Zond" minutes record, under the "Attractor" project, an experiment run between **Kyiv, Ukraine** and **Tomsk, Russian Federation** — a separation of **3,572 km**:

> "Participants of the experiment: operator V. Nikolenko, Kyiv, Ukraine (K) and biolocation specialist V. Kibitkin, Tomsk, Russian Federation (T). The distance between the participants of the experiment was 3572 km. … During the experiment, operator K in Kyiv repeatedly switched operation modes (3 modes) of torsion field generator (TFG). … The T operator in Tomsk made multiple measurements of water properties (8 litres in a conventional galvanized bucket) **without knowing which TFG mode of operation the K operator used at this moment in Kyiv**. After the end of the experiment, the operating modes of TFG in Kyiv and the properties of water in the bucket in Tomsk were compared. **Full 100% correlation was found.**"

The minutes draw five conclusions from it, including that the effect is not electromagnetic in origin ("the signal from the generator with a power of 0.5 W could be taken no further than the next room … rather than in Tomsk, 3572 km from Kyiv"), that distance plays no role, that it is not shielded by 24 km of Earth, and that the field persists **25–30 s after the generator is switched off**.

**Convergence source.** The scalarwave.cc account reports a shorter-distance version of the same structure: a **pulse-excited LED** as the transmitter (explicitly "a relatively weak torsion generator"), a **water-based EIS sensor** as the receiver, **2.2 km** apart, with a **photograph of the receiving sensor** as the medium of entanglement, and the two ends made "two absolutely independent systems" (the transmitter run from a 12 V battery precisely so a skeptic could not say the signal coupled through the 220 V mains).

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** if one household runs a claimed non-local transmitter in one of **N randomly chosen modes**, and a second household at a distance measures a water sample **blind to the mode**, does the receiver's reading track the sender's mode — or is the receiver's reading indistinguishable from what it would be with the transmitter off?

## Honest status of the claim

The archive holds **two statements of the claim and no independent replication.**

- The primary source is a **research society's own meeting minutes** — a deposited article in a session record, not a peer-reviewed paper. It reports "full 100% correlation" but gives **no raw numbers, no trial count, no chance model, and no blinding audit**; the receiver's "water properties" are not specified beyond the bucket and the volume.
- The convergence source is a **practitioner's own web page**; it reports a visible effect but again with no trial count and no chance model, and the author himself concedes of the earlier 11,000 km version that "many people also think it might have been a coincidence or the effect of random factors."
- The mainstream objection is **severe and structural.** No mechanism is proposed for a non-local transfer at any distance, and the two experiments' own framing — "distance plays no role", "not shielded by 24 km of Earth", "persists 25–30 s after switch-off" — are exactly the properties that make the claim **unfalsifiable by ordinary field physics** unless the receiver's reading is scored against a **blind chance model**. The 1955 KNAW dowsing report in this archive (dossier 071) is the standing model of how such a claim must be tested: the person assessing the result must not know the condition.
- **No card and no dossier in the queue tests a two-household, blind-mode transfer.** A grep of `synthesis/quest-queue/` and `synthesis/replication/` for `blind` + `transfer` returns only dossier 067 (water-to-water, one room, ~1 m) and dossier 059 (inter-observer concordance at one site).

**Overlap, stated plainly.** This card is in the same broad family as **dossier 067 (Water-to-Water Information Transfer Test)** — both test a non-contact transfer into water. They are distinct in four ways: (i) **structure** — 067 is two water bodies in one room; this is a **sender and a receiver in two separate households**; (ii) **discriminator** — 067 asks whether the receiver moves *toward* the sender's signature; this asks whether the receiver can **name which of N modes the sender is running**, scored against chance; (iii) **endpoint** — 067 reads a dried-drop pattern; this reads a **pH** at the receiving site; (iv) **what it isolates** — 067 isolates the sender's *state*; this isolates **distance and independence of the two ends** (the source's own reason for running the transmitter from a battery).

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the expected result is a **refutation** — the receiver's blind mode-identification is at chance and the pH readings do not track the sender's mode. That is a complete, useful result and earns the Skeptic's Star. A blind identification above chance, repeated and independently reproduced by a second pair of households, would be the surprise.

## Why it matters

- It is the queue's **first card whose two ends are a sender and a receiver in separate households with independent power** — not two arms in one room. Every prior transfer or dowsing card runs both ends under one roof; this is the first that makes the *distance and the independence of the two ends* part of the design.
- It is the queue's **first card whose discriminator is a blind mode identification** — the receiver must **name which of N modes** the sender is running, scored against a 1/N chance baseline, rather than compare two arms.
- It is the queue's **first card that tests a distance claim as a distance claim** — the separation is a stated, recorded number between two addresses, not a bench gap.
- It is the queue's **first card that is itself the "neighbourhood replication node"** — the community guild's own complement. The card cannot be run by one household; it needs two, and the protocol is the thing the two households share.

## Replicability: `home`

- Cost: **~$20–60** — a pH meter or a pack of full-range litmus paper, two identical water samples (one per household), a sealed container per sample, and a claimed transmitter (a pulse-excited LED built from an LED, a resistor and a 9 V/12 V battery, or any claimed torsion/radionic device the household already owns).
- Safety: **none** beyond ordinary battery handling. No mains work (the source's own design deliberately runs the transmitter from a battery).
- Accessibility: an evening to pre-register and build the transmitter, one session of ~20 trials across two households, an afternoon to score.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Two households**, each with a phone (for the pre-registered timing and the sealed mode sheet) and **independent power** — the transmitter runs from its own battery, not mains.
- **A claimed non-local transmitter.** The source used an Akimov torsion-field generator (not home-replicable). The home-replicable substitute, from the same research community, is a **pulse-excited LED**: an LED driven through a simple pulse circuit from a battery. If the household already owns a claimed torsion/radionic device, use it and record what it is.
- **A "link":** a **photograph of the receiver's water sample**, printed, placed on or beside the transmitter (the source's entanglement medium). One photograph per trial set.
- **A water sample per household**, from the **same source**, the same volume, in the same container type, at the same temperature — the receiver's sample is the one measured.
- **A pH measurement**: full-range litmus paper (the source's own endpoint) or a cheap pH meter. Record the method and, if a meter, its calibration.
- **A sealed mode list** (N ≥ 3 modes, e.g. transmitter off / mode A / mode B), pre-shuffled and held by the sender; the receiver never sees it until scoring.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — transmitter and receiver checks (mandatory).**
1. **Transmitter check:** confirm the transmitter actually does *something* the household can verify (e.g. the LED pulses, the meter reads a change at the transmitter itself). A transmitter that does nothing makes a null VOID, not a result.
2. **Receiver check:** take **three baseline pH readings** of the receiver's water with the transmitter **off and unknown to the receiver** — this is the receiver's own noise band. If the receiver's readings vary more than the effect the card is looking for, the run is VOID.

**Phase 1 — pre-register (before any trial).**
3. Write down and photograph: the two households and their **addresses/approximate separation**, the transmitter and its **N modes**, the link (the photograph), the water source and volume, the **pH method**, the **number of trials** (≥ 12), the **timing schedule** (e.g. one reading at a fixed minute after each mode change), the **chance model** (1/N), and the scoring rule. Seal the mode list.

**Phase 2 — run the trials.**
4. The **sender** randomly selects one mode per trial from the sealed list, runs it for the pre-registered interval, and records the mode and the time. The **receiver**, at the scheduled time, measures the pH of the sample and **records which mode they believe is running** — blind to the sender's choice, with no communication during the run.
5. Repeat for all ≥ 12 trials. Vary the trial times so the receiver cannot learn a pattern.

**Phase 3 — score.**
6. Only after the last trial, the two households exchange the sealed sheets and compute: (a) the receiver's **blind mode-identification accuracy** against the 1/N chance baseline, and (b) the **pH reading per trial** against the sender's mode.
7. Repeat the whole session a **second time** (a fresh sealed mode list) and, if possible, have a **second pair of households** run it, to test whether any effect repeats.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim survives — the surprise):** the receiver's blind mode-identification is **above the 1/N chance baseline** at **p < 0.05** (binomial), **and** the direction **repeats in the second session**, **and** at least one **second pair of households** reproduces it. Requires independent replication before it is believed.
- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** the receiver's blind mode-identification is **at chance** and the pH readings do not track the sender's mode — the two-household transfer is not reproduced (Skeptic's Star).
- **FAIL:** the blinding was broken (the receiver knew or could infer the mode), or the two ends shared mains power, or the mode list was not sealed, or the pH method changed between trials.
- **VOID:** Phase 0 failed (the transmitter did nothing the household could verify, or the receiver's baseline noise exceeded the effect sought), or a reading was taken outside the pre-registered schedule. **A VOID is not a NULL.**
- **ARTIFACT:** the receiver's readings track a mundane shared variable (time of day, temperature, a shared water delivery) rather than the sender's mode — detected by the off-mode trials and the baseline.
