## The Claim

Atsyukovsky and Leontyev (Book 5, Chapter 2.4) report that a wooden cylinder with spiral grooves (Dodonov "corbio") placed on a jar of water causes ink at the bottom to form a central vortex column extending upward. They interpret this as evidence of vertical ether flows entering Earth from space, which the spiral grooves convert from laminar to vortex flow.

**Key claim:** Ink spontaneously gathers toward the center of the jar and forms a vertical column/funnel shape within 1.5–2 hours of placing the corrector. The vortex dissipates within 15 minutes of removing the corrector.

## Protocol

### Materials

1. **Clear glass jar** — 500ml–1L, cylindrical, clear walls for photography
2. **Dark ink or dye** — India ink, food coloring, or similar (must be denser than water)
3. **Wooden corrector (corbio)** — Cylinder ~5-8cm diameter, ~5cm height, with helical grooves cut into sides
   - Alternative: Carve grooves into wooden dowel or branch section
   - Groove direction: Right-hand or left-hand spiral (document which)
4. **Tube or pipette** — To deliver ink to bottom of jar without mixing
5. **Camera/phone** — For time-lapse photography
6. **Timer** — For 15-minute intervals
7. **Still water** — Let jar sit undisturbed for 30+ minutes before starting

### Corrector Fabrication

The Dodonov corrector is a wooden cylinder with spiral grooves cut into its sides. The grooves convert laminar ether flow into vortex flow. For this experiment:

- Use hardwood dowel or branch section, ~5-8cm diameter, ~5cm height
- Cut helical grooves with ~5mm depth, ~10mm spacing
- Groove angle: ~45° to horizontal
- Document groove direction (clockwise or counterclockwise when viewed from above)

### Procedure

1. **Preparation**
   - Fill jar with still water (let sit 30+ minutes to eliminate currents)
   - Position camera for consistent top-down and side-angle views
   - Record ambient temperature and time of day

2. **Ink placement**
   - Use tube/pipette to deliver ~5-10 drops of ink to bottom center of jar
   - Let ink spread across bottom (should form thin disk)
   - Photograph initial state (time = 0)

3. **Corrector placement**
   - Gently place wooden corrector on top of jar (grooves facing down)
   - Avoid disturbing water
   - Start timer

4. **Observation phase (90 minutes)**
   - Photograph every 15 minutes (0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90 min)
   - Note any ink movement patterns
   - Keep jar undisturbed (no vibrations, no temperature changes)

5. **Control phase (15 minutes)**
   - Gently remove corrector at 90 minutes
   - Photograph at 105 minutes (15 min after removal)
   - Document any vortex dissipation

### Controls

- **Negative control:** Run same protocol without corrector (jar + ink only)
- **Alternative material control:** Try smooth wooden cylinder (no grooves) to test if grooves matter
- **Metal corrector control:** Try metal corrector under jar (as described in source)
