## The Claim

Kolisko's capillary dynamolysis (Steigbildmethode): a metal-salt solution rising through filter paper produces characteristic "rising pictures" whose form changes with astronomical configuration (Moon–Saturn conjunctions, eclipses). The community-scale extension this dossier tests: **Steigbild pictures made simultaneously in different households on the same evening resemble each other more than pictures made on different evenings** — i.e., the timing (not the operator, water batch, or paper) is what shapes the picture. That is the falsifiable core under the "cosmic formative forces" language.

**Honest status of the claim:** contested. The "Kolisko effect" is not accepted by mainstream science; the anthroposophical community's own modern replication attempts (sciencegroup.org.uk, 2021) reported mixed/inconclusive results at Moon–Saturn conjunctions. The method itself (metal-salt chromatography on filter paper) is real chemistry; what is contested is whether timing/configuration changes the pattern in a way that replicates. This dossier does NOT assume the claim is true — the protocol is designed so it can pass OR fail cleanly.

**Claimant:** Lili Kolisko / Biological Institute at the Goetheanum (Stuttgart)
**Verification status:** unverified; modern replication attempts inconclusive

## The Protocol — simultaneous Steigbild nights vs control nights

Testable core: **if the evening (its configuration) shapes the rising picture, then pictures from the SAME evening in DIFFERENT households should cluster together under blind rating, more than pictures from different evenings.** Operators, water batches, and paper lots are controlled by design: each household uses its own materials, so cross-household similarity cannot come from shared materials.

### Materials (~$20 per household)

1. **Filter paper**: round laboratory filter paper OR large coffee filters (a few dollars)
2. **Silver nitrate** (AgNO₃), 1-5g lab grade (~$10-15) — the classic Kolisko silver/Moon salt
   - Alternative if unavailable: iron(II) sulphate (moss killer, garden store, ~$5) — Kolisko used iron for Mars; any single consistent salt works for the clustering test
3. **Glass jars or dishes**, one per household
4. **Distilled water** (grocery store, ~$1-2) — same brand per household across all nights
5. **Camera** (phone) with consistent lighting and framing
6. **Gloves + eye protection** — silver nitrate permanently stains skin and fabric; keep away from children's hands, handle with care

### Setup

**≥2 households** (2-4 is ideal — neighbors, friends, other Village families). Each household runs the SAME procedure on the SAME evenings. No sharing of solutions, paper, or water between households.

### Procedure

1. **Pick 4 evenings**: 2 "event" evenings (ideally near a named astronomical configuration — full moon, new moon, or a Moon–planet conjunction listed on any astronomy app) and 2 "control" evenings (at least 5 days from any listed configuration). All households run the same 4 evenings.
2. **Each evening, per household**: dissolve a fixed amount of salt (e.g., 0.5g AgNO₃ in 100ml distilled water — keep the ratio identical across households and nights). Stand the filter paper in the solution (rolled cylinder in a jar, or wick to center of a round sheet). Let it rise 30-60 minutes undisturbed, same duration every night.
3. **Dry and photograph**: dry the paper flat, photograph under the same lighting at the same distance, label only with a secret code (household letter + night number — e.g., B3 — written on the back).
4. **Repeat for all 4 evenings.** Total: 4 pictures per household.
5. **Blind rating session**: one person (not a tester, or a tester who hasn't seen the codes) shuffles all photos, shows them to ≥3 raters individually, and asks each rater to **sort the photos into 4 groups by visual similarity** (pattern type, banding, texture). Raters must not know which evening any photo came from.
6. **Score the sorting**: for each rater, count how many same-evening photo pairs were placed in the same group vs different-evening pairs. Chance level depends on group counts — with 3 households × 4 evenings = 12 photos sorted into 4 groups, random placement puts ~25% of pairs together; use the rater average vs that chance line.

### Safety notes (non-negotiable)

- Silver nitrate is caustic on skin and permanently stains. Adults handle the solution; children observe the rising picture only. Gloves and eye protection.
- Do not ingest; dispose of leftover solution per label (small lab quantities of dilute AgNO₃ can go to a hazardous-waste drop-off; do not pour on soil — silver is a heavy metal).

## Pass/Fail Criteria (pre-registered)

**PASS (supports the claim):**
- Across ≥3 blind raters, same-evening photo pairs are grouped together at **≥1.5× the chance rate** (e.g., ≥37.5% vs 25% chance), AND
- The clustering replicates in a second 4-evening round with the same households

**FAIL (refutes, for these households and this protocol):**
- Same-evening pairs group at or below chance (~25%), or raters cluster by household instead of by evening (meaning operator/materials dominate, not timing)

**INCONCLUSIVE:**
- Only 1 household completes all evenings (no cross-household comparison possible)
- Fewer than 3 raters
- Clustering between 1.0-1.5× chance that doesn't replicate

**Honest note:** A FAIL is the expected result under mainstream chemistry — and is fully publishable (Skeptic's Star). A PASS would be genuinely surprising and would justify a bigger multi-family round with tighter controls. Either way the community learns something real about a method that shaped a century of Goethean science.
