## The Claim

The water-memory tradition holds that **water retains an imprint of what it has been
exposed to** — a spoken intention, an electromagnetic field, a substance since
diluted away — and that the imprint persists after the influence is removed and
shows up in the water's physical behaviour.

The claim has two distinct lineages in the archive, and they need separating:

1. **The human-influence lineage (Zenin / Emoto).** Zenin's laboratory monitored
   water after human contact and reported changes in electrical conductivity and
   in the water's cluster structure; Emoto photographed ice crystals from water
   exposed to words, music, and intention, and reported that "kind" influences
   produced symmetrical hexagonal crystals and hostile ones produced irregular
   forms. Zenin's own methods were refractometry, HPLC, and proton NMR — all
   instruments — and he explicitly claims the effect is real and measurable in
   principle.
2. **The EM-field lineage (Pollack-cited).** Low-frequency fields (7.8 Hz, 75 Hz)
   are reported to induce structural changes in water that persist 30+ minutes
   after the field is removed.

**Honest status of the claim — stated up front:**

- **Emoto's work is widely criticized as unblinded and subjective.** The crystal
  selection step (choosing "the most frequently occurring shape" from fifty
  photographs) has no published blinding or inter-rater protocol. The claim is
  not established.
- **Zenin's own article concedes the measurement problem:** *"Numerical values
  that would allow quantitative conclusions are practically impossible to obtain,
  since the principle of encoding information in the form of geometric images is
  unknown."* The source says quantification is impossible — and the point of this
  card is to test whether that is true, with instruments and a blind design.
- **Pollack's structural explanation for the EZ is itself contested.** J. Michael
  Schurr's ion-gradient/diffusiophoresis account predicts the measured EZ growth
  kinetics and does not require a distinct water phase (see
  `synthesis/2026-09-12-pollack-ez-water-validation.md`, Tier 2). The phenomenon
  is real; the mechanism is live.
- **Benveniste's high-dilution lineage** (`sources/2026-09-13-archive-raid-water-memory-expired.md`)
  is the historical root of the same idea and remains outside mainstream
  acceptance.

So the paper does not establish the claim. It establishes a **method** (Emoto's
freezing protocol), a **claimant** (Zenin/Emoto/Pollack-cited), and a **puzzle** —
which is exactly what makes it worth a family's bench.

**Claimant:** the water-memory tradition, as formulated by S.V. Zenin (1999) and Masaru Emoto
**Verification status:** unverified; no independent replication known to the archive; the crystal method has never been blind-scored
**Mainstream position:** liquid water's structure relaxes on picosecond timescales; no accepted mechanism for a persistent imprint; conductivity and refractive index are well-understood functions of dissolved ions and temperature

## The Protocol — two influence arms, three hard endpoints, one blind endpoint

The testable core: **if water retains an imprint, a blinded measurement of physical
properties after the influence is removed should show it.** If only temperature,
dissolved CO₂, evaporation, and handling are changing the readings, it will show
that instead — which is a real result too.

### Materials (~$40–120)

1. **Handheld refractometer** (~$25–60) — reads refractive index to ±0.0001.
2. **Kitchen scale, 0.01 g resolution** (~$15–30) — for evaporation rate.
3. **Surface-tension drop-count kit** — a clean glass slide, a 1 mL syringe, and a
   counted-drop stand (~$10); or a cheap tensiometer if available.
4. **12 identical glass jars with lids** — lids matter (see confounds).
5. **Thermometer** (±0.1 °C).
6. **Freezer** (already owned) + **Petri dishes** + **USB microscope** (~$20,
   optional) — for the ice-crystal arm.
7. **Labels/codes** + a sealed key (whoever codes the jars does not measure).
8. **Timer** and a log sheet.

### Procedure

1. **Split one batch.** Pour from a **single** bottle/container of water across all
   jars, so batch identity is controlled. Six jars per arm.
2. **Code the jars.** A family member who will *not* measure and will *not* send
   assigns each jar a random code and seals the key. Nobody else sees it until
   analysis.
3. **Measure the noise floor.** Take 10 repeated readings of one jar on each
   instrument. The standard deviation is the meter's repeat-measurement noise —
   the threshold everything else is judged against.
4. **Baseline.** Measure all three hard endpoints on every jar, same operator, same
   order, same room temperature.
5. **The attempt — Arm A (spoken intent).** Half the jars receive a spoken
   intention (the Emoto/Zenin form: a repeated phrase, or a group intention). The
   other half is **handled identically** — same number of handlings, same duration
   of contact, same ambient conditions — but with no attempt. The sender does not
   know the coding.
6. **The attempt — Arm B (EM field).** A separate run: half the jars sit inside a
   coil driven at **7.8 Hz** for 30 minutes; the other half sit in an identical
   coil with no drive current. Same room, same duration, same distance.
7. **Re-measure.** Re-measure all three hard endpoints, blinded to group.
8. **The ice-crystal arm.** Freeze 0.5 mL from each jar in a Petri dish at −25 °C.
   Photograph the crystals under the microscope (working cold); score morphology
   **blind** with 2–3 raters against a symmetry/regularity rubric written down
   before the first photograph.
9. **Repeat 3 sessions** on different days, re-coding jars each session.
10. **Analyze.** Compare mean endpoint change in the influenced group vs the
    handled-only group, against the measured noise floor. Unseal the key *after*
    the numbers are recorded.

### Controls and confounds (document all)

- **Temperature:** refractive index and evaporation rate are strongly
  temperature-dependent. Measure at a recorded, matched temperature; keep the room
  stable.
- **Dissolved CO₂:** open water absorbs CO₂ and its properties drift. Keep jars
  lidded, equal exposure time, same headspace.
- **Evaporation:** this is *itself* an endpoint — so weigh with lids on, and use a
  separate identical set for the evaporation arm, or accept and record the
  interaction.
- **Handling:** the sham jars must get the same handling count as the influenced
  jars, or you are testing warmth and agitation, not intent.
- **Instrument drift:** re-check the noise floor each session; recalibrate the
  refractometer with distilled water.
- **Selection bias in the crystal arm:** this is the failure mode of the original
  work. The rubric and the raters must be fixed *before* any photograph is taken.
- **Operator:** one measurer throughout, blinded to group; same order.

## Pass/Fail Criteria (pre-registered)

**PASS (supports the claim):**
- Blinded mean change in the influenced group is **≥ 2× the measured instrument
  noise floor** on **≥ 2 of the 3 hard endpoints**, AND
- The direction is **consistent in ≥ 2 of 3 sessions**, AND
- The handled-only control group shows **no** comparable shift, AND
- The blind crystal scoring shows a **consistent** morphology difference
  (agreement between raters above chance)

**FAIL (refutes, for this family/bench):**
- No difference between influenced and control groups, OR
- Both groups shift equally (that is handling/temperature/CO₂, not imprint), OR
- Crystal scoring shows no consistent difference

**INCONCLUSIVE:**
- Effect present but below the 2× threshold
- Fewer than 3 sessions completed
- Temperature, CO₂, or evaporation drift dominated the readings
- Raters disagreed on the crystal rubric

**Honest note:** the most likely outcome for a family is **FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE**,
and that is a genuinely useful outcome: it converts "water memory" from an
unexamined belief into a measured null on your own bench, with the archive's own
source cited. A clean FAIL earns the Skeptic's Star. A PASS would be a serious
anomaly worth telling the world about — which is why the protocol is blinded
before anyone touches the water.

## Honest caveats (do not skip these)

- **The source says the effect may be unquantifiable.** Zenin's article states that
  numerical values are "practically impossible to obtain." This card tests that
  assertion directly. If the hard endpoints show nothing while the crystal arm
  shows something, that is itself the interesting finding — and it is exactly the
  result the original work's critics predict.
- **The crystal arm is the weak arm.** It is included because it is the archive's
  own documented method, not because it is strong. Score it blind or do not score
  it at all.
- **A null result is not a scandal.** Water memory has been tested and failed
  before (Benveniste's 1988 Nature paper was followed by a controlled investigation
  that found no effect). This card is not a rescue attempt; it is a clean local
  measurement.
- **Home instruments are crude.** The refractometer and scale exist to make the
  readings *comparable*, not to make them publishable. Say so in your report.
