## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The report's own summary, translated:

> "Through magnet action, a densification of the structure is recognisable in various waters. Through vortexing, structural elements can be cancelled (hatching and crossing lines, for instance in water vortexing), while others, such as the bluish core observed in the centre, can be strengthened. Comparisons of similar waters with one another suggest that through water vortexing, non-original structural elements are cancelled, while tendencies originally belonging to the water are strengthened. This would, however, have to be verified on a series of similar waters as well as on waters with completely different structure formation. Minerals act in part in a harmonising and strengthening way on existing structural tendencies. In part, a far-reaching reordering of the structure arises."

The method, in the report's own words:

> "Glass microscope slides with defined properties are rubbed dry with soft, absorbent but abrasion-resistant paper and a microfibre cloth. … In order to keep the influence of the Earth's magnetic field constant, the slide is aligned in a north-south direction. Then, as small as possible drops are applied to the slide manually with a sterile disposable syringe. After being applied, the drops dry in the air under normal room conditions. Favourable room conditions are between 19 and 25 °C at a humidity between 50 and 70 % … Observation under the microscope is carried out at relatively low magnifications in the dark field or using phase contrast. A whole drop is as a rule captured with a 4× objective … for sections and details, objectives with focal lengths of 10× and 20× were used."

The report's three treatment arms and their claimed signatures:

| Arm | Treatment (as reported) | Claimed signature in the dried drop |
|---|---|---|
| **Vortexing** | Martins vortexer (with/without shower attachment), Vita Vortex, and a large hyperbolic funnel after Walter Schauberger | Hatching and crossing lines largely disappear; the outer edge becomes more pronounced; the bluish core in the centre is strengthened ("particularly strongly with the vita-vortex attachment") |
| **Magnet** | 30 mL samples standing **30 min** on a permanent magnet | Densification of the structure; flow forms strongly reduced and concentrated in the centre; edge and centre "no longer set apart" |
| **Minerals** | A mineral cuff wrapped on the pipe; a rock crystal placed in the water | Harmonising / strengthening of existing tendencies; in part "a far-reaching reordering" |

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a treated water's dried-drop residue differ from an untreated sample of the *same* water in a way a **blind** rater can read — and are the vortex and magnet signatures distinguishable from each other, as the report claims they are "of a different kind"?

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the report and no home test of it.** A grep for `drop-drying`, `Trocknungsstruktur`, `micro-optical`, `Kübler`, and `drying structure` across `living-library/synthesis/` returns no card and no dossier. The queue's nearest water-structure cards test **light scattering** (058, form-field), **thermal memory** (042), **dissolved composition** (054), and **light-scattering cluster structure** — **none tests a dried-drop pattern, and none tests whether a *blind* observer can read a treatment signature at all.**

Four things the card must say out loud, because they are what a family is actually testing:

1. **The report is unblinded, and it says so.** Every sample in it was prepared, dried, and read by an operator who knew which water was which. The report's own conclusion — "non-original structural elements are cancelled" — is an interpretation of images the reader already knew the provenance of. **The card's central design choice is blinding:** coded slides, raters who never see the preparation, and a forced-choice discrimination score. This is the control the source lacked.
2. **The endpoint is perceptual, and the card scores it as such.** The dried-drop pattern is judged by a human eye, so the honest endpoint is not "the structure changed" but "**a blind rater can tell the difference at better than chance**." A perceptual rating is a legitimate measurement *if* it is blinded, repeated across raters, and scored against chance — the same discipline the queue's dowsing cards use.
3. **The report itself calls for verification.** Its own words: "This would, however, have to be verified on a series of similar waters as well as on waters with completely different structure formation." The card is that verification, at home scale, on one water.
4. **The expected result is a null, and that is a complete result.** The dried-drop pattern is sensitive to dust, humidity, drop size, slide cleanliness, and the water's own aging — all of which can move the image more than the treatment does. The most likely outcome is that blind raters score at chance. That is a clean refutation of the *readability* claim, and it is scored as a complete result, not a failure.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that the drop-drying pattern does not carry a treatment signature a blind rater can read, and the honest verdict is *"the dried-drop structure is not a discriminator for these treatments at home scale."* A discrimination rate above chance, replicating across raters and sessions, would be genuinely surprising and would justify the taller follow-up (a series of waters, a pre-registered feature checklist, and an instrumented image metric).

## Why it matters

- It is the queue's **first drop-drying / micro-optical water-structure card** — the method the whole Schauberger "water revitalisation" market implicitly appeals to, and which no card has ever tested.
- It is the queue's **first card whose endpoint is a blinded visual-pattern discrimination** — the perceptual analogue of the dowsing cards' forced-choice scoring, applied to water rather than to a hidden target.
- It is the queue's **first card that tests whether three treatments are distinguishable from one another** (vortex vs. magnet vs. control) rather than whether one treatment beats a baseline — the report's specific claim is that the changes are "of a different kind," which is a *discriminability* claim.
- It teaches the transferable lesson: **a perceptual method is tested by blinding the perceiver, not by arguing about the images.** And: **a method that has not been shown to separate a known-different pair is not a measurement** — the instrument check is mandatory.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$40–200** — a USB microscope or a low-power microscope with a camera (~$30–150), a pack of clean glass microscope slides (~$5), soft absorbent paper + a microfibre cloth (~$3), a sterile disposable syringe or a micropipette (~$2–15), a permanent magnet (~$5), a vortexing device (a Schauberger-style vortexer, or a simple magnetic stirrer / a funnel — ~$0–60), and a coding envelope. A dark-field or phase-contrast setup is desirable; a dark background with oblique illumination is a workable substitute.
- Safety: **low.** No high voltage, no chemicals, no sharps beyond the syringe. The only real hazards are the microscope lamp and a slippery floor. Family-safe; suitable for a supervised older child.
- Accessibility: an afternoon to prepare and dry the slides (drops dry in the air), one session to photograph and rate.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Glass microscope slides** of consistent type, cleaned with soft absorbent paper and a microfibre cloth (the report's own cleaning step — it removes the finest quartz dust, which would otherwise show in the dark field).
- **A sterile disposable syringe** (or a micropipette) — the report applies "as small as possible drops" manually; **fix a drop volume** (e.g. 2 µL) so drop size is not a variable.
- **A microscope with a camera** — dark field or phase contrast preferred; a **4× objective** for the whole drop, **10×/20×** for detail (40–200× through the eyepiece). A USB microscope with a fixed stand and fixed lighting is an acceptable substitute **if** the stand and lighting are identical for every slide.
- **A vortexing device** — the report used a Martins vortexer (with and without a shower attachment), a Vita Vortex, and a large hyperbolic funnel after Walter Schauberger. Any device that puts the water into a rotating vortex motion is a faithful arm; **record exactly which device and how long**.
- **A permanent magnet** — the report stands **30 mL** samples on it for **30 min**.
- **A mineral sample** (optional third arm) — the report used a mineral cuff and a rock crystal; a clean quartz crystal in the water is a faithful arm.
- **A north–south alignment guide** — the report aligns the slide N–S to keep the Earth's field constant. A compass and a marked tray do this.
- **A thermometer and a hygrometer** — the report's favourable conditions are **19–25 °C, 50–70 % RH**; record them, because they move the drying pattern.
- **Coding envelopes and a log sheet** — for blinding and pre-registration.

## Protocol (blinded, pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — instrument check (mandatory).**
1. Prove the method can separate a **known-different pair**: e.g. distilled water vs. hard tap water, or tap water vs. tap water with a trace of salt. Prepare, dry, photograph, and have a blind rater sort them.
2. **If the method cannot separate the known-different pair, a no-difference result is VOID, not a null.** "No difference" and "the method could not have seen a difference" are different results and get filed identically if this check is skipped.

**Phase 1 — prepare and pre-register.**
3. Draw **one batch** of water (the same tap or well water for every arm). Split it into four arms:
   - **A — vortexed:** pass through the vortexing device for a fixed time (e.g. 1 min); record the device and duration.
   - **B — magnet-treated:** stand **30 mL** on a permanent magnet for **30 min** (the report's own parameters).
   - **C — control:** the untreated water, drawn at the same time.
   - **D — sham:** handled identically to A and B (poured, waited 30 min, decanted) but **not treated** — this controls for pouring, waiting, and handling.
4. Write the **pre-registration sheet** before any slide is dried: the arms, the drop volume, the drying conditions, the number of slides per arm (≥5), the coding scheme, the raters (≥3), the questions, and the thresholds. Photograph it.

**Phase 2 — dry and photograph.**
5. Clean the slides (paper + microfibre), align them **N–S**, and apply a **fixed-volume drop** to each with the syringe.
6. Let the drops dry in the air at **19–25 °C, 50–70 % RH**. Record the actual temperature and humidity.
7. Photograph each dried drop under **identical lighting, magnification, and camera settings**. Capture the whole drop at 4× and a detail at 10×.
8. **Code the images** (random 3-digit codes) and have a second person hold the key. The rater must never see the preparation.

**Phase 3 — rate blind.**
9. Each rater answers two forced-choice questions, one image at a time:
   - **Q1 (detection):** "Is this treated or untreated?" (2-way, chance = 50 %)
   - **Q2 (discrimination):** among the treated images, "Is this vortexed or magnetised?" (2-way, chance = 50 %)
10. Raters work alone, without discussion, and may not see the key until every sheet is in.

**Phase 4 — score.**
11. Compute each rater's accuracy for Q1 and Q2, and the pooled accuracy.
12. Compare against chance. **Repeat the whole run on a second day** with a fresh batch.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** blind raters distinguish treated from untreated at **≥ 1.5× chance** (≥ 75 % on a 2-way choice) **and** vortexed from magnetised at **≥ 1.5× chance**, replicating across raters **and** in a second session — the report's "different kinds of change" claim survives and needs the taller follow-up (a series of waters, a pre-registered feature checklist, an instrumented image metric).
- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** discrimination is **at chance** (within the binomial noise band) in ≥ 2 of 3 sessions — the dried-drop pattern does not carry a treatment signature a blind rater can read. A complete result; Skeptic's Star.
- **FAIL (void, not a null):** the method cannot separate the **known-different instrument-check pair** — the run measures nothing and is void.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** fewer than 3 raters; one session; the rater saw the preparation or the key; the slides were not coded; drop volume not fixed; room conditions outside 19–25 °C / 50–70 % RH; lighting or magnification differed between slides; fewer than 5 slides per arm.
- **ARTIFACT:** the discrimination tracks **dust or slide cleanliness** rather than the treatment (repeat with all slides from one cleaned batch); the discrimination tracks **drop size** (the rater can see a bigger drop); the discrimination tracks **drying order or position on the tray** rather than the arm; the discrimination tracks the **rater's expectation** (unblinded); or the **sham arm (D) is distinguished from the control (C)** — handling alone produces the signal.

## Evidence

Photo of the setup (slides, syringe, microscope, magnet, vortexer) + the **pre-registration sheet** (arms, drop volume, conditions, N, raters, questions, thresholds) + the **instrument-check result** (the known-different pair, sorted blind) + the coded slide images (whole drop + detail) + the **rater score sheets** (Q1 and Q2, per rater) + the accuracy table against chance + the recorded temperature and humidity + the second-session repeat + the void/artifact checks reported whether or not they void the run.

## Confounds (named in advance)

- **Dust and slide cleanliness — the central confound.** The report's own cleaning step exists because quartz dust shows in the dark field. If the slides are not cleaned identically, the rater may be reading dust, not water.
- **Drop size.** A larger drop dries to a larger, differently-structured residue. **Fix the volume** and check that the rater cannot simply read size.
- **Room conditions.** Temperature and humidity move the drying pattern more than most treatments. Record them; keep them inside the report's band; if they drift between arms, the run is inconclusive.
- **Drying order and tray position.** The first drop to dry and the drop nearest the window can differ. Randomise the slide order and the tray positions.
- **The water's own aging.** Water drawn at the start and used at the end has aged differently. Draw one batch, split it, and treat all arms within the same session.
- **The Earth's field.** The report aligns slides N–S to keep it constant. Do the same, or randomise the orientation and record it.
- **The rater's expectation.** The whole card is built around blinding; if the key leaks, the run is void.
- **The sham arm.** If the sham (D) is distinguished from the control (C), the signal is handling, not treatment.
- **One session is a sample of one.** Repeat on a second day before concluding anything.

## Verdict rules

Q1 and Q2 both above chance, replicating across raters and sessions → **the claim survives** (surprising; needs the taller follow-up). Q1 and Q2 at chance in ≥ 2 of 3 sessions → **the readability claim is refuted** (the expected, complete result). The instrument check fails to separate the known-different pair → **void, not a null**. Sham distinguished from control, or the key leaked, or drop size readable → **artifact**. Fewer than 3 raters, one session, or conditions outside the band → **inconclusive, not a refutation**.
