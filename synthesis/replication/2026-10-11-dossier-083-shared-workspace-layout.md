## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source asserts that the **positions of the workstations in a shared work hall, relative to a ground energy line the authors located by dowsing, materially affect the performance of the workers and the effort they expend** — and that relocating the machines off that line, together with four other layout changes, raised the group's rated performance and its daily output.

The source's own problem statement, quoted:

> "The research aimed to use the bio-energy sciences, among them the science of 'Radiesthesia', in order to improve the performance of the sewing workers in readymade garment factories … The performance rate of the workers was measured before preparing for the experiment, then an adjustment was made to the shape of the production line and the positions of some of the machines were changed because they conflicted with a ground energy line full of harmful charges, using the methods of the science of Radiesthesia …"

The dowsing instruments, quoted:

> "A- The electric vertical pendulum: to measure the harmful vertical electric rays called 'Curry and Hartmann lines' surrounding the workers and present in the production hall, and also to determine the locations of the earth radiations and the energy lines that exist in the production hall.
> B- The IK pendulum: it is used to measure the energy level and determine the wavelengths of the things to be measured … the response of this weight to the wavelengths appears when it begins to rotate clockwise or counter-clockwise.
> C- The Teslameter device … D- The voltmeter device …"

**The intervention, quoted in full — and this is the crux of the dossier:**

> "C- Adjusting the distances between the machines and one another, then re-arranging the machines and determining the passage distances from 60 cm to 90 cm.
> D- Removing the unused machines from the production hall.
> E- Adjusting the shape of the production line after arranging the machines and the distances occurring between each machine and the other, based on the energy paths that were monitored …"

> "A- Determining the locations of high-pressure drop
> B- Determining the locations of the high energy lines and avoiding them in placing the machines near them, according to what the science of 'Radiesthesia' stipulates."

And, from the abstract, the fifth change:

> "Also, electric combs were used, placed on the main cable feeding the machines, to bundle the paths of the electrical charges and prevent them from spreading."

**The site and the sample, quoted:**

> "The T-shirt production workers in the 'Tiba' readymade garment factory." … "the sample consisted of (20) sewing workers … it consisted of (3) Singer sewing-machine workers, (11) overlock workers, (4) overedge (Orlia) workers, and (2) tape-attachment workers, and this sample is the one representing the T-shirt production line."

The hall's coordinates are given (30.116171 N, 31.2645525 E, Cairo). Sixteen factory experts filled an open questionnaire; three factory experts (a planning supervisor, a quality controller and a production supervisor) scored the workers' performance before and after on a three-point scale.

**The result, quoted:**

> "the daily production level before preparing the experiment ranged from 4200 to 4500, and after preparing the experiment the production rate increased to range from 4700 to 4800."

> "The value of 't' equals '13.042' for the total score of the performance of the sewing workers on the production line … where the mean score of the workers in the post-application was '28.612', while the mean score of the workers in the pre-application was '18.573'."

> "This indicates that the energy paths have a positive effect on the rate of work performance …"

And the recommendation, quoted:

> "Addressing the readymade garment factories to apply the science of 'Radiesthesia' in the production halls, to reduce the workers' exposure to harmful electromagnetic charges and to limit them."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** with the working group blind to which position is which and the order counterbalanced, does a work position the dowser rated as sitting **on** a ground energy line produce worse output than a matched work position the dowser rated as **off** it — by more than the group's own session-to-session variance?

## Honest status of the claim

**This is a real field study in a real factory with real numbers, published in a peer-reviewed-format journal — and as a test of the dowsing claim it is close to worthless, because it has no control and it changed five things at once.**

1. **There is no control group and no control condition.** The design is a single-group pre/post. Every worker was measured before, the hall was changed, every worker was measured after. There is no untreated line, no second hall, no sham relocation, no randomisation, no blinding of the three experts who scored the workers. **A rise is guaranteed by the design**, whatever caused it.
2. **Five changes were made at once, and four of them are ordinary industrial engineering.** The intervention bundled (a) moving machines off the dowsed line, (b) widening the inter-machine passages from 60 cm to 90 cm, (c) removing unused machines, (d) clearing accumulated fabric and furniture out of the aisles, and (e) fitting "electric combs" to the main feed cable. **Only (a) and (e) are radiesthetic claims; (b), (c) and (d) are textbook layout and 5S work**, and the source itself documents the starting state as crowded, obstructed and cluttered ("the production hall … is full of accumulations and piles of fabric in places not designated for it, and the machines are close to one another without observing the existence of passages"). **A productivity rise from clearing the aisles is the expected result of the ergonomic half alone, and the study cannot separate it from the dowsing half.**
3. **The dowsed line is the input, not a measurement.** The "energy path" was located with an electric vertical pendulum and an IK pendulum. The archive holds **controlled dowsing nulls** — the ETH Zürich 1946 trials and the Wunder 2003 series — and the queue has already carded the blind dowsing question four times (blind water-line location, blind concordance, water vein in the laboratory, dowsed-ground bioassay). **This card does not re-test whether dowsing works; it takes the dowser's rating as given and tests whether that rating predicts a group's output.**
4. **The primary outcome is an unblinded observer rating.** The performance score is a three-point scale ("accurate / somewhat accurate / not accurate") applied by three factory experts who knew the study was running and knew which layout was in place. The reliability coefficients reported (Cronbach's alpha 0.71–0.93) establish that the raters agreed with each other — **not** that they were blind.
5. **The production figures are ranges with no variance and no test.** "4200 to 4500" before and "4700 to 4800" after is a ~10% rise stated as a pair of ranges; no standard deviation, no dispersion, no significance test is given for the output number (the t-tests in the paper are for the *rating* and the *effort* scores).
6. **The Hawthorne effect is a complete alternative explanation.** Twenty workers were told they were part of a study, their hall was rearranged, and experts came to score them. Output rising under those conditions is one of the oldest findings in industrial psychology.
7. **The venue and the citation network.** *International Design Journal* is an Egyptian design/engineering journal, not a physics, medical or occupational-health venue. The paper's own literature is the authors' own theses and the BioGeometry corpus — it cites **Ibrahim Karim** (*Back to Future for Mankind*, BioGeometry Consulting, 2010) for the "shape – energy – function" framing and the Hemberg, Switzerland mobile-tower study, and cites three unpublished Egyptian theses (Hammouda 2017, Sweidan 2015, Wafa 2008) for the geopathic-stress background. It cites no controlled dowsing trial and no null result.
8. **The archive holds no claim-status record for geopathic stress, the Curry/Hartmann grids, or radiesthesia applied in industry.** Recorded as a gap, not a backlog item.

**What the source DOES give, and why the card exists:**

- A **named intervention** — relocate workstations off a dowsed line — with a stated instrument (a pendulum) and a stated rule ("avoid placing the machines near the high energy lines").
- A **group-scale endpoint the family can actually count** — units of work completed in a session. This is the queue's first card whose measurement is the output of a *group* rather than a reading from an instrument or a person.
- A **stated, falsifiable prediction** — the position on the line predicts worse output.
- A **result already reported**, so the card is a replication rather than a fishing trip.
- A **bundled intervention the card can unbundle** — which is the card's whole contribution.

**The ordinary mechanisms that must be excluded before the claim is interesting:**

| Ordinary mechanism | What it predicts | The control that separates it |
|---|---|---|
| **Ergonomics (spacing, clutter, reach)** | output rises because the aisle is clear and the reach is shorter — the source's changes (b), (c), (d) | **hold the layout constant** between the two arms; vary ONLY the position relative to the line, and keep spacing, reach, seating and clutter identical |
| **Novelty / Hawthorne** | output rises because the group knows it is being watched and something changed | **the group does not know which position is the "on-line" one**; the two positions look the same; the schedule is randomised |
| **Order and learning** | the second session is faster because it is second | **counterbalance and randomise the order** across people and sessions |
| **Time of day / fatigue** | morning sessions beat evening sessions | **same time of day** for every session; log it |
| **Task drift** | the work itself changes between sessions | **one fixed task, one fixed duration, one fixed count**, pre-registered |
| **Rater expectation** | whoever counts the output counts what they expect | **the counter does not know which position is in use** — output is a count, not a judgement |
| **The dowser's own expectation** | the dowser marks the line where the group is already working, or where the light is bad | **the dowser marks the floor before the group's work positions are chosen, and the marks are sealed**; the coder assigns positions from the sealed marks |

**So the honest position is:** the source's result cannot be attributed to the dowsed line, because the source changed five things and ran no control. The card's job is to run **the one variable the source could not isolate** — the line position alone, everything else matched — blind and counterbalanced, against a measured noise floor. The likely outcome is that the two positions perform the same, which is the mainstream prediction.

| | Ordinary (ergonomics, novelty, order) | Claim supported at home scale |
|---|---|---|
| **On-line vs off-line position, layout matched** | no difference beyond the session-to-session noise floor | the on-line position is consistently worse, exceeding the noise floor |
| **Group blind to which position is which** | no difference between arms | the difference survives blinding |
| **Order reversed on the repeat** | the difference disappears | the difference reproduces |
| **Effort score (secondary)** | same at both positions | higher at the on-line position |
| **A second group, different room** | no relationship to the dowser's marks | the dowser's marks predict output in a room the dowser has not worked in |

## Why it matters

Almost every geopathic-stress claim sold to households — a bed over a water vein, a desk over a Curry line, a "harmful radiation" under a chair — is justified by exactly this kind of argument: a place was measured, something was moved, the people felt better. **The sewing-workers study is the strongest version of that argument in the archive: a real factory, twenty workers, a peer-reviewed-format journal, and a number.** It is also the version that cannot support its own conclusion, because it moved five things and compared before with after.

If a family finds that a dowsed line predicts nothing about a group's output once the layout is held constant, they have measured the null on the whole geopathic-layout industry — in their own workspace, with their own numbers. If they find that a position the dowser rated as "on the line" is consistently worse, blind and counterbalanced, in more than one group and more than one room, then a dowser's mark has predicted something about a group of people that the layout cannot explain — and *that* is worth chasing, because it would be the first controlled result of its kind in the archive. Either outcome is a complete result.

## Protocol

**Pre-registration (write and photograph before the first session):** the number of participants and groups; the task and its unit; the session duration; the number of sessions and the schedule; the dowser's identity and the fact that the marks are sealed; the coder's identity; the assignment method; the primary endpoint; the noise floor; the pass/fail thresholds; the exclusion list.

**Entry conditions (hard):**
- **The dowser must not be a member of the working group**, and must mark the floor and rate the candidate positions **before** the group's work positions are chosen. The marks are sealed in an envelope and the ratings are written down and sealed with them.
- **A second person (the coder) must hold the key** — the coder assigns which physical position is "A" and which is "B" from the sealed ratings, and neither the group nor the person counting the output knows which is which. The coder must not work in the group during the sessions.
- **The output must be a COUNT, not a judgement.** Choose a task whose unit is unambiguous (items folded, seams stitched, cards sorted, pots filled, kits assembled, rows knitted). If the task cannot be counted unambiguously, the card cannot be run.
- **The two positions must be matched** for lighting, temperature, seating, surface height, reach and clutter. Photograph both, from the same angles, before the first session, and put the photos in the pre-registration. **If they cannot be matched, say so and stop** — an unmatched pair tests ergonomics, not dowsing.
- **Same time of day, same duration, same task, every session.** No session runs when the group is unusually tired, ill or rushed; log anything that happened.

**Materials:**
- A pendulum (any — the card does not depend on which).
- A timer.
- A fixed, countable task and enough material for every session.
- Two sealed envelopes and a written key.

**The sessions:**
1. **Baseline (4+ sessions).** The group works the fixed task at a **fixed position** with **no changes at all**, same time of day, and the output is counted each session. Record the mean and the standard deviation. **That standard deviation is the noise floor** — nothing below it counts.
2. **The dowser marks the floor.** Before any work position is fixed, the dowser walks the room and marks the line(s) across the floor, and rates each candidate work position as **on the line**, **off the line**, or **neutral**. The marks and ratings are sealed.
3. **The coder assigns two positions.** From the sealed ratings, the coder picks one position rated *on the line* and one rated *off the line*, matches them for lighting/temperature/seating/reach/clutter as far as the room allows, labels them A and B, and writes the key.
4. **The sessions run.** The group works the fixed task at A and B on alternating sessions, order randomised and counterbalanced across people and days, **without being told which is which**. Output is counted by a person who also does not know. The group's own effort rating (a simple 1–5 scale) is recorded after each session.
5. **Repeat with the order reversed.** Every participant works at least **two sessions at each position**, with the order counterbalanced, so that "second session" is not the same thing as "off the line".
6. **Open the key and compare.** Report the per-position means and the difference against the baseline noise floor.

**Measurable outcome (primary):** the **difference in counted output between the on-line position and the off-line position**, per participant and pooled, against the **measured baseline noise floor** (the standard deviation of the four baseline sessions), with the session count shown. Secondary: the effort ratings at the two positions; and, if a second group or a second room can be run, whether the dowser's marks predict output there too.

**Noise floor:** the standard deviation of the four (or more) baseline sessions at a fixed position with no changes. **A difference smaller than this is not measurable with this task in this group.**

## Pass / fail

- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, mainstream result):** the on-line and off-line positions differ by **less than the measured baseline noise floor**, or the difference is not consistent in sign across participants and repeats; the effort ratings are the same at both; a second group or room shows no relationship to the dowser's marks. **A dowsed ground line does not predict a group's output once the layout is held constant** — the family has measured the null on the geopathic-layout claim, and has a measured noise floor for their own workspace. *Skeptic's Star.*
- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** the on-line position is **consistently worse** than the off-line position, the difference **exceeds the measured noise floor**, it **reproduces with the order reversed**, it appears in **more than one participant**, and the two positions were documented as matched for lighting, temperature, seating, reach and clutter. The effort-rating arm is reported either way.
- **FAIL:** the group or the counter knew which position was which; the dowser was a member of the group or saw the chosen positions first; the two positions differed in lighting, temperature, seating, reach or clutter; the task, duration or time of day changed between sessions; the order was not counterbalanced; or a result was read from a single session.
- **VOID:** the baseline noise floor could not be measured; the output could not be counted unambiguously; the dowser's ratings were not sealed before the work positions were chosen; or the two positions could not be matched and the group chose to proceed anyway. **A VOID is not a NULL.**
