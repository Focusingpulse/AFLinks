## The claim (as asserted by the source)

Balck (TU Clausthal) describes the apparatus plainly. A long water hose is laid on a meadow, fed from a raised tank:

> "It proved sensible to choose small water speeds below 0.3 m/s, because presumably due to the still predominantly laminar flow, large sensible structures arise that could be well observed in the range of decimetres and metres."

and the structures he reports:

> "When water flows in the hose, there are on both sides of the hose in each case three sensible zones, which are also labelled L3, L2, L1 and R1, R2, R3 and whose position is marked with sticks (dowsing at walking height approx. 1.1 m)."

> "Concentrically around the hose there are still two double screws. These rotate in opposite directions. The lateral extent is about 0.5 m for the inner ones and about 1 m for the outer ones."

The central quantitative result is that the zone positions **depend on the flow velocity**:

> "Among well seekers there is a rule that one could infer the depth from the lateral distance of the zones. (Bishop's Rule; it is said to have been recognised in 1780 by the Bishop of Grenoble.) How far is it correct?"

> "At different flow velocities of about 5 mm/s to 50 mm/s the distances of the zones R1, R2 and R3 from the hose were determined, in each case for the left and right edges of the zones. The observer moved at normal height on the grass. Surprisingly, a clear dependence shows up (Fig. 9b): with decreasing flow the zones migrate further outward."

> "The greater the velocity, the smaller the distance to the centre at observation height. The angle grows with the velocity."

Two further results the source reports, both countable:

- **The double-screw pitch.** For a 1/4" hose the inner double screw has a pitch of **0.54 m** and the outer **0.73 m** — a ratio of about 2/3.
- **The pipe does not matter; the flow does.** Five aluminium pipes of different diameters (16, 12, 10, 8, 6 mm) were connected **in series** so the same water passed through all of them: "With all five pipes the zones had the same distances from the pipe." The source's own reading: "Evidently, with a laminar flow one has good chances of finding clearly delimited structures outside the hose. At the small gradient stretches at the 'Bächla' the water swirled strongly and the sensible structures there were not clearly recognisable."

And the deeper claim, from a vacuum experiment:

> "It turned out that noble gases such as argon in the air are the mediators for the propagation of sensible structures. If, for example, one places a water coil in a glass vessel and pumps out the air and thereby also the argon, the sensible structures disappear."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** can a family **build** a water vein — a hose of flowing water — and does a dowser find it, blind to whether the water is running?

## Honest status of the claim

**The source is a physicist's field notebook, not a controlled trial, and the card must say so.** Balck is a professor at TU Clausthal (Institute for Energy Research and Physical Technologies); his Fulda 2012 lecture notes the cost contrast himself — "TU Clausthal experiments for 30 Euro" against CERN's 30 billion euro — and his own framing is that the observations are unexplained and worth recording. The experiments are **unblinded**: the observer knows the water is flowing, knows the flow rate, and knows where the hose is. Every zone position in the paper is an **operator-reported** measurement with no control for expectation. The paper does not report a null run, a randomised order, or a blind condition.

**The archive holds the counterweight, and it is strong.** Two controlled dowsing trials sit beside this source:

- **ETH Zürich, 1946.** Seven test fields, multiple dowsers, sealed envelopes opened only after all experiments, no prior knowledge of the locality. The report's conclusion: *"on none of the seven test fields are there reaction areas indicated concordantly by several dowsers that stand out from an undoubtedly reaction-free environment. The goal of the experiments set out at the end of § 1 has therefore not been achieved."* The author is careful in the other direction too: *"to conclude that the rod deflections have no physically graspable cause at all would, I consider, be mistaken. For that purpose the experiments were evidently laid out too primitively and too summarily."*
- **von Zeppelin, 2001 (re-analysed by Wunder, 2003).** Over one square kilometre of built-up Schramberg, "irritation zones" were mapped radi-aesthetically and residents of 100 dwellings reported illnesses. Wunder's re-analysis: *"the results are not conclusive, owing to numerous methodological errors. Moreover, the morbidity of the persons living on 'irritation zones' is not significantly elevated relative to a control group."*

**So the honest position is:** the claim is specific, cheap to test, and has never been tested *in this form* — a manufactured water vein, blind, with the flow rate as the independent variable. The two controlled trials in the archive both returned nulls, but neither tested a **flowing-water hose**; they tested dowsers on natural fields. That gap is exactly what this card closes.

| | Ordinary (no flow-dependent field) | A flowing-water hose produces a sensible field |
|---|---|---|
| **Blind flow/no-flow discrimination** | the dowser's hit rate is at chance | the dowser discriminates flow from no-flow significantly above chance |
| **Zone position vs. flow velocity** | the marked zones do not move with the flow rate | the zones migrate inward as the flow increases (the source's own direction) |
| **The no-flow control** | with the hose full but still, the dowser marks the same zones (or none) | with the hose full but still, the zones vanish |
| **The pipe-swap (optional)** | the zone distances follow the pipe diameter | the zone distances are the same for pipes of different diameter at matched flow |

Four things the card must say out loud:

1. **The claim is a claim, not an established result.** The source is unblinded field observation; the archive's two controlled dowsing trials both returned nulls. Both facts travel with the card.
2. **The expected result is the null, and that is a complete result.** A blind hit rate at chance, with the zones not tracking the flow, is a genuine finding — exactly what the "Skeptic's Star" honours.
3. **The blinding is the experiment.** Without it the family has waved a rod over a hose, not tested a claim. The dowser must not know whether the water is running.
4. **A positive would be genuinely interesting and would need to repeat.** A blind above-chance discrimination *plus* a zone distance that tracks the flow rate *plus* the zones vanishing with the water still would be a real anomaly — and it would justify the taller follow-up (the vacuum/argon mediator test, which is lab-scale).

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is a blind hit rate at chance and zone marks that do not track the flow. That is the honest verdict, and the card is designed to reach it cleanly.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$20–60** — a length of garden hose or HDPE pipe (~15–25 m, ~$10–20), a raised tank (a bucket or barrel on a stand) and a short suction hose (~$5–10), an inline flow meter (~$10–20) or a litre jug and a stopwatch (free), wooden rods or pegs and a tape measure (~$5), and a log sheet. A second person to record the true flow state is required for the blind condition.
- Safety: **low.** No mains water work, no electricity (gravity feed), no chemicals. The only hazards are a slippery grass patch and lifting the tank.
- Accessibility: any garden, yard, or field edge. The hose arc needs a few metres of clear ground.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A length of garden hose or HDPE pipe** (~15–25 m) — the "water vein" body. Laid straight with a 90° arc at one end.
- **A raised tank** (bucket or barrel on a stand, ~1 m up) and a **short suction hose** — gravity feed, no pump.
- **A flow meter** (inline, ~$10–20) **or** a litre jug and a stopwatch — to set and record the flow rate.
- **Wooden rods or pegs** and a **tape measure** — to mark and measure the zone positions.
- **A second person** (the recorder) — required: they set the flow state and record it, blind to the dowser.
- **A log sheet** and a camera.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — pre-register (mandatory).**
1. Write down, before any trial: the hose layout, the flow rates to be tested (e.g. 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5 L/min), the number of blind trials, the randomisation method, the scoring rule, and the thresholds. Photograph the sheet.

**Phase 1 — build the "water vein".**
2. Lay the hose straight on grass with a 90° arc at one end. Set the tank up. Confirm the flow rate with the meter or the jug-and-stopwatch method.

**Phase 2 — the blind condition (the experiment).**
3. The **recorder** sets the state — **flow ON** or **flow OFF (hose full, water still)** — from a pre-generated randomised list, and does not tell the dowser.
4. The **dowser** walks the line at about 1.1 m height and marks any zones with rods, or reports "none".
5. The recorder records the true state and the dowser's marks. Repeat for the pre-registered number of trials.

**Phase 3 — the flow sweep.**
6. With the flow ON and known, run the sweep: at each pre-registered flow rate, the dowser marks the zones L1–L3 and R1–R3 and the recorder measures each zone's distance from the hose with the tape. Plot distance against velocity.

**Phase 4 — the pipe swap (optional, the source's own control).**
7. If two pipes of different diameter are available, connect them **in series** at matched flow and check whether the zone distances follow the pipe or the flow.

**Phase 5 — repeat.**
8. Run the blind series on **two separate days**. A single session is one afternoon's attention.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (no effect — the expected, complete result):** the dowser's marks do not distinguish flow-on from flow-off (blind hit rate at chance), and the zone distances do not track the flow rate (Skeptic's Star).
- **PASS (effect — surprising):** the dowser discriminates flow from no-flow **significantly above chance** in a pre-registered blind series, **and** the zone distances track the flow velocity in the source's direction (zones migrate inward as flow increases), **and** the zones vanish with the hose full but still — reproducibly across two days.
- **FAIL:** the dowser knew the flow state; the trial order was not randomised; the zone positions were not measured; or a result was read from a single trial.
- **VOID:** the flow could not be held constant, the hose was disturbed, or the water ran out mid-trial — a VOID is not a NULL.
