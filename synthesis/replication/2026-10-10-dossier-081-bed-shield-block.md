## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source asserts that underground watercourses, geological faults and cavities emit a **pathogenic radiation** that rises through a dwelling as a band (a "flow"), that sleeping on such a band causes illness, and — the claim this card tests — **that a material placed on the flow blocks it.**

The source's own account of the blocking experiments, quoted in full because it is the whole basis of the card:

> "Can the flows of underground radiations be blocked in some way? Abbot Mermet stated in his book that they can, with iron objects, if these are placed on such a flow. Since I have a water flow in my own yard, I went to investigate this.
>
> First, I placed an iron rod on the flow. When I stepped over it, the rod stood still (remained motionless). This should mean that the flow is blocked. When I removed the rod and carried out a test with the rod, the rod turned again. So the radiation flow returned again when I removed the iron rod. Something did not give me peace, so I went to investigate further.
>
> I placed the rod on the flow again. The rod again did not turn. I asked my wife to remove the rod, which she did. I carried out the check again with the rod. This time, to my surprise, the rod did not turn, as if the flow had remained blocked. Now I removed the rod. When passing over the flow, the rod now showed that the radiation again exists.
>
> From this follows a rule: If someone has placed a block over a flow of radiation, that block is retained until the one who placed it removes it.
>
> I went on to investigate further. Instead of the iron rod I placed a wooden broom handle. Even now the rod did not turn, just as before over the iron rod. When I removed that wooden stick, the radiation appeared again. And this time too my wife could not unblock it.
>
> Then I tried with a pear leaf. The effect completely the same. I took a single strand of hair from my wife's head and placed it on the flow. The flow was blocked.
>
> So for blocking a radiation it does not matter what material is applied. The block exists, and it probably has a mental basis.
>
> When I lifted my wife's strand of hair from the flow, the block disappeared. However, when she lifted her own strand of hair from the flow, the flow returned again, although I had not unblocked it.
>
> Later I did this just by waving my hand, without placing anything on the flow. The block was there, and I could likewise cancel it with my hand.
>
> Later I could do this by merely casting a glance at the place of the flow. The block was there. With the eyes one could afterwards unblock that same block.
>
> I also tested the duration of the block. I placed it mentally on the flow and checked it every two hours during the day. It existed. I left it overnight too; in the morning it still existed, and so through the day until I again cancelled it.
>
> When a well-known radiesthesist of ours came to my home, I mentally blocked that flow, of which he knew nothing, and asked him to investigate whether there was any underground flow in my yard. He found it very easily, although my rod still did not react to the flow. This means: for me there is no flow, for him it is there. This opened new dilemmas for me, and the foreign literature that I possess and study has not to this day clarified it for me. It seems that I am able to block my own rod so that it does not react, but not the flow.
>
> This is one of the very important questions that should be clarified."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** when one person places a "block" on a claimed pathogenic flow, does a **second, blind operator's** reading of that spot change — and does the material matter?

## Honest status of the claim

**This is a practitioner's self-published manual, and its author reports the experiments that undermine his own claim.** The source is unusually candid, and the card carries every one of his own findings:

1. **The source's own conclusion is that the material is irrelevant.** *"So for blocking a radiation it does not matter what material is applied. The block exists, and it probably has a mental basis."* Iron, wood, a leaf, a single hair, a bare hand, and finally a glance all "worked." A claim in which a glance is as effective as a lead plate is a claim about the operator, not about the material.
2. **The source's own cross-operator test came back negative.** The visiting radiesthesist, who knew nothing of the block, found the flow immediately while the author's own rod did not react. The author states the result plainly: *"for me there is no flow, for him it is there."* This is the single most important sentence in the source, and it is the card's primary endpoint.
3. **The source's own rule is unfalsifiable as stated.** *"If someone has placed a block over a flow of radiation, that block is retained until the one who placed it removes it."* A block that only its placer can remove, and that a glance can create, cannot be distinguished from the operator's own expectation — unless the reading is taken by someone else, blind.
4. **The source's own health data is a small uncontrolled series.** *"Of 24 dwellings examined, in 22 cases the sick had a harmful flow beneath the bed."* The author immediately qualifies it: *"the number of dwellings examined would have to be considerably greater in order to be able to assert with certainty that this pathogenic radiation really is the cause of the illness."* There is **no control** — no count of how many beds in the general population sit over a claimed flow. The card does not use this as an endpoint; it is context for why the blocking market exists.
5. **The source's own caveat on blocking is that it is second-best.** *"It should be clearly said that no blocking of pathogenic radiation is as effective as removing oneself from the radiation."*
6. **The source's own list of commercial blockers is a market, not a result.** Lead plates and barite plaster (Jurdana), the Wastra foil (Swiss), the Sirius foil (Croatian), the RET 1 / RET 2 plates (Polish), straw. The source names them without testing any of them against a control.
7. **The source's own mechanism is asserted, not measured.** The 180°-phase-shift interference model is offered as an illustration ("if we represent the amplitude of one pathogenic radiation as a sinusoid…"), not derived from a measurement.

**What the source DOES give, and why the card exists:**

- A **named apparatus and a named procedure** — locate the flow with rod and pendulum, mark it, then place a blocker on it and read the spot again.
- A **specific, falsifiable prediction from the source itself** — the material does not matter, and the block does not transfer to another operator. Both are testable in an afternoon.
- A **stated endpoint a family already knows how to produce** — a present/absent reading at a fixed marked spot.
- **A candid call for exactly this test:** *"This is one of the very important questions that should be clarified. In Zagreb there are gifted radiesthesists who have devoted themselves expressly to this question, the question of blocking. It remains for us to wait a little for the results that they will present to us."*
- **A real-world stake.** People buy lead plates, barite plaster, foils and RET plates to block a claimed radiation they cannot see. The source's own experiments suggest the material is beside the point — which is worth knowing before anyone spends money on it.

**The ordinary mechanisms that must be excluded before the claim is interesting:**

| Ordinary mechanism | What it predicts | The control that separates it |
|---|---|---|
| **The operator's expectation (ideomotor effect)** | the operator reads the spot as "blocked" because they know a block was placed | **a blind setter** — a third person sets the condition from a pre-generated randomised list; neither reader knows it |
| **The placer's own knowledge** | the block "works" only for the person who placed it (the source's own rule) | **the cross-operator arm** — A places, B reads; and C places, A reads |
| **The material** | a dense conductor (iron, lead) blocks and a leaf or a hair does not | **four blocker types at matched placement** — iron rod, wooden stick, a single hair, hand-wave only |
| **The spot drifting** | the "flow" is re-located each time and lands somewhere new | **one fixed marked spot**, taped and photographed before the first trial |
| **The reading scale** | "blocked" and "not blocked" are read loosely and post-hoc | **a forced binary call per trial**, pre-registered, with a stated chance baseline |
| **The trial order** | the operator learns the pattern across a predictable sequence | **randomised order**, generated before the first trial and sealed |

**So the honest position is:** the claim is testable with a rod, a pendulum, tape and a handful of household objects, the source itself supplies the falsifiable prediction, and the source's own single cross-operator observation already points at the answer. **The card's first job is to make the reading blind and second-person**, because that is the one thing the source never did systematically.

| | Ordinary (the block is in the operator) | Claim supported at home scale |
|---|---|---|
| **Blind second operator (B) reads** | B's call is at chance whether or not A placed a block | B's call tracks the block significantly above chance |
| **Blind first operator (A) reads, C placed the block** | A's call is at chance — the block does not transfer from C | A's call tracks C's block |

## Why it matters

The bed is where a person spends a third of their life, and the claim that a band of radiation under it causes illness is one of the oldest and most commercially active claims in the geobiology corpus. The blocking arm of that claim is where money changes hands: lead plates, barite plaster, foils, RET plates. The source's own experiments — a leaf, a hair, a glance — say the material is irrelevant, and his own cross-operator test says the block does not transfer. **If both hold under blinding, the honest conclusion is that the block is a property of the operator's expectation, and the shielding market is selling a placebo.** If instead a second operator's reading tracks a block placed by someone else, the claim has survived the one test it has never been given, and that is worth chasing. Either outcome is a complete result.

## Protocol

**Pre-registration (write and photograph before the first trial):** the marked spot's coordinates on the room plan; the four blocker conditions plus the no-block condition; the number of trials per condition (recommend ≥ 10 per condition per reader, ≥ 50 total); the randomisation method and the sealed list; the chance baseline (0.5 for a two-alternative forced choice); the scoring rule; the pass/fail thresholds; the two readers' names and their prior experience.

**Entry conditions (hard):**
- **A claimed flow must be located first.** Operator A walks the room with rod and/or pendulum and marks the spot where the reading is strongest. **If no flow can be located anywhere in the home, the run is VOID — not a null.** (This is the source's own first step and cannot be skipped.)
- **The spot is fixed.** Tape it, photograph it, and do not re-locate it between trials.
- **Three people minimum:** reader A, reader B, and setter C. C must not read. A and B must not set.
- **C sets the condition from the sealed randomised list** and records it. A and B each call "flow present" or "flow absent" **without knowing the condition**.
- **The blockers are placed identically** (same position, same orientation) for every condition.

**The conditions (five):**
1. **Nothing** (baseline).
2. **Iron rod** — the source's and Mermet's material.
3. **Wooden stick** — the source's second test.
4. **A single hair** — the source's decisive test; a human hair, placed the same way.
5. **Hand-wave only** — no object at all; the setter passes a hand over the spot.

**The arms:**
- **Arm 1 (transfer, primary):** C sets; A reads; B reads. Does either reader's call track C's condition?
- **Arm 2 (placer, the source's own rule):** A places the block; B reads. Then A removes it; B reads again. Does B's reading change when A places or removes?
- **Arm 3 (material):** within Arm 1, compare the hit rate across the four blocker types. The source predicts **no difference**.

**Measurable outcome:** the **blind hit rate per reader per condition**, against the pre-registered chance baseline, with the trial count and the randomisation shown. Secondary: the **material-dependence** (hit rate by blocker type) and the **removal arm** (does the reading return to baseline, and does it matter who removes the block).

**Repeat on a second day** with a freshly generated list, at the same spot, to test reproducibility.

## Pass / fail

- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** neither reader's call tracks the condition above chance (hit rate within the chance baseline for all five conditions), the material makes no difference, and the block does not transfer between operators. **The block is in the operator, not the flow** — the source's own cross-operator observation reproduced at home scale. *Skeptic's Star.*
- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** both readers discriminate blocked from unblocked significantly above chance in the pre-registered blind series, the discrimination **transfers between operators** (B tracks a block A or C placed), and it reproduces on a second day. The material-dependence is reported either way.
- **FAIL:** a reader knew the condition; the trial order was not randomised; the spot was re-located between trials; the setter also read; or a result was read from a single trial.
- **VOID:** no flow could be located in the home at all; the spot was disturbed mid-series; or a reader dropped out mid-series. **A VOID is not a NULL.**
