# Replication Dossier 016 — Wood Acoustic Resonance Test

**Status:** protocol registered
**Created:** 2026-09-13
**Source:** Schauberger, "Water - The Blood of the Earth" (German, translated)
**Guild complement:** Round Wood (material resonance)
**Domain:** shelter (building materials)

## Claim

Schauberger claims that "resonance wood" — slow-grown wood with tight annual rings (almost invisible to the naked eye) — has superior acoustic properties and "almost unlimited durability." This wood was used by Stradivari for violins and is characterized by:

- Extremely tight annual rings
- Uniform organic structure
- Cylindrical trunk form
- Grown in sheltered, shaded locations

The claim implies that wood grain density correlates with acoustic resonance quality.

---

## Protocol

### Materials

- 3-5 wood samples from different trees or species (ideally: one fast-grown, one slow-grown if available)
- Smartphone with free sound analyzer app (e.g., Spectroid, Phyphox, or similar)
- Ruler or caliper for measuring grain density
- Camera for documenting grain
- Consistent tapping implement (same force each time)

### Procedure

1. **Sample collection:**
   - Collect wood samples approximately 6-12 inches long
   - Samples should be similar dimensions for fair comparison
   - Note the source tree species and approximate age if known

2. **Grain density measurement:**
   - Count annual rings visible in cross-section
   - Measure distance across rings
   - Calculate rings per inch (grain density)
   - Photograph grain for documentation

3. **Acoustic resonance test:**
   - Set up sound analyzer app to record decibel decay over time
   - Hold wood sample freely (not clamped) or suspend by string
   - Tap sample with consistent force (practice until consistent)
   - Record the sound
   - Measure sustain time: how long the note rings before decaying by 20dB
   - Repeat 3 times per sample, average results

4. **Analysis:**
   - Compare grain density vs. sustain time across samples
   - Look for correlation: does tighter grain = longer sustain?

### Controls

- Use same tapping force for all samples
- Use same recording distance and environment
- Use same wood dimensions where possible
- Blind analysis: measure sustain time without knowing which sample is which

---

## Pass/Fail Criteria

**PASS:** Sample with tightest grain shows ≥20% longer sustain time than sample with widest grain.

**FAIL:** No consistent correlation between grain density and sustain time (within measurement error).

**INCONCLUSIVE:** Mixed results across samples, no clear pattern.

---

## Evidence Required

- Photos of each wood sample showing grain structure
- Audio recordings of tap tests
- Sustain time measurements from sound analyzer
- Data table: Sample ID | Grain density (rings/inch) | Sustain time (seconds)
- Optional: Frequency spectrum showing fundamental tone

---

## Expected Results

If Schauberger's claim is correct:
- Slow-grown wood with tight rings should sustain longer
- Fast-grown wood with wide rings should dampen quickly
- Correlation should be visible in the data

If the claim is incorrect:
- No correlation between grain density and sustain time
- Other factors (species, moisture, shape) may dominate

- This test measures acoustic resonance, not subtle energy fields
