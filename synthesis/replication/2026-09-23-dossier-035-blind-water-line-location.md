## The claim (as asserted by the sources)

The dowsing/radiesthesia tradition holds that a sensitive person can **locate underground water** — its line, its depth, and its yield — and, in the formulation this card tests, can **tell which way the water is flowing**.

Three distinct pieces of the tradition are in the archive:

1. **The artificial-water-vein experiment (Balck, 2018).** A garden hose laid on the ground with water flowing through it produces a set of **six parallel zones** (three to each side, labelled L3 L2 L1 / R1 R2 R3 in the source), plus **concentric rings and "vortex cells"** around a bend. The source reports that the **zone distances depend on water velocity** — the higher the velocity, the smaller the distance to the hose — with fitted regressions (e.g. `y = 0.62x + 0.86`, `y = 0.84x + 1.15`, `y = 1.28x + 0.78`, `y = 1.38x + 1.22`), and that **dowsers can distinguish left from right (counter-clockwise vs clockwise) with matter in motion**. Balck's own summary claim is the strongest and the strangest: *"Flowing water, moving air, electrical current and light have similar zones: Thus all of this matter in motion shows equal properties."*

2. **The instrumented follow-up (Stängle, 1972).** In January 1929, Gustav Freiherr von Pohl dowsed three water veins in Vilsbiburg (Bavaria) and drew them on a city map. In October 1972, Jakob Stängle — a well-finder credited with "many hundreds" of successful drillings — walked the same three lines with a **scintillation counter and a chart recorder** and reported **more than double the background radiation intensity inside the mapped vein boundaries** compared with the surrounding ground, with the curve shape characteristic of a water-carrying structure. He inferred depths (A: 80–90 m; B: 35–45 m; C: 80–95 m) and yields (2–4, 1–2, 2–5 L/s). The author notes Wüst had reported elevated gamma over geopathic zones in 1956.

3. **The field record (UCR, 2020).** A University of Costa Rica geophysicist documents the practice: two schools (physical — the instrument as a radiation receiver; mental/psychic — the whole action inside the operator, the instrument merely amplifying imperceptible neuromuscular movement), the standard instruments (pendulum, L-rods, Y-rod, biotensor), and a set of training exercises — including **coded envelopes** (identify coffee/salt/sugar blind; "7 of 10 correct indicates you have begun to develop sensitivity") and **determining the direction of underground flow** (walk the line both ways; the pendulum turns faster in the direction of flow). The same document records Hans Dieter Betz (Munich, 1995), head of the German GTZ arid-zone water program, **pairing dowsers with vertical electrical soundings** to site water supplies in Africa and Asia.

**Honest status of the claim — stated up front:**

- **Dowsing is classified as pseudoscience.** The claimed mechanism — a "subtle matter" whose traces a sensitive person can feel — has **no accepted physical basis and no instrument that measures it directly**. Balck says so himself: the "ether" has never been demonstrated experimentally in the visible range.
- **The Stängle result is a single historical case, not a controlled study.** The measurements were made by an interested party over lines drawn by another interested party 43 years earlier; there was no sham vein, no blind re-mapping, and no independent replication. The "geopathic zones cause cancer" claim attached to the same map is **not** part of this card and is not supported.
- **The Balck regressions are the source's own fits to the source's own dowser readings** — no error bars, no blinded operators, no independent site.
- **The UCR document is a survey of a practice, and its author is candid that no comparative technical studies exist.** Its training exercises are teaching conventions, not published results.
- **A null is the likely outcome on a family bench** — and a null is a genuinely useful result: it converts an unexamined belief into a measured null with the archive's own sources cited. The engine's honesty rule applies: this is a test of a claim, not an endorsement of it.
- **The hose is not a water vein.** This is the card's central limitation and it must be stated in the report: a garden hose has no aquifer, no rock strata, no depth, and no yield. A **pass** shows only that the operator can detect *flowing water in a buried hose* better than chance. It does **not** show that anyone can find a real aquifer, and it says nothing about depth or yield. A card that implied otherwise would be exactly the unfalsifiable filler the honesty rule forbids.
- **Safety and honesty:** this is a test of a phenomenon, **not** advice to rely on dowsing to site a real well. Never substitute a dowsing protocol for a hydrogeological survey or a licensed driller.

**Claimants:** Friedrich H. Balck (Germany, 2018); Jakob W. F. Stängle (Germany, 1957–1972) over Gustav Freiherr von Pohl's 1929 map; the radiesthesia tradition documented by Arias Salguero (Costa Rica, 2020)
**Verification status:** unverified; no independent blinded replication known to the archive; the Stängle result is a single interested-party measurement; the Balck zone structure has never been measured by a non-dowser
**Mainstream position:** no accepted mechanism; the reported effect is attributed to ideomotor movement, sensory leakage, and chance unless controlled

## The protocol — one buried line, one blind operator, two endpoints

The testable core: **if a sensitive person can detect flowing water, then an operator kept blind to the layout should (a) mark the hose line closer to where it actually is than a non-dowser control does, and (b) name the direction of flow better than a coin.** If both are at chance, the claim is retired honestly.

### Materials (~$20–40)

1. **A garden hose, 15–30 m** (~$15–25). Half-inch is what Balck used. A soaker or a plain hose both work; a plain hose is better because it has no visible wet line.
2. **A water source and a way to reverse the connection** — a tap, or a bucket and a small pump. The hose is laid out with **both ends emerging at the same edge of the test field**, so the referee can connect the supply to either end without the operator seeing which.
3. **Tape measure and garden stakes or string** (~$5) — to record the true line and to mark the operator's line.
4. **Dowsing rods** — a forked branch (free) or a pair of angle rods (~$0–15). The source tradition uses both; the operator should use whichever they normally use.
5. **A pre-registration sheet and a pen.**
6. **Two or three people**: the **operator** (the person being tested), a **referee** (lays the hose, connects the supply, flips the coin, keeps the key — a child is ideal), and ideally a **control operator** (someone who does not claim the skill, run through the same trials).

### Optional deeper arm — the instrumented cross-check (Stängle arm)

If the family has a **Geiger–Müller counter or scintillation counter** (~$100–200), the Stängle arm can be run: take background readings on a grid perpendicular to the hose, flowing vs static, and ask whether the reading inside the hose's line differs from the surrounding ground. This is the arm that would test the *radiation-over-water-line* claim directly, and it is the only arm in this card that does not depend on a human sensor. It is **optional** because of the instrument cost; the card's pass/fail does not depend on it.

### Design — the parts that make it a measurement

1. **Ground truth first.** The referee lays the hose in a marked field — a gentle S or zigzag, **not a straight line** (a straight line can be guessed). The referee records the true path by driving stakes or laying a string beside it, then **covers the stakes/string** so the operator cannot see them, and **covers the hose** with a thin layer of soil, mulch, or a board so it is not visible. The true path is measured and written down before any trial starts.
2. **The operator is blind.** The operator does not watch the hose being laid and does not know the path. They walk the field with their rods and **mark where they think the line runs** — at least five marked points spread along the field, each measured by the referee as a perpendicular distance from the true line.
3. **Flow direction is randomised and hidden.** For each direction trial, the referee **flips a coin** to decide which end of the hose the supply connects to, connects it out of the operator's sight, and starts the flow. The operator, standing over the line, **states the direction of flow in writing before the referee reveals the coin**. The referee records it.
4. **A static (sham) block.** Some trials are run with the **water off** (hose full but not flowing). The operator is not told which trials these are. A real detector should say "no flow" or fail to locate; a guesser will produce confident answers anyway. This is the false-positive control.
5. **A non-dowser control arm.** The control operator runs the same direction trials. This is the chance-baseline check — it catches a rig that leaks the answer (a wet patch, a sound, a vibration the referee did not think of).
6. **Pre-register everything.** Trial counts, thresholds, the chance model, the scoring rule, and the key-holder are written down **before** the first trial. The key is sealed and photographed.
7. **Unblind only after the last number is written.**

### Endpoints

- **Primary — flow direction:** hit rate over **20 blind trials** against a **50% chance baseline**, one-tailed binomial test. (A 20-trial design can only detect a *large* effect — a real operator should be near-perfect; it cannot resolve a small one. Say that in the report.)
- **Secondary — line location:** the operator's **median perpendicular error** (in metres) at their marked points, compared with the **control operator's** median error and with the **random-guess error** for the field width. A pass requires the operator's error to be clearly smaller than both.
- **Control — sham block:** false-positive rate on the static trials (the operator should say "no flow" or miss the line).

### Pre-registered pass/fail

- **PASS:** blind direction hit rate **≥15/20** (binomial p<0.05, one-tailed, chance 0.5) **AND** operator median location error **< 1.0 m and at least half the control operator's error** **AND** sham false positives **≤2/10** — and the direction result **repeats in a second session**.
- **FAIL:** operator median location error **at or above the control operator's** **AND** blind direction hit rate within chance (7–13/20, p>0.05) — an honest negative, Skeptic's Star.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** the operator cannot produce a signal even on an **open** line (walking the visible hose with the water running) — the blind arms are then *uninformative rather than a refutation*; the key was opened before the final tally; the hose was visible or the flow audible; N was not pre-registered; or sham false positives exceed 20%.

## Why it matters

This is the **community/shared-infrastructure card** in the queue. Every other card tests a device or a treatment; this tests a **shared, teachable skill** that a neighbourhood would use to answer a shared question — *where do we put the well?* — and it does so with **ground truth** (the hose's real position) rather than a perception.

It is also the **first card in the queue with a non-dowser control arm**, which is the design element that makes the result interpretable either way. Card 005 (Blind Geopathic Mapping) compares two dowsers' maps to each other; card 029 (Psi-Track Dowsing) tests a mental target; this card compares a dowser's map to the **actual object**, with a control operator beside them. Different claim, different design, different endpoint — and the overlap (dowsing, blinding) is stated here, not hidden.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build cost: **~$20–40** (hose, stakes, tape, rods). The optional Geiger arm adds ~$100–200.
- Safety: **none beyond ordinary care** — water on the ground, no mains electrical work, no chemicals, no sharp tools. Do not dig near buried services; check for utility lines before driving stakes.
- Accessibility: one afternoon. The whole protocol is a single session plus one repeat.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- Garden hose, 15–30 m, laid in a gentle zigzag.
- Water source + a way to connect either end out of sight.
- Stakes or string (to record the true path, then cover it), tape measure.
- Dowsing rods (forked branch or angle rods).
- A coin, a pen, a pre-registration sheet, a sealed envelope for the key.
- Optional: Geiger–Müller or scintillation counter for the instrumented arm.

## What a result means (and what it does not)

- A **pass** means: this operator, on this field, on this day, beat chance at detecting a buried flowing hose and beat the control operator at locating it. It is a real, local, measured result. It does **not** mean the operator can find an aquifer, and it says nothing about depth or yield.
- A **fail** means: at home scale, with a hose as the target, the claim did not survive blinding. That is a complete and publishable result.
- Either way, the two confounds must be named in the report: **ideomotor movement** (the operator's own hand may move the rods without intent — blinding removes their *knowledge*, not their movement) and **the hose is not a vein** (no aquifer, no strata, no depth, no yield).
