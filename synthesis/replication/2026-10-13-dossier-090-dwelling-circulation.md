## The claim (as asserted by the source)

**"The subtle-energy circulation inside a dwelling has the form of a spiral whose centre is (often) the geometric centre of the room; nooks and obstacles make that flow chaotic."**

The source's own words, from the "Energy circulation" section:

> "The analysis also includes a more subtle approach which indicates to us how energy circulates in the place analysed. This aspect is much more tangible than it appears. One can compare it to the way in which a draught could circulate in a room; if there are many nooks or obstacles, the flow of air will be chaotic; the same goes for the more subtle energies that traverse your dwelling.
> In an ideal situation, this circulation has the form of a spiral whose centre is often the geometric centre of the room."

And, from the same guide, the method by which the practitioner reads it:

> "This study relies on the practitioner's felt sense and on a methodology of investigation. The geobiologist most often uses rods or a pendulum. These instruments merely bring to light what is felt."

And the source's own distinction between the two ways a pendulum is used — which matters for how the card is scored:

> "For resonance dowsing, the movement of the pendulum or of the rods is induced by a physiological phenomenon. It results from the interaction between an external phenomenon and our organism… Mental dowsing relies on a mental questioning and seeks an answer among predefined choices."

The claim, restated as three separable assertions:

1. **Spiral.** The circulation in a room is a *spiral* (a coherent, rotating flow), not a diffuse field.
2. **Centre.** The spiral's centre coincides with the room's **geometric centre** — the source hedges with "often", which is itself part of what the card measures.
3. **Obstacle.** Nooks and obstacles make the flow **chaotic** — i.e. the flow's shape is a function of the room's contents.

## Honest status of the claim

- **It is a practitioner's observation, not a measurement.** The guide states the method plainly ("the practitioner's felt sense… instruments merely bring to light what is felt") and offers **no numbers, no trial count, no control, and no independent replication** for the circulation claim. It is one geobiologist's description of what he sees.
- **The source itself names the failure mode.** Its own "mental dowsing" paragraph concedes that a pendulum can answer an *internal questioning* rather than an external stimulus — i.e. the operator's expectation can drive the instrument. That is exactly the confound this card is built to separate.
- **The claim is hedged in the source's own text** ("often the geometric centre"). A card that scored the claim as "the centre is always the geometric centre" would be testing a stronger claim than the source makes. The card therefore scores **agreement between operators** and **distance from the geometric centre against a pre-registered tolerance**, and treats a loose-but-non-random clustering as a partial result.
- **The archive's own counterweights apply.** The ETH Zürich 1946 field trial (seven fields, sixteen dowsers, sealed envelopes, no concordant reaction areas) and Wunder's 2003 re-analysis of the Schramberg study (morbidity on rod-detected "Reizzonen" not significantly elevated) are the standing nulls for dowsing-based claims. Neither tested interior circulation; that is the gap.
- **No mechanism is proposed** for why a room's subtle-energy flow should form a spiral centred on its geometric centre, and none is needed for the test — but the absence is stated, not hidden.

## The apparatus

- **A room** you can empty — ideally roughly square, so "the geometric centre" is unambiguous.
- **A rod (L-rod or forked) and/or a pendulum** — ~$0–20. The card accepts either; the operator uses whichever they already use.
- **A floor plan of the room at a fixed scale** (1:50), one copy per operator, plus a marked copy for the setter.
- **Masking tape** to mark the room's geometric centre (by measurement, before any reading).
- **A soft obstacle** for the obstacle arm — a foam board or a curtain on a stand, silent to place and easy to remove.
- **A blindfold** (a scarf) for the obstacle arm.
- **Four or more operators**, plus one setter who does not read.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Pre-register before anything runs.** Photograph a sheet carrying: the room's measured dimensions and its geometric centre (marked on the floor with tape by measurement, not by dowsing); the number of operators (target ≥ 4); the number of trials per arm; the tolerance for "the centre is at the geometric centre" (e.g. within 15% of the room's half-diagonal); the obstacle (what it is, where it goes); the order randomisation; the scoring rule; and the pass/fail thresholds below.

**Arm A — the centre (primary).** Empty the room. Each operator works **alone and privately**: they walk the room, read the circulation with their rod or pendulum, and mark on their own plan where the spiral's centre is and which way it turns. Operators do **not** see each other's plans, do not discuss, and are not told the hypothesis beyond "map how the energy circulates in this room." The setter collects the plans. Then measure: (a) the scatter of the marked centres (inter-operator agreement); (b) the distance of the consensus centre from the taped geometric centre.

**Arm B — the obstacle (secondary).** The setter places the obstacle (or not) according to a randomised, sealed list. The operator is **blindfolded**, led in, and maps the circulation by walking the room with the rods; the operator is not told whether the obstacle is present. Repeat for at least 10 trials per operator, half with the obstacle and half without, order randomised. Endpoint: can the operator's map (spiral vs chaotic) be matched to the condition above chance?

**Repeat** both arms on a second day with a fresh list.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** the operators' marked centres scatter no more tightly than chance (no agreement beyond what random marks on a floor plan would give), the consensus centre is not at the geometric centre within tolerance, and the blindfolded operator's maps cannot be matched to the obstacle condition above chance — the circulation is not a reproducible property of the room at home scale (Skeptic's Star).
- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** independent operators, working privately, place the spiral's centre within the pre-registered tolerance of each other **and** of the room's geometric centre, the turn direction agrees, and the blindfolded obstacle arm discriminates above chance — replicating on a second day. This would be the first home-scale evidence for the claim and would justify the taller follow-up (more rooms, more operators, an instrumented arm).
- **FAIL (void, not a null):** the room was not emptied; the geometric centre was marked by dowsing rather than by measurement; operators saw each other's plans or discussed; the obstacle was visible; the operator was not blindfolded in Arm B; or the room changed mid-series.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** fewer than 4 operators; one day only; the order was not randomised; or an operator dropped out mid-series.
- **ARTIFACT:** the marked centres cluster around a *visible* feature of the room (a door, a window, the tape itself) rather than the geometric centre; the result disappears when the tape marking the geometric centre is removed; or the Arm-B discrimination tracks the setter's footsteps or the obstacle's sound rather than the condition.

## Artifacts to watch for

- **The tape as a cue.** If the geometric centre is taped before Arm A, operators may aim at the tape. Run a **no-tape** sub-arm: measure the geometric centre, mark it only on the setter's plan, and see whether the operators' consensus still lands there. This is the single most important control in the card.
- **The visible room.** Arm A cannot be blinded to the room's shape, so the endpoint is *agreement*, not *detection*. Say so in the write-up.
- **The setter's footprint.** In Arm B the setter walks in to place the obstacle; a blindfolded operator may hear them. Have the setter place both conditions (obstacle and sham) with identical footsteps and timing.
- **The operator's prior.** An operator who already believes the claim is the flattery confound; record each operator's prior before the first trial and report the result split by prior.

## What would change my mind

- A second, independent geobiology source describing the same spiral-centred-on-the-geometric-centre claim with a protocol or numbers — that would move the claim from one practitioner's description to a documented tradition worth a taller test.
- A home-scale result where private operators agree on the centre **and** the no-tape sub-arm holds — that would be the first evidence, and would justify instrumenting the room (thermal, acoustic, EM) to look for a physical correlate.
- A null that reproduces across several rooms and operator sets — that would let the queue state plainly that a room's interior circulation is not a reproducible property, which is the more useful thing for a family to know before buying any "harmonising" device.
