## The claim (as asserted by the source)

Egely states the claim in his own words, and states the mechanism as a hypothesis he cannot name:

> *"The speed of the rotation depended on how tired or fit they were and how much they could or could not concentrate."*
> *"Our unanimous experience was that seriously ill people could barely rotate the wheel (even if they had high fever — showing that it's not the heat radiating from their hand that moves the wheel), while healers or successful businessmen had a high revolution number … no sick person could reach a high revolution number, if they could make it move at all; while vibrant personalities who were in good shape always had a high revolution number."*
> — George Egely, Ph.D., *The Story of the Egely Wheel*, Budakeszi, 1994.

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a light rotor spin when a person holds a hand near it, and if so, is the spin **caused by the person's state** — or by the heat and air currents their hand carries?

The source is explicit that the mechanism is **unknown** — Egely says so repeatedly ("I just could not decide what kind of an energy transfer might be the reason for it"; "none of the known interactions can explain the fact of this rotation"). That matters: the card tests the *effect*, and the source's own uncertainty is the honest frame the card inherits.

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds both sides, in one language, from the same decade.** This is the rare case where the claim and its counter-claim are already paired in the holdings:

- **The manufacturer's side** (find 4): a commercial device since 1994, a patent, a VQ scale calibrated against **1,000+ measured persons** (average adult baseline 9 rpm; the inventor reports Ernő Rubik's best at 35 rpm ≈ VQ 600%), and **Egely's own control experiments** — gloves to exclude heat, glass boxes to exclude wind, and hand-shaped metal tanks filled with warm water (up to 113 °F) that produced "at most 10% of the good results with men." He reports the effect is not explained by heat or wind.
- **The skeptic's side** (find 5): the Hungarian Skeptic Society (BME) protocol — the wheel **turns under a heated iron** (no vitality required) and **stops under a plate that blocks airflow**; Iván Gábor's repeatable-experiment study is cited as the systematic refutation.

**Both sides ran controls, and they disagree about what the controls show.** That is exactly the shape of a card worth building: the two sides agree on the apparatus and disagree on the mechanism, so a family that builds the wheel and runs **both** controls — the matched warm object *and* the airflow block — is doing the experiment the disagreement is actually about. A grep across `living-library/` for `egely` returns only the scout find, the database index entries and this card family — **the archive holds the claim and the refutation, but no home replication of either.**

**The leading explanation is physical, and the card says so up front.** A hand near a light rotor on a near-frictionless bearing supplies **thermal convection** (a warm object drives an air plume) and **air currents** (a hand is a moving object). Egely's own account shows he knew this and tried to exclude it (gloves, glass boxes, warm tanks); the skeptics say the exclusions do not hold. The card therefore makes the physical alternatives **separable and pre-registered**, and its honest expectation is that a hand-shaped warm object at the same distance will spin the wheel.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **under $50** — a light rotor (thin card, balsa, or a cut aluminium disc, ~0.3–0.8 g per the patent), a low-friction bearing (a sewing needle point, or a magnetic bearing for the sensitive version), a stand, a marker for counting turns, a stopwatch, a compass (not needed here), a thermometer, and the two control objects (a hand-shaped vessel of warm water ~37–40 °C, and a transparent airflow baffle). A commercial Egely wheel may be bought, but the card does not require it — the patent gives the buildable spec.
- Safety: **no hazard.** No mains wiring, no chemicals, no heat above hand-warm. Keep the room still and free of drafts; keep mains-powered devices off (they are a confound, not a danger).
- Accessibility: one afternoon to build, ~5 minutes per trial, many trials.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Rotor** — a light, flat, **non-ferromagnetic** rotor: a thin card or balsa disc, or a cut aluminium disc, **0.3–0.8 g** (the patent's range is 0.1–2.0 g), with **at least one marking on the edge** for counting turns (patent WO910870). Magnetic permeability must be low (the patent requires < 50) — do **not** use a steel rotor.
- **Bearing** — the lowest-friction bearing you can make: a **sewing needle point** (the commercial device uses a hardened needle whose tip melts under load and the wheel rides on it), or a **magnetic bearing** for the sensitive version. The rotor must spin freely and come to rest slowly on its own; if it stops within a second, friction is too high to measure anything.
- **Stand + draft shield** — a fixed stand that holds the bearing and rotor at a recorded height, plus a **transparent enclosure** (a glass or clear-plastic box) that blocks room drafts but **is not part of the airflow-blocked arm** — the enclosure is for the baseline only; the airflow block is a separate arm (below).
- **Control object 1 — matched warm body (heat control):** a **hand-shaped or hand-sized vessel filled with water at ~37–40 °C** (body temperature). It must sit at the **same distance and height** as the hand in the hand arm. This is the card's thermal control.
- **Control object 2 — airflow baffle (air control):** a **transparent plate or baffle** placed between the hand and the wheel that blocks the convective plume and air currents but is not warm and does not touch the wheel. (The skeptic protocol's "plate that blocks airflow.")
- **Thermometer** — log the room temperature and the hand/warm-object temperature every trial.
- **Stopwatch + log sheet** — count turns per minute.

## Protocol (four-arm vital-force test, pre-registered)

**Phase 1 — build and pre-register.**
1. Build the rotor and bearing. Verify it spins freely and coasts (record its free-spin decay time). Photograph the apparatus.
2. Write down, before any trial: the arm order (randomized), the distance and height of the hand/object, the trial length, the number of trials per arm, the temperature-matching rule, and the PASS/FAIL thresholds below.

**Phase 2 — run the four arms, randomized order, same spot, same session.**
- **Arm A — hand.** Hold the hand at the recorded distance from the rotor (not touching). Count turns per minute.
- **Arm B — matched warm object.** Replace the hand with the **hand-shaped vessel of ~37–40 °C water** at the **same distance and height**. Count turns per minute. *(Heat control.)*
- **Arm C — hand, airflow blocked.** Hand at the same distance, with the **transparent baffle** between hand and wheel blocking the convective plume. Count turns per minute. *(Air control.)*
- **Arm D — baseline.** No hand, no object; enclosure in place. Count turns per minute (should be ~0).
- **Randomize the order** of A–D on each session; **repeat across ≥3 separate days** and, ideally, **≥2 operators** (the claim is about the *person*, so it must be tested across people).

**Phase 3 — score.**
- Compare turns-per-minute across the four arms.
- **A real "vitality" effect must beat the matched warm object AND survive the airflow block:** A must exceed B **and** A must exceed C, repeatably.
- **Then blind it once:** have someone else decide, without telling the operator, whether the warm object or the hand is in place, and see whether the operator (or the reading) can tell them apart.

## Pass / Fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS:** the hand (A) produces a repeatable spin that is (i) absent in the baseline (D), (ii) larger than the matched warm object (B), and (iii) larger than the airflow-blocked hand (C) — reproduced in **≥2 of 3 sessions** and across **≥2 operators**. The claim survives both its heat control and its air control.
- **FAIL:** no repeatable difference between arms; **or** the hand's spin is matched or exceeded by the warm object (B) and/or removed by the airflow block (C). A null is a complete result (Skeptic's Star): the archive's own skeptic protocol is confirmed at home, and the "vitality" reading is a heat/airflow reading.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** bearing friction too high to coast; distance or height not matched between arms; arm order not randomized; fewer than 3 sessions or fewer than 2 operators; room not draft-free.
- **ARTIFACT (report whether or not it voids the run):** the spin tracks the **temperature difference** between hand/object and room rather than the object's identity; the spin follows **air currents** (hand movement, breathing, door drafts); the spin follows the **operator's expectation** (unblinded); the reading tracks the meter's warm-up or the room's thermal drift.
