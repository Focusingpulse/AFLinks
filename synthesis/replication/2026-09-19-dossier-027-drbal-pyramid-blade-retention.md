## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The patent claims a method of **keeping a razor blade's edge sharp without any auxiliary energy source** — "no mechanical, thermal, or chemical means are used, nor electrical means from an artificial electrical source." The blade is placed in the Earth's magnetic field **under a hollow cone of dielectric material** (stiff paper, paraffined paper, hard cardboard, synthetic material).

The patent's stated construction, quoted precisely:

- **Cone geometry:** a four-sided cone with a square base, "wherein the side of the square advantageously equals the height of the cone multiplied by half of Ludolph's number (π/2). Thus, for example, for a height of 10 cm a base of 15.7 cm is chosen."
- **Support:** the blade rests on a support of dielectric material (cork, wood, stoneware, paraffined paper) "whose height is chosen between 1/5 and 1/3 of the height of the cone," and that support "likewise rests on a surface of dielectric material."
- **Orientation:** the cone's side edges point NW, SW, SE, NE. The blade is placed "so that its cutting edge points west or east respectively, and thus its lateral sides, as well as its longitudinal axis, point north and south."
- **Consistency rule:** after each shave "the same cutting edge always points toward the same compass direction."
- **Pre-treatment:** "It is advantageous to leave a new blade in the cone for one to two weeks before beginning its use."

The patent's stated mechanism — this is the part that makes it a **metallurgical** claim rather than a geometric one:

> "…a reduction of tension in the lattices of crystal bundles and a decrease in the number of defects in them — in other words, a recovery that renews the mechanical and physical properties of the edge."

The patent's own example, quoted as the source states it:

> "By using the device according to the invention, 1778 shaves were achieved with 16 blades of the Czechoslovak brand 'Dukát Zlato' (Dukát Gold), which makes an average of 111 shaves per blade. The smallest number was 51 and the largest 200. With medium beard strength, an average of 50 shaves can be considered easily attainable."

For comparison the patent uses **5 shaves without storage** — i.e. it claims roughly a **10× service life**.

**Provenance note, stated up front.** The 1778-shaves figure is the *inventor's own* example. It names no trial design, no blinding, no independent tester, and no instrument. It is an assertion inside a patent specification, not a report. The patent is a **primary document** — which is a claim about its provenance, not about its truth.

**A second, weaker anecdote — flagged as anecdotal.** Czech secondary sources (the scout report's find #3, from `silesia.wz.cz` / `psychotronika.cz`, both fragile free-host pages) report that the patent office's chief opponent — a leading metallurgist — obtained **370 shaves from a single blade** during the decade-long patent dispute. This appears **only** in Czech popular sources, carries no protocol, and is recorded here as an anecdote, not as data.

**An internal inconsistency in the patent, stated plainly.** The specification body says the cutting edge points **west or east** (so the longitudinal axis runs north–south); claim 1 says "the position of the blade or razor is chosen so that the cutting edge points from north to south." Both cannot be right. A replicator must **pick one and hold it constant**, and say which. This dossier uses **edge east–west, longitudinal axis north–south** (the body text, which is the more specific statement), because the body is where the reasoning lives.

**Honest status of the claim.** Pyramid power is classified as pseudoscience, and this patent is its founding document. It has **never been independently replicated** at home or in a lab, and the mechanism it proposes (resonant-cavity amplification of ambient microwaves; elastic rather than plastic deformation of the edge) has no supporting measurement. That is exactly why it is worth a test — and the test must be built so that a null result is as publishable as a positive one.

**Why it is worth testing anyway, stated plainly.** Three reasons:

1. **It is a granted patent with complete, exact construction numbers.** Most of the archive's claims arrive as prose. This one arrives with a geometry, a support height, an orientation rule, and a use protocol. That makes it *replicable by construction* — the apparatus is not in question, only the effect.
2. **It is the cheapest hard-ended probe the archive holds of a much larger thesis** — that the geometry of a built enclosure changes the state of what is inside it. The archive's other probes of that thesis (cards 011 and 021) both used perceptual endpoints (dowsing rods, magnetometer-vs-noise). This one has a *countable* endpoint: strokes to sever, shaves to failure.
3. **The claimed effect is enormous.** A 10× service-life change is not a subtle effect that a kitchen cannot resolve — it is a difference a family can see in two weeks. If the effect is real at anything like the claimed size, this test finds it. If it is not, the test retires a 77-year-old claim honestly.

**Under-promise, stated plainly.** A household with two razor blades and a cardboard pyramid cannot resolve a 10% effect. It **can** resolve a 10× one. Arm 1 (fresh-blade storage) is designed to give a first reading in **two weeks**; Arm 2 (shave count) needs weeks to months and will be noisy. Report both arms separately.

This is the **founding document of Czech psychotronika** — the only psychotronics-related patent ever granted in Czechoslovakia, and the primary text that every secondary account of "pyramid power" cites and almost never links. The Vault now holds it in full English translation plus the archived PDF, and **nothing in the archive has ever been replicated at home**.

Every other metal card in the queue (023, hammer-charged blade) tests a cutting *effect*; this one tests the blade's **material**, with the patent's own words naming crystal-lattice tension and defect count as the thing that changes.

And it is real literacy at almost zero cost: a family that runs this learns what a **single-variable control** is (the cone is the only difference), what a **sham** is (a plain box of the same footprint), why the person measuring must not know which blade is which, and how a granted patent can still be wrong — patents are granted on novelty, not on efficacy.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build cost: **~$0–10** (stiff cardboard, tape, ruler, compass, one pack of identical blades; a USB microscope at ~$20 is optional, not required).
- Safety: **the blades are sharp and that is the only hazard.** Adults cut and load the blades; keep a blade bank or taped disposal container; keep blades away from small children; never leave a loose blade on a bench. No mains power, no chemicals, no heat, no magnets.
- Accessibility: the pyramid is a 20-minute build a child can do with supervision. Arm 1 pays off in 2 weeks — fast enough that the quest gets run.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Cardboard**, stiff (corrugated box stock), plus scissors, tape, a ruler.
- **A compass** (phone compass is fine) for orientation.
- **Two identical blades from the same pack** — same brand, same model, ideally the same batch. (Arm 2 needs two; a third blade for the sham arm is optional and triples the runtime.)
- **A dielectric support** for each blade — cork, a small block of wood, or a stack of paraffined paper, cut to **1/5–1/3 of the cone height**. The control blade gets an *identical* support.
- **A dielectric base surface** — a sheet of cardboard or a wooden board, on which both stations sit.
- **Optional sham:** a plain cardboard box with the same footprint as the pyramid base and a flat (non-pyramidal) top, to separate "any enclosure" from "this geometry."
- **A simple cutting jig** (cardboard, 5 minutes): a fixed slot that holds the blade at a constant angle and depth so strokes are comparable between blades and between sessions.
- **Standard medium** for the cut test: one sheet of the *same* newspaper, cut into strips of identical width, from the same page, prepared in advance. (Paper varies; the medium must not vary.)

## Protocol

### Setup

1. **Build the pyramid to the patent's numbers.** Choose a height — 10 cm is the patent's own example, giving a base side of **15.7 cm** (height × π/2). Cut four triangles with that base and that height, tape them into a square-based cone, and cut an opening at the bottom large enough to slide a blade in.
2. **Build the control station.** An identical dielectric support, on the same dielectric base surface, in the same room, the **same distance from any wall or metal object**, with the blade lying in the **same compass orientation** as the pyramid blade. The cone is the *only* difference. This is the whole design.
3. **Orient.** Cone side edges to NW/SW/SE/NE. Blade **edge east–west, longitudinal axis north–south** (see the inconsistency note above — pick one and record it).
4. **Code the blades.** An adult labels the two blades A and B, seals the key, and hands the loaded razor to the shaver/tester without saying which is which wherever possible.

### Arm 1 — fresh-blade storage (2 weeks, first reading)

The patent's own pre-treatment claim: a new blade left in the cone for one to two weeks before first use is improved.

1. Take two **new, unused** blades from the same pack. Code them A and B; seal the key.
2. Blade A goes into the pyramid, on its support, per the geometry. Blade B goes onto the identical open support, same orientation, same room.
3. **Wait 14 days.** Do not use either blade. Keep both stations untouched (photograph them at setup so you can prove they were not disturbed).
4. On day 14, run the **standardized cut test** on both blades, blinded:
   - Using the jig, draw the blade across a prepared newspaper strip in one stroke.
   - Count **strokes to sever the strip**. Do **5 strips per blade**, alternating blades strip-by-strip so the medium and the tester's fatigue are matched.
   - Record the median strokes-to-sever for A and for B.
5. Unblind only after the numbers are written down.

### Arm 2 — shave-count arm (weeks to months, second reading)

1. Use **both** blades in actual shaving, **alternating**: one shave with A, the next with B, same razor handle, same routine, same person. Alternating matches beard and wear by construction — this is what makes the comparison meaningful.
2. After every shave, return A to the pyramid and B to the open support, **same orientation each time** (the patent's own consistency rule).
3. **Every 5 shaves**, run the blinded cut test on both blades (5 strips each, alternating) and record strokes-to-sever.
4. **Define "failed" once, up front**, before the first shave — e.g. *"the blade no longer severs the standard strip in ≤ N strokes"* or *"the shaver reports tugging rather than cutting."* Write it down before you start. Do not move the goalposts later.
5. Record **shaves-to-failure** for A and for B, plus the full strokes-to-sever series.

### Optional third arm — the sham enclosure (if you have a third blade)

Blade C lives in a **plain cardboard box of the same footprint** (enclosure, no pyramid geometry). This separates two different claims: *"being covered does something"* (A/C vs B) from *"this specific geometry does something"* (A vs C). Without C, a positive result cannot tell you which claim you have supported.

## Pass / fail criteria (pre-registered)

- **PASS (Arm 1, storage):** blade A (pyramid) severs the standard strip in **≥30% fewer strokes** than blade B (open), median of 5 strips, **and** the direction repeats in a second 14-day run with a fresh pair. → The patent's pre-treatment claim shows up at home scale.
- **PASS (Arm 2, shaves):** blade A reaches **≥2× the shaves-to-failure** of blade B, with the strokes-to-sever series never crossing over. → The patent's headline claim shows up at home scale. (Note: the patent claims ~10×; a 2× threshold is deliberately generous so that a *real but smaller* effect is not thrown away with the null.)
- **FAIL:** A and B within **±10%** on strokes-to-sever in Arm 1 and within ±20% on shaves-to-failure in Arm 2. → The claim does not reproduce with household apparatus. **Skeptic's Star** material: this would be the first honest home burial of the founding document of Czech psychotronika.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** run-to-run spread exceeds 30% in either arm, the blades were not from the same pack, the stations were not orientation-matched, the tester knew the assignment, or the failure definition was changed mid-run.

Report the arms **separately**. A storage result and a shave-count result are independent findings, and either can stand alone.

## What would change the verdict

- A **second independent household** running the same protocol with the same result moves this dossier from `protocol` to `replicated` or `refuted`. One kitchen is an anecdote; two is a datum.
- A **microscopy stage** (USB microscope, ~$20, fixed magnification and lighting) imaging the blade edge before and after storage would test the patent's *actual mechanism* — lattice recovery — rather than only its service-life consequence. That is the natural second-stage dossier, and it is the first time anyone would have looked at the edge the patent is about.
- A **positive** result would be the first home-scale support for the founding document of an entire national research school. A **negative** would be the first honest burial of it, and would be worth publishing just as loudly.

## Honest caveats (do not skip these)

- **A granted patent is not evidence.** Patents are granted on novelty and utility as *claimed*, not on demonstrated efficacy. The 1949 filing and 1959 grant establish that the claim was made and the office accepted it — nothing more.
- **Suppression is not evidence either.** Nothing here was suppressed; the patent was published, and the claim simply was never tested. Do not import a suppression narrative into this card.
- **The patent contradicts itself on blade orientation** (body: edge E–W; claim 1: edge N–S). Stated above; pick one, hold it constant, record which.
- **The 370-shaves anecdote has no protocol** and appears only in Czech popular sources. It is not data.
- **The lineage is not the claim.** Antoine Bovis's Paris pyramid mummification experiments and the later commercial "pyramid power" fad are context for how the idea travelled, not evidence for this claim. The card tests the blade claim on its own.
- **Home sharpness measurement is crude.** The jig and the fixed medium exist to make it *comparable*, not to make it precise. Say so in your report.
