## The claim (as asserted by the sources)

The radiesthesia tradition holds that a sensitive person can locate underground water — its **line**, its **depth**, and its **yield**. The first two are already carded (dossier 035 tests the line and the flow direction). This card tests the third and hardest: **the quantity.**

Three distinct pieces of the tradition are in the archive:

1. **The named method (Saevarius, mid-20th c.).** The manual's own table of contents carries two consecutive chapters on it: Ch. IX *"Busca de uma corrente d'água — Método de Fr. Padey"* (finding a water current — the Fr. Padey method) and Ch. X *"Cálculo de produção líquida de uma corrente d'água subterrânea (Método de Fr. Padey)"* (calculation of the **net production** of an underground water current — the Fr. Padey method). The tradition therefore claims not merely to detect water but to **put a number on how much it gives** — and it names the number *net production*, i.e. a usable yield.

2. **The procedure (UCR, 2020).** The Costa Rica field record documents the working method. For **depth**, the operator asks the pendulum a sequence of bracketed questions — *"the groundwater is between 0 and 10 m"*, *"between 11 and 20 m"*, and so on — then refines by counting aloud (*"twenty-one metres, twenty-two metres…"*) until the pendulum turns. For **yield**, the document's own section heading is *"Determinación del caudal que puede aportar el pozo"* — determining the flow rate the well can supply — and it states that it uses the same counting procedure. The method is a **convention**: the pendulum's answer is read against a pre-agreed code, and the operator's mental convention is what fixes the meaning of the movement.

3. **The instrumented context (Betz / Munich GTZ, 1995).** The same document records that Hans Dieter Betz, of the University of Munich physics department and head of the German GTZ arid-zone water program, **paired dowsers with vertical electrical soundings** to site water supplies in Africa and Asia. This is the closest thing in the archive to an external check on the practice — and it is a *pairing* (dowser proposes, geophysics confirms), not a blinded trial of the dowser's numbers.

**Honest status of the claim — stated up front:**

- **Dowsing is classified as pseudoscience.** The claimed mechanism — a "subtle matter" or "radiation" whose traces a sensitive person can feel — has **no accepted physical basis and no instrument that measures it directly**. The UCR document itself presents both schools (physical and mental) and notes that in the mental school the whole action is *inside the operator*, the instrument merely amplifying an imperceptible neuromuscular movement. **That second school is the one that makes this card's blinding essential**: if the answer lives in the operator's unconscious, then a blind test measures the operator's unconscious, not the water.
- **No comparative verification exists — by the source's own admission.** The UCR author writes that no technical comparative studies have been published to verify the results. That is the gap this card fills at family scale.
- **The Saevarius method is cited by its chapter title, not its text.** This run verified the table of contents live but did not reach the body of Ch. X. The card therefore tests the *claim the tradition names* (a yield estimate), not a specific arithmetic procedure from a page we did not read. Stated, not hidden.
- **A null is the likely outcome on a family bench** — and a null is a genuinely useful result: it converts an unexamined belief into a measured null with the archive's own sources cited. The engine's honesty rule applies: this is a test of a claim, not an endorsement of it.
- **A hose is not a vein.** This is the card's central limitation and it must be stated in the report: a tap or a hose has no aquifer, no rock strata, no recharge, and no seasonal drawdown. A **pass** shows only that the operator can estimate the flow rate of a *hidden pipe* better than chance and better than a non-dowser. It does **not** show that anyone can size a real well.
- **Safety and honesty:** this is a test of a phenomenon, **not** advice to rely on dowsing to size a real well or to decide a community's water investment. Never substitute a dowsing protocol for a hydrogeological survey or a licensed driller.

**Claimants:** the Fr. Padey method as carried in the Saevarius manual (French physical-radiesthesia lineage, mid-20th c.); the radiesthesia tradition documented by Arias Salguero (Costa Rica, 2020); the Betz/Munich GTZ program (1995) as the practice's instrumented context
**Verification status:** unverified; no independent blinded replication known to the archive; the source itself states no comparative technical studies have been published
**Mainstream position:** no accepted mechanism; the reported effect is attributed to ideomotor movement, sensory leakage, and chance unless controlled

## The protocol — one hidden flow, one blind operator, one number

The testable core: **if a sensitive person can estimate the yield of a water source, then an operator kept blind to the flow should produce estimates that track the true flow rate better than a non-dowser's guesses.** If the estimates carry no information about the true flow, the quantitative claim is retired honestly.

The design has one job the detection card (035) did not have: **the endpoint is a number, so the scoring must be about the number, not about a hit.** That is why the primary statistic is a *rank correlation* between estimated and true flow, not a hit rate.

### Materials (~$10–40)

1. **A water source with a settable flow** — a garden tap or a hose with a valve (~$0–20). Any household tap works; a hose with a clamp valve gives finer control.
2. **A measuring container and a stopwatch** (a 1–2 L jug and a phone stopwatch; ~$0–10). The referee measures true flow as *litres per minute* by timing a fill.
3. **A way to hide the flow from the operator** — the outlet discharges into a lidded bucket or around a corner, out of sight and out of earshot, or the operator stands with their back turned and headphones on. (~$0–10)
4. **A pendulum** — any small weight on a thread; the tradition's own instrument (~$0–15).
5. **A pre-registration sheet and a pen.**
6. **Two or three people**: the **operator** (the person being tested), a **referee** (sets the flow, measures the truth, keeps the key — a child is ideal), and a **control operator** (someone who does not claim the skill, run through the same trials).

### Design — the parts that make it a measurement

1. **Positive control first (the calibration arm).** Before any blind trial, the operator estimates the flow of a **visible, already-measured** source — a tap they can watch running, whose true flow the referee has just measured and told them. This is the tradition's own first exercise: *calibrate your sensitivity on a known water course.* If the operator cannot produce a signal here, **the blind arm is uninformative rather than a refutation** — record it as INCONCLUSIVE and stop.
2. **The flow is randomised and hidden.** For each blind trial the referee sets the tap to one of a pre-registered set of rates (e.g. **1, 2, 4, 8 L/min**), chosen by a shuffled list, and discharges it out of the operator's sight and hearing. The operator, blind, **writes down an estimate in litres per minute** before the referee reveals anything.
3. **Ground truth is measured, never assumed.** After the operator writes their estimate, the referee measures the true flow with the container and stopwatch and records it. The order matters: **estimate first, truth second, every trial.**
4. **A non-dowser control arm.** The control operator runs the same trials on the same flows. This is the chance-baseline check — it catches a rig that leaks the answer (a hum, a vibration, a wet sound the referee did not think of).
5. **A sham block.** Some trials are run with the **water off** (the tap closed, the operator not told). A real estimator should say "no flow" or give near-zero; a guesser produces confident numbers anyway. This is the false-positive control.
6. **Two sessions on different days.** The result must repeat.
7. **Pre-register everything.** The flow set, the trial count, the thresholds, the scoring rule, and the key-holder are written down **before** the first trial. The key is sealed and photographed.
8. **Unblind only after the last number is written.**

### Optional deeper arm — the real-source arm

If the household has its own source (a spring, a well with a pump, a cistern outlet, a rain tank), run the real version: the operator estimates the source's yield **blind**, then the household measures the actual yield the same day (bucket and stopwatch at the outlet, or a flow meter). This is the *actual shared-infra decision* — *is our source big enough?* — and it is the arm the community cares about. It is **optional** because not every household has a variable source; the card's pass/fail does not depend on it. Where it is run, it is reported separately and never pooled with the hose trials.

### Endpoints

- **Primary — does the estimate carry information about the flow?** The **rank correlation (Spearman ρ)** between the operator's estimates and the true flows across the blind trials. A number-guesser yields ρ ≈ 0; an estimator yields ρ well above 0.
- **Secondary — how close?** The **proportion of estimates within ±25% of the true flow**, and the **median absolute percentage error**, compared with the **control operator's**.
- **Control — sham block:** the operator's estimates on the water-off trials (should be near zero or declared "no flow").
- **Optional — real-source arm:** the operator's estimate versus the measured yield of the household's own source, reported separately.

### Pre-registered pass/fail

- **PASS:** blind rank correlation **ρ ≥ 0.6** **AND** **≥60% of estimates within ±25%** of the true flow **AND** clearly better than the control operator on both — **and the result repeats in a second session**.
- **FAIL:** rank correlation **ρ ≤ 0.2** (the estimates carry essentially no information about the true flow) **OR** the operator is not better than the control — an honest negative, Skeptic's Star.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** the operator cannot produce a signal on the **visible, known** flow (the calibration arm) — the blind arm is then *uninformative rather than a refutation*; the flow was visible or audible; the key was opened before the final tally; N was not pre-registered; or the sham block shows the operator answering confidently on water-off trials.

## Why it matters

This is the **second community/shared-infrastructure card** in the queue, and it answers the question the first one (035) explicitly left open. 035 asks *where is the water and which way does it run*; this asks *how much does it give* — the number a community actually needs before it decides whether a shared well is worth the digging. The two cards share an instrument and a tradition; they test different claims with different endpoints, and that relationship is stated here, not hidden.

It is also the **first card in the queue whose endpoint is a continuous number scored by correlation** rather than a hit rate against a chance baseline. That is a genuinely different measurement design: a hit rate asks *did you get it right?*; a correlation asks *did your answers carry any information at all?* A guesser can pass a hit-rate test by luck on a small N; a guesser cannot produce a rank correlation.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build cost: **~$10–40** (a tap or valved hose, a jug, a stopwatch, a pendulum). No purchased instrument.
- Safety: **none beyond ordinary care** — water on the ground, no mains electrical work, no chemicals. Do not leave a tap running unattended; mind slip hazards on wet ground.
- Accessibility: two short sessions. The protocol is a tap, a jug, a stopwatch, and a written estimate.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- A tap or valved hose with a settable flow.
- A 1–2 L measuring container and a stopwatch (or a phone).
- A way to hide the flow (a lidded bucket, a corner, headphones).
- A pendulum (any small weight on a thread).
- A pen, a pre-registration sheet, a sealed envelope for the key.
- Optional: a flow meter for the real-source arm.

## What a result means (and what it does not)

- A **pass** means: this operator, on this tap, on these days, produced estimates that tracked the true flow and beat the control operator. It is a real, local, measured result. It does **not** mean the operator can size an aquifer, and a hidden pipe has no recharge, no strata, and no seasonal drawdown.
- A **fail** means: at home scale, blind, the estimates carried no information about the flow. That is a complete and publishable result.
- Either way, the confounds must be named in the report: **ideomotor movement** (blinding removes the operator's *knowledge*, not their hand) and **the hose is not a vein** (no aquifer, no yield curve, no community-scale meaning).
