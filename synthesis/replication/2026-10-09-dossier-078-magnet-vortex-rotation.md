## The claim (as asserted by the source)

Seiler's account, in his own framing (translation, §1 and §2):

> "at least the main component of a magnet's aetheric motion must be of a rotational character … a magnetic field line corresponds primarily not to an aether current but to an aether vortex thread. The aether therefore does not flow essentially parallel to the magnetic field lines, but swirls around them."

and the two experiments he reports:

> "In 1993 … I succeeded for the first time in making a small magnetic float rotate slowly along the inner glass wall of a laboratory glass cylinder filled with water and wrapped with a coil, after a constant current was switched on. The rotation of the floating magnet, dependent on the direction of rotation of the current or the magnetic polarisation, clockwise (CW) or in the opposite direction (CCW), could — under favourable conditions — run through several times 360 degrees without difficulty."

> "The direction of rotation of the force effect (CCW with the magnetic field directed downward, CW with it directed upward) was always unambiguously correlated with the orientation of the magnetic field. Particularly noteworthy is the fact that the measuring arrangement also showed a constant slight CCW tendency even with the current switched off, and this over several days. It is probable that this stems from the permanent influence of the Earth's magnetic field rotating toward CCW."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a magnetic field exert a **twisting (rotational) displacement** on a body suspended so it can only rotate — and does that displacement persist, in the same direction, after the current is switched off?

## Honest status of the claim

**The source is self-published, non-peer-reviewed, and the author himself asks for it to be re-checked.** His closing line: *"Of course a critical experimental and also theoretical re-examination of these promising results is now called for."* He also names the artefact risks himself: the 1993 magnet-in-water experiment *"proved not to be arbitrarily reproducible"* (friction between water, float and glass wall; the field not always homogeneous); the 2006 rotations *"reach at most only just about 90 degrees … [and] can of course also be caused by artefacts, primarily by axial deflections of the test magnet with alignment in the not altogether homogeneous magnetic field"*; and the coil experiment *"initially … showed very strong disturbance fluctuations, which were very probably caused by thermal influence with the circuit switched on."*

**The ordinary mechanisms that must be excluded before the claim is interesting:**

| Ordinary mechanism | What it predicts | The control that separates it |
|---|---|---|
| **Magnetic torque on a magnet in a non-uniform field** | the hung bar magnet turns toward the fixed magnets — a real, ordinary torque | replace the three fixed magnets with three **matched non-magnetic dummies** (same mass, same geometry, same positions); the deflection must vanish |
| **Einstein–de Haas effect** (magnetising a ferromagnetic body rotates it; real, measured) | a ferromagnetic body rotates when its magnetisation changes | Seiler's own footnote argues this should not apply here (the total magnetisation in the weak interaction field is not changed, and the effect is a brief angular impulse, not a lasting displacement) — the family checks the **magnitude and the persistence**, not just the sign |
| **Thermal drift / convection** | the pendulum creeps as the room warms; a coil dissipates heat | the **ambient-temperature log** and the **sham coil** (an identical coil carrying no current); the deflection must not track the temperature |
| **Air currents** | the laser spot wanders with draughts | the **enclosure** (Seiler's own transparent-film tube over the mirror); the deflection must survive with the enclosure closed |
| **Residual ferromagnetism / susceptibility of the "non-magnetic" body** | sand, glass and water are not perfectly non-magnetic | the **matched dummy** and a second body of a different material; a real effect should not depend on the body's iron content |

**So the honest position is:** the effect is claimed, the apparatus is buildable, the author calls for replication, and the artefact floor — thermal drift, air currents, ordinary magnetic torque — is high enough that **the card's first job is to measure the floor, not the effect.** A family that measures the floor and finds the effect inside it has produced a real result.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$50-150** — a nylon thread (~$5), a glass jar and sand (~$5), copper wire for the coil (~$15), a laser pointer (~$10-20), a small mirror (~$5), a compass (~$5), insulating mat (~$10), and (for the magnet arm) three bar magnets plus one hung bar magnet (~$20-60). No mains work, no lab.
- Safety: **low.** A low-voltage DC coil (a battery or a bench supply at a few volts) is the only electrical element. Do not run the coil from mains. Keep the laser pointer below eye level and never point it at anyone.
- Accessibility: any home with a doorway or a stairwell tall enough for a 1-3 m suspension.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A torsion pendulum** — a light non-magnetic body (a sand-filled glass medicine bottle, ~400 g) hung from a fixed point by a **thin nylon thread** (1-3 m; longer = more sensitive). It must be free to rotate about the vertical axis and must hang clear of everything.
- **A coil** — copper wire wound around a glass jar (~20 cm diameter × 25 cm height) that the test body hangs inside; driven from a low-voltage DC supply or battery, with a switch and a way to reverse polarity.
- **A readout** — a small mirror glued to the suspension thread, a laser pointer aimed at it, and a screen or wall at a **measured distance**; the laser spot's displacement is the reading (mm of spot movement → degrees of azimuthal rotation).
- **A compass** — to confirm the coil's polarity (which way the N pole points).
- **Thermal shielding** — ~3 cm of insulating mat around the test body.
- **An enclosure** — a transparent film tube over the mirror (the source's own air-current shield).
- **A thermometer** — the ambient-temperature log is mandatory.
- **(Magnet arm only)** one bar magnet hung freely on a nylon thread with a stabilising weight below, and three fixed bar magnets on a board with a central opening, movable into a symmetric lateral position.
- **(Controls)** an identical **sham coil** (no current) and three **matched non-magnetic dummies** (same mass and geometry as the fixed magnets).

## Protocol (pre-registered)

1. **Pre-register, before measuring.** Write down: the body and its mass, the thread length, the screen distance, the settle time, the scoring rule (spot displacement in mm at that distance, signed by direction), N trials, the thermal log, and the enclosure state. Photograph the sheet.
2. **Measure the floor first.** With the coil **off**, let the pendulum settle and record the rest position and its **drift over 10 minutes** with no current. This is the artefact floor; any deflection below it is not a result.
3. **Coil on, N pole down.** Switch on, wait the settle time, record the spot displacement and its direction.
4. **Coil on, reversed.** Reverse the polarity (N pole up), wait, record.
5. **Coil off.** Switch off, wait, record — then record at fixed intervals for **several days** (the claimed persistent tendency).
6. **Repeat N ≥ 10 times**, alternating polarity, **with the enclosure closed**, logging the ambient temperature at every reading.
7. **Controls.** (a) the **sham coil** — an identical coil carrying no current; (b) the **enclosure open vs closed**; (c) the **matched non-magnetic dummy** in place of the coil; (d) a second body of a different material.
8. **Magnet arm (optional but it is the source's own headline).** Hang the bar magnet freely; settle; bring the three fixed magnets into the symmetric lateral position; record the deflection and direction; reverse the fixed magnets' polarity and repeat; then replace the fixed magnets with the **matched non-magnetic dummies** and repeat. Remove the fixed magnets and confirm the system swings back to its initial position.
9. **Compute.** Deflection in degrees for each condition, the sign, the ratio to the baseline drift, and the correlation with the ambient temperature.
10. **Post.** Photo of the pre-registration sheet, the raw readings, the apparatus, the controls, and a one-paragraph verdict.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim supported at home scale):** the deflection **reverses sign with the coil polarity**, AND the **sham coil** produces no comparable deflection, AND the deflection is reproducible across N ≥ 10 trials with the enclosure closed and **does not track the ambient temperature**, AND (for the persistence claim) the current-off tendency is present over **≥3 days** and differs from the no-coil-at-all baseline.
- **REFUTED:** the **sham coil reproduces the deflection** (the effect is not the field); OR the deflection **tracks the ambient temperature** (thermal); OR the deflection **disappears when the enclosure is closed** (air currents); OR the deflection **does not reverse with polarity**; OR the **matched non-magnetic dummy** reproduces the magnet-arm deflection.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** the deflection is present but **below the measured baseline drift**, or the pendulum will not settle, or the readings scatter more than they separate.
- **VOID:** the thread is twisted or kinked at the start; the reading is taken before the pendulum settles; the laser spot is not on a fixed screen; the coil was driven from mains. A VOID is not a NULL.

## Independent-variable discipline

The one variable is **the field** (its presence, its polarity, and its removal). Everything else — the body, the thread, the screen distance, the settle time, the enclosure, the time of day — must be held fixed, or the comparison measures the conditions, not the field. The **baseline drift** is the control that catches a pendulum that is simply creeping; the **sham coil** catches a coil that is simply a heat source; the **matched dummy** catches a body that is simply magnetic.

## What a result would mean

- **Deflection reverses, sham flat, dummy flat, temperature uncorrelated** → the effect is real at home scale and is not the ordinary mechanisms. The honest follow-up is a blinded run (the operator does not know which polarity is on) and a second household.
- **Deflection tracks temperature, or the sham coil reproduces it** → the effect is thermal. A complete, useful result: the family has measured the artefact floor and found the claim inside it.
- **Deflection disappears when the enclosure is closed** → air currents. Also complete.
- **No deflection above the floor at all** → the claim does not reproduce at home scale with this apparatus. A complete result (Skeptic's Star), and the honest one the card expects.
