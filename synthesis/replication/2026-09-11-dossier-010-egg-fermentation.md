# Replication Dossier 010: Egg-Shaped Fermentation Vessel Test

## Claim
Egg-shaped (ovoid) containers produce superior fermentation outcomes compared to cylindrical containers, due to natural vortex formation and temperature stability at the container's center.

## Source
- **Primary**: `translations/2026-09-09-compendium-of-vortex-physics-de.md`
- **Secondary**: `translations/2026-09-09-schauberger-water-blood-of-the-earth-de.md`
- **Claim excerpt**: "The egg or pear shape is the ideal form for storing water... at certain temperatures (between +4° and +9°C) and with exclusion of light and heat, a higher-grade fermentation process takes place, in which not an oxidation but a reduction occurs."

## Protocol

### Apparatus
1. **Test vessel A**: Egg-shaped ceramic or glass container (1-2L capacity)
   - Can use commercially available ceramic egg fermenters or glass egg vessels
   - Alternative: DIY papier-mâché egg mold lined with food-safe resin
2. **Control vessel B**: Cylindrical container of same volume (1-2L)
3. **Ingredients**: Basic sauerkraut recipe
   - 1kg green cabbage, shredded
   - 20g sea salt (2% by weight)
   - Optional: 1 tsp caraway seeds
4. **Environment**: Dark storage area, ambient temperature 18-22°C
5. **Measurement tools**:
   - Digital pH meter or strips
   - Kitchen thermometer
   - Visual/taste assessment log

### Procedure
1. Prepare identical sauerkraut batches (divide cabbage/salt mixture evenly)
2. Pack vessel A (egg) and vessel B (cylinder) with same compression force
3. Ensure brine covers cabbage in both (add brine if needed: 2% salt solution)
4. Cover with airlock lids or water seal
5. Store in dark location at room temperature
6. **Daily measurements** (Days 1-14):
   - pH reading (target: pH 3.5-3.7 for completed sauerkraut)
   - Visual observations: bubble activity, color, mold presence
   - Aroma notes
7. **Day 7 and Day 14**: Taste test (small sample from each)
   - Record: crunchiness, tanginess, off-flavors
8. **Day 14**: Final assessment
   - Final pH
   - Blind taste test with 3+ family members
   - Photo documentation

### Pass/Fail Criteria

**PASS** (supports claim):
- Egg vessel reaches target pH (≤3.7) faster than cylinder, OR
- Egg vessel shows more consistent fermentation (fewer off-flavors, no mold), OR
- Blind taste panel consistently prefers egg vessel sample

**FAIL** (refutes claim):
- No measurable difference between vessels, OR
- Cylinder vessel performs better on any metric

**INCONCLUSIVE**:
- Both vessels fail to ferment properly (ambient temp issue)
- Contamination in one or both vessels

### Evidence to Collect
- Daily pH log (photograph pH meter readings)
- Photos of vessels at Days 1, 7, 14
- Taste test scores (blind, 1-5 scale for tanginess, crunch, overall preference)
- Temperature log of storage area

### Cost Estimate
- Egg-shaped fermenter: $15-40 (commercial) or $5-10 (DIY)
- Cylindrical jar: $5 (standard mason jar)
- Cabbage + salt: $3
- pH strips: $8
- **Total**: $30-60

### Safety Notes
- Discard if mold grows on surface (white film is normal kahm yeast; fuzzy/colored mold is not)
- Trust smell: properly fermented sauerkraut smells tangy, not putrid
- If pH doesn't drop below 4.6 by Day 7, discard (botulism risk zone)
