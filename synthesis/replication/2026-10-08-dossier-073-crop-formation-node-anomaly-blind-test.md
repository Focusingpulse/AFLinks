## Source lineage

- **The claim.** William Levengood's 1990s biophysical studies of crop-formation plants (Levengood 1994, *Physiologia Plantarum* 92:356–363) reported that growth nodes (pulvini) in flattened crop are abnormally elongated. Haselhoff's sampling of the **Hoeven 1999** formation added the structure: the elongation was **highest at the circle's centre and fell off radially to control levels at the edges**, with a symmetry that matched the radiation profile of a point source at h = 4.1 m (R = 0.99 on one transect). The **BOL ("ball of light") model** made that the signature of an electromagnetic source hovering over the field.
- **The critique.** Grassi, Cocheo & Russo (2005, *JSE* 19(2):159–170), working from the original raw data, concluded the electromagnetic claims were unsupported — small samples, control criteria never pre-stated — and that the data showed nothing beyond a difference between flattened and upright plants. Haselhoff (2007) rebutted, arguing Grassi had read averages of many stems as single measurements and had the control positions wrong.
- **The test that was run.** Haselhoff, Boerman & Bobbink (2014) made the man-made control both sides had asked for: a 9 m circle in matched barley, board and rope, sampled six days after creation with the identical scheme. It reproduced the elongation (**144% peak, 111% average**) but **not the radial symmetry** (regional correlation with the 1999 formation **R = 0.06**). Their conclusion: the 1999 findings "could not be reproduced and hence remain anomalous." Their own stated limit: **the test was not blind.**
- **The test that has not been run.** A **blind** version — measurers not knowing which samples came from where, criteria fixed before collection. Cairn's audit names it as "the test nobody has run"; both sides of the dispute have asked for it.

## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The tradition holds that the node-length distribution inside a formation is **structured** — a smooth radial gradient from centre to edge — and that this structure is **not** what ordinary flattening produces, implying a non-mechanical cause (heat, electromagnetic energy, a "ball of light"). The specific, falsifiable core:

> **Deliberate flattening with board and rope, in matched crop at matched maturity, produces a node-length increase but not a centric radial gradient; a centric radial gradient, if it appears, therefore marks something other than flattening.**

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** when you flatten a circle yourself, blind, does the node-length distribution come out with the centric radial structure the claim rests on — or flat and irregular, as it did in 2014?

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the claim and one incomplete test of it.** The 2014 control is the best evidence in the record and it points one way — the structure did not reproduce — but it carries the flaw its own authors name first: it was not blind. Two further limits are honest to state up front:

1. **The floor claim is already dead as evidence.** Flattened plants differ from upright plants; a man-made circle shows this at 144% peak. So *"the nodes grew"* proves nothing. Only the **structure** (centric symmetry, radial fall-off) carries the claim.
2. **The 2014 test is one control circle, n = 1.** It cannot establish the natural distribution of node length inside flattened circles in general. A single home circle is likewise n = 1 and cannot close the case on its own — but a *blind* one adds the dimension the 2014 test lacked, and the pooled version (several households, several circles) begins to say something.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that a hand-made circle reproduces the 2014 result — elongation without the centric gradient — which **leaves the 1999 anomaly unexplained and unreproduced**. That is a real result (it strengthens the "not explained by flattening" side while failing to explain it), not a refutation and not a confirmation. A hand-made circle that *does* come out with a clean centric radial gradient would be the more surprising result — it would dissolve the anomaly by giving flattening a mundane mechanism — and it is exactly the outcome that would need to repeat in a second circle before being believed.

## Replicability: `sand` (home/garden-scale)

- Build/obtain cost: **~$0–40** — a wooden board and a rope (~$0, or ~$10 to make), a tape measure (~$0–10), digital **calipers or a micrometer** for node lengths (~$15–30; the cost driver), labels and a log sheet (~$0). Optionally a **referee** (a household member or neighbour who is not the sampler).
- Safety: **low.** No mains work, no chemicals, no heat. The one real rule: **do not flatten someone else's growing crop.** Making a formation in a field without the landowner's permission is criminal damage, and it was prosecuted as such in Britain in 2000. Use your own ground, or a patch of tall grass, or get written permission. A legal note, not a how-to.
- Accessibility: a household with a garden, an allotment, or permission to use a corner of a field can run it. **The crop must be a stemmed cereal or tall grass at the right maturity** — this is the one genuine constraint, and it is why the card is `sand` rather than `straw`.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A patch of standing cereal or tall grass** — barley, wheat, oats, or a tall meadow grass, in late stem-extension / early soft-dough, stem height ≥ ~40 cm. **One growing season of lead time.**
- **A flattening board and rope** — a board ~1.0 × 0.2 m with a rope attached to both ends (the documented circlemaker's method).
- **A tape measure** and stakes/string — to lay out the circle and the sampling transects.
- **Digital calipers or a micrometer** — to measure penultimate-node lengths (±0.1 mm).
- **A referee** — the person who labels the samples and holds the key. **The referee is not the sampler or the measurer.**
- **Labels, bags, a log sheet, a camera** — for pre-registration, storage, the counts, and the evidence.
- **A dark, dry room** — for the three-month drying step (this mirrors the 1999 and 2014 protocols and matters: nodes are measured dry).

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — pre-registration (mandatory).**
1. Write down and photograph, **before making the circle**: the crop and its stage, the circle diameter, the number and spacing of sampling points, the number of stems per sample, the upright-control locations, the measurement rule (penultimate node), the drying time, the blinding procedure, and the pass/fail thresholds below.

**Phase 1 — make the circle.**
2. Flatten a circle with the **board and rope**, diameter matching a real formation you are comparing against or simply a fixed 9 m. Record the date and the weather.

**Phase 2 — sample, blind.**
3. **Six days after creation**, sample flattened stems on **at least three transects** through the circle (edge-to-edge), at equally spaced points, ~20 stems per point, exactly as in 1999/2014. Sample the **upright control** (≥ 3 sets of ~25 stems) in the surrounding standing crop, away from the circle.
4. The **referee** labels every sample with a code only they can map to location, and keeps the key sealed. **The person who measures the nodes must not know which samples came from the centre, the edge, or the upright control.**

**Phase 3 — dry and measure, blind.**
5. Dry all samples **three months** in the same dark dry room.
6. Measure the **penultimate node** of every stem with calipers, coded and blind. Normalise all values to the mean of the upright control.

**Phase 4 — score and, optionally, pool.**
7. Plot node length against distance from the circle's centre, per transect. Test for **centric symmetry** — is the peak at the centre, with a monotonic radial fall-off to control levels?
8. **Pooled arm (optional, stronger):** if more than one household runs it, pool the transects. A structure that appears only in one garden is noise; a structure that repeats across circles is a fact.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS-A (the 2014 result — the expected, complete outcome):** the flattened circle shows a **significant node elongation** vs the upright control (peak ≥ ~130%) **but no centric radial gradient** — the peak is off-centre or the distribution is flat/irregular, and the normalised means track no smooth radial function. This reproduces the 2014 control under blind conditions and *strengthens* the "the structure is not produced by flattening" side. Recorded as a genuine result (a Skeptic's Star is warranted if the family expected otherwise), **not** as a refutation of the anomaly.
- **PASS-B (the claim's mundane mechanism survives — surprising):** the hand-made circle **does** show a clean centric radial gradient (peak at the centre, monotonic fall-off, ≥ ~180% of control at the centre) **and it repeats in a second circle**. This would give flattening a mundane mechanism and dissolve the anomaly. It is the outcome that most needs a second run before belief.
- **FAIL:** the blinding was broken (the measurer knew the sample locations); the control patch was not matched (different crop, maturity, or soil); the transects were not laid out as pre-registered; or the samples were not dried identically.
- **VOID:** **no elongation at all** in the flattened area (wrong crop stage, or the crop was already senescent) — the test failed to reproduce even the known flattening effect, and a no-structure result means nothing. A VOID is not a NULL: an unverified measurement is not a negative result.

## What this card does NOT test

The 1999 case also involved **cell-wall pit structures, malformed embryos, and charring of the outer epidermal layer**. Those require microscopy and are **out of scope** here; leave them out rather than approximate them. This card tests the **node-length structure only** — which is the anomaly the 2014 control was built around, and the one a household can measure.
