## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source asserts that a **pendulum rotation count, taken by a stated procedure, measures a person's radiesthetic sensitivity** — and that the count therefore tells you whether that person is fit to do sample work.

The protocol, quoted in full:

> "In the experiment room, the subject must first be turned to face due west. Standing upright, they should be as free [of tension] as possible. The left hand should be placed on the upper part of the body above the stomach (the solar plexus). The fingers of the hand should touch one another. Once these conditions are met, the pendulum should be dangled in our right hand. The whole of the string or thread should be let out. The pendulum should be about 23 cm away from the left hand."

> "First a slight movement is felt in the pendulum. This movement quickly develops into a rotation. The movement should be in the clockwise direction. There are a few rare cases in which the rotations are in the counter-clockwise direction. As soon as the first full rotation is obtained, one should begin counting and continue counting until the last full rotation. The incomplete rotations at the beginning and the end of the experiment are not counted."

The scale, quoted in full:

> "Below 15: insufficient sensitivity for further experiments with samples until the rotation count increases.
> Rotation count of 25 - 35: very weak, but strong enough to continue the experiments,
> Rotation count of 35 – 60: medium sensitivity,
> 60 rotations and above: good
> There also appear over-sensitive individuals whose rotation count exceeds the numbers 500, 1000 and 2000 for the first time. Those with low sensitivity should practise every day until they rise above a rotation count of 25."

The source's own framing of what the school does, quoted:

> "Alisthesia is an ancient knowledge in which, by measuring, evaluating and interpreting with the forked rod and pendulum known as radiesthesia, and by matching underground resources with earth-science and engineering information, results of up to 95% clear success are obtained."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a person's rotation count, taken exactly as the source states, predict how well that person actually performs on a blind target-finding task — scored against exact chance — better than the count being independent of performance?

## Honest status of the claim

**This is a stated screening rule with no experiment behind it.** The source is a training page for a Turkish radiesthesia school. It gives a precise procedure and a precise numeric scale, and it reports **no test of any kind** — no group, no trial count, no result, no comparison against chance, no comparison of high scorers against low scorers. The scale is asserted, not measured.

Three things the card keeps in front of the family:

1. **The source reports no experiment.** The page is a protocol and a scale. There is no evidence in it that a rotation count predicts anything. The "up to 95% clear success" line at the top of the page is a promotional claim about the school, not a measured result, and it is attached to no study.

2. **The page is a training page that sells training.** It is the front matter of a school's blog; the scale's practical function is to tell a student to keep practising and to tell the school who is ready for paid sample work. That does not make the scale false — it makes it a claim with an incentive behind it.

3. **The archive holds controlled dowsing nulls.** The queue has carded dowsing four times, and the archive carries the controlled literature: the **ETH Zürich 1946** trial, the **Wunder 2003** review, the **Munich 1987–88** Betz study (500 dowsers screened, 43 selected, 843 trials), the **Kassel 1990** double-blind test, the **Iowa 2006** grave-location review, and the **Scheunen** field protocol. The mainstream prediction is that **the blind task itself is at chance**, and therefore that the count cannot predict it. The card is built to reach that result cleanly if it is the true one.

**What the card is NOT testing:** it is not re-testing whether dowsing works. It takes the blind task's chance-level outcome as the likely baseline and asks a narrower question — **does the school's own stated screening number predict anything at all?** A null here is informative: it would mean the community's own rule for who is qualified has no predictive content, and that a shared survey would be no better off screening its participants than picking them at random.

## What the card adds

The source supplies the **independent variable** — a stated, numeric, repeatable score — and the **screening decision** it is meant to drive. It supplies no design. The card adds:

- the **blind target-finding task** and its exact chance rate (the dependent variable the score is supposed to predict);
- the **sealing of the scores before the blind task** (so the scorer cannot be influenced by performance, and the participant cannot be influenced by their own score);
- the **trial count** and the **re-randomisation of the target every trial** (the minimum needed for a hit rate to mean anything);
- the **correlation analysis** — the actual question, which the source never asks: does the number track the performance?
- the **chance baseline** as the comparator, so a "60+ is good" score can be judged against what guessing achieves.

## Protocol

1. **Recruit** at least six participants who have never done the blind task, plus one **recorder** who does not dowse and does not take the sensitivity test.
2. **Build the blind rig.** One target hidden among a fixed set of identical positions the participant can neither see nor feel — a coin under one of eight identical cups, a magnet under one of ten identical tiles, a water vial behind one of twelve identical screens. The participant must not be able to tell the target's position by sight, touch, weight or sound. Photograph the rig.
3. **Take the sensitivity scores FIRST**, exactly as the source states: standing, facing due west, left hand flat on the solar plexus with the fingers touching, the pendulum in the right hand with the whole string let out and the bob about 23 cm from the left hand; wait for the swing to become a rotation; count only full clockwise rotations; write the number down. **Do this before anyone touches the blind task.** The recorder records every score and **seals the sheet**.
4. **Run the blind task.** Each participant gets the same number of trials, at least **20**, in a randomised order. The recorder re-randomises the target's position before every trial and records the participant's call. **No feedback of any kind until the participant's run is over.**
5. **Score.** For each participant: hit rate, the exact chance rate for that rig (1/8, 1/10, 1/12), and a binomial p-value against chance. Then the correlation between rotation count and hit rate across participants.
6. **Repeat** with a second group, or the same group on a second rig, before believing any positive result.

## Pre-registered pass/fail

- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, mainstream result):** the rotation count does **not** predict the blind hit rate — the correlation is indistinguishable from zero, the high scorers do not beat chance, and the sub-25 scorers do not do worse; a second group repeats it. *(Skeptic's Star.)*
- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** the rotation count **predicts** the blind hit rate — the correlation is positive and survives a permutation test, the high scorers beat chance and the low scorers do not, and the pattern reproduces in a second group or on a second rig.
- **FAIL:** any participant knew the target's position; the recorder also ran the sensitivity test and scored their own; the target was not re-randomised every trial; feedback was given mid-run; the rotation count was taken **after** the blind task; or a result was read from a single participant.
- **VOID:** the sensitivity protocol was not followed as stated; the blind was not held; or fewer than 20 trials per participant were run. **A VOID is not a NULL.**
