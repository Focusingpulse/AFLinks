## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source's own text, quoted:

> "The Swiss researcher Dr. Joseph A. Kopp states that in the stimulus bands of water veins, anomalies measurable with a magnetometer can be experienced, but changes in the conductivity of the soil and the air can also be demonstrated. He further experienced that the frequencies of the UHF band and the intensity of electromagnetic waves in the infrared range rise in earth radiation (especially above water veins). **French researchers used an oscilloscope and a Geiger-Müller counter to experimentally confirm that in the stimulus bands of certain water-vein and fault-line earth radiations the radioactive gamma radiation is also strengthened.**"

The source's own frame for why this matters:

> "The most important goal of radiesthesia is to seek out and secure healthy places free of geopathic radiation zones (including electrosmog and radioactivity) within a dwelling or property."

The source's own ordered survey — the sequence this card borrows its instrument-first posture from:

> "I. Radioactivity measurement, II/a. Electrosmog measurement at low frequency, II/b. Electrosmog measurement at high frequency, III. Measurement of earth radiations, examination of geopathogenic areas, IV. Protection against earth radiation and electrosmog, designation of a neutral place, consultation."

And the source's own candid limit, stated in its own words — the reason this card is a test and not an endorsement:

> "**There is no instrumental earth-radiation measurement.** The measurement of earth radiations is possible exclusively by radiesthetic methods, in which the measuring instrument is the human being itself…"

> "Despite all these attempts, however, the nature of the water-vein, hartmann, curry, ley, Szent György net … etc. earth radiations, and of the vortices (and the rest), **has remained just as undefinable as it was.** There is no correspondence with the concepts occupied by physics, such as electricity and magnetism."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** is the gamma dose rate above a dowsed water-vein line — or above a *known* physical water body — higher than at control points on the same ground, measured with the same instrument, at the same time, on the same day?

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the claim and no home test of it.** A grep for `Geiger`, `gamma`, `radioactiv` and `water vein` across `living-library/synthesis/quest-queue/` and `living-library/synthesis/replication/` returns **no card and no dossier** on this claim. The queue's shelter cards are about *locating* zones (005, 044), *feeling* a form (011), *scanning* a grid with a magnetometer (021), or a *built form's* field (027, 033, 050). **None measures ionising radiation over a claimed geopathic band.** The queue's water cards measure a *physical* property of water (vortex, structure, retention, composition) — none measures the ground.

Four things the card must say out loud:

1. **The claim is second-hand, and the source that carries it says the thing it is about cannot be measured.** The source's own position is that earth radiations are *not* instrumentally measurable — *"there is no instrumental earth-radiation measurement"* — and yet it cites a French team that used a Geiger–Müller counter to measure a correlate of exactly those radiations. **The source is candid that the nature of the earth radiations "has remained just as undefinable as it was."** The card's posture is the source's posture: test the cited claim, do not endorse it, and do not pretend the source claims more than it does.
2. **The physics points the other way, which is the whole reason the test is interesting.** Water *attenuates* gamma — a wet vein is extra shielding between the soil's natural potassium/uranium/thorium and the counter. So the naive physical prediction is a **lower** reading over a wet vein, not a higher one. The claim asserts the opposite direction. **A null is therefore the expected result, and it is a complete result.** The one mechanism that could produce the claimed *rise* is **radon** — groundwater can carry radon, and radon daughters deposit on the ground surface, adding a local gamma signal. The card names that possibility in advance so a positive result is not over-read.
3. **A dowsed line is a claim, not a fact — so the card separates "water" from "dowsing".** If the family only measured over a dowsed line and found nothing, they would not know whether the vein was mis-located or the effect absent. **Arm B (a known physical water feature)** is the discriminator: if gamma rises over a buried pipe or a stream but not over the dowsed lines, the effect (if any) tracks physical water, not the radiesthetic method; if it rises over the dowsed lines but not over the known water, the opposite. **Neither arm alone can answer the question.**
4. **A Geiger counter that has not been shown to respond is not a measurement.** A cheap counter may be insensitive, or may be reading its own background. **The instrument check (arm D — a known gamma source) is mandatory:** if the counter does not rise over a bag of potassium chloride or a granite offcut, the run is void, not a null.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that the dowsed lines, the known water feature and the control points are indistinguishable once background is subtracted. That is a complete, useful result — it files the first home test of a 20th-century claim the archive has held untested, and it tells a family that the gamma half of the geopathic-survey tradition does not reproduce at home. A repeatable elevation would be genuinely surprising and would need a radon measurement and a second site before any conclusion.

## Why it matters

- It is the queue's **first card on ionising radiation** — every prior geobiology card (005, 021, 044) measures magnetism or dowsing response; this one measures gamma, a quantity with a defined unit and a real instrument.
- It is the queue's **first card whose expected result is opposite to the claim's direction on physical grounds** — the claim says gamma *rises* over water, and water *shields* gamma. The card states the counter-prediction before the run, so a null cannot later be re-read as a surprise.
- It is the queue's **first card that borrows an ordered survey from its source and inverts the order's purpose**: the source puts instruments *first* and dowsing *second*; the card uses the instrument to test the dowsing rather than to precede it.
- It carries a **checkable, non-contested number** from the source — the dwelling radioactivity threshold (0.21 µSv/h maximum, 0.07–0.14 µSv/h ideal) — so even a null on the vein claim leaves the family with a measured baseline for their own home.
- It is genuinely **home-scale**: a Geiger–Müller counter, a bag of potassium chloride, some markers, and a notebook.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$60–150** — a Geiger–Müller counter or gamma-capable dosimeter (many consumer counters read in µSv/h and cpm; a counter with a stated sensitivity in cpm per µSv/h is preferable). A known source: a bag of potassium chloride (water-softener salt or "NoSalt"-type salt substitute) ~$5, or a granite countertop offcut (often free from a stone yard). Markers and a notebook.
- Safety: **low.** The known sources are ordinary household materials with negligible dose at arm's length; do not open a smoke detector, do not handle bare Am-241, and do not ingest the salt substitute as a source. A Geiger counter is passive. **The real hazard is over-reading a null as a health verdict** — the card is a physics test, not a health assessment, and the card says so.
- Accessibility: an afternoon to build the source check and mark the arms; three sessions of ~1 hour each. The discipline is in the blinding and the same-time-of-day repeat.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A Geiger–Müller counter or gamma dosimeter** — with a stated sensitivity and a stated integration/response time. Record the model and its cpm-per-µSv/h figure.
- **A known gamma source for the instrument check** — a **bag of potassium chloride** (K-40 gamma) or a **granite offcut** (natural K/U/Th). Both are safe at arm's length. This is arm D and it is mandatory.
- **A dowsing tool** — a pair of L-rods or a pendulum, for the family member who will locate arm A.
- **Markers** — pegs, stakes or chalk, to mark the measurement points.
- **A tape measure** — to fix the counter's height above the ground and the spacing of the points.
- **A notebook** — for the pre-registration sheet and every reading.

## Protocol (four-arm gamma test, pre-registered)

**Phase 1 — instrument check and background.**
1. **Write the pre-registration sheet before running anything:** the counter model and sensitivity, the source used for arm D, the arms, the number of points per arm, the counter height, the integration time, the number of sessions, and the criteria below. Photograph it.
2. **Establish the counter's background.** Take **10 × 1-minute readings** at a fixed spot at least 1 m above the ground, indoors, with nothing nearby. Record the mean and the standard deviation. **This SD is the noise floor** against which every arm is scored.
3. **Run the instrument check (arm D).** Take 5 × 1-minute readings over the known source at a fixed distance (e.g., 5 cm). **If the mean does not rise clearly above the background mean (target: ≥ 2× the background SD), the counter is not sensitive enough and the run is void — not a null.**

**Phase 2 — locate the arms.**
4. **Arm A — dowsed water-vein lines.** Have the dowsing family member mark **three candidate water-vein lines** in the yard or garden. **Mark three points on each line**, 1 m apart. If a second person is available, have them re-label the points with codes so the measurer does not know which arm a point belongs to.
5. **Arm B — a known physical water feature.** Pick one: a **buried water service line** (locate it from the meter or the valve), a **stream**, or a **pond edge**. Mark three points along it, the same distance apart as arm A.
6. **Arm C — control points.** Mark three points on the same ground with **no water feature** — mid-lawn, away from pipes, drains and the dowsed lines.

**Phase 3 — measure, blind.**
7. **Randomise the order** in which the 15 points (3 arms × 3 lines/features × ... adjust to your count) are measured, and record the order. Measure each point **once per session**, holding the counter at a **fixed height (e.g., 5 cm) for a fixed integration time (e.g., 3 minutes)**, and record cpm (and µSv/h if available).
8. **Run all arms in the same session, on the same day, at the same time of day** — radon flux has a diurnal cycle and soil moisture changes after rain. **Log the time, air temperature, humidity, and whether it rained in the last 48 hours.**
9. **Repeat the whole set on at least 3 separate days.**

**Phase 4 — score.**
10. **Subtract the session's background** from every reading. Score the **background-subtracted cpm per arm** (mean and spread), and whether arm A or arm B exceeds arm C repeatably.
11. **Optional secondary measurement:** record the **indoor baseline** of the dwelling (a 1-hour reading in a bedroom, 1 m up) and compare it to the source's stated threshold — **0.21 µSv/h maximum, 0.07–0.14 µSv/h ideal.** This is a standard health-physics number, not a test of the vein claim, and is reported separately.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (the claim survives — surprising, needs follow-up):** arm A (dowsed lines) **or** arm B (known water) shows a **repeatable elevation above arm C (control)** of at least **2× the background SD**, in **≥ 2 of 3 sessions**, with the instrument check (arm D) confirming the counter responds. Report as a surprising result needing a **radon measurement** and a **second site** before any conclusion.
- **PASS (the claim is refuted — the expected, complete result):** arms A, B and C are within the scatter of each other once background is subtracted. The gamma half of the geopathic-survey tradition does not reproduce at home. Reported as the expected result, not a failure (Skeptic's Star).
- **FAIL:** arm A or B is elevated but **arm C is equally elevated** — the reading is the site, the soil or the day, not the water feature. The vein claim fails while the measurement succeeded.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** the instrument check (arm D) did not rise above background; fewer than 3 sessions; the arms were not measured in the same session; the counter height or integration time changed between points; or the dowsed lines were re-marked between sessions.
- **ARTIFACT:** the reading tracks the counter's own warm-up (a rising trend across the first minutes); the reading follows the time of day or recent rain rather than the arm; the reading follows the counter's position rather than the marked line; or the "control" points sit over an unmarked pipe or drain.

## Confounds (named in advance)

- **Water shields gamma — the central physical confound.** A wet vein attenuates the soil's natural gamma. **The claim's direction (a rise) is the opposite of the naive prediction.** A null is therefore expected; a rise needs a mechanism (radon) and a second measurement.
- **Radon.** Radon flux varies with time of day, weather and soil moisture, and radon daughters deposit on surfaces. **This is the one mechanism that could produce a rise, and the card names it in advance** so a positive result is not over-read. A follow-up radon test is the natural next step.
- **Soil composition.** Natural gamma varies with the soil's potassium, uranium and thorium content — a granite-rich soil reads higher than a sandy one. **Measure all arms on the same ground** so the soil is held constant.
- **The instrument.** A cheap counter may be insensitive, may read its own background, or may have a long warm-up. **The instrument check (arm D) and the stated sensitivity are mandatory.**
- **The dowsing.** A dowsed line may be mis-located. **Arm B (known water) is the control that separates the water from the dowsing** — without it, a null is ambiguous.
- **Diurnal and weather drift.** Radon and soil moisture change hour to hour. **Run all arms in one session, at the same time of day, and log recent rain.**
- **The health over-read.** A null does not mean the home is "safe" and a rise does not mean it is "unsafe". **This card is a physics test of a cited claim, not a health assessment**, and the card says so.

## Verdict rules

Arm A or B above arm C, repeated, with the instrument check passing → **the claim survives** (surprising; needs radon and a second site). Arms A, B and C indistinguishable → **the claim is refuted at home** (the expected, complete result). Arm A or B elevated but arm C equally so → **the site, not the water** (the vein claim fails). Instrument check failed, fewer than 3 sessions, or arms not run in the same session → **inconclusive, not a refutation**.
