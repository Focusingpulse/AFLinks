## The claim (as asserted by the source)

Lee measured the infrared radiation in the **3–5.6 µm band** emitted from a qigong master's palm (Laogong point) at a **15 cm standoff**, using a liquid-nitrogen-cooled InSb detector with a chopper and lock-in amplifier — i.e. he measured the palm's **temperature**, since a body near 300 K radiates as a blackbody. He reports **two distinct emission modes** in the same Daoist master:

| Mode | What the detector saw | Lee's reading |
|---|---|---|
| **"Regulating qi"** (調氣) | ~10 s after emission began, IR radiation **increased greatly** — the palm warmed. After stopping, the palm **kept** the temperature; the signal fell only when the hand moved away. | Parasympathetic stimulation → **vasodilation** → blood flows to the palm → palm warms. |
| **"Strong qi" / "ice palm"** (寒冰掌) | The AC signal went **negative** — the palm was **colder than the environment**, absorbing heat from it and reducing the background photocurrent. On withdrawal the palm **instantly became hot**. | Sympathetic stimulation → **vasoconstriction** → blood cannot reach the palm → palm cools. |

Lee's own conclusion: *"this Daoist master had mastered two different practice methods: one controlling the parasympathetic nervous system, the other controlling the sympathetic nervous system, used alternately to achieve control over the body."*

**A second, separate claim in the same paper** — the EEG "qigong resonance state": during practice, frontal/parietal **alpha power rises 1.7–4×** and the spectrum narrows (half-width ~0.5 Hz → <0.3 Hz); the contrasting "entering stillness / samadhi" state shows alpha **decline**. Lee's own baseline: *"when an ordinary person sits quietly with eyes closed, the peak alpha-wave power changes little within 6 minutes — within about 10%."*

### Honest status of the claim

**Unreplicated, and the mechanism is mundane.** Lee's own explanation is autonomic vasodilation / vasoconstriction — ordinary physiology, not an exotic field. That is what makes the claim *testable at home*: it does not require a detector for a new force, only a thermometer for a hand.

The mainstream frame: **thermal biofeedback is real.** People can learn to change peripheral skin temperature with feedback (it is used clinically for Raynaud's and migraine). So the interesting question is not "is this possible?" but **"is it direction-specific and volitional without feedback?"** — i.e. can a person told *warm* warm and told *cool* cool, on command, with no biofeedback loop and no physical means? That is the discriminating test, and it is exactly what Lee's two modes assert.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** this card cannot show that "qi" is anything. It can show whether a person in this family can drive their own palm temperature **up on one instruction and down on another**, and whether the cooling mode can push the palm **below ambient** — Lee's sharpest and most falsifiable prediction. A null is the expected result for most people, and it is a complete and valuable finding: it establishes what ordinary voluntary thermal control looks like, which is the baseline the whole tradition's claim rests on.

## Why it matters

- It is the queue's **first card whose object is the practitioner's own body** — every prior health card tests an *external* object (water conductivity 022, water memory 028, a pendulum 034, a recipient's EEG 039). None asks whether a person can voluntarily move one of their own physiological variables, on command, in a specified direction.
- The **discriminator is the direction of change within one person**, not a between-person comparison — which removes the "is this person a master?" problem entirely. Anyone can run it on themselves.
- It has a **real-world payoff if it passes**: voluntary hand-warming is a genuine self-regulation skill (thermal biofeedback). A family that learns it has learned something useful regardless of what it means about qi.
- It links a **fresh primary source** (Lee Si-Chen's NTU paper, translated 2026-09-26) whose original host is dead — a preservation candidate the archive now holds.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build cost: **~$25** (a non-contact IR thermometer with a skin/body mode and 0.1 °C resolution; a second thermometer or the same gun on matte tape for ambient; a phone stand or a fixed mark for the 15 cm standoff).
- Safety: **none.** No mains, no chemicals, no heat source, no sharp tools. Adult or child; the only requirement is sitting still.
- Accessibility: an afternoon, three sessions.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Non-contact IR thermometer, skin/body mode, 0.1 °C resolution** (~$20–35). Clinical forehead thermometers are calibrated for skin emissivity (~0.98) and are the right class of instrument. **Note the accuracy caveat:** a cheap IR gun may be ±0.2–0.5 °C absolute — irrelevant here, because every measurement is a **within-session delta from the same instrument at the same distance**, and the pass threshold (1.0 °C) is set well above that.
- **A fixed standoff** — a phone stand, a box, or a mark on a table that holds the gun at the **same distance (15 cm, matching the source) and the same angle** for every reading. Hand-held readings at varying distance are the single largest source of noise.
- **Ambient reference** — a second thermometer, or the same gun aimed at a piece of matte black tape on the table (emissivity-matched to skin is not possible, so use a contact thermometer for ambient if available). Ambient is needed only for the "below ambient" sub-claim.
- **A palm rest** — a folded towel or a stand so the hand can be held palm-up, relaxed, without tremor, for 5 minutes at a time.
- **A sealed instruction set** — three cards (WARM / COOL / REST) in envelopes, shuffled by the referee before the session.
- **A timer** and a **recording sheet** (time, temperature, condition — the condition column filled by the referee, not the measurer).
- **Phone camera** for evidence.

## Protocol (within-subject crossover, direction as the discriminator)

1. **Validate the instrument first (positive control).** Sit at rest 5 minutes. Measure the palm. **Rub the hands together briskly for 30 seconds and measure again.** The palm must rise **≥ 0.5 °C**. If it does not, the setup or the instrument cannot resolve the effect — **VOID the session and fix the setup before continuing.** (This is the card's validity gate, the same role card 039's α/β check plays.)
2. **Pre-register.** Write the thresholds, the scoring rule, the condition order rule, and who is blind to what, on the sheet **before** the first session.
3. **Roles.** A **practitioner** (the person attempting control), a **measurer** (reads and records the thermometer, **blind to the condition**), and a **referee** (holds the key, shuffles the envelopes, fills the condition column after the session). The practitioner must **not** be the measurer.
4. **Baseline.** Practitioner sits, hand on the rest, palm up at the fixed standoff. Measure every 30 s for **5 minutes**. Record the mean of the last 3 readings as the baseline.
5. **Condition.** The referee draws one sealed card and shows it **only to the practitioner**. The practitioner attempts that condition for **5 minutes** — WARM (the "regulating qi" intent), COOL (the "ice palm" intent), or REST (sit quietly, no intent). The measurer records temperature every 30 s without knowing which condition is running.
6. **Recover.** 5 minutes rest, hand off the stand, between conditions.
7. **Repeat** for all three conditions in **randomized order**, then repeat the whole set on **three separate days** (three sessions).
8. **Score** (referee, after the session, with the key):
   - **W** = palm temperature change during the WARM condition (end-mean minus baseline).
   - **C** = change during the COOL condition.
   - **R** = change during the REST condition (the drift control).
   - **The discriminating quantity is W − C**, not W alone. A palm that warms in both conditions has not demonstrated direction-specific control; it has demonstrated relaxation.
   - Secondary: **did the palm fall below ambient during COOL?** (Lee's sharpest prediction.)

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS:** **W − C ≥ 1.0 °C** in **≥ 2 of 3 sessions** — direction-specific voluntary control, with the palm moving in opposite directions on opposite instructions.
- **PASS (strong form):** additionally, the palm falls **≥ 1.0 °C below its own baseline AND below ambient** during COOL in ≥ 2 of 3 sessions — Lee's "ice palm" prediction confirmed.
- **FAIL:** **W − C ≤ 0.3 °C** in ≥ 2 of 3 sessions — no direction-specific control; the palm drifts with rest and arousal only (Skeptic's Star; a complete and valuable result).
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** between the two, or fewer than 3 sessions.
- **VOID:** the rub-hands positive control did not register ≥ 0.5 °C; OR ambient temperature moved more than 1.5 °C during a session; OR the hand left the stand mid-condition; OR the measurer knew the condition. **Report the resolution achieved and the control required — not a verdict.**

## Confounds named in advance (each is an explicit ARTIFACT test)

- **Ambient drift** — a warming room warms every hand. Mitigation: record ambient each session; VOID if it moves >1.5 °C; run sessions at the same time of day.
- **Arousal / relaxation** — sitting quietly and attending to the hand may warm it regardless of instruction. **This is what the REST condition measures**, and it is why the discriminator is W − C, not W.
- **Standoff and angle** — hand-held readings at varying distance dominate the noise. Mitigation: the fixed standoff, and the positive control.
- **Expectation** — the practitioner knows their own instruction; that is unavoidable, because volition *is* the claim. The blinding is on the **measurer and the scorer**, not the practitioner. Say this plainly rather than pretending the practitioner is blind.
- **Instrument resolution** — a ±0.5 °C gun cannot resolve a 0.3 °C effect. Mitigation: the positive control, and a threshold (1.0 °C) set above the instrument's error.
- **Practice history** — a person with years of qigong or biofeedback training is not a naive subject. Record each participant's practice history; a PASS in a trained person and a FAIL in an untrained one are both informative.

## What a result would mean

- **PASS** would be the queue's first evidence that a person can move their own peripheral temperature **in a specified direction on command**, without feedback and without physical means. It would not show that "qi" is a field; it would show voluntary autonomic control of a kind the mainstream literature already accepts is trainable — and it would put a number on how fast and how large.
- **FAIL** would be the honest negative: in this family's hands, palm temperature follows rest and arousal, not instruction. That is a real result about the tradition's most concrete physiological claim, and it is the baseline the claim needs.
- Either way the archive gains the **positive control** — the rub-hands number — which is the missing calibration for every future thermal claim.
