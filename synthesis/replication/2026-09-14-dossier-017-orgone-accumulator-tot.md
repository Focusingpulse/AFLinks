## The Protocol — triple-blind To-T differential

The testable core: **if the accumulator produces an anomalous heating effect, it must exceed a thermodynamically-matched control enclosure under identical conditions.**

### Materials (~$150-200)

1. **Accumulator (ORAC):** 3-ply minimum (steel wool + cotton/wool layers), ~30cm cube or larger, per Reich's published specs
2. **Control enclosure:** Solid-wall box of same external dimensions, thermal mass matched to ORAC's thermal time-constant (calibrate with heat pulse test)
3. **Temperature sensors:** 2x calibrated differential thermocouples or high-precision RTDs (0.01°C resolution)
4. **Data logger:** Arduino/Raspberry Pi with SD card, or commercial thermocouple logger
5. **Weather station:** Basic barometer, hygrometer, or nearby weather service data
6. **Blinding:** Third party sets up sensor labels (A/B) without experimenter knowing which is ORAC

### Setup

1. Place both enclosures in same room, same height, same distance from walls/windows
2. Install one temperature sensor inside each enclosure, one ambient sensor nearby
3. Log temperature every 5 minutes for 30+ days continuously
4. Record weather data (barometric pressure, humidity, cloud cover) from nearby source
5. Analyze after experiment ends — experimenter still blinded to which enclosure was ORAC

### Procedure

1. **Calibration phase (3 days):** Verify both enclosures show same baseline behavior with lids open
2. **Experiment phase (30 days):** Lids closed, continuous logging, no disturbance
3. **Analysis phase:** After 30 days, third party reveals enclosure identities
4. **Statistical test:** Compare To-T difference (ORAC vs control) across all weather states

## Pass/Fail Criteria (pre-registered)

**PASS (supports the anomalous heating claim):**
- ORAC interior temperature exceeds control by ≥0.1°C on average across 30 days, AND
- The difference correlates with weather states in the predicted direction (higher in clear/dry weather), AND
- Second-harmonic analysis shows a component not present in control

**FAIL (refutes, for this protocol and tester):**
- No statistically significant difference between ORAC and control (p > 0.05)
- Or control runs warmer than ORAC
- Or difference is fully explained by humidity/pressure regression without residual

**INCONCLUSIVE:**
- Small difference (0.02-0.1°C) that doesn't reach statistical significance
- Equipment failure or insufficient days logged
- Only one trial run

**Honest note:** A PASS would be genuinely extraordinary — it would mean Einstein's 1941 convection explanation was incomplete. A FAIL would be the expected result given 80 years of no mainstream confirmation, and would honestly retire the accumulator heating claim for family purposes. Either outcome is publishable.
