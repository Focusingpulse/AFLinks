## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source's own framing, in its opening:

> "Our approach rests on the observation of the hive organism with the tools of form-wave radiesthesia. We have tried to imagine a device producing form waves capable of supporting the bees."

And its own summary of what it observed:

> "We have observed these last two years a progressive improvement of the state of the brood in both hives. Up until 2004 the spring visits revealed the well-known image of brood heavily affected by varroatosis … In 2005 and 2006 a clear improvement is noted with the total disappearance of the usual signs of the infestation such as perforated cappings or uncapped cells. … And yet varroa is terribly present!"

And the author's own mechanism hypothesis:

> "We make the hypothesis of a delay in laying by the female varroa after the capping of the cell. … Under the effect of the treatment the laying female would delay her laying notably, to the point that the different metamorphoses no longer succeed, for lack of time, in forming a constituted or viable individual at the moment of uncapping."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a hive with the source's passive form-wave device hung at its apiary count **fewer fallen varroa mites on its drop cloth** — and yield **no less honey** — than a matched hive with a plain object of the same mass hung the same way?

## Honest status of the claim

**This is a practitioner's observational account, self-published and non-peer-reviewed, and the author says so himself.** The source is unusually candid about its own limits, and the card carries every one of them:

1. **No control.** Both hives received the device. There is no untreated or sham-treated comparison anywhere in the 2007 account, and the author states: *"It is therefore not a matter of following an experiment that would claim to be scientific but rather of the account of the different stages that led to the present form of the device."*
2. **The device was changed mid-observation.** *"We intervened several times at the apiary in order to change the operating conditions of the device."* Any effect is therefore not attributable to one fixed apparatus.
3. **The site's own position is that the claim is untestable.** *"Radiesthesia is an art; it is vain to want to test it scientifically"* (as recorded in the scout's assessment). The card does not take that position — it takes the opposite one, and says so.
4. **An earlier version of the same technology was abandoned by its own author.** The 1991 radionic treatment form, tested in the 28-hive apiary, was dropped: *"having been judged, in use, of a very relative effectiveness when it came to treating heavily infested hives, and, in certain respects, not respectful enough of the bees."*
5. **The author's own verdict on the varroa effect is negative.** *"It is clear that in the present state of the research the effect of the medication on the varroa populations is not mastered. The natural cycles of the varroa are disturbed, but the level of infestation remains too high in the hives."*
6. **The claimed effect is said to fluctuate.** The source says the field's production is *"entirely subject to the earth's cosmo-tellurism"* and that this explains *"the swings in the effectiveness of the treatment from one year to the next, and even from one season to another."* A claim that can be weak in any given season is a claim that is hard to refute in any given season — the card must therefore fix the schedule and the scoring rule in advance.
7. **The author is not a beekeeper.** *"For our part, not being a beekeeper, we do not envisage repeating observations on a few isolated hives, which would not have much meaning."* The card requires a beekeeper, which is a real entry condition.

**What the source DOES give, and why the card exists:**

- A **fully specified, buildable apparatus** (the parts and their dimensions are given component by component).
- A **stated endpoint that a family already knows how to measure** — the varroa fall count on a screened bottom board, which the source presents as standard practice and describes in detail.
- A **design template from the source itself** — the 1991 professional-apiary experiment: 28 colonies, randomly split into two equal groups, one treated and one control, paired by random draw, harvests compared (**94 kg treated vs 79 kg control**, "a little more than 1 kg on average for each treated hive"). The author himself calls for a larger test (40 hives).
- **A candid call for replication:** *"Friend beekeepers, this device deserves to be tested on a larger scale. The task exceeds our means."*

**The ordinary mechanisms that must be excluded before the claim is interesting:**

| Ordinary mechanism | What it predicts | The control that separates it |
|---|---|---|
| **Colony strength / queen** | a stronger colony with a better queen has fewer mites per bee and more honey, whatever is on the roof | **match the two clusters** for bee race and starting frames of bees and brood, and log every queen event |
| **Weather and season** | mite populations and honey both track the season; a good or bad year looks like a treatment effect | **run both clusters in the SAME season**, at the same time, in the same climate |
| **Reinfestation / robbing** | mites arrive from a neighbouring apiary and swamp any effect | the two sites **≥1.5 km apart**, and a note of every apiary within range |
| **The counting method itself** | the counts drift because the schedule slips, the cloth is fouled, or ants remove mites | a **fixed ten-day schedule**, a clean cloth, and **ants cleared** (the source's own warning) |
| **The object itself, not its form** | any object hung at the apiary changes something (shade, a perch, a landmark) | the **matched-mass inert object** at the control cluster — the source's own "ball alone" state, which it reports as an 8–10 cm field versus ~5 m for the full assembly |
| **The observer** | the family expects the device hives to do better and counts them differently | **count blind**: have a second person record the counts without knowing which cluster is which, or label the cloths and count them later |

**So the honest position is:** the claim is measurable with hardware a beekeeper already owns, the apparatus is cheap and fully specified, the source is candid about having run no control — and the source's own history contains an abandoned version of the same technology and an admitted failure to master the varroa effect. **The card's first job is to establish a fair comparison**, not to chase the effect.

|---|---|---|
| **Matched control cluster** | the two clusters count the same | the device cluster counts lower, reproducibly |

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$40–90** — a steel pétanque ball (~$10), a cast half-sphere (lead if obtainable, else tin roofing alloy; ~$20–40), copper wire for the solenoid (~$5), a small piece of quartz (~$5), a suspension net or cord (~$5). A plaster mould and a wood rasp for the casting.
- The real cost is **access and time**: a beekeeper with **at least two matched clusters of hives** (two apiaries ≥1.5 km apart, or two well-separated groups), screened bottom boards on every hive, and one full season of ten-day counts.
- Safety: **low.** No power, no moving parts. The only hazards are the ordinary ones of beekeeping (opening hives, lifting) and of casting metal (molten lead — do it outdoors, with eye protection and ventilation; tin alloy is safer). Do not hang the device where it can fall onto a person or be reached by children.
- Accessibility: any beekeeper. A household without hives cannot run this card — the honest answer is that this one is for the apiary.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A steel pétanque ball**, ~700 g, ~7 cm diameter — the sphere.
- **A cast half-sphere of the same diameter**, in lead if obtainable (the source reports the lead half-sphere as taking the field to ~5 m; tin roofing alloy is the stated substitute, with a shorter claimed range). Made from a plaster mould, finished with a wood rasp and emery cloth.
- **A copper-wire solenoid** — 12 turns stretched to a minimum length of 12 cm, base diameter 4.5 cm, 1 mm wire, insulated or bare, work-hardened copper. Mounted on the vertical axis.
- **A small piece of quartz** — the source specifies the amorphous, not crystalline, form, and says it appears necessary "for the well-being of the bees." Its role is the author's own hypothesis.
- **A suspension net** of flexible wire (the source advises three strands only) — the device is **suspended**, not resting on a support, so the wind moves it.
- **A matched-mass inert object** — a plain pétanque ball of the same weight, with no half-sphere, no solenoid and no quartz. This is the control.
- **Screened bottom boards and drop cloths** for every hive — the measurement hardware. The source advises an aluminium cloth covered with a sheet of paper the size of the hive floor.
- **A notebook and a fixed ten-day schedule.**

## Protocol (pre-registered)

1. **Pre-register, before building.** Write down: the device's parts and their masses, the two sites and their separation, the hive numbers, each colony's starting strength (frames of bees and frames of brood), the counting schedule, the scoring rule, and the season's dates. Photograph the sheet.
2. **Match the two clusters.** Same bee race, same starting colony strength, the same number of hives, screened bottom boards on all of them, and the two sites **≥1.5 km apart** so the colonies do not mix. Note every other apiary within range.
3. **Establish the baseline before the device goes up.** Count the drop cloth of every hive on the same ten-day schedule for at least three counts before hanging anything. This is the reference the season is measured against.
4. **Build the device to specification** and hang it at the device cluster, suspended at ~1.8 m, so that the hive roofs stay below the plane of the sphere's equator. Hang the **matched-mass inert object** the same way, at the same height, at the control cluster.
5. **Count every ten days, all season.** For each hive: lift the drop cloth, count every varroa mite on it (dark red-brown adults and the pale young ones), record the count and the date, clean the cloth, and replace it. **Clear the ants** — the source warns they carry mites off the cloth and ruin the count. Keep the cloth clear of the mesh (the source's own remark: if the bees can reach the cloth they clean it and the count is worthless).
6. **Count blind.** Have a second person record the counts without knowing which cluster is which, or label the cloths and count them later.
7. **Log every colony event** — requeening, supersedure, swarming, robbing, absconding, disease, collapse — with its date.
8. **Harvest and weigh.** Record the honey harvested from each hive in kg.
9. **Compute.** For each hive: the seasonal total of fallen mites, the January count (the source's low-water mark — a hive yielding fewer than 5 mites in January is, on the source's own rule, a first-year-type infestation), and the honey yield. Compare the device cluster with the control cluster hive by hive and as cluster totals.
10. **Post.** Photo of the pre-registration sheet, the device and the control object, the raw counts table, the January counts, the honey weights, the colony-event log, and a one-paragraph verdict.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim supported at home scale):** the device cluster's **seasonal mite-fall total** and/or its **January count** is **lower** than the matched control cluster's by a margin **larger than the season's own run-to-run scatter**, AND the device hives' **honey yield is not lower**, AND the difference is **not explained** by colony strength, queen events, weather or reinfestation (all logged), AND it survives blind counting.
- **REFUTED:** the device cluster and the control cluster **do not separate** — their mite counts track each other within the season's scatter — OR the device cluster is **worse**.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** the two clusters are not comparable (a colony absconded, was requeued, was robbed, or collapsed), or the season's conditions broke the comparison.
- **VOID:** there was no control cluster; the two clusters were not matched for bee race and starting colony strength; the drop-cloth counts were not taken on a fixed ten-day schedule; ants were allowed to remove mites from the cloths; the device was modified mid-run. **A VOID is not a NULL.**

## Independent-variable discipline

The one variable is **the device's presence, at matched mass and matched placement** — the control object is the same weight, hung the same way, at the same height, at a matched cluster of hives in the same season. The **starting-strength match** catches colony strength; the **same-season, same-climate** design catches the weather; the **≥1.5 km separation** catches reinfestation; the **fixed ten-day schedule and ant control** catch the counting method; the **blind count** catches the observer; the **honey yield** catches a treatment that helps the mites' host at the colony's expense. If the two clusters count the same, the card has answered its question: the form — the thing the source's whole account rests on — does nothing.

## What a result would mean

- **Device cluster lower, control cluster higher, matched strength, blind count, honey not lower** → the claim survives a controlled test at home scale. The honest follow-up is a second season, a second household, and a larger apiary on the source's own 28-hive template.
- **The two clusters track each other** → the form did nothing. A complete and useful result — and the one the source's missing control left open.
- **The device cluster is worse** → a result worth reporting plainly; it would suggest the object's presence, not its form, matters, or that the season confounded the design.
- **No separation at all, and a clean season of counts on the family's own hives** → the claim does not reproduce at home scale with this apparatus. A complete result (Skeptic's Star) — and, whatever the verdict, the family now owns a season of baseline mite data on their own colonies, which is worth having on its own.
- **A colony event breaks the comparison** → INCONCLUSIVE, not a null. Say so and run the next season.
