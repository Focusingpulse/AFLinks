## Source lineage

- **Louis Pasteur (1848)** — the founding separation of sodium ammonium tartrate crystals by hand, establishing that crystal handedness is a real, countable property.
- **The sodium-chlorate model system (20th c.)** — NaClO₃ is an *achiral* molecule that crystallizes into *chiral* (d or l) crystals; the standard testbed for spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking, and the reason a small external bias is even worth asking about.
- **Beverly Rubik (2021), Institute for Frontier Science** — 28 controlled experiments applying "subtle external stimuli" to NaClO₃ crystallization and reading the d/l ratio by polarimetry. Reported significant d-bias for a 432 Hz acoustic field (67%, p=0.049), an Amezcua BioDisc-3 (81%, p=0.034) and a Purple Plate (64.9%, p=0.003); **null** for a dynamic magnetic field, a wooden pyramid, and a commercial structured water.
- **The claim under test:** a specified subtle stimulus biases which handedness of crystal forms from an otherwise unbiased solution.

## The claim (as asserted by the source)

That growing sodium chlorate crystals from an aqueous solution under a **specified subtle external stimulus** — a 432 Hz tone, a proprietary "structurizer" disc, a "subtle energy" plate, a static magnet, or a pyramidal enclosure — changes the **proportion of d- to l-crystals** relative to an untreated control, with the strongest reported effect for the devices and the tone, and no effect for the magnet, the pyramid, or a commercial structured water.

**Honest framing:** this is a *claim* from a single-author 2021 campaign. The chemistry is real and the model system is well chosen; the statistical design (pooled crystals) is the weak point. We hold no brief — the test is the point, and a clean null is as useful as a positive.

## Why it matters

The shape-and-water rail is full of claims that a *shape* or a *device* influences matter. This dossier isolates the question into a form that is cheap, countable, and **blinded** — and it targets the exact design flaw that could manufacture the original result. If the d/l bias survives when the **dish** (not the pooled crystal) is the unit of analysis and the counter is blind to the condition, that is a real, home-reproducible effect with a published precedent. If it does not, the reported p-values dissolve, and the Yard has retired a positive with data rather than with argument.

Either outcome sharpens the rail. This is also the archive's cheapest decisive test in the crystallization domain: the entire experiment is a stock solution, a row of dishes, and two polarizing filters.

## Replicability: `home/homelab`

- Build cost: **~$80–200** (sodium chlorate, distilled water, dishes, polarizing filters or a cheap polariscope, a tone generator + speaker, a small magnet, cardboard/wood for a pyramid, a gram scale).
- Safety: **low–moderate** — sodium chlorate is a strong oxidizer. Keep quantities small, keep it away from organics, fuels, and open flame, and do not store the stock solution long-term. Eye protection when handling crystals.
- Accessibility: kitchen/lab bench; the fiddly part is reading crystal handedness, which is why the blinded counting step below matters.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

### Core

1. **Sodium chlorate (NaClO₃)** — lab-grade, ~50–100 g. *(Strong oxidizer; store and handle accordingly.)*
2. **Distilled water** and a clean glass beaker for the stock solution.
3. **A stock solution** at a fixed, recorded concentration (e.g. ~30 g / 100 mL) — one batch for the whole run, so every dish starts identical.
4. **Shallow crystallisation dishes** — identical, clean, flat-bottomed; at least 8 (one per condition per round, plus spares).
5. **A dust cover** for each dish (a paper tent) — dust nucleation is the main nuisance variable.

### Stimuli (one at a time)

6. **432 Hz tone:** a tone generator (phone app is fine) + a small speaker placed at a fixed distance and orientation.
7. **Static magnetic field:** a small permanent magnet (record its surface field), placed at a fixed position under/next to the dish.
8. **Pyramid:** a wooden or cardboard pyramidal enclosure sized to cover one dish (this is the rail's canonical shape claim — and it was *null* in the original).
9. **Device stand-in:** a "structurizer"-type disc or plate, if the replicator has one. **Record exactly what it is.** If none is available, this arm is simply omitted — do not substitute a random object and call it a device.

### Measurement

10. **A polariscope** — two polarizing filters with a light source between/below, or a cheap student polarimeter. This is the instrument that distinguishes d- from l-crystals.
11. **A camera or loupe** — to count crystals reliably and to keep a photographic record per dish.
12. **Labels/codes** — for blinding (see Protocol step 4).

## Protocol (pre-register before round 1)

1. **Pre-register the sheet.** Write down: the stock concentration and its preparation time, the dish type and the volume per dish, the incubation temperature and duration (24–30 h, isothermal), the number of rounds, the **unit of analysis (the DISH)**, the primary statistic (mean per-dish d-fraction per condition), and the operator's name. Photograph it before starting.
2. **Prepare the stock and aliquot.** Mix one stock solution; aliquot an **equal volume** into each dish. Randomise dish positions on the bench (so no condition sits permanently in one spot).
3. **Run one condition per round.** Each round has one **control** dish and one **stimulus** dish (or more, if you have the bench space), all from the same stock. Expose the stimulus dish for the full incubation; leave the control untouched. **Rotate which bench position holds the control** between rounds.
4. **Blind the counter — this is the whole design.** An assistant labels every dish with a **random code** and records the code→condition key separately; the counter (the person reading handedness) must not see the key. Count the crystals per dish **by code**, recording d-count and l-count, and photograph each dish. **Only after all counts are logged** does the assistant unblind.
5. **Count handedness.** Under the polariscope, classify each crystal as d or l. *(If using the polariscope, this is the step to practise first on known crystals before the run — an operator who cannot reliably classify a control crystal will produce noise, not data.)*
6. **Repeat.** Run **at least 6 rounds per condition**, on at least 3 different days. A single dish is one measurement, not a result.
7. **Analyse at the dish level.** For each dish, compute the d-fraction. Then compare the **distribution of per-dish d-fractions** between control and stimulus (mean ± SD; a simple two-sample test). **Do not pool crystals across dishes to inflate n** — that is the design flaw this protocol exists to avoid. Report both the per-dish result and, for comparison, the pooled-crystal result, so the difference between the two is visible.
8. **Report the nulls honestly.** If a condition shows nothing, that is a result. The original campaign's most useful output was its nulls (magnet, pyramid, structured water); a home run that reproduces *those* nulls is already informative.

## Pass / fail criteria (pre-registered)

- **PASS — the effect survives proper analysis.** A stimulus produces a per-dish d-fraction distribution **significantly different from the control's** (by a pre-declared test, at the pre-declared unit of analysis), reproducibly across ≥6 rounds and ≥3 days, **with the counter blind**. Report the effect size, not only the p-value.
- **PASS — the null (the expected, and still useful, result).** No condition differs from control at the dish level, even though pooled-crystal counting may look "significant." **This retires the reported p-values as a unit-of-analysis artefact** and is a clean, publishable null. **(Skeptic's Star.)**
- **FAIL (design, not claim):** the counter was not blind; the key was visible; the stock concentration or aliquot volume varied between dishes; the unit of analysis was switched after seeing the data; the polariscope classification was not validated on known crystals first; fewer than 6 rounds per condition; a single day's data.
- **VOID:** the stock solution was contaminated or evaporated to dryness mid-run; a dish was disturbed or knocked; the temperature was not held constant. **A VOID is not a NULL.**

## Notes

This dossier tests the **chirality-bias** claim specifically — not "does water remember," and not the mechanism. The design's single most important feature is the **unit of analysis**: the original campaign pooled crystals, which makes the effective n look large; fixing the *dish* as the unit is the honest test, and the pooled-vs-per-dish comparison is itself the experiment's most interesting output. A negative result here is a complete result, and a cheap one.
