## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source asserts that a **short, dosed static magnetic field applied to skin areas rich in reflex points produces a reproducible, short-lived restructuring of the vegetative-humoral and cardiovascular systems**, measurable as a change in blood pressure, pulse, skin temperature and electrocutaneous resistance — and that the size and direction of that response depend on the state of the Earth's magnetosphere at the moment of testing.

The source's own method, quoted because it is the whole basis of the card:

> "The method is based on the influence, for 10 minutes, of a PMF of elastic magnetic carriers of grade ALM … with limiting values of the testing signal from **15 to 25 mT** on areas of the skin covering with an extensive representation of reflexotherapy points (RP). The influence leads to a short-term restructuring of the vegetative-humoral and cardiovascular systems, the dynamics of whose parameters is recorded by heart rate, AP values, skin temperature and electrical resistance at the RP."

The procedure, quoted:

> "After a 15-minute stay of the subject, oriented face to the north, in a sitting position, under conditions of comfortable air temperature, measurements are made of the initial values of the recorded physiological parameters. Then the subject's feet (without shoes and socks) are placed on **2 prepared magnetic pads of size 250×125 mm**. **Magnetic headphones are placed on the auricles**, after which the time of the start of the magnetic load is recorded. During this period, the registration of the selected parameters is repeated three times, in one and the same sequence. After the cessation of the magnetic load … the physiological parameters are recorded two more times (during recovery)."

The control, quoted — and this is the reason the card exists:

> "The control studies were conducted according to the same scheme as in the magnetic testing, but instead of magnetic carriers, **a material of the same chemical composition as the base of the magnetic carriers, but devoid of magnetic properties**, was used on the foot pads and in the headphones. **There were no visual differences in the technical means used in the control and the experiment.** In the control series … the variant of using control or experimental magnetic means was determined by the researcher's assistant and recorded in the protocol. **The researcher did not know during the work which of the two variants was applied in each specific case, nor did the subject know it, i.e. a double-blind method was applied. 93 control measurements were made.**"

The result, quoted:

> "If in the experimental series, with the use of magnetic carriers, significant differences of the parameters before and after the influence were noted (**AP: 128.6 ± 2.4 – 121.8 ± 2.3 mm Hg**; ITP: 3.1 ± 0.2 – 2.3 ± 0.2 µA; T°: 28.9 ± 0.2 – 29.5 ± 0.1°; P < 0.05), then in the control there are no significant differences (**AP: 126.4 ± 2.6 – 122.7 ± 2.4 mm Hg**; ITP: 3.3 ± 0.2 – 3.1 ± 0.2 µA; T° 28.7 ± 0.2 – 29.0 ± 0.2)."

The geomagnetic dependence, quoted (Part 4):

> "The magnetotropic reactions of a healthy person, in the assessment by the dynamics of BP, prove to be dependent on the background magnitudes of solar activity during the examination."
> "The number of hypertensive magnetotropic reactions in healthy people may depend on the state of the Earth's magnetosphere at the moment of magnetic testing …"
> "Systolic BP more often rose (40.8%) when magnetic testing was conducted while the planet was in the (+) sector of the IMF; diastolic BP more often rose (48.6%) during periods when the magnetic action was applied while the Earth was passing through the boundary of two sectors."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** with the subject blind and the operator blind, does a 10-minute static magnetic load on the feet and auricles change resting blood pressure relative to an identical-looking non-magnetic sham — and does the size of that change track the day's geomagnetic activity?

## Honest status of the claim

**This is a real controlled experiment reported by a single institute in its own journal, and it is stronger than most of the queue's sources — and it is still a single lab.**

1. **The source ran a double-blind sham control and reports it.** 93 control measurements, an assistant who coded the arm, neither researcher nor subject knowing which was applied, and a sham made of the same base material with the magnetic properties removed. That is the design the queue usually has to *add* to a claim. Here the source supplies it.
2. **The source's own numbers are small and the control arm moved too.** Experimental systolic AP fell **6.8 mmHg** (128.6 → 121.8, p<0.05). Control systolic AP fell **3.7 mmHg** (126.4 → 122.7, *not* significant). **The claimed effect is the difference between two falls, not a fall against no change.** A 15-minute seated rest lowers blood pressure on its own; the source's own control shows it. Any home test must therefore be **within-subject and paired**, not a before/after in one arm.
3. **The venue is the institute's own journal.** *Physics of Consciousness and Life, Cosmology and Astrophysics* is published by the same Novosibirsk school (ISRICA) that produced the work. It is a niche Russian venue, not an independent peer-reviewed journal in the ordinary sense. The 2016 paper was also given as a report to the 15th International Congress on Circumpolar Health — a real congress, which is a point in its favour.
4. **The population is specific and not a family in a temperate suburb.** The field work was on 120 men aged 18–20 at Dikson (73°30′ N) during polar nights, in their first year of Arctic adaptation; the method paper adds Kamchatka, the Kursk Magnetic Anomaly and Western Siberia. The source itself reports that the response differs by *where the subject was born* and by the geomagnetic situation of their **prenatal** period — a claim the card does not test and cannot.
5. **The source's own mechanism is asserted, not measured.** "Non-specific electromagnetic receptors — acupuncture points" is offered as the transducer. The card does not need the mechanism to run the test.
6. **The source's own downstream claim is extraordinary and un-replicated.** The same programme reports that drinking water exposed in a portable geomagnetically-shielded device ("TRODR") lowers systolic AP (127.3 → 117.4, p<0.05, n=48). That is a separate claim, it is not this card, and it is recorded here only so the reader knows what the same lab reports next to the result being replicated.
7. **The mainstream position.** Static magnetic fields at 15–25 mT (roughly 150–250 gauss — the surface field of ordinary flexible magnetic sheet) are far below any established acute hazard threshold, and the mainstream literature has **no established acute effect of such fields on blood pressure**. Magnet-therapy trials generally report effects indistinguishable from placebo. The card's expected null is therefore the mainstream prediction, and a null is a complete result.
8. **The archive holds no claim-status record for magnet therapy or for magnetotropic reactions.** Recorded as a gap, not a backlog item.

**What the source DOES give, and why the card exists:**

- A **named apparatus** — two magnetic pads of stated size (250×125 mm) and a stated field range (15–25 mT), applied to the feet and the auricles.
- A **named procedure** — 15 minutes seated facing north, baseline, 10-minute load, three measurements during, two during recovery.
- A **sham that a household can actually build** — the same base material with the magnetic properties removed, visually identical. Flexible magnetic sheet and plain rubber sheet of the same thickness and colour are exactly that pair.

**The ordinary mechanisms that must be excluded before the claim is interesting:**

| Ordinary mechanism | What it predicts | The control that separates it |
|---|---|---|
| **Rest and habituation** | BP falls over 25 minutes seated regardless of any magnet (the source's own control fell 3.7 mmHg) | **a paired within-subject design** — the same person on a magnet day and a sham day, and the *difference of the differences* |

## Why it matters

Blood pressure is the single most-measured number in a household that owns a cuff, and the claim that a **passive object with no power, no moving parts and no consumable** changes it is one of the oldest claims in the biomagnetism corpus — and one of the most commercially active (magnetic bracelets, pads, mattresses, insoles). The source here is unusual: it does not sell a device, it publishes a *method*, and it ran the sham control itself. If the paired, blinded home test finds nothing, the family has measured a null on the cheapest version of the claim and knows what a magnetic bracelet is worth. If the magnet day falls significantly more than the sham day, reproducibly, in more than one person, then a 20 mT static field has done something to a cardiovascular endpoint that the textbooks say it cannot — and that is worth chasing. Either outcome is a complete result.

## Protocol

**Pre-registration (write and photograph before the first session):** the number of participants and sessions; the pad size and the field measured at the pad surface with a phone magnetometer or a gauss meter; the sham material; the coding method; the order randomisation; the measurement schedule; the primary endpoint; the noise floor; the pass/fail thresholds; the exclusion list.

**Entry conditions (hard):**
- **Adults only.** The source's population was adults; a child needs an age-appropriate cuff and a different consent path. Do not run this on children.
- **Exclusions (absolute):** anyone with a **pacemaker, implanted electronic device** (insulin pump, cochlear implant, neurostimulator, cardiac monitor), **metal implant or metal fragment** (including in the eye), or any implanted magnetic material. Magnets near these are a real hazard and this test is not worth it for them.
- **Keep the pads away from bank cards, watches, phones, hard drives and mechanical devices** during storage and use.
- **A blood-pressure cuff is required.** A wrist cuff is acceptable only if the same cuff is used for every reading; an upper-arm cuff is better.
- **One person must be able to code and hold the key** — the person who sets the pads must not take the readings, and the person taking the readings must not know which pads are in use.

**Materials:**
- **Magnetic pads:** flexible magnetic sheet (the rubber-bonded ferrite sheet sold as "magnetic sheet" / "fridge magnet sheet"), cut to two foot pads of **250 × 125 mm** and two auricle pads. Measure the surface field with a phone magnetometer app or a gauss meter and record it — the target is **15–25 mT (150–250 gauss)** at the surface. If the sheet measures far outside that band, say so in the report; the card is testing the source's stated range, not "a magnet".
- **Sham pads:** plain rubber or neoprene sheet of the **same thickness, colour and flexibility**, cut to the same sizes. If a visually identical non-magnetic sheet cannot be found, wrap both sets in the same opaque fabric sleeve — the requirement is that the subject and the reader cannot tell them apart, and the report must say how that was achieved.
- **A home blood-pressure cuff**, and optionally an infrared thermometer for the skin-temperature arm.

**The session (per the source, adapted for home):**
1. Sit for **15 minutes**, facing north if convenient, at a comfortable temperature. No caffeine, nicotine or exercise for 30 minutes before.
2. Take the **baseline**: three BP readings one minute apart on the same arm, plus pulse. Record all three, not the average alone.
3. The setter places the pads — **feet (bare) on the two foot pads, auricle pads on the ears** — from the coded envelope. Record the start time.
4. Take **three readings during the 10-minute load**, at the same intervals each session.
5. Remove the pads. Take **two readings during recovery** (at 2 and 5 minutes).
6. Record the **day's geomagnetic index** (the planetary Ap or Kp index for that date — a free public daily value) and the time of day.
7. **Repeat on a second day with the other pad set**, order randomised, same time of day, same reader, same cuff.

**Design:** each participant completes **at least two magnet sessions and two sham sessions** (four sessions minimum), order counterbalanced. With more than one participant, randomise the order across people so that "second session" is not confounded with "sham".

**Measurable outcome (primary):** the **paired difference of differences** — (mean systolic BP during the load − baseline) on magnet days, minus the same quantity on sham days, per participant, with the session count shown. Secondary: the same for diastolic BP, pulse and skin temperature; and the **relationship between the size of the magnet-day response and the day's geomagnetic index**.

**Noise floor:** before the first session, take **ten repeated readings** on one arm without any pads and record the standard deviation. That number is the threshold below which a difference is not measurable with this cuff.

## Pass / fail

- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, mainstream result):** the paired difference of differences is **within the measured cuff noise floor**, or is not consistent in sign across participants and sessions; the sham arm falls as much as the magnet arm; the skin temperature is unchanged in both. **A passive magnetic pad does not move blood pressure** — the family has measured the null on the cheapest version of the claim. *Skeptic's Star.*
- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** the magnet arm falls **significantly more than the sham arm** in a paired, blinded, counterbalanced design, the difference exceeds the measured noise floor, it **reproduces on a second magnet session with the order reversed**, and it appears in more than one participant. The geomagnetic-dependence arm is reported either way.
- **FAIL:** the subject or the reader knew which pads were in use; the pads were visibly different; the order was not counterbalanced; the cuff, arm, posture or time of day changed between sessions; or a result was read from a single session.
- **VOID:** the cuff failed the noise-floor check; a participant was excluded mid-series; or the pad field was measured far outside 15–25 mT and the family chose not to proceed. **A VOID is not a NULL.**
