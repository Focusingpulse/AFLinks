## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source asserts that the **Hartmann network** — a grid of dowsed "earth radiation" bands covering the whole land surface — has **nodes** at the crossings of its lines, and that a building sited on one of those nodes has its **radio, TV and mobile-phone communications "altered" or made impossible.**

The source's own geometry, quoted:

> "This 'network' is oriented towards the cardinal points: in the North-South direction there is an invisible 'wall' every 2 metres, and in the East-West direction there is one every 2.50 m. This at our latitudes; indeed the E/W side tends to narrow gradually towards the North and reaches its maximum extent at the equator. Between these geometric lines there is a neutral or microclimatic zone, without disturbances. **The width of the bands is 21 cm.**"

> "The points of intersection between the North-South and East-West lines determine a telluric crossing or node with a pathogenic tendency."

The source's own node claim, quoted:

> "On these nodes, defined as 'geopathic points', one observes an increase in radioactivity (gamma rays), which in some cases (a fall in atmospheric pressure) can reach up to 100%."

> "The effect of these telluric nodes is further amplified when they are situated above an underground watercourse, or geological or magnetic anomalies of artificial origin (reservoirs, cars, metal frames, etc.)."

> "For example, if in an old house a central heating system is installed or water pipes are laid, the primary effects are multiplied by 20 or 30 times."

**The claim this card tests, quoted in full — and the crux of the dossier:**

> "Physical Science and Medicine deny the existence of the Hartmann network, but eighty per cent of American, English and French architects never build a multi-storey building on a 'disturbed' point of the Hartmann network, **because by building a house on a 'nodal' point all radio, TV and mobile-phone communications would be irremediably 'altered' or made impossible.** Some illnesses, such as certain osteomas in the initial phase and certain sarcomas, could be worsened by certain nodal points of this network. This 'network' therefore concerns not only Physics, but also Medicine and Architecture."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** with the measurer blind to which points the dowser rated as nodes and which as neutral, does the received level from a fixed transmitter read **lower at dowsed nodes than at matched neutral points** — by more than the receiver's own measured noise floor?

## Honest status of the claim

**This is a claim stated as a fact about the built environment, offered as the reason for a professional practice — and the source gives it no instrument, no number, no site and no citation.**

1. **The claim is asserted, not measured.** The source names no receiver, no station, no distance, no reading and no study. It is a sentence in a practitioner synthesis, offered as the explanation for what architects supposedly do. **There is nothing to replicate in the source — only a claim to test.**
2. **The network itself has never been instrument-detected.** The queue's own grid card (`2026-09-17-dossier-021`) records the honest ledger: the Hartmann grid has never been found by an instrument, and the archive holds **controlled dowsing nulls** — the ETH Zürich 1946 trials and the Wunder 2003 series. The node's *location* is the dowser's judgement, not a measurement.
3. **The ordinary explanation is complete and local.** Radio-frequency propagation indoors is governed by metal, water, geometry and reflections. A point where a signal drops is, in the ordinary case, a point near a pipe, a rebar cage, a wet patch, a corner or a metal frame. **The source itself says metal frames, cars, reservoirs and water pipes amplify its effects** — so a "node" that reads differently on a radio may simply be a place with metal or water in it, which the source would also call an amplifier.
4. **The source's own mechanism does not reach the claim.** The stated node effect is an increase in **gamma radiation** (ionising, ~10¹⁹ Hz) under falling atmospheric pressure. The claim is about **radio, TV and mobile-phone** signals (~10⁵–10⁹ Hz). Nothing in the source connects an ionising-radiation anomaly to a communications failure, and no mechanism is proposed.
5. **The source's own framing concedes the mainstream position** — "Physical Science and Medicine deny the existence of the Hartmann network" — and then rests the claim on an uncited statistic about architects.
6. **The archive holds no claim-status record for the Hartmann network, the Curry grid, or geopathic stress.** Recorded as a gap, not a backlog item.

## Why it matters

Every geopathic claim sold to a household rests on something a person felt. **This one rests on something a radio can see** — and it is the source's own stated reason that architects avoid building on nodes. If a family measures a radio at a dowsed node and at a matched neutral point, blind, and finds nothing, they have retired a piece of building folklore with a device they already own, and they have a measured noise floor for their own house. If they find a repeatable difference at points with no metal or water near them, that is the first instrumented result of its kind in the archive — and it immediately needs a second home and a second dowser before it means anything. Either outcome is a complete result.

## Protocol

**Pre-registration (write and photograph before the first measurement):** the transmitter (a named station or a fixed access point at a fixed distance), the receiver and its app version, the height, the orientation, the integration time, the number of points, the dowser's identity, the coder's identity, the assignment method, the primary endpoint, the noise floor, the pass/fail thresholds, the exclusion list.

**Entry conditions (hard):**
- **The receiver must report a NUMBER** — dBm, RSSI, SNR or a signal-quality figure — not bars or a subjective "good/bad". If the device only shows bars, use a cheap radio with a signal-strength readout or a USB SDR instead. **If no numeric reading is available, the card cannot be run.**
- **The transmitter must be fixed and outside the house** — a broadcast station, or an access point in a neighbouring building. A transmitter inside the house measures the house's own geometry, not the ground.
- **The dowser must not be the person who measures**, and must mark the floor and rate the points **before** the coder assigns labels. The marks are photographed and the ratings are written down and sealed.
- **A second person (the coder) must hold the key** — the coder pairs each node with a neutral point matched for distance to walls, height and orientation, labels the pairs in a random order, and keeps the key. The measurer never sees it.
- **Every point must be surveyed for metal and water within 1 m** (a magnet, a visual check, a look at the plumbing) and the result logged per point. **A point with metal or water within 1 m is not excluded — it is flagged, and the analysis is reported both with and without it.**

**Materials:**
- A phone with a signal-strength app, or a cheap AM/FM radio with a numeric readout, or a USB SDR — **one device, used for every reading.**
- A tape measure, chalk or floor markers.
- A metal object as a positive control (a baking tray, a filing cabinet, a steel plate).
- A magnet (to find hidden metal), a notebook, two envelopes and a written key.

**The sessions:**
1. **Instrument check and noise floor (before anything else).** At a fixed reference point, take **10 readings** over 60 s and record the mean and the standard deviation. **That standard deviation is the noise floor — nothing below it counts.** Then hold the receiver against the metal object and confirm the reading **falls** clearly. If the metal does not change the reading, the receiver is not sensitive enough and the run is **VOID**, not a null.
2. **The dowser marks the floor.** Following the source's own geometry (bands every ~2 m north-south and ~2.50 m east-west, 21 cm wide), the dowser finds and marks the lines and their crossings, and marks matched neutral points at the centres of the cells. Each point is rated **node** or **neutral** and the ratings are sealed.
3. **The coder codes the points.** From the sealed ratings, the coder pairs each node with a neutral point matched as closely as the room allows for distance to walls, height and orientation, labels the pairs in a random order, photographs each pair from the same angle, and writes the key.
4. **Measure blind.** At each point, at a fixed height (e.g. 1 m) and a fixed orientation, take **5 readings over 60 s** and record the value, the time, and the metal/water survey. Re-measure the reference point after every 4 points.
5. **Repeat on three days, at the same time of day.** The marks stay where they are; the order is re-randomised each day.
6. **Open the key and compare.** Report the mean received level at nodes vs neutral points, per point and pooled, against the noise floor, split by the metal/water flag.

**Measurable outcome (primary):** the **difference in mean received level (dBm or equivalent) between dowsed nodes and matched neutral points**, per point and pooled, against the **measured noise floor**, with the metal/water flag shown. Secondary: the metal positive control; and whether the difference reproduces on all three days.

**Noise floor:** the standard deviation of the 10 reference-point readings at a fixed spot with nothing changed. **A difference smaller than this is not measurable with this receiver in this house.**

## Pass / fail

- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, mainstream result):** dowsed nodes and matched neutral points differ by **less than the measured noise floor**, or the difference is not consistent in sign across points and days; the metal positive control works; the reference point is stable. **A dowsed node does not change what a radio receives** — the family has retired the claim in their own house, and has a measured noise floor. *Skeptic's Star.*
- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** nodes read **consistently lower** than matched neutral points, the difference **exceeds the measured noise floor**, it **reproduces on all three days**, and it **persists at points with no metal or water within 1 m**. A second home and a second dowser are then required before any conclusion.
- **FAIL:** the receiver reports only bars or a subjective reading; the points were not matched for distance to walls, height or orientation; the measurer knew which point was which; the transmitter moved or changed power; the height or orientation changed between points; or a result was read from a single day.
- **VOID:** the instrument check did not respond to the metal object; the noise floor could not be measured; the dowser's ratings were not sealed before the coder assigned labels; or the marks were moved between days. **A VOID is not a NULL.**
