## The claim (as asserted by the source)

**"Water held for 10–15 days in a strong inhomogeneous magnetic field (0.5 T) with access to scattered sunlight becomes a source of an unusual radiation when irradiated with a low-power visible laser."** The radiation is claimed to differ "sharply in its manifestation from the radiations, fields and particles known to date," to have rest mass (hence a magnetic moment), and to register on photographic film **through an aluminium-foil wrap** — i.e. it is claimed to be unscreened by ordinary media.

The source's own apparatus, verbatim in substance:

1. **Hold.** 0.1 L of water in a glass cuvette, held in a **0.5 T** magnetic field for **10–15 days**, with access to **scattered solar radiation**.
2. **Irradiate.** The cuvette is removed and irradiated with a **633 nm He–Ne laser** or a **644 nm semiconductor laser**, power **≤ 1.5 mW**, for **10–25 minutes**.
3. **Detect.** Photographic film (aerial "Isopanchrom", 1000 GOST, resolving power ≥ 110 mm⁻¹) is placed **in the path of the beam**, in a cassette **wrapped in aluminium foil** for light insulation, then developed by the standard method.
4. **Result.** Traces on the film: points 10–300 µm (circles/ovals), **regular chains with 30–300 µm periods**, half-rings/tori, "mushroom" shapes, up to 10 mm. The authors report the effect as "completely reproducible" under the right conditions.

**The source's own controls** (this is the part that makes the claim testable):

- Control films **outside the irradiation zone** — **no traces**.
- Films irradiated with water **not held in a magnetic field** — **no traces**.
- Films irradiated with water held in a **weak** magnetic field — **no traces**.

**The authors' interpretation** (recorded as theirs, not endorsed): the traces are microdroplets of a condensate of solar **"massive photons"** (~5 eV) accumulated in the water under the magnetic field; the microdroplets carry a magnetic moment (hence the regular chains); and the radiation is capable of **cold transmutation of nuclei** (a mass-spectrometric analysis of a "mushroom cap" is cited). The paper's reference list is conference proceedings and the Academy's own publications.

## Honest status of the claim

**Not independently replicated; the interpretation is not accepted physics.** The mechanism the authors propose (a massive-photon condensate, cold transmutation) has no standing in mainstream physics, the publisher is a fringe academy, and the related Urutskoev "strange radiation" claim (which the paper cites as its method source) is likewise unreplicated. The archive holds the *supporting* theoretical line (Etkin's alternative EM theory, the trinitas vein) but no independent replication of the film traces.

**What is nevertheless testable** — and this is the whole justification for the card — is the **observable**, not the interpretation: *does laser light passed through water that was held in a 0.5 T field leave traces on photographic film that matched controls do not?* The source supplies the three negative controls itself, so the card can be built as a direct replication of the source's own design rather than an invented one. The film trace is a **binary, archivable outcome** — far easier to score than a small count difference — and the whole rig is cheap.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the expected result is a **refutation** — no traces on any film, or traces appearing equally on the controls (in which case they are film handling, development chemistry, or the film's own grain). That is a complete, useful result and earns the Skeptic's Star. A clean result — traces on the magnetized-water film and on **none** of the controls, replicating across two sessions — would be the surprise, and would need a second independent family before it is believed.

## The apparatus

| Item | Spec | ~Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Magnet assembly** | A field of **0.5 T** over the water sample: two large N52 neodymium blocks in an attracting configuration with a gap sized to the cuvette, or a strong horseshoe/halbach array. Verify the field with a gaussmeter or a phone magnetometer app at the sample position. | $40–100 |
| **Cuvette** | A glass jar or cuvette, ~0.1 L, clear-sided, with a flat window for the beam | $5–15 |
| **Laser** | A **633 nm He–Ne** laser or a **644–650 nm** semiconductor laser, **≤ 1.5 mW** (a low-power red laser pointer; measure or de-rate a standard pointer) | $10–30 |
| **Film** | 35 mm photographic film, high sensitivity preferred (the source used aerial "Isopanchrom", 1000 GOST); loaded into a cassette **in darkness** | $10–20 |
| **Foil** | Aluminium foil to wrap the cassette (the source's light insulation — and the test of the "unscreened" claim) | $2 |
| **Development** | A changing bag + developer/stop/fixer, or a lab that develops 35 mm film | $20–50 |
| **Inspection** | A flatbed scanner (≥ 1200 dpi) + a cheap USB microscope or loupe for the fine structure | $0–50 |

**Total: ~$100–250.** Home/homelab; no mains wiring beyond a low-voltage laser supply, no chemicals beyond standard film developer.

**Why the controls are the whole card.** The claim is not "a laser marks film" — a laser *can* mark film directly. The claim is that a **radiation emitted by the water** marks a film that is **wrapped in foil**, i.e. shielded from the laser light. So the decisive controls are: (a) a foil-wrapped film in the same room but **out of the beam path** (does anything mark film in this room?); (b) a foil-wrapped film in the beam path with **water that was not held in a field** (is the field the cause?); and (c) a foil-wrapped film in the beam path with water held in a **weak** field (is the field strength the cause?). The source ran all three and reports no traces on any of them. The card replicates exactly that.

## The protocol

1. **Pre-register everything before anyone runs** — the field strength at the sample, the hold time, the laser wavelength and power, the film type and ISO, the irradiation time, the number of films per arm, the controls, the development procedure, the threshold, and the analysis — and photograph the sheet.
2. **Set up the hold.** Fill the cuvette. Place it in the 0.5 T field on a windowsill with **scattered sunlight** (not direct sun) for **10–15 days**. Record the daily field reading and the weather.
3. **Run the control holds in parallel** (same water source, same window, same 10–15 days): **Arm B** — no field (sunlight only); **Arm C** — field but **dark** (no sunlight); **Arm D** — **weak** field (< 0.1 T) + sunlight.
4. **Irradiate.** In a dim room, load a film into a cassette **in darkness**, wrap the cassette in aluminium foil, and place it in the laser beam path beyond the cuvette. Irradiate **10–25 min**. Do this for each arm (A, B, C, D) with a fresh film.
5. **Run the out-of-beam control.** A foil-wrapped film in the same room, **not** in the beam path, for the same duration.
6. **Develop** all films together, identically, by the standard method.
7. **Inspect.** Scan each film at high resolution; examine any marks with a loupe or USB microscope. Record the mark type (point, chain, torus, "mushroom"), size, and — for chains — the **period in µm**.
8. **Repeat** the whole run on a second batch of water, on a different day, with a second operator scoring the films blind to which arm is which.

**Measurable outcome:** the **presence or absence of traces** on each developed film, per arm, and the geometry of any traces (chain period in µm). Scored blind.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim survives — the surprise):** traces (points and/or regular chains with 30–300 µm periods) appear on the **magnetized-water + sunlight** film (Arm A) and on **none** of the controls (B, C, D, and the out-of-beam film), **replicating in a second session** — the observable is real at home scale and needs the taller follow-up (a shielded detector, an independent lab, a second family).
- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** **no traces on any film**, or traces appearing **equally across arms** — laser light through magnetized water does not mark film in a way that survives its own controls (Skeptic's Star).
- **FAIL (void — not a null):** the field at the sample is below 0.5 T; the hold is shorter than 10 days; the laser exceeds 1.5 mW or is not the stated wavelength; the film was fogged by light before or after the run; the films were not developed identically; or the foil wrap was not light-tight (the laser reached the film directly).
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** one session only; no out-of-beam control; no unmagnetized-water control; fewer than 2 films per arm; the scorer saw the arm labels.
- **ARTIFACT:** the marks track **film handling** (scratches, dust, static, fingerprints); **development chemistry** (streaks, chemical marks); the film's own **grain or perforation**; or the marks appear on the **out-of-beam control** film too (something in the room, not the water).

## Confounds this design controls (and the one it cannot)

| Confound | Control |
|---|---|
| The laser itself marking the film | The film is wrapped in foil; the out-of-beam control tests the room |
| Something in the room marking film | The out-of-beam control film |
| Water composition, not the field | Arm B (same water, no field) |
| Sunlight, not the field | Arm C (field, dark) |
| Field strength | Arm D (weak field) |
| Operator expectation | Blind scoring; pre-registered thresholds |
| **A home rig is not the source's rig** | **Not controllable — stated plainly below** |

**The limitation the card cannot remove.** A home rig is not the source's rig — the film type, the exact field geometry, and the laser differ. So a null here **bounds the claim at home scale**; it does not disprove an effect that requires the source's exact apparatus. Two things make the test worth running anyway: (1) the source claims the effect is **"completely reproducible"** under the stated conditions and gives **its own three negative controls**, so the card is a direct replication of a stated protocol, not an invention; and (2) the claimed outcome is **binary and dramatic** — a foil-wrapped film either carries traces or it does not — so a home rig that follows the stated conditions should see *something* if the claim holds.
