## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source states both the hypothesis and its own uncertainty, in its own words:

> *"De gedachte is dat de piramide zal gaan stralen als één van de zijvlakken is gericht op het aardmagnetische Noorden (vlakbij het geografische Noorden). Aangenomen wordt dat deze straling van elektromagnetische aard is, zodat er iets te meten zou kunnen zijn."*
> — "The idea is that the pyramid will radiate when one of its faces is oriented toward magnetic north (near geographic north). **It is assumed** that this radiation is electromagnetic in nature, so that there might be something to measure."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a pyramid form produce a measurable electrical signal at a foil held against one of its faces, and if so, is the signal **caused by the shape** — or is it just *an object near a foil*?

The source is explicit that the mechanism is an **assumption**, not a finding. That matters: the card tests the assumption, and the source's own posture is the one the card inherits.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **under $50** — four equal wooden sticks and string/tape (~$5), kitchen foil (~$2), a low-leakage capacitor (~$2), and the one real requirement: a **high-input-impedance voltage measurement** (~$15–40 for a FET-input buffer + a cheap DMM, or a used electrometer). A plain 10 MΩ DMM alone will bleed a small capacitor and must not be used bare (see Apparatus).
- Safety: **no hazard.** No mains wiring is touched; the measurement is passive. Keep mains-powered devices off and away during a run (they are a confound, not a danger). Do not connect anything to the mains.
- Accessibility: one afternoon to build, ~30 minutes per arm per session, three sessions.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Pyramid frame** — **four wooden sticks of equal length**, joined at their tips so the free ends form a square base (the source's own construction: *"vier houten stokjes van gelijke lengten, die aan de uiteinden met elkaar zijn verbonden"*). Record the stick length, the wood, and the mass. A 30–50 cm stick gives a workable frame.
- **Sham frame (arm D)** — the **same four sticks**, arranged as a **non-pyramid**: a flat square frame, or a cube frame. Same material, same mass, same distance from the foil; only the *form* differs. This is the card's shape control.
- **Foil** — one sheet of kitchen foil, fixed flat in front of one face of the pyramid, at a recorded distance. The same foil, the same position and the same distance in every arm.
- **Capacitor** — a **low-leakage** capacitor (a film type, ~10 nF–1 µF) from the foil to ground. Low leakage matters: a leaky capacitor's own drift is an artifact.
- **Ground** — a real earth ground (a rod driven into soil, or a known-good building ground). Record what you used; a floating "ground" is not a ground.
- **Voltmeter / buffer** — a **high-input-impedance** measurement: an electrometer (≥10 GΩ input), or a **FET-input op-amp buffer** (e.g. an LMC6001-class part) feeding an ordinary DMM. **A bare 10 MΩ DMM across a small capacitor will drain it (τ = RC) and read its own loading, not the foil.** State the input impedance in the log.
- **Compass** — to set arm A's face to magnetic north and arm B's to a recorded offset.
- **Thermometer + log sheet** — temperature is the single most likely confound; log it every reading.

## Protocol (four-arm shape test, pre-registered)

**Phase 1 — build and pre-register.**
1. Build the pyramid frame and the sham frame from the same four sticks (record length, mass, wood). Photograph both.
2. Set the foil, capacitor, ground and meter exactly as described. **Verify the meter's input impedance and write it down before you start.**
3. Write down, before any reading: the arm order (randomized), the sample interval, the run length, the temperature-matching rule, and the PASS/FAIL thresholds below.

**Phase 2 — run the four arms, back to back, same spot, same session.**
- **Arm A — aligned pyramid.** One face toward magnetic north (compass-verified). Log the foil-to-ground voltage at ≥1 sample/minute for ≥30 minutes.
- **Arm B — misaligned pyramid.** Same pyramid, rotated so **no face** points north (e.g. 45°). Same foil, same distance, same duration.
- **Arm C — no pyramid.** Pyramid removed entirely; foil, capacitor, ground and meter unchanged. This is the source's own control.
- **Arm D — sham shape.** The non-pyramid frame of the same sticks in the pyramid's place, same distance. This is the card's shape control.
- **Randomize the order** of A–D on each session so no arm is always first (first-run charge and warm-up are artifacts). **Repeat the whole set on ≥3 separate days.**

**Phase 3 — score.**
- Compare the voltage **drift and spread** across the four arms within each session and across sessions.
- **A real effect must be orientation-dependent AND shape-dependent:** A must exceed B, C **and** D, repeatably.
- **Then blind it once:** have someone else set the pyramid's orientation without telling you, and see whether you can read the orientation off the meter.

## Pass / Fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS:** the aligned pyramid (A) produces a repeatable voltage that is (i) absent in the no-pyramid arm (C), (ii) larger than the misaligned arm (B), and (iii) larger than the sham-shape arm (D) — reproduced in **≥2 of 3 sessions** with randomized arm order and temperature-matched arms. The claim survives its own shape control.
- **FAIL:** no repeatable difference between arms; **or** any difference that tracks temperature, foil handling, or mains on/off rather than orientation. A null is a complete result (Skeptic's Star): the design's first execution returns nothing.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** fewer than 3 sessions; meter input impedance too low to hold the charge; arms not temperature-matched; arm order not randomized; the foil position or distance changed between arms.
- **ARTIFACT (report whether or not it voids the run):** the reading follows the foil's handling or the room's temperature; the "effect" also appears in the sham-shape arm (D); the reading tracks mains-powered devices switching on and off; the reading follows the meter's own warm-up.
