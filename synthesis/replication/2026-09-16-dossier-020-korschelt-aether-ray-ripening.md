## The Claim

Oskar Korschelt, *Die Nutzbarmachung der lebendigen Kraft des Aethers*
(The Utilization of the Living Force of the Aether in Medicine, Agriculture,
and Technology), Berlin, 1892, reports:

1. **Wine/spirit ripening.** Sealed bottles of wine and spirits placed inside
   the sheet-metal tube of his "aether ray apparatus" (a galvanic element —
   chromic-acid or Meidinger cell — driving plates/wire spirals ~4 cm apart)
   undergo a **substantial improvement in taste** after 2-7 days of
   irradiation. A retired wine merchant (Herr Moths, formerly of a Berlin
   wine business) and later a circle of wine connoisseurs judged the treated
   samples better than untreated controls — "a considerable change — and
   indeed for the better," especially with the youngest Rhine wine, Bordeaux,
   and port. Korschelt frames the result explicitly as **commercial value**
   ("by how much — expressed in money — the treated samples... had become
   more valuable").

2. **Plant growth promotion.** Potted myrtle stocks in an unheated, dimly lit
   room (a few degrees above zero) developed fresh shoots while the constant
   galvanic elements were running; the shoots **withered each time the
   elements were switched off** and revived when they were switched on again.

**Key claim:** Irradiation with "long waves" alters a sealed beverage's taste
(and promotes plant growth) with no chemical additive and no heating.

**Korschelt's own caveats (from the source):** he distrusted his own palate
and used a professional taster; he noted that evaporation could confound open
cups and re-ran in **sealed** flasks; he could not measure the waves and could
only detect their presence via a subjective "healing-magnetic" sensation on
the palm.

## Protocol

### Materials

1. **Sheet-metal tube** — iron/steel tube, ~40-100 cm long, ~8-10 cm diameter,
   open at one end. (Korschelt used 1.5 m; shorter is acceptable for a first
   trial — document the length.)
2. **Two metal discs** — one fixed, one movable, sized to fit the tube bore.
   A perforated disc with a rod (as in the source) or plain plates.
3. **Low-voltage DC source** — a chromic-acid cell is the historical source;
   a **1.5-9 V battery or adjustable DC supply** is the safe modern substitute.
   Document voltage and polarity.
4. **Wire** — copper wire / wire spiral / spiral chain, wound radially on a
   wooden disc (for the plant-growth form, Fig. 10: a 10-point star).
5. **Beverage samples** — 3 matched pairs (6 bottles) of the same wine or
   spirit, ideally a young/raw one (young Rhine wine, Bordeaux, or raw spirit
   as in the source). Split into treated vs control by a coin flip.
6. **Cups and a taster** — 3 identical cups per trial, opaque or numbered.

### Apparatus (simplified Korschelt ray apparatus)

The source's apparatus is a sheet-iron can with a truncated-cone attachment
and a 1.5 m soldered tube; two discs inside are connected to the poles of a
galvanic element, and the "long waves" emerge at the open tube end. For a
home build:

- Mount the two discs inside the tube, ~4 cm apart.
- Connect the inner disc to the negative pole, the outer to the positive pole
  (Korschelt's arrangement).
- Run the wire spiral around/along the tube wall as the "ray apparatus" form.
- Place **sealed** bottles inside the horizontal tube for the irradiation.

### Procedure

1. **Baseline.** Photograph and label all 6 bottles. Record batch, vintage,
   and storage. Have the taster score each untreated sample once (this is a
   baseline, not the test).
2. **Split.** Randomly assign 3 bottles to TREATMENT, 3 to CONTROL (coin flip,
   recorded). Both sets stored identically except for irradiation.
3. **Irradiate.** Place treatment bottles inside the tube, energize the
   apparatus, and run **2-7 days** (run at least two durations, e.g. 2 days
   and 5 days, on separate pairs). Control bottles stay in the same room,
   same temperature, but outside the tube.
4. **Blind triangle test.** For each pair, pour three cups: two from one
   bottle, one from the other (assignment recorded but hidden from the taster).
   The taster identifies the odd cup and states which they prefer. Repeat
   **9 trials** (3 pairs × 3 repeats), re-randomizing each time.
5. **Plant arm (optional).** Set two matched potted plants (same species,
   same size). Run the Fig. 10 star-disc apparatus under the treatment plant
   continuously for 14 days; control plant identical but unenergized. Measure
   new shoot length and leaf count at day 0 and day 14.

### Controls

- **Negative control:** identical bottles, same room, no apparatus (rules out
  simple time-in-bottle / temperature effects).
- **Sham control:** apparatus assembled and bottles placed in the tube, but
  **power off** (rules out tube/placement effects).
- **Blind:** the taster must not know which cup is treated; the pourer records
  the answer key before tasting.

## Measurable Outcomes

### PASS Criteria
- Taster correctly identifies the odd cup in **≥ 6 of 9** triangle trials
  (p < 0.05, one-tailed binomial) **and** prefers the treated sample in a
  majority of trials.
- Plant arm: treatment plant shows **> 20% greater** new shoot growth than
  control over 14 days, repeated in a second run.

### FAIL Criteria
- Correct identifications at or below chance (3 of 9) — the taste effect is
  not detectable blind.
- Treated and control samples judged indistinguishable, or treated judged
  worse.

### INCONCLUSIVE Criteria
- 4-5 of 9 correct (near chance).
- Effect seen in one beverage type but not others, or in one run but not a
  repeat.
- Plant arm differs but within measurement noise.

## Alternative Explanations

1. **Expectation/suggestion:** the original 1892 trials were **unblinded** —
   the taster knew which samples had been treated. The blind triangle design
   in this protocol exists specifically to remove this. If the effect
   disappears under blinding, the original result was suggestion.
2. **Evaporation / oxidation:** open cups lose alcohol and change taste.
   Control: sealed bottles only; identical storage for both sets.
3. **Temperature:** apparatus may warm the samples. Control: sham run
   (power off) and record sample temperature before/after.
4. **Simple aging:** any wine changes over days. Control: negative control
   bottles aged identically.
5. **Electrolysis / metal ions:** a DC current in a metal tube could in
   principle produce trace ions. Control: sham run; sealed glass bottles
   (no direct contact with electrodes).
6. **Statistical:** small n produces false positives. Control: pre-register
   9 trials and the 6/9 threshold; do not stop early on a lucky run.

## Expected Results (Per Source)

Korschelt reports:
- Substantial taste improvement in sealed bottles after 2-7 days.
- Improvement most distinct in young Rhine wine, Bordeaux, and port; spirits
  (cognacs) pleased least.
- Raw potato spirit became drinkable after > 1 week.
- Plant shoots grew in cold/dark conditions only while the elements ran, and
  withered when switched off.
- No measurement of the "waves" was possible; detection was subjective.

Skeptical interpretation:
- No mainstream mechanism predicts taste change from this apparatus.
- The original trials were unblinded and the outcome subjective.
- The expected result under the blind protocol is **no detectable effect**.

## Theoretical Context

Korschelt's theory: galvanic elements decompose molecules into "aether
particles"; the ray apparatus aligns them; the aligned stream ("long waves,"
identified by him with Hertzian waves) acts on matter — ripening beverages,
promoting plant growth, and producing a felt sensation in humans. He cites
Reichenbach's *od* and Hertz's long-wave experiments as context.

This is a **pre-modern aether claim**. It is not endorsed here — it is a
historical claim with a concrete, falsifiable taste/plant protocol. The
1892 apparatus is cheap to reconstruct and the blind test is decisive for
the taste arm.
