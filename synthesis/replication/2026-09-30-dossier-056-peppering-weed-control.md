## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source's own text, quoted:

> "Peppering is a method used by biodynamic practitioners to clear an area of unwanted plants or animals that have become pests, reduce diversity and are resistant to other control measures. … By understanding the forces that feed growth and reproduction for any given species, biodynamics also shows us how to break or impair the connection between that species and the forces that sustain it. Instead of trying to kill those species that offend us, making a pepper using the organism's seed, exoskeleton or hide shuts down or breaks its connection with the in-streaming energies that sustain it and give it vigour. In the case of plants they dry up and struggle to grow or reproduce. In the case of animals, they tend to move out and go elsewhere."

> "For weeds, the basic method involves collecting seeds of the plant we intend to pepper and burning these, usually over hot a wood fire, so all that is left is ashes and perhaps a little charcoal which can be filtered out. It does not matter if a little of the wood used ends up in the ashes; the key is the seeds."

> "The history of making peppers successfully is spotty and sometimes a grower will make a pepper that works brilliantly wherever it is applied, while in other cases there is no noticeable effect and the weed, insect or animal pest seems to keep on thriving as before. … The fact that some peppers work extremely well shows the method does and can work. The fact that some peppers have failed to yield clear-cut results indicates there needs to be further research."

> "There are indications that peppering works better where the farm is already under biodynamic management and all the preparations are already at work. Also, for some reason if herbicides have been used recently, this seems to stimulate rather than retard weeds and success may not be immediate. … well-established weeds may require as much as four years to clear an area."

**The source's own internal controversy, stated in its own words:**

> "One of the controversies concerning peppering is whether the material should be burned completely to ash or simply pyrolized so the water, nitrogen and sulphur are driven off but the carbon framework is still intact. … Those who recommend an extremely hot fire and burning to ash don't seem to be the people who have the best record of success, though they seem to have had some successes. … Maybe the weed seeds were burned in the wrong Moon constellation for that weed. Or maybe they were burned completely to ash so the unique signature of their carbon atom arrangement was lost."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does spreading the ash (or char) of a target weed's own burned seeds over an area reduce that weed's germination and seedling establishment there — and if so, does the *specific* pepper do it, or would any char do it?

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the method and no home test of it.** A grep for `pepper`, `pest control` and `weed control` across `living-library/synthesis/quest-queue/` and `living-library/synthesis/replication/` returns no card and no dossier on this claim. The queue's food cards are all about **producing** growth — germination (014, 015), electro-culture (026), sowing timing (032), succession order (038), soil transplant (049) — or about **storing** food (010, 033, 043). **None tests a method for suppressing a plant or animal without chemicals.**

Four things the card must say out loud:

1. **The claim is a claim, and the source itself calls its record spotty.** The mechanism the source offers — severing a species' "connection with the in-streaming energies" — is not a mechanism any conventional account recognises. **The source does not hide this: it says plainly that some peppers work brilliantly and others have no noticeable effect, and that the method "needs further research."** The card's posture is the source's posture: test, do not endorse.
2. **The confound is obvious and must be controlled: any char or ash is not inert.** Wood ash is alkaline and adds potassium and calcium; char adds surface area and affects soil biology. **A card that spread only pepper ash and saw fewer weeds would be measuring a fertility effect, not a pepper.** That is why the design includes an **inert control (arm C)** — plain wood char/ash of the same mass — as the discriminator. This is the whole test.
3. **The source's own controversy is worth testing rather than picking a side.** The source reports that the practitioners who burn to white ash "don't seem to have the best record of success," and suggests the carbon framework may be the active part. **The card runs both a pyrolized pepper and a fully-ashed pepper** so the family gets its own answer to the controversy, instead of inheriting one.
4. **The effect, if real, is expected to be slow and cumulative.** The source says well-established weeds may take four years, and that peppering works better where biodynamic management is already in place. **The card is therefore a one-season test on a fast annual weed in a prepared bed**, and says plainly that a null result at home does **not** refute the method — it may need years, or a biodynamic-managed farm, or the correct moon constellation.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that the pepper arms are indistinguishable from the inert control, and the honest verdict is *"the pepper did nothing beyond what any char would do."* That is a complete, useful result — it tells a family not to spend a season on the method — and it is the result the source's own "spotty record" language predicts. A pepper that beats the inert control would be genuinely interesting and would need a second season and a second weed before any conclusion.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$0.** A fire (or a wood heater), a metal tin or a cast-iron pan with a lid (thistle-type seed heads pop), a fine sieve, a kitchen scale (optional, for matched masses), and four marked quadrats (string and pegs). A refractometer or a soil thermometer are optional refinements.
- Safety: **low, with one real hazard — the fire.** Burn outdoors where smoke can dissipate, in a hole or on a fire-safe surface, away from dry vegetation, with water at hand. Never burn in a confined space. Do not burn treated or painted wood. **The pepper itself is a fine dust** — wear a dust mask when sieving and spreading, and keep it away from eyes. Nothing here is caustic or pressurised.
- Accessibility: a weekend to collect seed and make the peppers; 6–8 weeks of weekly counting. The counting is the labour and the point.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Seed of ONE target weed** — a fast annual that reproduces by seed, is abundant on the site, and is easy to collect in bulk: **fat hen / lamb's quarters**, **chickweed**, or **purslane** are the good candidates. Collect mature, dry seed heads (or strip the whole seeding plant). Enough to yield a small pile of ash/char — a few grams is plenty.
- **A fire and a container** — an outdoor fire; a small metal tin, a cast-iron pan with a lid, or a wok. The source's own guidance: *"Dig a hole in the ground for the fire and use a wok or inverted plough disc to contain the material being burned. A hibachi will also work. … For small amounts of seeds or insects a small tin placed on hot coals in a wood heater is recommended."*
- **A lid** — for seed heads that pop (thistles and the like).
- **A fine sieve** — to remove unburned charcoal from the fully-ashed pepper.
- **A kitchen scale (0.1 g)** — to match the masses of the pepper and the inert control. **This is what makes the test fair.**
- **Plain wood char/ash for the inert control** — made the same way, from a non-target material (e.g., a handful of clean hardwood shavings), so it carries no target-weed seed.
- **Four quadrats** — four equal, adjacent, marked plots (e.g., 50 × 50 cm) on the same ground, in a weedy area where the target weed already grows. String, pegs, and a label for each.
- **A notebook** — the source's own instruction: *"Keep records and share these with the association to build a data base."*

## Protocol (four-arm pepper test, pre-registered)

**Phase 1 — choose, collect, pre-register.**
1. **Pick ONE target weed** that is a fast annual, abundant on the site, and easy to collect seed from. Photograph it and name it. **Write down its Maria Thun constellation** if you can (the source lists: Aries — canola/wild mustard; Taurus — wild carrot/Queen Anne's lace; Cancer — buttercups/creeping weeds; Leo — dock and many weeds; Virgo — thistles/morning glory; Libra — daisy family; Scorpio — nightshades; Sagittarius — **fat hen** and couch grass; Aquarius — shepherd's purse; Pisces — **chickweed**, grasses, wild oats).
2. **Collect the target weed's mature seed.** Enough for a small ash pile. Photograph the seed before burning.
3. **Write the pre-registration sheet** before running anything: the target weed, the four arms, the quadrat size and locations, the application rate (pepper mass per quadrat), the counting interval, the number of counts, and the criteria below. Photograph it.

**Phase 2 — make the peppers.**
4. **Arm A — pyrolized pepper.** Burn the target seed in the tin/pan until the water, nitrogen and sulphur are driven off but **the carbon framework is still intact** (black char, not white ash). This is the source's better-record method.
5. **Arm B — ashed pepper.** Burn a second, equal portion of the target seed **completely to white/grey ash**, then sieve out the unburned charcoal.
6. **Arm C — inert control.** Make a third portion of **plain wood char/ash** the same way (pyrolize or ash — match arm A or B, whichever you are comparing), from non-target material, and **match its mass to the pepper arms** on the scale.
7. **Arm D — untreated.** No application.

**Phase 3 — lay out and apply.**
8. Mark four equal adjacent quadrats on the same ground, in a weedy area where the target weed already grows. Randomise which quadrat gets which arm (draw lots), and record the assignment.
9. **Count the baseline** — the target-weed seedlings already present in each quadrat — and record it. **If the four quadrats differ by more than 30% at baseline, move the quadrats and start again.**
10. Spread each arm's material **evenly** over its quadrat at the pre-registered rate. (The source says the pepper may be applied dry, in fertiliser or seed, or as a D8 spray; this card uses the dry form for simplicity.)

**Phase 4 — count and score.**
11. **Count the target weed's new seedlings in each quadrat weekly for 6–8 weeks.** Count only the target weed, not other species. Photograph each quadrat at each count. If possible, have a second person re-count one week blind to the arm labels.
12. Score the pre-registered endpoint: **the target-weed seedling count in arm A (pyrolized pepper) and arm B (ashed pepper) against arm C (inert) and arm D (untreated).**

**Optional refinement (second round):** burn in the target weed's own constellation, or in a fire constellation (Leo or Sagittarius) in a fourth-quarter moon — the source's stated timing — and compare against a pepper burned at an arbitrary time. This tests the timing claim, which the source itself hedges (*"some growers have burned their insect pests with considerable success whenever they were active rather than looking for a perfect or 'correct' time"*).

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (the pepper works — surprising, requires a second season):** the target-weed seedling count in **arm A (pyrolized pepper)** is **≤ 50% of arm C (inert control)** and of arm D (untreated) over the count period, and the direction holds in a **second season or a second weed**. Report as a surprising result needing independent replication.
- **PASS (the ashed pepper works):** the same result in **arm B**. Report which form worked — that is the answer to the source's own controversy.
- **PASS (the pepper is refuted — the expected, complete result):** arms A and B are within the scatter of arm C, with no consistent reduction. The peppering method does nothing at home beyond what any char or ash does. Reported as the expected result, not a failure.
- **FAIL:** arm A or B is lower than arm D but **arm C (inert) is equally low** — the effect is the char/ash itself (fertility, alkalinity, light interception, soil biology), **not the pepper**. The pepper claim fails while the measurement succeeded.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** the four quadrats differed by more than 30% at baseline; the target weed failed to germinate in any quadrat (nothing to measure); a frost, drought, flood or pest hit one quadrat and not the others; the pepper and the inert control were not mass-matched; or fewer than four counts were taken.
- **ARTIFACT:** the material was applied unevenly (visible drift); the inert control was made from a different burn method than the pepper it is compared against; or the target weed was misidentified between the seed collected and the seedlings counted.

## Evidence

Photo of the target weed and its seed before burning + the **pre-registration sheet** (weed, arms, quadrat size and locations, application rate, counting interval, criteria) + the four quadrats marked and labelled before application + the baseline seedling count per quadrat + photos of each pepper (char and ash) and of the inert control + the matched masses on the scale + the weekly seedling counts per quadrat for every count + the same-angle photos of all four quadrats at each count + the blind re-count if taken + the full results table with the arm A-vs-C and B-vs-C ratios shown.

## Confounds (named in advance)

- **Char and ash are not inert — the central confound.** Wood ash raises pH and adds potassium and calcium; char changes soil surface area and biology. **Arm C (inert char/ash of matched mass) is the control that separates the pepper from the material.** Without arm C, a positive result means nothing.
- **Baseline patchiness.** Weeds grow in patches. Four adjacent quadrats can differ by more than the effect. **Count the baseline first and move the quadrats if they differ by more than 30%.**
- **The moon-timing claim is untested in the main arms.** The source says each weed has an optimum burn constellation. The card's main arms burn at whatever time is convenient; the timing is a stated refinement, not a controlled variable. **A null result therefore does not refute the timing claim.**
- **The effect is expected to be slow.** The source says well-established weeds may take four years and that peppering works better under existing biodynamic management. **A one-season null on a fast annual does not refute the method** — it is a null at home scale, in one season, on one weed.
- **Weed identification.** The pepper must be made from the same species whose seedlings are counted. Photograph both.
- **Application drift.** A fine dust blows. Apply on a still day, shield the quadrat edges, and photograph the applied quadrats.
- **The enthusiasm factor.** The source itself notes Steiner's remark that *"the enthusiasm one has for making the remedy is a factor in its success."* **The card treats that as an untestable claim and does not build it in** — but it is worth stating, because it is exactly the kind of claim that makes a null result easy to explain away.

## Verdict rules

Pepper arms below the inert control, repeated → **the pepper works** (surprising; needs a second season and a second weed). Pepper arms equal to the inert control → **the pepper is refuted; any effect is the char/ash** (the expected, complete result). Pepper arms below untreated but equal to inert → **the material, not the pepper** (the pepper claim fails). Baseline patchiness, no germination, or unmatched masses → **inconclusive, not a refutation**.
