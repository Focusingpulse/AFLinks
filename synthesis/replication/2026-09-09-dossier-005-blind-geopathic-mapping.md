## The Claim

Practitioners of radiesthesia (dowsing) claim that "geopathic stress zones" — earth-energy lines such as the Hartmann grid (claimed global N-S / E-W grid, ~2m spacing) and Curry lines (claimed diagonal grid) — can be located in a home with simple L-shaped rods, and that these zones matter for where people sleep, sit, and work. The Furter dissertation (UZH, veterinary medicine) took the claim seriously enough to run a cross-over study on 23 dairy cows with a "Geowave" neutralizer, and found only *tendencies* toward effects (milk somatic cell counts) — not significant results.

**Honest status of the claim:** contested. The best-known controlled test (Kassel, Germany, 1991; analyzed by J.T. Enright, *Skeptical Inquirer* 1999) found skilled dowsers performed at chance when test conditions were blinded. No geopathic grid has been detected by instruments. The Furter dissertation itself reports tendencies, not significance. This dossier does NOT assume the claim is true — the protocol is designed so the claim can pass OR fail cleanly.

**Claimant:** radiesthesia / geobiology tradition (Hartmann, Curry et al.); Furter (UZH) for the veterinary application
**Verification status:** unverified; prior controlled tests negative (Kassel 1991)

## The Protocol — blind inter-rater agreement with a built-in chance control

The testable core: **if the lines are real and detectable, two people mapping the same room independently — blind to each other — should agree on where the lines are.** If they can't agree, the "detection" is not detecting anything shared. A cross-room comparison gives the chance baseline for free.

### Materials (~$5)

1. **Two L-rod sets**: metal coat hangers or welding rod bent into L shape (long arm ~30cm, short handle ~10cm), one set per mapper. A drinking straw over the handle makes the rod swing freely.
2. **Tape measure** (or two, one per mapper)
3. **Graph paper + pencils** (one sheet per mapper per room)
4. **Painter's tape** (optional, for floor marking — but mappers must remove marks before the next mapper enters)

### Setup

Two family members ("Mapper A" and "Mapper B") who have NOT discussed the room. A third person (witness/referee, any age — good kid job) coordinates so mappers never see each other's maps or marks.

### Procedure

1. **Room 1 (test room):** Mapper A walks the room slowly with L-rods held level, relaxed. Wherever the rods cross, swing, or dip, they record the position as (x, y) coordinates measured from two fixed walls (e.g., "1.4m from north wall, 2.2m from west wall"). Continue until the whole floor is covered. Hand map to the referee. Mapper B repeats the identical procedure in the same room, with no access to A's map and any floor marks removed. Hand map to the referee.
2. **Room 2 (control room):** Both mappers repeat the same procedure in a different room, same blind conditions.
3. **Repeat:** at least 3 separate sessions (different days ideally) to average out good/bad days.

### Scoring (the referee does this)

1. **Same-room agreement:** overlay A's Room-1 map with B's Room-1 map. Count a "match" when a marked position from A lies within 30 cm of a marked position from B. Agreement % = matched A-positions ÷ total A-positions (and symmetric for B).
2. **Chance baseline (cross-room control):** overlay A's Room-1 map with B's *Room-2* map (different rooms — any agreement here is pure chance + shared bias, e.g. both mappers marking near doorways). Same 30 cm rule.
3. Also compute A-Room2 vs B-Room1 for a second chance estimate.

---

## Pass/Fail Criteria (pre-registered)

**PASS (supports the claim):**
- Same-room agreement ≥ 70% on average across sessions, AND
- Same-room agreement clearly exceeds the cross-room chance baseline (by ≥ 25 percentage points)

**FAIL (refutes, for this home and these mappers):**
- Same-room agreement within 10 percentage points of the chance baseline (the rods are finding nothing shared)

**INCONCLUSIVE:**
- Same-room agreement above chance but below 70%, or fewer than 3 sessions completed
- Only one mapper available (no inter-rater test possible — do not count solo mapping as evidence of anything)

**Honest note:** a PASS here would be a genuinely surprising result given the Kassel 1991 outcome — which is exactly why it's worth family-scale testing. A FAIL is a fully publishable validation (Skeptic's Star) and would honestly retire the "move the bed off the Hartmann line" advice for this family.
