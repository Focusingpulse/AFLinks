## The claim (as asserted by the sources)

Electro-culture is the claim that **applying electricity, magnetism or a shaped conductor to seeds, plants or soil changes how they grow** — faster germination, larger growth, higher yield. It is one of the oldest and most persistent claims in the archive: the source dates the first experiments to 1746 (Maimbray, Edinburgh) and the collection continues to the 1980s.

The source's own headline results, quoted as the source states them:

**Magnetism (the arm this dossier tests first):**
- **U.J. Pittman:** *"Many seeds germinate and grow about two times faster if they are exposed to the N pole of an artificial field before they are planted than they are not so treated — wheat seed in particular grows about 5 times as much in the first 48 hours as unexposed seed."* Effects persist in some seeds for at least 18 months. Maximum response at **0.5–100 Oersted applied for 240 hours**; responses are larger at 48, 144, 240 or 336 hours than at intermediate periods.
- **Albert R. Davis, US Patent 3,030,590:** treating above-ground seeds with the **South** pole of a magnet (1,500–2,500 gauss) increases germination and growth and enlarges leaves; for **beets, potatoes, carrots and turnips the North pole** gives the better result.
- **Pittman, potatoes:** excised, magnetically treated eyes yielded **17% more marketable tubers, 38.5% more weight** than untreated eyes.
- **Russian work:** wheat and barley seeds pre-magnetised (2,000 Oe) for 30 minutes *with the major axis aligned with the flux* germinate more vigorously; germination is **retarded** when aligned against the flux.
- **Strevoka et al.:** 60 Oe increased growth rate of beans, cucumbers, lupines, maize and rye — but rye was unaffected by a 100 Oe field.

**The Lakhovsky oscillator circuit (the second arm):**
- *"In 1924, Georges Lakhovsky devised his Oscillator Circuit, a one-turn copper coil with overlapping ends separated by a gap. Capacitance generates oscillating currents that benefit the plants. The ring is supported by an insulator such as a plastic rod. This extremely simple arrangement stimulates plant growth."*

**The rest of the collection, for context (not tested by this card):** Laemstrom's aerial system (1885, ~40% average increase, up to 70%, within 8 weeks; raspberry +95%, carrot +125% — but cabbage, turnips and flax grew *better* without electrification); Blackman (~50% average increase with 60 V DC / 1 mA through three 32-ft wires); the 1918–21 British farmers' electrified seed soak (~2,000 acres, 25–30% yield increase, reported in *Scientific American*, 15 Feb 1919); Grandeau & Leclerq's 1898 wire-net shielding experiment (uncovered plants grew 50–60% better than shielded); the 1964 USDA tree electrode trial (60 V DC, foliage +300% in a year); and the electrogenics machine (US Patent 4,302,670).

**Honest status of the claim.** Electro-culture is a **250-year accumulation of unreplicated and partly contradictory results**, compiled by an enthusiast (Robert A. Nelson / rexresearch) rather than produced by a controlled programme. The source itself records a large counter-example in the same paragraph as its best positive result: Laemstrom's crops of cabbage, turnips and flax grew *better without electrification*. That is the signature of a real-but-conditional effect, or of an artefact — and the source cannot tell you which.

**Why this arm is worth testing anyway, stated plainly.** Pre-sowing magnetic treatment of seeds is the **one part of electro-culture that is not wholly fringe**: it has a modern mainstream agricultural research literature (usually called *magnetopriming*), and the effect direction reported there is generally the same as the archive's. So the honest question for a family is **not "does anything happen" but "how big is the effect, and does it reproduce in a kitchen"** — a magnitude-and-reproducibility question, which is exactly what a home test can answer and what the archive has never answered.

**Provenance note, stated up front:** the magnetopriming literature is cited here from general knowledge, **not** from a document read in this run. The archive documents above are the ones actually read. Treat the magnetopriming sentence as a pointer to go and check, not as a verified citation.

**Under-promise, stated plainly.** A kitchen test with a couple of neodymium magnets and two trays of seeds will not resolve a 5% effect. It **can** resolve a large one — the source claims 2× germination speed and 5× 48-hour growth, which is enormous and would be visible immediately. If the effect is that large, a family will see it in two days. If it is not, the family will see parity, and that is the honest negative the archive wants.

## Why it matters

This is the **foundation claim of an entire collection the Vault already holds** — the ElectroCulture library (`doc:1812` and its fifteen siblings), a body of material the archive carries with no replication attached to any of it. Every electro-culture document in the Vault assumes the same premise: that a static field or a shaped conductor changes plant growth. Nothing in the archive has ever tested it at home scale.

It is also the cheapest and fastest test the engine has yet emitted. The primary arm costs two magnets and two trays of seeds, and the first meaningful number arrives in **48 hours** — which matters for a family game: a quest that pays off in two days gets run, and a quest that pays off in six months does not.

And it is real literacy: a family that runs this learns what a control is, what a sham is, why the person reading the ruler must not know which tray is which, and how a 250-year-old claim survives on anecdotes. That lesson transfers to every other card in the queue.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build cost: **~$20–40** (two or three neodymium disc magnets, one non-magnetic steel slug of the same size for the sham, seed trays, potting mix, a ruler, a phone camera; the Lakhovsky arm adds ~$5–10 of copper wire and a plastic rod).
- Safety: **no mains, no chemicals, no heat.** Two cautions only: neodymium magnets are strong enough to pinch fingers and to wipe cards/phones — keep them apart and away from devices, and keep them away from small children who might swallow them (swallowed magnets are a surgical emergency). The Lakhovsky coil is a passive piece of wire with no power source.
- Accessibility: the seed arm is an afternoon to set up and 48 hours to a first reading — a good quest for a young child. The coil arm is a 10-minute build plus 4–6 weeks of measuring.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

**Arm A — magnetic seed treatment (primary, fast):**
- **Seeds:** one fast, uniform germinator. **Wheat, radish, or mung bean** are ideal — cheap, uniform, fast, and the source's own examples (wheat for the magnetic claim). Buy one packet; the whole lot must be from the same packet so genetics are not a variable.
- **Magnets:** 2–3 **neodymium disc magnets**, ~10–20 mm, ~1,000–3,000 gauss at the face (any strong hobby magnet). You need to be able to present a **North** face and a **South** face to the seeds — mark which is which with a compass, or use two magnets back-to-back so opposite faces point outward.
- **Sham:** one **non-magnetic steel or brass slug** of the same size and mass as the magnet, handled identically. This is the control that catches "the seeds were just handled more."
- **Spacers:** a fixed, measured gap between magnet face and seeds (e.g. a stack of card or a plastic tube cut to length). Field strength falls off steeply with distance, so the gap must be the same for every treated batch and **recorded**.
- **Trays:** 3 identical shallow trays or plates, with identical medium (paper towel + measured water, or the same potting mix by weight).
- **Ruler, labels, phone camera.**

**Arm B — Lakhovsky oscillator circuit (second, iconic):**
- **Copper wire**, stiff enough to hold a ring — ~30–60 cm of 2–3 mm bare or enamelled copper.
- **A plastic rod or block** as the insulating support.
- Two matched potted seedlings (same species, same pot size, same soil, same light position — swap their positions every few days).

## Protocol

### Arm A — magnetic seed pre-treatment (48-hour first reading)

1. **Split one seed lot three ways** — **N-pole**, **S-pole**, **sham**. Equal counts (30–50 seeds each is plenty), assigned by an adult and **labelled with a code, not a name**, so the person measuring does not know which is which. Keep the key sealed until the end.
2. **Treat.** Lay each batch in its own dish with the magnet (or sham slug) at the **same measured gap** for the **same duration**. The source's own numbers disagree on duration (Pittman: 240 hours for maximum response at 0.5–100 Oe; Russian work: 30 minutes at 2,000 Oe), so **pick one duration and hold it constant** — 24 hours is a reasonable first choice — and record it. Do not vary it mid-run.
3. **Plant.** Sow each batch into its own identical tray, same medium, same water volume, same depth, same spacing. Tray position on the shelf is a real variable — **rotate tray positions daily** or run them side by side in the same light.
4. **Measure blind.** At **48 hours**, count **germinated seeds** (define germination once, up front — e.g. radicle ≥ 2 mm) and measure **shoot/radicle length** of every germinated seed with the ruler. Whoever measures must not know which tray is which. Then at **day 7** and **day 14**, repeat: germination %, mean shoot length, and (day 14) fresh weight.
5. **Unblind and log everything** — including the seeds that never germinated and any tray that dried out or got knocked over. A botched tray is logged as excluded, not deleted.

### Arm B — Lakhovsky oscillator circuit (4–6 weeks)

1. **Build the coil.** Bend the copper wire into a **single turn** — a ring roughly 15–30 cm across — with the two ends **overlapping** and separated by a small gap (a few mm). Support the ring on the plastic rod so it stands vertically and does not touch soil or pot.
2. **Place it around one plant**, ring centred on the stem, not touching the plant.
3. **Match the pair.** Two seedlings of the same species and age, same pot, same soil, same water schedule. One gets the coil, one does not. **Swap their positions every 2–3 days** so light and draught are not confounded with treatment.
4. **Measure weekly** for 4–6 weeks: height, leaf count, and (at the end) fresh weight. Measure blind if you can — have someone else record which plant is which.
5. **Optional polarity control:** a second ring with the ends **not overlapping** (an open gap) tests whether the overlap is doing anything at all.

## Pass / fail criteria (pre-registered)

- **PASS (magnetic arm, directional):** the **N-pole** batch shows **≥20% higher germination %** *or* **≥30% greater 48-hour shoot length** than the **sham**, as a mean across **two independent runs**, **and** the S-pole batch does not simply equal the N-pole batch (i.e. the effect is not just "any magnet"). → The source's directional claim replicates at home scale. Report the magnitude honestly; it will not be 5× unless you actually measure 5×, and you should say so explicitly if you do.
- **PASS (coil arm):** the coiled plant shows **≥20% greater growth** than its matched control over 4–6 weeks, repeated with a second plant pair. → The Lakhovsky circuit claim replicates at home scale.
- **FAIL:** all three seed batches within **±10%** of each other on both germination and 48-hour length, and the coil pair within ±10%. → Electro-culture does not reproduce with simple apparatus at home scale. **Skeptic's Star** material. This is a genuinely valuable result: it would mean the foundation claim of the entire Vault ElectroCulture collection is not reproducible with household equipment.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** run-to-run spread within one arm exceeds 20%, or tray conditions were not matched (different light, different water, different medium), or the person measuring knew the assignment.

Report the two arms **separately**. A magnetic result and a coil result are independent findings, and either can stand alone.

## What would change the verdict

- A **second independent household** running the same protocol with the same result moves the dossier from `protocol` to `replicated` or `refuted`. One kitchen is an anecdote; two is a datum.
- A **blinded, replicated trial at larger n** (hundreds of seeds, several runs, proper randomisation) is the natural second-stage dossier — that is the design the archive has never had.
- A **positive** result here would be the first home-scale support for the Vault's ElectroCulture collection. A **negative** would be the first honest burial of it, and would be worth publishing just as loudly.
