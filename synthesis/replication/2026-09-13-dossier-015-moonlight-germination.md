## The Claim

The biodynamic tradition (Steiner 1924, extended by Lili Kolisko's lunar experiments) holds that sowing and germinating crops in rhythm with the moon improves growth and yield. The broad claim was comprehensively refuted by Mayoral et al. (2020, *Agronomy* review of 120+ references: no reliable evidence for lunar-phase agriculture). But in 2023, Singiri et al. published the first controlled mechanistic study showing full moonlight (FML) is *perceived* by plants as a signal: tobacco and mustard seedlings exposed to FML for 5h showed ~2.4× nuclear volume increase, Phytochrome B up 30.6-fold, stress-response proteome/metabolome shifts, and — critically — mustard seedlings exposed to 3 consecutive FML nights showed significantly higher fresh weight, dry weight, and root weight vs dark controls (n=50, p<0.05). New-moon nights showed no difference from dark controls.

**Inference under test:** if full moonlight is a real biological signal for germinating food-crop seedlings (not just tobacco/mustard lab strains), then identical seeds germinated under direct full-moonlight nights should outgrow identical seeds kept dark at night — with the same daytime conditions.

**Claimant lineage:** Steiner/Kolisko lunar planting (archive) → Singiri et al. 2023 (modern mechanistic evidence, *Plants*, peer-reviewed)
**Verification status:** PARTIALLY SUPPORTED at lab scale — one peer-reviewed mechanistic study (Singiri 2023), not yet field-replicated for practical food crops. The broad biodynamic-calendar claim (Thun sidereal trigons) remains UNSUPPORTED (Spiess 1990 found no sidereal effects; Mayoral 2020 refutes broad lunar-phase agriculture). This test isolates the ONE mechanism with modern evidence — moonlight as a light signal — and does not test zodiac calendars.

## The Protocol

### Materials

1. **Seeds**: one packet of a single fast-germinating food crop (mustard greens — the crop Singiri used — or radish; ~$2). Count out 50 seeds.
2. **Two identical trays or pots** with the same soil, same depth sowing, 25 seeds each.
3. **An opaque box or bin** large enough to cover one tray (cardboard is fine).
4. **A spot receiving direct moonlight** at night: balcony, porch, windowsill facing the open sky, or garden bed — away from streetlights, porch lights, and windows glowing with indoor light.
5. **A lunar calendar** (any printed/online calendar showing the next full moon).
6. **Notebook** for the count sheet.

### Setup — two arms, 25 seeds each

| Arm | Night treatment | Day treatment |
|-----|----------------|---------------|
| A — moonlight | Placed in the direct-moonlight spot, uncovered | Both arms: same daylight spot, same watering |
| B — dark control | Same spot, covered by the opaque box from dusk to dawn | Both arms: same daylight spot, same watering |

Arm B controls for position, temperature, and daytime conditions — only the night light differs.

### Timing

Run the experiment on the **3 nights around a full moon** (check any lunar calendar for the next full moon; run the night before, night of, and night after). This matches Singiri's 3-consecutive-FML-nights design. **Cloud check:** note each night whether the moon was visible (clear / partly / overcast).

### Procedure

1. Sow 25 seeds per arm, same depth, same soil moisture. Label arms.
2. Each night of the run: place both arms in the moonlight spot; cover arm B with the opaque box at dusk; uncover both at dawn. If rain threatens, shelter both equally (a clear patio roof still passes moonlight; a covered porch may not — pick a spot that genuinely receives moonlight).
3. Water both arms equally each morning (same volume, same water).
4. Record daily: emerged seedling count per arm; sky condition each night.
5. At day 10–12: cut all seedlings at soil line, count them, and weigh fresh mass per arm (kitchen scale, grams).

### Measurements

- **Germination rate** per arm at day 7: (emerged / 25) × 100
- **Mean days to emergence** per arm
- **Total fresh mass** per arm at day 10–12 (grams, kitchen scale)

## Pass/Fail Criteria (pre-registered)

**PASS (supports the moonlight-signal claim at home scale):**
- Arm A fresh mass ≥ arm B by ≥15%, AND
- Arm A germination ≥ arm B (no penalty in emergence)

**FAIL (refutes the applied claim):**
- Arm A ≤ arm B on fresh mass (moonlight nights gave no growth advantage), or
- Arm A underperforms arm B

**INCONCLUSIVE:**
- Overcast skies blocked the moon on ≥2 of the 3 nights (rerun next full moon)
- Germination < 40% in both arms (bad seed batch — rerun with fresh seeds)
- Any artificial light reached the arms at night (streetlight, porch light, phone check with flashlight) — rerun

**Honest note:** Mayoral et al. (2020) represents the mainstream position — no reliable lunar effect — and Singiri (2023) is a single lab study on mustard/tobacco. If your dark control matches or beats your moonlight arm, that is a valid home-scale refutation of the applied claim: log it, earn the Skeptic's Star. If the moonlight arm wins by the margin above, that is a genuinely surprising result worth a second run on the following full moon with a different seed variety. Either way the family learns something real about experimental controls — the dark-control box IS the lesson.
