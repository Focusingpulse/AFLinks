## The Claim

The geobiology tradition holds that the Earth is covered by a planet-wide grid of
"earth radiation" lines — most famously the **Hartmann grid**, described by the
German physician Ernst Hartmann (1915–1992) as a global geomagnetic grid running
N–S and E–W at roughly **2 m spacing**, and the **Curry grid** (diagonal, ~3 m
spacing). Practitioners claim these lines can be located with dowsing rods and
that sleeping or working directly on them harms health. The Hotie de Viviane
survey in the vault documents the claim in the field, describing a "positive
North/South Hartmann network of level 3" crossing a Breton tomb site.

**Honest status of the claim:** contested, and the instrument question is open.
Mainstream science classes geobiology as pseudoscience; the best-known controlled
dowsing test (Kassel, Germany, 1991; analyzed by J.T. Enright, *Skeptical
Inquirer* 1999) found skilled dowsers performed at chance under blinding. The
Furter dissertation (UZH) found only *tendencies*, not significance, in 23 dairy
cows. Critically — as the archive's own record notes — **no geopathic grid has
ever been detected by an instrument.** Every prior test used a human with rods.
That gap is what this protocol addresses.

**Claimant:** geobiology / radiesthesia tradition (Hartmann, Curry, Wittmann et al.)
**Verification status:** unverified; prior controlled dowsing tests negative; never instrument-tested

## The Protocol — instrument scan for periodic magnetic structure

The testable core: if the Hartmann grid is a real *geomagnetic* structure with
~2 m spacing, a magnetometer walked systematically across a room should reveal a
repeating pattern at that spacing. If the field is only noise, the grid is not a
magnetic phenomenon — and the tradition's instrument-detection gap stays open,
honestly.

### Materials (~$0–30)

1. **Magnetometer:** a free phone magnetometer app (reads the phone's built-in
   magnetometer in µT) — or, better, a cheap handheld gauss meter (~$30).
2. **Cardboard jig:** a small box/tape rig that holds the phone at a **fixed
   height (~1 m) and fixed orientation**, aligned to magnetic north. This is the
   single most important control — a hand-held phone drifts and rotates.
3. **Tape measure** + **painter's tape** to lay out a 1 m measurement grid.
4. **Graph paper / spreadsheet** to log readings.

### Procedure

1. **Lay out the grid.** Mark a 1 m × 1 m measurement grid across the room floor
   (at least 6 × 6 points to cover ~25 m²). Note any known metal (rebar, pipes,
   radiators, appliances) and mark it — those points are excluded from analysis.
2. **Null reading.** With the phone in the jig, stand still and record 30 readings
   at one point. This gives the instrument's **noise floor** (standard deviation
   in µT).
3. **Scan.** Walk the grid point by point, holding the jig at fixed height and
   orientation, and log the field (µT) at each point. Take the reading after
   standing still 3 seconds at each point.
4. **Repeat 3 times** (ideally on different days), re-laying the grid from the
   same wall references so points are comparable.
5. **Repeat in 2 more rooms** and, if possible, outdoors in the yard.
6. **Analyze.** Average the 3 passes point-by-point. Plot field vs position along
   each row. Look for a repeating pattern at ~2 m spacing whose amplitude rises
   clearly above the noise floor.

### Controls and confounds (document all)

- **Orientation:** the jig fixes it. Never hand-wave the phone.
- **Metal/electronics:** exclude or note. Rebar in concrete floors can produce
  real magnetic structure — if found, that is a finding about the *building*, not
  the grid.
- **Drift:** re-zero / re-check the null reading between passes.
- **Walking speed:** stand still at each point; don't sweep.

## Pass/Fail Criteria (pre-registered)

**PASS (supports the claim):**
- A repeating ~2 m-period structure is present in **≥2 of 3** averaged runs, AND
- Its amplitude is **≥3× the measured noise floor**

**FAIL (refutes, for these rooms):**
- No periodic structure above the noise floor in any room

**INCONCLUSIVE:**
- Structure present but below the 3× threshold, or fewer than 3 passes completed
- Known metal sources dominate the readings and cannot be excluded

**Honest note:** the geomagnetic field is ~50 µT and phone magnetometers resolve
~0.1–1 µT, so a real grid would have to be a magnetic anomaly of detectable
strength. Given that global geomagnetic surveys have never reported a ~2 m
grid, a PASS here would be a genuinely surprising result — which is exactly why
it is worth a family's evening. A FAIL is a fully publishable validation
(Skeptic's Star) and honestly retires the "move the bed off the line" advice for
that home.
