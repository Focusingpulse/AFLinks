## The Claim

Viktor Schauberger held that water approaching +4°C undergoes "positive temperature movement": its energy rises, it becomes "fresh and alive," and its biological quality improves — while water warmed above +4°C loses energy and develops "pathogenic germs." He extended this to agriculture: rivers regulated by his methods "build their own fertile banks on which the whole richness of the plant world would grow."

**Inference under test:** if cooled (+4°C) water is biologically superior, seeds watered with it should germinate at least as well as — and by the claim's logic better than — identical seeds watered with room-temperature tap water.

**Claimant:** Viktor Schauberger, via the SAFE Compendium of Vortex Physics (1999)
**Verification status:** UNVERIFIED — the +4°C water-vitality claim has no mainstream replication. The related Stuttgart 1952 experiments (Popel) tested friction reduction in vortexed water, not biological quality. Note the honest tension: mainstream plant science predicts the OPPOSITE — cold water slows germination in most common garden seeds. That makes this a genuinely discriminating test, not a rigged one. A null or negative result is a real finding.

## The Protocol

### Materials

1. **Seeds**: one packet of a single fast-germinating variety (radish recommended — 3–7 day germination, ~$2). Count out 75 seeds.
2. **Three identical trays or jars** with the same soil (or paper-towel method in jars).
3. **Tap water**: ~2 liters, let stand 24h to offgas chlorine (same water for all arms).
4. **Thermometer** (~$5).
5. **Refrigerator** (to hold water at ~4°C).
6. **Notebook** for the count sheet.

### Setup — three arms, 25 seeds each

| Arm | Water treatment | Temperature at watering |
|-----|----------------|------------------------|
| A — cold vortex | Stirred into a vortex (spoon, ~30s, consistent direction) each morning, then chilled to ~4°C | ~4°C |
| B — cold control | Same water, chilled to ~4°C, NOT stirred | ~4°C |
| C — room control | Same water, NOT stirred, kept at room temperature | ~20°C |

Arm B isolates the vortex variable; arm C isolates the temperature variable. Without B you cannot tell whether any effect is the stirring or the cold.

### Procedure

1. Sow 25 seeds per arm, same depth, same soil moisture at start, same light exposure. Label arms.
2. Water each arm once daily with the same volume (e.g., 30ml), using that arm's prepared water. Prepare water the night before so A and B are at 4°C at watering time.
3. Record daily: count of emerged seedlings per arm, date.
4. At day 10: measure seedling height of 10 random seedlings per arm; if possible, cut and weigh fresh mass per arm.

### Measurements

- **Germination rate** per arm at day 7: (emerged / 25) × 100
- **Mean days to emergence** per arm
- **Mean seedling height** at day 10 (10 samples per arm)

## Pass/Fail Criteria (pre-registered)

**PASS (supports the claim):**
- Arm A germination ≥ arm C by ≥15 percentage points, AND
- Arm A mean days-to-emergence ≤ arm C, AND
- Arm A ≥ arm B (the vortex matters, not just the cold)

**FAIL (refutes the applied claim):**
- Arm A underperforms arm C, or
- A ≈ B (any effect is the cold alone, not the vortex) while both underperform C — mainstream expectation confirmed

**INCONCLUSIVE:**
- Germination < 40% in ALL arms (bad seed batch — rerun with fresh seeds)
- Arms differ by <5 points (within seed-to-seed noise)
- Protocol deviations (missed waterings, uneven light/heat)

**Honest note:** mainstream plant physiology predicts C ≥ B ≥ A. If that is what you find, that is a valid refutation of the applied claim — log it, earn the Skeptic's Star. If A wins, that is genuinely surprising and worth a second run with a different seed variety.
