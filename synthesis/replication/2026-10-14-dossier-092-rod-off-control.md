## The claim (as asserted by the practitioners)

The archive's radiesthesia corpus states the instrument claim plainly. From the Persian-language master's-level radiesthesia/radionics syllabus the library holds:

> "Instruments used: the pendulum (shaghul)."

and, in the same syllabus, the operating convention that a pendulum is **programmed** before use ("Programming conventions on your pendulum") — i.e. the instrument is treated as a **calibrated transducer**, not as a passive stick. From the Hungarian practitioner page:

> "Measuring Hartmann and water-vein radiation therefore cannot be enough on its own! … in radiesthetic measurement the instrumental examination of radioactivity and electromagnetic radiation is of decisive importance."

**The claim under test, in one sentence:** the rod (or pendulum) is the **sensing instrument** — the operator detects the target *through* it, and without it there is no detection.

## The rival claim (as asserted by the critics)

The Polish scientific analysis states the opposing claim with equal plainness:

> "Experimental research on radiesthesia — from historical tests to contemporary double-blind trials — shows unambiguously that dowsers do no better than chance. Any successes can be explained by known phenomena: **the ideomotor effect**, confirmation bias, the laws of statistics, or simply the field knowledge of experienced water-seekers."

and defines the mechanism it names:

> "ideomotor effect (ideomotor action) — This is the phenomenon in which subconscious expectations and imaginings cause small, involuntary muscle movements in the subject."

**The rival claim, in one sentence:** the rod is an **ideomotor readout** — it moves because the operator's own hand moves unconsciously. Removing the rod should therefore change **how the operator reports** a detection, not **whether they detect**.

## Why this card exists — the gap both sides leave open

Every dowsing card in this queue tests the **operator** (does the dowser beat chance? do dowsers agree with each other? does a pendulum score predict skill? does a dowsed line read on an instrument?). **None tests the instrument.** The two claims above make *opposite predictions about the same manipulation* — take the rod away — and that manipulation has never been run as the primary endpoint. It is the cheapest possible test of the whole family, it needs no electronics, and both sides have already stated what they expect.

The archive's own claim-status record for the **sibling** lineage (radionics, Abrams era) names exactly this test as its cheapest unrun candidate, and says why it matters: *"every test that ended it worked by removing the input rather than by measuring the output."* This card is that test, on the dowsing side.

## Honest status of the claim

1. **The practitioners state the instrument claim but publish no test of it.** The corpus describes what the rod is *for* and how to *program* it; it reports no trial in which the rod was removed and the operator's accuracy compared. The claim is asserted, not demonstrated.
2. **The critics' null literature is real and citable** — the Polish analysis carries the standard references: Kiszkowski & Szydłowski (1999, *Radiestezja a nauka*); Razowska-Jaworek, Cudak & Stachura (2018, *Radiesteta kontra hydrogeolog*, *Hydrogeologia* 2018(2)); the **Kassel dowsing test** (König, Moll & Sarma 1991, *Skeptiker* 4(1)); the **Scheunen / Munich experiments** (Enright 1995, *Naturwissenschaften* 82(8), 360–369; and 1996, 83(6), 275–277); McCarney et al. (2002, *J R Soc Med* 95(4), 189–191 — homeopaths could not distinguish a homeopathic solution from placebo by dowsing); Huttunen, Niinimaa & Myllylä (2012, *Pathophysiology* 19(2), 89–94 — dowsers' reactions interfered with by radio-frequency radiation, suggesting an unconscious reaction to radio waves rather than to water); and Olson, Jeyanesan & Raz (2017, *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2017(1), nix014 — the ideomotor basis of pendulum action).
3. **But none of that literature isolates the rod.** The Kassel, Scheunen and McCarney trials all test the **operator's discrimination**; they are silent on whether the rod itself contributes. So the critics' claim is *consistent* with the literature but is **not itself demonstrated** by it. The card tests the critics' mechanism directly, not the literature's conclusion.
4. **The archive holds no claim-status record for dowsing, the rod, or water divining.** The nearest record is the radionics one cited above (status: died), which is a **different apparatus and a different lineage** — cited here as the family's named analogue, not as evidence about rods. Recorded as a gap, not a backlog item.
5. **The critique's own hydrogeological claim is separate and is not what this card tests.** The Polish analysis also asserts that *"within the aquifer layer in which radiesthesists mark out so-called veins … there occur any significant local differences in the value of the filtration coefficient, the gradient of the magnetic field, or the chemistry of the water contained in it."* That is a claim about the **ground**, already carded elsewhere in this queue (the water-vein gamma test, the dowsed-ground bioassay, the water-vein-in-the-lab hose test). This card tests only the **instrument**.

## The ordinary explanation the card must carry

**The rod is a mechanical amplifier of hand tremor, and hand tremor is not a signal.** This is the confound the card is built around, and it cuts both ways:

- **The ideomotor account predicts the rod is decorative.** If the operator's detection (if any) is unconscious knowledge, the rod is a readout — removing it should leave accuracy unchanged while possibly *lowering confidence* (the operator loses their familiar indicator).
- **The expectancy confound runs the other way.** The operator knows which condition they are in, and a practitioner who believes the rod is the instrument will *expect* to fail without it. That expectation is itself a plausible cause of a worse no-rod result — which is why the card must (a) randomise the target position, (b) counterbalance the order and run the no-instrument condition **first** in half the sessions, (c) record confidence per trial, and (d) state this limitation in the verdict.
- **Neither condition may beat chance at all.** That is the outcome the null literature predicts, and it is a complete result: it means the rod adds nothing *and* the operator detects nothing at this task.

## What the sources DO give, and why the card exists

- A **named apparatus** (the operator's own rod or pendulum) and a **named manipulation** (take it away) that both sides have already staked a prediction on.
- A **known ground truth** the archive already uses for this family: a hose of flowing water, laid blind (cards 2026-09-23 and 2026-10-08 both use it).
- A **rival mechanism** (ideomotor action) stated precisely enough to be tested, with a citable literature behind it.
- A **cost of about $10** and a **two-hour protocol** — the cheapest test in the family.

## The protocol (pre-registered)

**Apparatus (home, ~$10–30):**
- A garden hose (10–15 m) fed from a tap, laid **on the surface** of a lawn or field — not buried. Surface-laid is deliberate: it removes the "you can see where the trench is" confound and matches the archive's own hose standard.
- The operator's **own** rod (forked hazel/willow, or L-rods) or pendulum — whatever they normally use.
- A tape measure, ground markers (pegs or flags), a notebook, and a **third person: the coder**, who is not the operator and not a believer-or-skeptic advocate for the outcome.

**Plot and target (hard):**
- A rectangular plot, e.g. **6 m × 10 m**, on open grass. The operator walks a fixed transect down the long axis.
- The hose is laid **straight across** the transect, perpendicular to it. Its position along the transect is drawn at random each trial from a **4 m band** (so it is never in the same place twice, and never at the ends).
- **The coder sets the hose position and does not tell the operator.** The coder records the operator's mark. The operator is blind to the hose position in **both** conditions.

**Conditions (paired, same operator, same plot, same session where possible):**
- **R — with the instrument:** the operator uses their rod or pendulum as they normally would.
- **N — without the instrument:** the operator uses **bare hands** — they point, or hold a plain stick in the hand, and the coder marks the spot. No rod, no pendulum, no dowsing tool of any kind.

**Trials and order (hard):**
- **20 trials per condition (40 total), paired.** Target position re-drawn each trial.
- **Order counterbalanced:** alternate R, N, R, N … or randomise; and in **half** the sessions run the **N condition first**, so the operator is not anchored on the rod.
- Between trials the operator steps off the plot while the coder re-lays the hose.

**Scoring:**
- **Hit** = the operator's mark within a pre-registered tolerance of **50 cm** of the hose's centre line. (Tolerance and band are fixed before trial 1 and do not change.)
- **Chance baseline is measured, not assumed:** after the run, the coder re-scores the same marks against **200 shuffled hose positions** drawn from the same 4 m band, giving an **empirical chance hit rate** for this operator's marking behaviour. (A uniform 50 cm ÷ 4 m = 25% is the naive figure; the shuffled baseline is the one to use, because operators do not mark uniformly when they have no signal.)

**Primary endpoint:** the **paired difference in hit rate (R − N)**, and each condition's hit rate against the **empirical shuffled baseline**.

**Secondary endpoints:**
1. **Mean absolute distance** from the mark to the true centre line, per condition.
2. **Stated confidence** per trial (0–10), per condition — the ideomotor account predicts confidence may differ even when accuracy does not.
3. **First-half vs second-half drift** within each condition (does the operator improve as they learn the plot?).

**Pass/fail (pre-registered):**

| Outcome | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **PASS — the rod is the sensing instrument** (practitioners' claim) | hit rate R ≥ **50%** (≥ 2× the naive 25% baseline) **AND** R − N ≥ **20 percentage points**, with the paired difference beyond the shuffled-baseline noise floor |
| **PASS — the rod is a readout** (critics' claim; the expected, ordinary result) | \|R − N\| ≤ **10 percentage points** **AND** neither condition beats the shuffled baseline — the rod changed nothing about what the operator detected (**Skeptic's Star**) |
| **PASS — the operator detects without the instrument** (neither claim) | R ≈ N **AND both** beat the shuffled baseline — the detection, if real, is the operator's, not the rod's; **requires replication before it means anything** |
| **FAIL** | fewer than 20 paired trials; the operator knew the hose position on any trial; the coder not blind; the tolerance, band or hose flow changed mid-run; the hose visible from the operator's start position |
| **VOID** | the operator changed instrument or method mid-run; illness; a change to the plot mid-run (mowing, heavy rain, standing water) — **a VOID is not a NULL** |

**Reproduction:** the result is **one operator, one plot, one day-set**. The claim under test is about the instrument, so **≥ 2 independent operators** (ideally one practitioner and one non-practitioner) reporting the same R−N direction is what would move this from a single session to a statement about the rod.

## What a result would mean

- **Both at chance (the likely result):** the rod adds nothing *and* the operator detects nothing at this task. The whole claim fails at the task level, and the manipulation that produced the null is the one the lineage's own record says ended the sibling claim — *removing the input*. **This is a complete and useful result.**
- **R ≈ N, both above chance:** the operator detects something **without** the rod. That would be the most interesting outcome in the queue — and it would point at the operator, not the instrument. It needs replication before it is anything.
- **R ≫ N:** the rod is the sensing instrument. The practitioners' claim survives its cheapest test.
- **R ≪ N:** the rod *interferes* with the operator's detection — a result neither side predicts, and one worth chasing.

## Gates

- **No health claim.** The endpoint is a hit rate against a measured chance baseline. Nothing here diagnoses, treats, or recommends anything.
- **The operator knows the condition — stated, not hidden.** This is the design's central limitation and it is written into the card, the dossier and the `verify` field, with the mitigations (randomised target, counterbalanced order, N-first in half the sessions, per-trial confidence).
- **No claim about the ground.** The card tests the instrument, not whether veins exist, not whether they are harmful, and not whether a well should be dug. Those are separate cards.
- **Own summaries, attribution, links only.** The Polish translations stay in the archive; the card publishes our own analysis and short attributed quotes.
