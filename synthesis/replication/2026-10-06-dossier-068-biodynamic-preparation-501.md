## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source describes the preparation and its claimed action, translated:

> "Horn-silica is composed of quartz crystals, as pure as possible. This is pulverised and, with an addition of water, is placed in the horn and buried in the summer period. Silicon is the second most abundant element on Earth. One of the two main biodynamic preparations along with 500 (horn manure), it does not have an action aimed at the soil in itself, but helps the main activities of the plant in perceiving the sun's rays and also in the processes of defence against pathogen attacks."

The companion synthesis (quoted in dossier 062) states the same division of labour:

> "…one sprayed preparation promotes soil fertility ('horn manure' preparation) [preparation 500], and another sprayed preparation is used to improve the ripening of the cultivated plants ('horn silica' preparation) [preparation 501]."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a foliar spray of the biodynamic horn-silica preparation (BD 501) change the plant's **own form and water status** — the leaf insertion angle and the leaf's relative water content — compared with an untreated plot and a dynamized-water sham?

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the trial and no home test of it.** A grep for `501`, `horn silica`, `BD 501`, `corno-silice`, `leaf angle` and `internode` across `synthesis/quest-queue/` and `synthesis/replication/` returns **no card and no dossier** on this preparation. The queue's nearest food cards test germination (015, 014), fermentation (010), ripening (020), sowing timing (032), growth stimulation (026), storage (033, 043), planting order (038), the soil community (049), weed control (056) and the **companion soil preparation 500** (062) — **none tests a foliar spray or a plant-shape endpoint.**

Four things the card must say out loud, because they are what a family is actually testing:

1. **The source's own results are mostly NULL, and it reports them as null.** SPAD chlorophyll index: *"No result was obtained for the SPAD measurement in the individual plots. No difference was noted…"* Internode distance: *"did not give significant results."* Disease pressure: *"no substantial differences were noted in the presence of larvated downy mildew and downy mildew on leaf."* Bunch count: no difference (one extra bunch in the treated plot, which the authors themselves discount — *"It is not however taken into consideration since the after-flowering treatment cannot have affected the number of bunches."*). Ripeness curves: *"did not show notable differences."* Blind tasting: *"The parameters analysed do not have a significant difference according to the ANOVA statistical test"* — and the panel ranked the **Control** first.
2. **One endpoint showed a tendency: leaf insertion angle.** *"The measurement of the leaf insertion angles showed an interesting tendency after the treatment… Of the three measurements carried out, significant data were recorded for three leaves in each survey carried out. A greater effect was noted in the upper part of the canopy and in the lower part."* The authors are candid that it is hard to measure: *"This difference visible to the naked eye… is difficult to measure and quantify… the procedure to obtain a truthful data not influenced by survey errors was difficult."*
3. **One endpoint showed a non-significant trend: water stress.** The Scholander pressure chamber read **lower** water stress in the treated plot — *"significant for row 9 but not for row 27. The general average shows the same trend for all the surveys, but the results are not significant."* The night readings showed no difference at all, which the authors note is consistent with the theory (night values represent available water, not stress).
4. **The expected result is a null, and that is a complete result.** The source's one positive is a *tendency* in a hard-to-measure quantity; everything else is a null. A home test is a vote, not a verdict.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that the 501 plot's leaf angles and relative water content match the control and the sham, and the honest verdict is *"a foliar 501 spray does not visibly change leaf angle or leaf water status at home scale in one run."* A clear, replicating positive (a mean angle ≥6° lower than **both** control and sham, and/or a higher RWC) would be genuinely interesting and would justify the taller follow-up (a second season, a porometer or pressure chamber, and a laboratory leaf analysis).

## Why it matters

- It is the queue's **first card on a FOLIAR spray** — an application to the plant above ground — as distinct from card 062 (BD 500), which is a soil drench, and every other food card, which acts on soil, seed, or storage.
- It is the queue's **first card whose endpoint is a leaf's ANGLE** — the plant's own geometry, read off a photograph against a printed grid. Every other card counts, weighs, measures a length, or scores a visual pattern; this one measures a **shape**.
- It is the queue's **first card that pre-registers the source's OWN null as a built-in check** — the source found **no** internode-distance difference, so the card carries internode distance as a *negative* endpoint: if the treated plot's internodes diverge, something other than 501 is at work.
- It is the **second card on the biodynamic preparation lineage** and the first on the **silica / light pole** of the system (500 is the earth / manure pole). The two preparations are the pair the method is built around.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$40–150** — quartz crystals (~$10–20; a purchased BD 501 preparation, ~$15–30, is an acceptable substitute if no horn is available), a cow horn (~$20–40), a bucket and a whisk (~$10), a fine hand sprayer (~$10), a protractor or a printed angle grid (~$0–5), a kitchen scale with 0.1 g resolution (~$20), a cork borer or small punch for leaf discs, and a warm dry place or a low oven for drying.
- Safety: **low.** No high voltage, no chemicals. The only real hazards are the spade and, if a drying oven is used, its heat. Family-safe; suitable for a supervised child.
- Accessibility: the preparation is **seasonal** — the horn is buried in **summer** and retrieved in **autumn** (note the opposite season to BD 500, which is buried over winter). A purchased BD 501 shortens the loop to one growing season. A fast crop (beans, tomatoes, basil) lets a family run two rounds in one season.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Quartz crystals** (as pure as available) — pulverised, the substance of the preparation.

- **A fine hand sprayer** — to apply the preparation as a mist to the foliage.

- **A kitchen scale (0.1 g)** — for the relative-water-content weighings.
- **A cork borer or small punch** — to cut matched leaf discs.
- **A warm dry place or a low oven** — to dry the discs.
- **A log sheet** — for pre-registration and the record.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — measurement check (mandatory).**
1. Prove the **leaf-angle measurement can detect a difference at all**: photograph the same marked leaves twice on the same day and compare the two readings, and photograph a deliberately **bent** leaf beside a **straight** leaf of the same plant and confirm the method separates them by more than the run's threshold.
2. **If the method cannot separate a bent from a straight leaf, a no-difference result is VOID, not a null.** "No difference" and "the measurement could not have seen a difference" are different results and get filed identically if this check is skipped. (This is the exact failure the source names: *"the procedure to obtain a truthful data not influenced by survey errors was difficult."*)

**Phase 1 — make the preparation (or use a purchased one).**
3. Pulverise quartz crystals as finely as possible; mix with a little water; pack into a cow horn; bury **pointed-end down, ~40–60 cm deep, in SUMMER**; retrieve in **AUTUMN**. (Record the burial and retrieval dates. A purchased BD 501 is an acceptable substitute — record which was used.)

**Phase 2 — prepare and pre-register.**
4. Split the plot into **matched sub-plots** (≥3 per arm, same soil, same light, same slope, same crop and sowing date):
   - **A — BD 501:** stir **2–5 g** of the preparation in **10–15 L** of water for **one hour**, alternating the stirring direction to form a vortex; apply as a **fine mist over the foliage** (not the soil) at a defined phenological phase — the source's clearest arm was **after flowering**.
   - **B — control:** untreated.
   - **C — sham:** 10–15 L of plain water, **stirred identically for one hour** and sprayed identically — this controls for the stirring, the water, and the act of spraying.
5. Write the **pre-registration sheet** before applying anything: the arms, the preparation quantity, the stirring time, the application rate, the number of sub-plots, the marked-leaf protocol, the angle threshold, the RWC protocol, and the crop. Photograph it.

**Phase 3 — apply and measure.**
6. Apply the arms on the same day, in the same way, at the same time of day; water all plots identically thereafter.
7. **Leaf angle:** mark the **5th–10th leaf from the shoot tip** on ≥10 shoots per sub-plot. Photograph each marked leaf against the printed grid, with the shoot held vertical, and read the shoot/petiole angle in degrees. Measure **before** treatment and **1–2 days after** (the source found the day after treatment the best moment). Score the mean angle per arm.
8. **Relative water content (RWC):** punch **10 matched leaf discs** per sub-plot. Weigh **fresh** (FW). Float them in water in the dark for ~4 h, blot, and weigh **turgid** (TW). Dry at ~70 °C for 24 h and weigh **dry** (DW). RWC = (FW − DW) / (TW − DW) × 100.
9. **Internode distance (the source's own null — carried as a check):** measure the internode distances from the 1st to the 10th leaf on the same shoots. The source found **no** difference here; a difference in your run is a signal that something other than 501 is at work.

**Phase 4 — score.**
10. Compute the mean leaf angle, mean RWC and mean internode distance per arm. Compare **A vs B** (the preparation effect) and **A vs C** (the preparation effect net of handling).
11. Repeat the run with a fresh preparation (a second crop round in the same season, or a second season).

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** the 501 plot shows a mean leaf angle **≥ 6° lower** than **both** the control and the sham (the source's own observed gap was 6–10°), **and/or** a higher RWC, replicating in a second run — the foliar preparation's effect on plant form is real at home scale and needs the taller follow-up (a second season, a porometer or pressure chamber, a laboratory leaf analysis).
- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** the 501 plot is **at the control and sham level** on every endpoint (within the noise band) in two runs — a foliar 501 spray does not change leaf angle or leaf water status at home scale. A complete result; Skeptic's Star.
- **FAIL (void, not a null):** the **measurement check** fails — the method cannot separate a bent from a straight leaf by more than the threshold — the measurement measures nothing and the run is void.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** fewer than 3 sub-plots per arm; one run; the sub-plots differed by >30 % at baseline; a pest, frost, drought or flood hit one arm and not the others; the preparation was not made or sourced as stated; the stirring time, dilution or application differed between arms; the leaves were measured at different times of day between arms.
- **ARTIFACT:** the **sham (C) is distinguished from the control (B)** — handling, stirring or water alone produces the signal; the result tracks **sub-plot position, light, slope or drainage** rather than the arm; the operator knew which plot was which (unblinded application or measurement); or the result disappears when the sub-plots are re-randomised in position.

## Evidence

Photo of the preparation and its burial/retrieval + the **pre-registration sheet** (arms, quantity, stirring time, application rate, N, endpoints, thresholds) + the **measurement-check result** (repeat readings on the same leaf; bent-vs-straight separation) + the **angle photographs** with the printed grid, per marked leaf, before and after + the **RWC weighings** (FW, TW, DW) per sub-plot + the **internode measurements** per sub-plot + the results table with the A-vs-B and A-vs-C differences shown + the recorded burial/retrieval and application dates and conditions + the second run + the void/artifact checks reported whether or not they void the run.

## Confounds (named in advance)

- **The dilution is homeopathic — the central confound.** The preparation is applied at a very low concentration, so the *stirring, the water, and the act of spraying* are plausible causes of any effect. **The sham arm (C) is the whole experiment.**
- **The measurement's own error — the confound the source names.** The authors state the angle measurement was *"difficult to obtain a truthful data not influenced by survey errors"* (adjacent shoots, curved growth). Hold the shoot vertical, mark the leaves, and run the Phase-0 check.
- **Time of day.** Leaf angle and water status both move with the sun and with transpiration. Measure every arm at the same hour.
- **Season and weather.** A single run is a sample of one; drought, frost or a pest can swamp a small effect. Repeat.
- **Sub-plot position.** Light, slope, drainage and edge effects vary across a plot. Randomise the arms and record the layout.
- **The operator's expectation.** The whole card is built around the sham and the measurement check; if the operator knows which plot is which when reading the angles, the run is weakened.
- **The preparation's provenance.** A home-made preparation and a purchased one are not the same object. Record which was used.

## Verdict rules

501 ≥ 6° lower mean leaf angle than **both** control and sham, and/or a higher RWC, replicating in a second run → **the claim survives** (surprising; needs the taller follow-up). 501 at the control and sham level in two runs → **the home-scale claim is refuted** (the expected, complete result). The measurement check fails → **void, not a null**. Sham distinguished from control, or the result tracks position, or the operator was unblinded → **artifact**. Fewer than 3 sub-plots per arm, one run, or baseline mismatch → **inconclusive, not a refutation**.
