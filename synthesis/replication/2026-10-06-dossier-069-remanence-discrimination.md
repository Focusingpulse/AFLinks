## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The Turenne monograph states the Law of Remanences directly, translated:

> "In this connection one must recall the numerous failures in the famous searches for hidden treasures. For example: if, holding a piece of gold (or the witness of gold), one moves about a house or an archaeological zone with rod or pendulum, it is possible — and indeed very frequent — to register numerous reactions of the detectors indicating the presence of gold, but for the inspections then to prove vain. The reason is simple: it is possible that in that place there was once gold or precious metals, but that they were subsequently removed, and that what the detectors picked up was nothing other than the residual waves of those bodies. They are called precisely 'remanences' — the waves that have 'impregnated' the place where the gold resided. Such waves do dissolve, yes, but over times that may be very long."

And the quantitative form of the claim, in the very next line:

> "The 'REMANENCE lasts the longer the higher the metal's number in Mendeleev's Table, or the longer it has remained in place. In the same way it is very difficult to find a missing person, or to locate any person on a geographical map, since his home and the places he habitually frequented carry this phenomenon of remanences."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** after an object is removed from a spot, does the spot retain a **detectable residual imprint** that a blind operator can distinguish from an untouched control spot — and, if so, does a **higher-atomic-number** metal leave a longer-lived imprint than a lower one?

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the claim and no home test of it.** A grep for `remanence`, `remanences`, `residual wave`, `place memory`, `stone tape` and `vacated` across `synthesis/quest-queue/` and `synthesis/replication/` returns **no card and no dossier** on this phenomenon. The queue's nearest cards test dowsing against a **present** target — a buried line (035, 059), a water vein (057), a chemical in water (088), a hidden zone (004) — or against a **representation** of a target (083, reading a plan). **None tests a target that is absent.**

Four things the card must say out loud, because they are what a family is actually testing:

1. **The claim is a claim, not an established result.** The remanence doctrine comes from the Turenne radiesthesia lineage (1930s–1980s) and has no controlled replication in the archive. The card is a test, not an endorsement.
2. **The expected result is a null, and that is a complete result.** The most likely outcome is that a blind operator identifies the vacated spot at chance. A clean null is a genuine finding about the method — it is exactly what the "Skeptic's Star" honours — and it is worth as much as a positive.
3. **The measurement is the operator, and the operator is the instrument.** The source itself warns that the detector reading is filtered by the operator's nervous system (*"When these organs are weakened, tired, exhausted, irritated, ill, or simply saturated with harmful vibrations, the currents that then descend towards the hands… are altered"*). This is why the test **must be blind** and **must be scored against a known ground truth the operator cannot infer** — otherwise the result measures expectation, not remanence.
4. **A positive would need the differential to hold.** The source makes a *specific, falsifiable* prediction — remanence lasts longer for a higher-Z metal. A single "I found the spot" is weak; the differential (higher-Z spot detected longer than lower-Z spot, both against empty controls) is the strong version. If the primary test passes but the differential does not, the honest verdict is *"something is being detected, but not the atomic-number dependence the source claims."*

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that the operator cannot beat chance, and the honest verdict is *"a vacated spot is not distinguishable from an untouched control at home scale in this run."* A clear, replicating positive — the operator identifying the vacated spot in two independent series, **and** the higher-Z spot outlasting the lower-Z spot — would be genuinely interesting and would justify the taller follow-up (a longer persistence series, a second operator, and a pre-registered multi-site trial).

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$0–50** — two small metal objects of different atomic number (a **copper** coin or washer, Z=29, ~$0; a **bismuth** crystal or **tungsten** piece, Z=83/74, ~$5–15), four identical floor markers (tape, ~$0), a pendulum or a pair of L-rods (~$0–20; a nut on a thread works), a printed log sheet, and a helper who does not know which spot is which.
- Safety: **low.** No high voltage, no chemicals, no heat. ⚠ **Do not use lead** (Z=82) — it is toxic to handle and to store in a home, and the atomic-number differential can be tested with **bismuth** (Z=83, non-toxic, cheap as decorative crystals) or **tungsten** (Z=74). Wash hands after handling any metal object.
- Accessibility: the test needs **14 days of undisturbed placement** and a **blind operator**; a family can run it on a spare-room floor, a garage slab, or a porch. Two operators can be run in parallel to check whether the result is operator-specific.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A copper object** (coin or washer, Z=29) — the low-Z witness.
- **A bismuth crystal or tungsten piece** (Z=83 or Z=74) — the high-Z witness. (Not lead.)
- **Four identical floor markers** (tape squares or card squares) — spots A, B, C, D.
- **A pendulum** (a nut or washer on a 15–20 cm thread) or **a pair of L-rods** — the detector.
- **A printed log sheet** and a **pen** — for pre-registration and the trial record.
- **A blind operator** and a **tester** who places the objects and randomizes the trials. (The same person may not be both.)
- **A camera** — to photograph the setup and the pre-registration sheet.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — measurement check (mandatory).**
1. Prove the **detector responds at all**: with the operator present, hold the pendulum over a strong, *present* source (e.g., a large magnet, or a spot the operator has previously found responsive) and confirm the operator can read a clear response. **If the operator cannot produce a stable, repeatable reading on a present source, a no-difference result is VOID, not a null.** "No difference" and "the instrument was not working" are different results and get filed identically if this check is skipped.

**Phase 1 — place and imprint.**
2. Mark **four identical spots** (A, B, C, D) on a hard, undisturbed floor, ~1 m apart, in a line or a square, in a room with no traffic.
3. Place the **copper object on A** and the **bismuth/tungsten object on B**. Leave **C and D empty**. Record which is which on the pre-registration sheet (kept from the operator).
4. Leave the objects **undisturbed for 14 days**. Do not move them; do not let anyone else move them.

**Phase 2 — remove and blind.**
5. On day 14, **remove both objects**. All four spots now look identical (markers only).
6. The **tester** now runs the blind trials. In each trial the operator is brought to **one** spot (chosen by the tester from a pre-shuffled list) and, without knowing which spot it is, reports **"imprinted"** or **"clean"**. The tester records the call and the true identity.
7. Run **20 trials** (5 per spot, in a pre-shuffled order). Then run a **second, independent 20-trial series** on a different day.

**Phase 3 — persistence.**
8. Repeat the 20-trial series at **+1 day, +7 days, +30 days and +90 days** after removal, on the same four spots, with the same operator and the same blinding.

**Phase 4 — differential (only if Phase 2 beats chance).**
9. Compare the **hit rate on A (copper, Z=29)** against the **hit rate on B (bismuth/tungsten, Z=83/74)** across the persistence series. The source predicts **B outlasts A**.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (primary):** the operator identifies the vacated spots (A and B combined) **significantly above chance** — ≥ **15/20** in **each** of the two independent series (binomial p ≈ 0.02, one-tailed) — **and** the empty controls (C and D) are **not** called "imprinted" above chance. A hit on the vacated spots with a *matched* false-positive rate on the controls is the result that matters.
- **PASS (differential):** given a primary pass, the **high-Z spot (B) is detected at a later persistence point than the low-Z spot (A)** — i.e., B still beats chance at +30 days while A does not.
- **FAIL:** hit rate on the vacated spots is at or near chance (≤ 12/20), **or** the controls are called "imprinted" as often as the vacated spots (which means the operator is responding to something other than the imprint).
- **VOID:** Phase 0 failed, or the objects were moved, or the blinding was broken. A VOID is not a FAIL.
