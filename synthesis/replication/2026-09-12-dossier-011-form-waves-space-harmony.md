## The Claim

Practitioners of radiesthesia and sacred geometry claim that geometric forms emit "form waves" (ondes de forme) — vibratory energy that affects human energy fields. Beneficial forms (golden ratio spirals, Flower of Life, pyramids, rounded shapes) are claimed to expand the human energy field, while noxious forms (sharp angles, irregular shapes) contract it. The claim is testable with simple dowsing rods.

**Honest status of the claim:** contested. Dowsing itself failed controlled tests (Kassel 1991; Enright 1999). No form wave has been detected by instruments. The Vibratis protocol is self-described as a "test it yourself" exercise, not peer-reviewed evidence. This dossier does NOT assume the claim is true — the protocol is designed so the claim can pass OR fail cleanly.

**Claimant:** radiesthesia / sacred geometry tradition (Vibratis, Bovis, Chaumery, Belizal)
**Verification status:** unverified; prior controlled tests negative

## The Protocol — energy field measurement before/after form exposure

The testable core: **if form waves are real and detectable, holding a beneficial geometric form should measurably change the distance at which dowsing rods respond to a person's energy field.**

### Materials (~$5-10)

1. **L-rods**: Two sets, metal coat hangers or welding rod bent into L shape (long arm ~30cm, short handle ~10cm). Drinking straw over handle makes rod swing freely.
2. **Test forms** (printed on paper, ~15cm diameter):
   - Beneficial: Flower of Life, golden ratio spiral, Vibratis logo, pyramid outline
   - Control: blank paper, random squiggle
   - Noxious: irregular jagged shape (optional)
3. **Tape measure** (or marked floor)
4. **Camera** (phone) for documentation

### Setup

Two people: one as the **Subject** (stands still), one as the **Mapper** (walks toward subject with rods). A third person as **Witness** (optional, good kid job) records distances.

### Procedure

1. **Baseline measurement**: Subject stands on fixed point, arms at sides, relaxed. Mapper holds L-rods level, parallel, walking slowly toward subject from ~3m away. At the point where rods cross or swing apart, Mapper stops. Witness marks distance from subject's body to rod position (measure to nearest 5cm). Record as "Baseline 1". Repeat 3 times, average.

2. **Form exposure**: Subject holds one printed form (e.g., Flower of Life) at chest level, both hands, ~30cm from body. Wait 2-3 minutes.

3. **Post-form measurement**: Mapper repeats the approach procedure. Record distance as "Form A". Repeat 3 times, average.

4. **Control test**: Subject holds blank paper (same size). Wait 2-3 minutes. Mapper repeats approach. Record as "Control". Repeat 3 times, average.

5. **Repeat** with different forms (pyramid, spiral, etc.) and different Subject/Mapper pairs.

6. **Blind variation** (stronger protocol): Subject closes eyes. Witness hands Subject a form OR blank paper without Mapper knowing which. Mapper records distance. This controls for expectation effects.

## Pass/Fail Criteria (pre-registered)

**PASS (supports the claim):**
- Beneficial form distance ≥ 20% larger than baseline AND
- Beneficial form distance ≥ 20% larger than control (blank paper) AND
- Effect replicates across ≥2 different Subject/Mapper pairs

**FAIL (refutes, for this home and these testers):**
- No consistent difference between beneficial forms and control (within 10%)
- Or forms show opposite effect (field contraction)

**INCONCLUSIVE:**
- Small differences (10-20%) that don't replicate across pairs
- Only one Subject/Mapper pair tested
- Blind protocol not attempted

**Honest note:** A PASS here would be surprising given Kassel 1991 — which is exactly why family-scale testing matters. A FAIL is a fully publishable validation (Skeptic's Star) and honestly retires "hold a Flower of Life to expand your field" advice for this family.
