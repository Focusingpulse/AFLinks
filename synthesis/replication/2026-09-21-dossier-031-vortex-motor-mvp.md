## The Claim

The author claims that a **water vortex contained in a resonant cavity** can generate
enough energy to **light an LED** — framed within Schauberger's *implosion* (centripetal,
negentropic) physics rather than explosion (combustion). The companion theoretical paper
describes the architecture: a resonant cavity of **ovoid / logarithmic-spiral geometry**
that induces a centripetal vortex in a working fluid (air or water); an applied
electromagnetic resonance frequency is argued to reduce the "viscosity of the Ether,"
letting the vortex accelerate to "implosion," where a dynamic vacuum generates a net
traction force.

The MVP document is explicitly **not a results report** — it is an open-source
engineering manifesto and a build challenge to the maker community. Its own validation
metric is stated plainly: *light an LED with the energy generated by a water vortex in
a resonant cavity.*

**What the author's abstract confirms the doc contains** (primary receipt, retrieved
live 2026-09-21):

- a detailed materials and tools list (estimated cost **< 50 €**);
- step-by-step construction instructions for each component — **the cavity, the rotor,
  the turbine, and the circuit**;
- a testing protocol with clear validation metrics (**Basic, Intermediate, Advanced**
  success levels);
- a troubleshooting guide for the most common challenges.

**Honest status of the claim — stated up front:**

- **This is a self-published Zenodo deposit by an author listing affiliation
  "Laboratorio de Sinergia Humano-IA."** It is not peer-reviewed and carries no
  independent replication. Zenodo is a general-purpose repository; a deposit is not
  an endorsement.
- **The claim is extraordinary** — a water vortex producing net energy is an
  over-unity claim, which contradicts the second law of thermodynamics as ordinarily
  stated.
- **The build includes a turbine.** This is the central confound: an LED lighting up

## The Test (energy balance — the honest endpoint)

The MVP's own success metric ("the LED lights") cannot discriminate the claim from
ordinary generation. This dossier replaces it with a **net-energy measurement** that
is design-agnostic:

**Input** — total energy delivered to the system over a timed run:

- if the device is **gravity-fed**: the water's gravitational power,
  `P_in = ρ · g · Q · h` (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s², Q = volumetric flow in m³/s,
  h = usable head in m);
- if the device is **pump- or motor-driven**: the electrical power drawn by the pump
  or motor, measured at the wall (a plug-in power meter is sufficient);
- if both: the sum.

**Output** — electrical power delivered to the load over the same run:

- `P_out = V · I` at the LED/load, measured with a multimeter, or the LED's
  measured forward power.

**Run** — at least **3 timed runs of ≥ 10 minutes each**, on different days, with
flow rate and head (or pump draw) recorded each time.

### Pre-registered pass/fail

- **PASS (anomaly — the claim survives):** `P_out > P_in` in every run, with the
  margin larger than the combined measurement uncertainty. This would be a genuine
  over-unity result and should be reported as such, with the raw numbers.
- **FAIL (claim retired — the likely outcome):** `P_out ≤ P_in` in every run. This is
  ordinary turbine generation. An honest negative; the Skeptic's Star applies.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** fewer than 3 runs; input or output not measured; flow/head not
  recorded; or the device was not built to the doc's specification.

**The confound to name in the write-up:** if the LED lights but `P_out ≤ P_in`, the
correct statement is *"the vortex device generated electricity by ordinary means"* —
NOT *"the vortex device works."* Distinguishing these two sentences is the entire
point of the card.

## What "supports" would and would not mean

- A PASS would be a **major anomaly** and should be treated with maximum skepticism
  until independently replicated by a second household. A single home result, however
  carefully measured, is one data point.
- A FAIL is **not** a failure of the experimenter — it is the archive's honest result,
  and it retires a claim without needing anyone to believe anything.
- Either way, the builder learns to measure energy in and energy out, which is the
  transferable skill.

## Protocol summary

1. Obtain the build doc (`10.5281/zenodo.17635970`) and build the MVP to its
   specification. Photograph each stage.
2. Instrument the input: measure flow rate and head (gravity-fed) or pump/motor power
   draw (driven).
3. Instrument the output: measure voltage and current at the LED/load.
4. Run ≥ 3 timed runs of ≥ 10 minutes on different days, recording all numbers.
5. Compute `P_in` and `P_out` for each run; compare.
6. Write up the result with the raw numbers, the build photos, and the honest verdict.
