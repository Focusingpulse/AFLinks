## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The source does not assert that dowsing works; it sets out to find whether a *physical* basis for the rod deflection can be located at all. Its own framing, quoted:

> "The geophysicist will first consider whether he should reject work on problems of the kind described outright, or whether he should enter into them. He is thereby led to the fundamental question of whether a cause, graspable by physical methods, can be found for the deflection of the rod in the dowser's hand."

> "The study of the literature, as well as personal reports, made us acquainted with numerous experiments aimed at answering the question posed, but they were all, without exception, able to convince us neither in the positive nor in the negative sense. We therefore decided to carry out our own experiments."

The operational claim under test — the one a community actually cares about — is: **can independent dowsers, working blind, agree with each other about where a buried feature is, and does that agreement land on the feature?** The source's own design was built to answer exactly that: on each field, several dowsers were to mark the reaction zones *independently of one another*, and the marks were compared afterwards.

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** when several dowsers walk the same field blind and independently, do their marks agree with each other more than chance, and does any agreed mark land on a buried feature whose position they could not know?

## The measurement (the source's own numbers, carried into the card)

The source's apparatus and procedure are fully specified, which is why this is a community card:

| Element | Source specification |
|---|---|
| Test fields | **7** (A–G), in Zurich (Forsterstrasse park) and the Sihl valley; marked at the edges with pegs |
| Known features | Field B — a concrete heating duct, **1 m wide, 70 cm high, top edge 70 cm below the road**; Field G — a cast-iron water main, **55 cm diameter, 12 mm wall, top edge ~1.4 m deep, carrying ~16,000 L/min**; Field D — the edge of the Sihl valley groundwater stream; Fields E/F — one underflowed by groundwater, one not |
| Dowsers | **16** selected from 40 recommended; 15 male, 1 female; ages 37–63; 12–51 years' practice; 9 learned from experienced dowsers, 3 in courses, 4 self-study; 10 practised professionally |
| Companions | **16** academics/technicians, one assigned per dowser, given the field plans and 20 marking rods |
| Blinding | Each dowser got a particular test day and a particular companion; **the field's location was communicated only on the test day**; companions were not told why each field was chosen; dowsers had no contact with the Institute staff who knew the fields; each dowser signed that he had not previously walked or known the fields |
| Recording | Reaction spots entered on **1:100 plans to the millimetre**; no interpretation of the deflections required; an absolutely negative result had to be recorded too |
| Sealing | Plans and questionnaires **sealed in an envelope on the same day** and opened **only after all experiments concluded** |
| Period | **24 August – 1 December 1944** |

The source's headline result, quoted:

> "One sees at once that on none of the seven test fields are there reaction areas indicated concordantly by several dowsers that stand out from an undoubtedly reaction-free environment. The goal of the experiments set out at the end of § 1 has therefore not been achieved."

> "This main was in fact indicated by no dowser at all, while by several dowsers, at various spots (in field B obviously influenced by the visible shaft covers), non-existent water mains were indicated."

> "As far as a check was possible on the basis of the present material, the best performance is to be recorded for the dowser who alone correctly indicated the difference between fields E and F. Whether this performance is, however, more than a chance hit would first have to be proved by further investigations, for the same dowser indicated non-existent water mains on other fields, yet did not notice the large main running diagonally across field G."

The source's own honest caveat, quoted — and the card carries it:

> "From this fact, however, to conclude that the rod deflections have no physically graspable cause at all would, I consider, be mistaken. For that purpose the experiments were evidently laid out too primitively and too summarily."

And the source's own named next step — which this card runs:

> "Above all it seems important to us to clarify, through careful, extensive experiments, the question of the reproducibility of the statements of each individual dowser. The same dowser must, for example, walk the same field several times on the same day. In doing so, it must be avoided that on one walk he indicates, from memory, consciously or unconsciously, the same reaction spots as on an immediately preceding walk. However, measures (such as blindfolding) must be avoided that reduce the dowser's working capacity."

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the study and no home test of it.** A grep for `concordance`, `Reizzone`, `reaction zone` and `Wünschelrute` across `living-library/synthesis/quest-queue/` and `living-library/synthesis/replication/` returns **no card and no dossier** on multi-operator concordance. The queue's dowsing cards each score **one operator against a hidden target**: 005 (blind geopathic mapping), 029 (psi-track — a thought's track to an object), 034 (neutral pendulum discrimination), 035 (blind water-line location), 044 (plan-reading), 048 (pendulum water-quality), 057 (water-vein gamma). **None scores the operators against each other.** That is the gap this card fills.

Four things the card must say out loud:

1. **The source had no chance baseline for concordance, and that is the hole the card fills.** The 1946 report states that no concordant areas "stand out from an undoubtedly reaction-free environment" — but it never computed how much concordance sixteen dowsers marking large areas would produce *by chance*. **"They agree" is only a finding against "how much would they agree if their marks were random."** The card's primary methodological addition is a **shuffled-map baseline**: each dowser's plan is rotated by a random offset and the concordance statistic recomputed 1,000 times, giving the chance distribution. This is the arm the source lacked — the same shape of fix as dossier 058's paired-control-at-the-same-moment and dossier 035's non-dowser control.
2. **The source's own result contains a false-positive signal that the card must design out.** Several dowsers indicated **non-existent** water mains on field B, "obviously influenced by the visible shaft covers." **A visible surface clue can manufacture confident marks.** The card therefore removes every visible clue, photographs the field to prove it, and scores false positives explicitly — because a trial that leaves a shaft cover in view measures expectation, not detection.
3. **The source's own next experiment was repeatability, and it was never run.** The report explicitly asks for the same dowser to walk the same field several times, warns that memory can fake repeatability, and forbids blindfolding because it degrades the operator. **The card runs that experiment as its second endpoint** — a repeat walk with a different start point and direction, the first plan sealed and unseen — which makes this the queue's first **within-operator test–retest** measurement.
4. **A null here is a null for this protocol, not a proof of nothing.** The source itself refuses that inference ("too primitively and too summarily"). The card repeats the refusal verbatim in its framing: a negative result retires *this* protocol's claim, not the whole tradition.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that concordance is at or near the shuffled baseline and no agreed zone lands on the buried feature — i.e. the 1946 null reproduces at home scale. **That is a complete, useful result** — it files the first home test of the founding controlled dowsing experiment and delivers the repeatability experiment the source itself asked for. A concordant zone that lands on the hidden feature, repeatably, would be genuinely surprising and would justify the taller follow-up (more fields, more operators, a pre-registered geophysical measurement on the agreed zone).

## Why it matters

- It is the queue's **first card whose endpoint is inter-observer concordance** — do independent blind operators agree with *each other*? Every prior dowsing card scores one operator against a hidden target; this scores the group against itself, then against the feature.
- It is the queue's **first card built on a published controlled NULL result** — the source is a negative study, so the expected outcome is a null and a positive is the surprise. (Card 041 is the queue's first *already-refuted claim*, but that is a physics claim overturned by later literature; this is a null-result *study* being replicated.)
- It is the queue's **first card whose source is the founding controlled dowsing experiment** — the first time a geophysics institute tested dowsers under blind conditions (ETH Zürich, 1944/46).
- It is the queue's **first within-operator test–retest** measurement — and it runs the exact follow-up the source named and never performed.
- It is genuinely **community-scale**: four or more people, a marked field, a buried hose, and a referee — no instrument, no lab, no cost beyond string and envelopes.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$0–20** — a garden hose (or a length of pipe/board) to bury, wooden stakes and string to grid the field, graph paper and pencils for the plans, envelopes and tape for the sealed keys, and a camera/phone.
- Safety: **low.** No chemicals, no mains wiring, no voltage. The only real hazards are a spade and a hose — **call 811 / your local utility-locate service before digging anywhere near a real utility line.** The card's own buried feature is a garden hose you laid yourself; never dig blind near real infrastructure.
- Accessibility: an afternoon to lay and grid the field; ~20–30 minutes per dowser per walk; four dowsers × two walks fits a weekend.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A garden hose** (15–30 m) or a length of pipe/board — the buried feature. Lay it in a gentle zigzag, cover it, and **record its true path with stakes you then remove/hide**.
- **Wooden stakes and string** — to mark the field boundary and a **1 m grid** (a 10 × 10 m field is ample).
- **Graph paper and pencils** — one plan per dowser per walk, with the field boundary and grid drawn to scale.
- **Envelopes and tape** — for sealing each plan on the day it is made.
- **A camera/phone** — to photograph the field (proving no visible clue), the hidden path, the sealed plans, and the unsealing.
- **A referee** — the key-holder. Ideally someone who does not dowse.

## Protocol (two-endpoint blind concordance trial, pre-registered)

**Phase 1 — build and blind the field.**
1. **Write the pre-registration sheet before anything else:** the field size and grid, the number of dowsers, the number of walks, the concordance statistic, the shuffled-baseline procedure, the detection radius, and the criteria below. Photograph it.
2. **Lay the feature** (a hose in a gentle zigzag), cover it, record its true path on a master plan, then **remove or hide every surface clue** (stakes, spoil, footprints). **Photograph the finished field from several angles to prove nothing is visible.**
3. **Pre-sweep the field:** have the referee walk it and note any obvious feature (a tree, a drain, a slope) that a dowser might mark for non-dowsing reasons. Record it.

**Phase 2 — run the dowsers, blind and independent.**
4. **Recruit N ≥ 4 dowsers** (family + neighbours). Each is assigned a **companion** and a **separate time**; **dowsers must not meet, talk, or see each other's marks.**
5. **Each dowser walks the field once (walk 1)**, blind to the feature and to the other dowsers, and marks on a plan **every spot where the rod deflects** (as an area, a line, or a point — the source required no interpretation). The companion records it. **The plan is sealed in an envelope the same day.**
6. **Repeat walk (walk 2), for repeatability:** the same dowser walks the field again **after a gap, from a different start point and in the opposite direction**, with **walk 1's plan sealed and unseen**. This is the source's own named next step; the changed start/direction is the card's control against memory.

**Phase 3 — open, overlay, score.**
7. **Open all envelopes only after every dowser has finished both walks.** The referee overlays the plans on the master grid.
8. **Concordance:** count grid cells marked by **≥ 2 dowsers**. Report the **fraction of the field with ≥ 2 agreement**.
9. **Chance baseline (the card's key addition):** for each dowser's plan, apply a **random rotation/offset**, recompute the ≥ 2-agreement fraction, and repeat **1,000 times** → the chance distribution. Score the observed concordance against it.
10. **Detection:** does any **≥ 2-agreement cell** lie within **0.5 m of the true feature path**?
11. **Repeatability (walk 1 vs walk 2, same dowser):** the fraction of walk-1 marked cells that walk 2 also marks — **per dowser**, and compared to the same shuffled baseline.
12. **False positives:** does any dowser mark a "main" where there is none? List them.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (the claim survives — surprising, needs follow-up):** a **≥ 2-agreement zone lies within 0.5 m of the true feature path** AND the observed concordance **exceeds the shuffled baseline (p < 0.05)** in **≥ 2 of 3 fields** (run the trial on ≥ 3 separate hidden paths), with the field proven clue-free. Report as surprising; needs more fields and operators before any conclusion.
- **PASS (the claim is refuted — the expected, complete result):** **no** ≥ 2-agreement zone lands on the true feature AND the observed concordance is **within the shuffled baseline**. This reproduces the 1946 null at home scale. Reported as the expected result, not a failure (Skeptic's Star).
- **FAIL:** concordance is explained by a **visible surface clue**, by dowsers having **seen each other's marks**, or by a **shared systematic direction** (e.g. everyone marks the same tree) — the trial measured expectation, not detection.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** fewer than 4 dowsers; the feature was visible or the field carried an obvious clue; dowsers communicated or overlapped; the field was not pre-swept; the shuffled baseline was not computed; or walk 2 was run without a changed start point (memory uncontrolled).
- **ARTIFACT:** concordance tracks the **grid lines**, the **field boundary**, the **companion's position**, or the **time of day** rather than the feature; or repeatability is explained by the dowser re-walking the same **route** rather than re-finding the same **spots**.

## Confounds (named in advance)

- **No chance baseline — the central confound.** Sixteen (or four) dowsers each marking large areas will overlap by chance. **The shuffled-map baseline is the only thing that separates agreement from coincidence**; without it, "they agree" is unfalsifiable.
- **Visible surface clues.** The source's own false positives on field B came from **visible shaft covers**. **Remove every clue and photograph the field to prove it.**
- **Mutual influence.** The source's own strength was isolating the dowsers. **Dowsers must not meet, talk, or see each other's marks.**
- **Memory faking repeatability.** The source warns that a dowser may re-mark from memory. **Walk 2 uses a different start point and direction, with walk 1 sealed.**
- **Shared route, not shared spots.** Two walks along the same path can look repeatable while marking different spots. **Score cell overlap, not route overlap.**
- **Companion influence.** A companion who knows the field can steer. **The companion must not know the feature's path.**
- **The null over-read.** A negative does not prove dowsing has no cause — the source itself refuses that inference. **The card tests this protocol's claim only, and says so.**

## Verdict rules

A ≥ 2-agreement zone lands on the hidden feature, above the shuffled baseline, in ≥ 2 of 3 fields → **the claim survives** (surprising; needs more fields and operators). No agreed zone on the feature and concordance within the shuffled baseline → **the claim is refuted at home scale** (the expected, complete result — the 1946 null reproduced). Concordance explained by a visible clue or by communication → **the trial measured expectation, not detection.** Fewer than 4 dowsers, a visible clue, communication, or no shuffled baseline → **inconclusive, not a refutation.**
