## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The dowsing tradition holds that places above which the rod deflects are **"irradiated"** by **"earth rays"** (*aardstralen*), and that these places harm man, animal and plant — and that **"shielding"** apparatus can neutralise them. The TNO/KNAW working group was set up to test exactly that, and stated its own design requirement:

> "The care of animal and crop and the assessment of the results must be done by persons who are unacquainted with whether or not the objects are irradiated."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does a spot the dowser calls "irradiated" differ from a **matched control spot** in what grows on it — measured **blind**, and pooled **across households**?

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the claim and one thorough negative test of it.** The 1955 KNAW working group ran the largest controlled test of the dowsing rod then attempted: **19 Frisian dairy sheds** (three professional dowsers, blind), an **experimental apiary**, **potato-eelworm and Fusarium-wilt plots**, and the **marketed shielding boxes**. Its findings, quoted from the translation:

- **Livestock:** "on the stands where one or more of them got a reaction, no more objections occurred than on the stands without a dowsing reaction."
- **Bees:** two matched stands of 10 colonies, one on a dowsed path and one on unirradiated ground — average yield **8.75 kg vs 8.82 kg**, spring brood **2.5 vs 2 frames**, bees **5 vs 5.4 frames**. "Of a favourable influence of the location above a water vein, then, nothing has appeared."
- **Crops:** the Povelni apparatus showed no effect on potato eelworm — "the degree of infestation on the screened plot proved to have risen sharply". "Of a favourable action of the Povelni apparatus nothing was to be noticed."
- **The dowsers themselves:** three professionals gave "entirely different paths", and one dowser's own two surveys ten months apart "showed no agreement".

Four things the card must say out loud, because they are what a family is actually testing:

1. **The claim is a claim, not an established result.** The earth-ray doctrine comes from the geobiology/radiesthesia lineage and has no controlled positive replication in the archive. The one thorough controlled test of it is negative. The card is a test, not an endorsement.
2. **The expected result is a null, and that is a complete result.** The 1955 study predicts no difference. A clean null is a genuine finding about the method — exactly what the "Skeptic's Star" honours — and it is worth as much as a positive.
3. **The measurement is a plant, and the plant is the instrument** — so the test **must be blind** and the person who tends the pair must not know which spot is dowsed. Otherwise the result measures expectation, not earth rays. This is the source's own hard condition, carried into the card verbatim.
4. **A positive would need the paired difference to hold across households and repeat.** A single pair that does better on the dowsed spot is noise; the strong version is a pooled dowsed-spot advantage that repeats in a second sowing and holds across at least three households.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that the dowsed and control spots do not differ, and the honest verdict is *"a dowsed spot is not distinguishable from a matched control spot in what grows on it, at home scale, in this run."* A clean, replicating positive would be genuinely interesting and would justify the taller follow-up (a multi-site pre-registered trial with a second dowser and a soil-chemistry arm).

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$0–20** — a packet of fast seeds (~$2), two matched plots or two large pots per pair (~$0–10), labels and a log sheet (~$0), and a **referee** (a neighbour or family member who is not the tender). No instrument, no mains work, no chemicals.
- Safety: **low.** No high voltage, no chemicals, no heat. Ordinary garden hygiene.
- Accessibility: a family can run it in a garden bed, a raised box, or on a balcony in pots. The distributed design means **one household's small N is not the experiment** — the neighbourhood's pooled N is.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Seeds** — one fast, uniform variety (radish, lettuce, or bean), enough for every pair, from a single packet so the seed lot is constant.
- **Two matched spots per pair** — same soil, same sun, same watering, ~1–3 m apart, or two identical pots of the same soil mix.
- **Labels** — A and B for each pair.
- **A referee** — the person who labels the spots and holds the key. **The referee is not the tender.**
- **A dowsing rod or pendulum** — the dowser's own instrument.
- **A log sheet** and a **camera** — for pre-registration, the counts, and the evidence.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — detector check (mandatory).**
1. Prove the **dowser can produce a repeatable reading at all**: have them locate a *present*, known target (a buried hose with water running, or a spot they have previously found responsive) and repeat it. **If the dowser cannot produce a stable, repeatable reading on a present source, a no-difference result is VOID, not a null.** "No difference" and "the dowser was not working" are different results and get filed identically if this check is skipped.

**Phase 1 — dowse the ground (blind to the test design).**
2. The dowser walks the household's ground and **marks every spot where the rod deflects** (the "irradiated" spots). The dowser is told only that the household wants its ground read — not which spots will be planted or how they will be scored.

**Phase 2 — matched pairs.**
3. For each dowsed spot, the household selects a **matched control spot** the dowser says is **not** irradiated: same soil, same sun, same slope, same watering. Record the pairing on the pre-registration sheet.

**Phase 3 — blind planting.**
4. The **referee** labels each pair's two spots **A** and **B** from a pre-shuffled list and keeps the key sealed. The **tender** plants the same seeds in both and tends them identically, **without knowing which is which**.

**Phase 4 — measure.**
5. Record **germination %** (count emerged seedlings), **seedling height at 14 and 28 days**, and **any disease or death**. Photograph each pair at each measurement.

**Phase 5 — distribute and pool.**
6. At least **three households** run their own pairs. The referee opens all keys only after every household's final measurement, then the neighbourhood **pools the paired differences** (dowsed − control) and scores them together.
7. Repeat the whole sowing a **second time** (a second crop cycle) to test whether any effect repeats.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** the pooled mean paired difference **favours the dowsed spots** (higher germination and/or height) at **p < 0.05** on a paired test, **and** the direction **repeats in the second sowing**, **and** the effect holds across at least three households.
- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** the pooled mean paired difference is **indistinguishable from zero** — the 1955 null reproduced at home scale (Skeptic's Star).
- **FAIL:** the blinding was broken (the tender knew which spot was which), or the pairs were not matched (different soil, sun, or water), or the dowsed/control assignment was not made by the dowser.
- **VOID:** Phase 0 failed (the dowser could not produce a repeatable reading on a present source), or the key was opened before the final tally. A VOID is not a FAIL.
