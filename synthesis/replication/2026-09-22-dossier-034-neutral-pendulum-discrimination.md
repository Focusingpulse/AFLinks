## The claim (as asserted by the source)

A **neutral pendulum** — a plain weight on a string, with no built-in shape or angle — is an *extension of the operator's own energy field*. **String length** is the tuning mechanism: each length acts as an "antenna" tuned to a particular vibrational quality. When the operator tunes to a quality and brings the pendulum over a thing that carries that quality, they feel **a pull**. When the tuned quality is absent, there is **no pull** — the pendulum simply swings back and forth.

The demonstration in the source is **polarity testing on a battery**: tune the string length to the battery's positive pole, and the pull appears over that pole.

The source's own hard condition, quoted from the video record's assessment:

> "an external skeptic will never believe it whether your hand is rock still or not"

— i.e. **an unblinded result is worthless**. The instructor says this himself. That is why blinding is not an optional refinement of this card; it is the card.

The source also draws a distinction that must not be collapsed: **vibrational radiesthesia** (physical, instrument-based, tuned by string length / shape / angle) is presented as the precise method, and **mental dowsing** (programming the pendulum with words or questions) as the imprecise one. The card tests the *physical* method only.

**Honest status of the claim.** Radiesthesia is classified as pseudoscience. The claimed mechanism — a "vibrational quality" carried by an object and tuned for by string length — has **no accepted physical basis and no instrument that measures it**. The *effect* the source claims — a discrimination the operator can make, blind, better than chance — **is** falsifiable, cheaply, with a coin and a coded container. The card tests the effect and says nothing about the mechanism.

**The confound, stated plainly, because it is the whole design.** The signal is **operator-reported**. If the operator can see, feel, or infer which container holds which pole, the result is not a measurement — it is a guess with extra steps. Everything depends on the blinding being real: the operator must not know the target's identity, and the coding must survive until the last number is written. A second confound is **ideomotor movement** — the operator's own hand may move the pendulum without intent. The blind design does not remove ideomotor movement; it removes the operator's *knowledge*, which is what makes a hit informative either way. Stated, not hidden.

**Why it is worth testing anyway, stated plainly.** Three reasons:

1. **The endpoint is a number against a computed baseline.** This is rare in the archive's subtle-energy holdings, which mostly end in a perception. A blind hit rate against chance is exactly the kind of endpoint the engine's honesty rubric wants — and it is the same endpoint the queue's existing dowsing card uses, which means the two cards can be compared directly.
2. **It is a skill, and the queue has almost no skill cards.** Every other card tests a *device* or a *treatment*. This tests whether a family member can learn a measurement skill and whether it survives blinding. That is a different kind of self-reliance asset.
3. **It is the natural companion to the dowsing card, not a duplicate of it.** Card 029 (Psi-Track Dowsing) tests whether an *intense thought leaves a track in space* that a blind dowser can follow to a hidden object — a sender/receiver design. This card tests whether a *tuned instrument discriminates a known physical property* (battery polarity) with no sender at all. Different claim, different apparatus, different design. The overlap is the pendulum and the blinding discipline, and it is stated here, not hidden.

**Under-promise, stated plainly.** A family cannot resolve a 5% effect with 20 trials. The design below is sized to detect a **large** effect (a real operator should be near-perfect open), not a subtle one. A null result here refutes the *strong* claim (that the pull is a reliable discriminator), not a weak one. Say that in the report.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build cost: **~$10–20** (a pendulum, or a bob and a thread; a few identical batteries; an opaque container or bag; a coin for randomisation). No mains power, no chemicals.
- Safety: **none beyond ordinary care** — no heat, no sharp edges, no electrical hazard (batteries are not connected to anything). A coin cell or AA is fine.
- Accessibility: one evening. The whole protocol is a single session, which is why it is the cheapest card in the queue alongside the element-day sowing test.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A neutral pendulum** — a plain bob on a thread, ~20–30 cm. A purchased neutral pendulum is fine (~$10–20); a small brass or wooden bead on a doubled thread is a valid substitute. **No shape-tuned or angle-tuned instrument** — this card tests the string-length method specifically.
- **A set of identical targets** — 6–10 identical batteries (AA or coin cells) of the same type, so the operator cannot tell them apart by appearance.
- **An opaque container or bag** — one per trial, or one container reused with a coded label, so the operator cannot see the target.
- **A coin** (or a random-number source) for randomising polarity.
- **A pen and a pre-registration sheet.**
- **A second person** (a child is ideal) to prepare and code the trials.

## Protocol

### Setup

1. **Calibrate the operator (open).** The operator tunes the string length to a **known positive pole** and confirms they can feel the pull. Record the string length. This is the operator's calibration; it is held constant for the rest of the session.
2. **Pre-register.** Write down, before day 1: the number of trials in each arm, the pass/fail thresholds below, the chance model, the scoring rule, and who holds the key. Photograph the sheet.
3. **Fix the scoring rule.** A trial is a **hit** when the operator reports "pull" on a positive pole, or "no pull" on a negative pole. Anything else is a miss. Chance = 50% for a two-way design.

### Arm 1 — open, positive control (20 trials)

The operator **knows** each battery's polarity. This arm answers one question: *can this operator produce the signal at all under known conditions?*

1. The second person presents a battery, polarity known to both, and the operator reports pull / no-pull.
2. 20 trials, alternating polarity so neither side is over-represented.
3. Score hits.

**This arm is the design's spine.** If it fails, the blind arm cannot refute the claim — it can only show that this operator cannot produce the signal even when they know the answer. That is an *inconclusive* result, not a refutation, and it must be reported as such.

### Arm 2 — blind (40 trials)

1. The second person randomises polarity **by coin** for each trial, places the battery in an opaque container, and codes it (A, B, C, … or a written key) **without the operator seeing**.
2. The operator, holding the calibrated string length, reports **pull** or **no pull** for each container — and writes the report down **before** the container is opened.
3. The second person records the report and keeps the key sealed until all 40 trials are done.
4. **Unblind only after the last number is written.**

### Sham block (10 trials)

Interleaved with Arm 2, or run as a separate short block: **10 empty containers** (no battery). The operator reports pull / no-pull on each. A pull on an empty container is a **false positive**, and the false-positive rate is reported separately. This is the control for "the operator reports a pull because they expect one."

### Optional second operator

Run the whole protocol with a second family member. Two operators with the same result moves this dossier from `protocol` to `replicated` or `refuted`. One operator is an anecdote.

## Pass / fail criteria (pre-registered)

- **PASS:** Arm 1 **≥ 16/20 (80%)** AND Arm 2 hit rate **≥ 27/40 (67.5%)** with a binomial test **p < 0.05** (one-tailed, chance = 0.5) AND sham false-positive rate **≤ 2/10 (20%)**; **and** the direction repeats in a second run with a fresh set. → The pull discriminates, blind, at home scale.
- **FAIL:** Arm 1 **≥ 16/20** (the operator demonstrably can produce the signal open) AND Arm 2 hit rate **within chance** (16–24/40, p > 0.05). → The signal does not survive blinding. **Skeptic's Star** material: this would be the first honest home burial of the neutral-pendulum discrimination claim.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** Arm 1 **< 16/20** (the operator cannot produce the signal even open — the blind arm is uninformative, **not** a refutation); the key was opened before the final tally; the calibration string length was changed mid-run; the trial count was not pre-registered; or the sham false-positive rate exceeds 20% (the operator reports pulls without discrimination).

Report the arms **separately**. An open-arm failure and a blind-arm failure mean different things and must not be merged into one verdict.

## What would change the verdict

- A **second independent operator** running the same protocol with the same result moves this dossier from `protocol` to `replicated` or `refuted`. One hand is an anecdote; two is a datum.
- **Larger N** would let the design detect a smaller effect. 40 trials detects roughly a 20-point lift above chance; a subtle operator needs hundreds of trials and a pre-registered effect size.
- **A mechanical pendulum** (a motorised or remotely-observed rig) would remove the ideomotor confound entirely. That is the cleanest upgrade and the natural follow-up dossier — but it also removes the operator, which is the thing the claim is about.
- A **positive** result would be the first home-scale support for the string-length tuning claim. A **negative** would be the first honest burial of it, and would be worth publishing just as loudly.
