## The claim (as asserted by the sources)

The report describes the apparatus plainly:

> "The water is placed at, or made to flow through, a strong (usually static) magnetic field, usually > 0.3 T. In some treaters the water flow is influenced by the water being made to pass through part of the treater; in other cases the treater is placed directly on an existing pipe, i.e. only a purely indirect influence — no hydrodynamic influence due to changed flow geometry."

and reports the plant effects it has collected:

> "Generally the plants appear to get better root development, at least at the beginning of their growth. In some cases it has been noted that the increased root growth then shifts over toward increased fruit development, compared with untreated water."

> "Increased yield (11.8%) of and increased content of sugar (24.6%), lycopene (25.5%), extracted protein (6.6%) and trace elements in tomatoes, and greater growth during the early part of the plant's development (71% greater root and dry weight during this period) in a study by a Chinese agricultural research institute."

> "Increase in the yield of third-season pumpkin plants in Australia (normally a low yield in the last season) by 210% compared with expected harvest."

The Hungarian source records a specific trial:

> "In Immokalee, Florida, Dr. Everett conducted experiments on the Sunny open-field tomato variety. The plants planted in foil-covered, raised beds were irrigated with magnetically treated water, by drip. Even more surprising than the 12.5% crop increase was the realization that in the leaves of the treated-water plants there is less sulfur, manganese, copper, and zinc, and in their fruit the concentration of phosphorus, magnesium, calcium, potassium, copper, manganese, and zinc is lower; but of every nutrient element an adequate amount was available."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does running irrigation water past a strong static magnet change the **crop** — or is the crop responding to something else (water quantity, season, soil, position, plant-to-plant variation)?

## Honest status of the claim

**The report is honest about its own evidence, and the card must be too.** Its plants section is a compilation of **commercial-cultivation reports and a couple of scientific studies**, gathered across *several* treater types (Grander, Plus & Minus, Plocher, CIR, Nilemark, Gyllene strålen) — not a set of controlled magnetic-only trials. The report says so, and it names two things the family must carry into the test:

> "One may note that these high growth figures come from a relatively dry climate, where water availability can play a large role."

> "As regards reported effects on various plants, e.g. agricultural products, a more unified agronomic approach should be taken to assess the meaning of the results."

And on the magnetic treater itself:

> "There is a very large number of magnetic water treaters on the market, and this is a field that has been illuminated somewhat in the published scientific literature. From having been a controversial field, in the last 10–15 years better and better evidence has emerged for the effects of these — among other things on crystallization of calcium carbonate."

Note what that last sentence does and does not say: the *established* magnetic-water result is about **lime/scale crystallization**, not about crops. The Agcaoili 2025 review, which is specifically about magnetized irrigation, quantifies large effect sizes and then names the gaps — **standardisation, mechanism, and long-term validation** — i.e. the field's own authors say the evidence is not settled.

**So the honest position is:** the claim is plausible enough to test cheaply and important enough to be worth testing; the mechanism is not established; the effect is reported to be installation-dependent; and the expected result in a well-matched home trial is that **the two beds perform the same**. A clean null is a complete result.

| | Ordinary (no lasting magnetic effect) | Magnetic treatment changes the water |
|---|---|---|
| **Crop response** | the magnetized and matched-control beds perform the same | the magnetized bed grows more / yields more |
| **Water use** | the same water is needed for the same yield | less water is needed for the same yield |
| **The treater swap** | swapping the treater to the other bed changes nothing | the effect follows the treater |
| **The sham treater** | a hose with the same mass of non-magnetic metal performs like the control | the sham performs like the control, the real treater does not |

Four things the card must say out loud:

1. **The claim is a claim, not an established result.** Magnetic water treatment is a contested field. The *established* magnetic result is about scale in pipes; the crop effect is reported, not settled.
2. **The expected result is the null, and that is a complete result.** The card says so before the family starts. A clean "the beds performed the same" outcome is a genuine finding — exactly what the "Skeptic's Star" honours.
3. **The controls are the experiment.** Matched beds, matched plumbing, and a **sham treater** (same mass of non-magnetic metal) are the whole design. Without them the family has grown a garden, not tested a claim.
4. **A positive would need to repeat and to survive the swap.** A single season's difference is weather and position. The strong version is a difference that reproduces across two crops and **follows the treater when it is swapped**.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that the two beds are indistinguishable, and that any difference seen in the first season is explained by position, water volume, or weather. That is the honest verdict, and the card is designed to reach it cleanly.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$20–60** — a set of neodymium magnets (N42 or stronger, ~$15–30), a length of garden hose or irrigation pipe (~$5–15), hose clamps or zip ties (~$3), a second plain hose of the **same length and diameter** for the control (~$5–15), a kitchen scale, a measuring jug, and a log sheet. A **sham treater** (the same mass of non-magnetic metal — a stack of steel washers or a fishing weight) costs a few dollars more.
- Safety: **low.** Neodymium magnets are strong — keep them away from pacemakers, credit cards, and small children (a swallowed pair is a surgical emergency), and be careful not to pinch fingers between them.
- Accessibility: any garden, balcony, or windowsill with two matched containers. No mains work, no lab.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A set of neodymium magnets** (N42+, ~$15–30) — clamped around the hose in an alternating array.
- **A length of garden hose or irrigation pipe** — the treater body.
- **A second plain hose of the same length and diameter** — the matched-plumbing control.
- **A sham treater** — the same mass of non-magnetic metal clamped on the control hose (separates "magnets" from "a lump on the hose").
- **Two matched beds or containers** — same soil, same volume, same sun.
- **One seed lot**, split between the beds.
- **A kitchen scale** and **a measuring jug**.
- **A log sheet** and a camera.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — pre-register (mandatory).**
1. Write down, before planting: the crop and variety, the seed lot, the soil and its source, the bed positions, the watering schedule, the magnet array, the scoring rule, and the thresholds. Photograph the sheet.

**Phase 1 — build the treater.**
2. Clamp the magnets around the hose in an alternating array (north–south–north–south) so the water passes through a strong static field. Record the magnet grade and the number used.

**Phase 2 — matched beds.**
3. Fill two beds with the **same soil**, sow the **same seed lot**, and place them so they get the **same sun**. Water them on the **same schedule with the same measured volume** — one through the treater, one through the plain hose of the same length. (If a third bed is available, water it through the **sham treater**.)

**Phase 3 — measure.**
4. Record, per bed: **germination date**, **plant height and leaf count** at fixed intervals, **total water used**, and **harvest weight** (and, if you like, sugar or taste). Photograph the beds side by side at each measurement.

**Phase 4 — the treater swap.**
5. Mid-season (or in the second crop), **swap the treater to the other bed**. If the effect is the water, it should follow the treater; if it is the bed's position or soil, it should not.

**Phase 5 — repeat.**
6. Run the whole thing across **two crops** (or two seasons). A single season is weather.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (no effect — the expected, complete result):** the two beds are **indistinguishable** in germination, growth and harvest weight, and use the same water — and the sham bed performs like the control (Skeptic's Star).
- **PASS (effect — surprising):** the magnetized bed yields **more** (and/or uses **less water** for the same yield) **reproducibly across two crops**, in a matched design, with the **sham treater performing like the control** and the effect **following the treater when it is swapped**.
- **FAIL:** the beds were not matched (different soil, seed lot, sun or position); the **water volume was not matched**; the comparison was confounded by bed position; or **one season** was read as a result.
- **VOID:** the crop failed for unrelated reasons (pest, frost, drought, transplant shock) — a VOID is not a NULL.
