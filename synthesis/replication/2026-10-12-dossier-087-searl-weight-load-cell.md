## The claim (as asserted by the sources)

**"A rotating permanent-magnet ring-and-roller assembly loses weight at a critical speed."** Searl's own account (via Sandberg) is the strongest form: his 1952 generator, *"without ceasing to rotate, began to rise upwards, detached from the motor and soared to a height of about 50 feet… in the end the generator accelerated to a fantastic speed and disappeared from view, probably departing into space."* Searl's stated power-to-mass ratio for a one-ring generator: **180 kW/tonne**.

The one modern measured version is Roschin & Godin (2000): **35 % weight reduction at 6–7 kW** on a 110 kg stator / 115 kg rotor rare-earth-magnet converter, measured on a **350 kg platform mounted on springs and shock absorbers**, with "weight" read from an **inductive displacement transducer**.

**Searl's manufacturing procedure** (Sandberg, via private communication) — the part that makes the magnets "conditioned":

1. During magnetization a small alternating current (~100 mA) of radio frequency (~10 MHz) is added to the direct current.
2. At least 180 ampere-turns are required.
3. All magnets in one generator must be magnetized simultaneously.
4. Material: ferrite or magnetic ceramic.

## Honest status of the claim

**The claim is recorded as `died`.** The record's grounds are `replication-failure`, `mechanism-superseded` and `dismissal`, and the record's own corpus supplies the counterweight:

- **Roschin & Godin's own small-prototype test found nothing** — no self-acceleration, no magnetic walls, no thermal walls (`doc:5403`). The authors of the one modern measurement could not reproduce their own effect at smaller scale.
- **The movement's own device database has every measurement field blank** (`doc:2392774`) — no watts generated, no % over-unity, no % weight loss, no witness, no date, no tester.
- **The documentation burned in 1983**, so every reconstruction rests on Searl's memory (`doc:2752`).
- **The modern unit is a mockup** of magnetic levitation and eddy currents, not a working generator (`doc:1509856`, `doc:1509159`).
- **The 1991 Heerfordt investigation could verify nothing**, and found a son of Searl who had seen disks *suspended from wires* for photography but had never seen an antigravity demonstration (`doc:1460071`).
- **The mechanism was never fixed and has no successor** — it drifts across the corpus from "free electrons in spinning bodies" to "a new magnetic effect" to "the Law of the Squares" to "the zero-point field" to "the ether in a sense of Faraday-Maxwell-Mie". The only real physics in the lineage is the weak premise Searl started from — the radial voltage in a spinning conductor (the homopolar / Faraday effect), which is ordinary physics.

**What is nevertheless testable** — and this is the whole justification for the card — is that the record's `retry_now` names the weight claim as measurable *and names exactly how the 2000 measurement failed to measure it*. The 2000 apparatus could not separate **thrust** from **vibration** and from **magnetic force on the transducer**, because it floated the whole platform on springs and read displacement. A rigid load cell plus a matched-mass non-magnetic rotor separates all three.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the expected result is a **refutation** — no weight difference beyond the noise floor between the magnet rotor and the matched-mass non-magnetic rotor at any speed. That is a complete, useful result and earns the Skeptic's Star. A clean, speed-scaling, magnet-specific weight change would be the surprise, and would require a second independent family before it is believed.

## The apparatus

| Item | Spec | ~Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Rotor | A rigid disc or ring carrying permanent magnets (ferrite or neodymium), balanced, on a shaft | $20–60 |
| Drive | Small DC motor with speed control (PWM driver or a variable bench supply); a variable-speed drill is an acceptable substitute | $20–60 |
| **Rigid mount** | Motor + rotor bolted to a **rigid frame** (aluminium angle / plywood gusseted) that rests on the scale — **no springs, no shock absorbers, no rubber feet** | $10–30 |
| **Scale** | Digital scale, capacity ≥ 5 kg, resolution ≤ 0.1 g — or a **beam load cell (5 kg) + HX711 + microcontroller** so the reading is logged continuously | $20–80 |
| Tachometer | Optical or IR tachometer to record actual speed (a phone tachometer app is acceptable) | $0–25 |
| Power meter | USB power meter or multimeter on the motor supply, to record input power | $10–30 |
| **Enclosure** | A shroud/box around the rotor so its own airflow does not press on the scale | $10–20 |
| **Control rotor** | An identical disc carrying **matched-mass NON-magnetic** weights in place of the magnets (matched to within 2 % of the magnet rotor's total mass) | $5–15 |
| Gaussmeter (optional) | For the supporting field-at-distance observation | $30–60 |

**Total: ~$150–300.** Home/homelab; no lab, no chemicals, no mains wiring beyond a low-voltage motor supply.

**Why the control is the whole card.** The magnet rotor and the control rotor differ in **exactly one thing: whether the rotating mass is magnetic.** Same mass, same shape, same speed, same airflow, same mount, same scale. Any weight difference between them is attributable to magnetism, not to rotation — and any weight change that appears in *both* is rotation, airflow or vibration, which is precisely the confound that made the 2000 measurement uninterpretable.

## The protocol

1. **Pre-register everything before anyone runs** — the rotor mass, the magnet mass, the speed points, the run count, the control, the enclosure, the scale and its resolution, the threshold, and the analysis — and photograph the sheet. Target speed points: **0, 500, 1000, 2000, 3000 rpm** (or the motor's achievable maximum), each held for 30 s.
2. **Build and balance the magnet rotor.** Weigh it. Mount the motor and rotor **rigidly** on the frame; place the frame on the scale inside the enclosure. Confirm the rotor is balanced (no more than the noise floor of scale fluctuation when spun).
3. **Static control.** Motor off, magnets in place: record the scale reading for 60 s. This tests whether the static magnetic field affects the scale or the load cell.
4. **Magnet run.** Spin to each speed point in ascending order; at each, hold 30 s and record the scale reading (g, averaged over the hold) and the motor input power (W). Return to 0 between points. Repeat the sweep **≥ 5 times**.
5. **Control run.** Swap in the matched-mass non-magnetic rotor. Repeat the identical sweep, identical run count, identical order.
6. **Second set.** Repeat steps 4–5 on a different day, with the operator blind to which rotor is which if a second person can do the swap.
7. **Compute** Δ(speed) = mean(magnet run) − mean(control run) at each matched speed, with the spread.
8. **Supporting observation (recorded, not scored).** With the assembly static, measure the magnetic field at 0.1, 1, 3, 5, 10 and 15 m from the assembly with a Gaussmeter — the "magnetic walls" claim of 0.05 T out to 15 m. Record the numbers and the ambient background at each distance.

**Measurable outcome:** the weight difference **Δ in grams between the magnet rotor and the matched-mass non-magnetic rotor at each matched speed**, and whether Δ scales with speed.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim survives — the surprise):** a repeatable **|Δ| ≥ 5 g** that (a) appears **only** in the magnet run, (b) **scales with speed**, (c) **vanishes** when the magnets are replaced by matched-mass non-magnetic weights, and (d) **replicates in a second set** — the weight claim is detectable at home scale and needs the taller follow-up (a demagnetised-magnet arm, a force plate, a blind operator).
- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** **|Δ| below the noise floor at every speed in both sets** — a rotating magnet assembly does not change weight at home scale (Skeptic's Star).
- **FAIL (void — not a null):** the two rotors do not match in mass (within 2 %); the mount is not rigid (springs, rubber feet, a wobbly frame); the rotor is not enclosed and its airflow reaches the scale; the motor's own vibration or torque is not separated; or the scale is saturated or the speed was not measured.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** fewer than 5 runs per condition; one set only; no non-magnetic control; scale resolution coarser than 1 g; the enclosure was opened mid-run; or the operator knew which rotor was which and adjusted.
- **ARTIFACT:** the reading tracks the **motor's own vibration/torque** (it appears in **both** runs); the reading tracks **scale thermal drift** (it appears with the motor off); the reading tracks **enclosure airflow**; or the reading **reverses when the rotor is turned over** — a magnetic-force-on-the-scale artifact, not a weight change.

## Confounds this design controls (and the one it cannot)

| Confound | Control |
|---|---|
| Vibration / motor torque on the transducer — **the 2000 failure** | Rigid mount on a load cell; the matched-mass control rotor has the same vibration and torque |
| Airflow from the spinning rotor pressing on the scale | Enclosure; and the control rotor has identical geometry and speed |
| Static magnetic field pulling on the scale or load cell | Static control (step 3); rotor-over test (ARTIFACT) |
| Thermal drift of the scale | Static control; second set on a different day |
| Operator expectation | Blind swap where possible; pre-registered thresholds |
| **A home rotor is not the SEG** | **Not controllable — stated plainly below** |

**The limitation the card cannot remove.** A home rotor is **not** the Searl Effect Generator. The SEG's own geometry — concentric magnetised rings with rollers orbiting them, proportioned by the "Law of the Squares" — is specified in `doc:2753`, but no working device survives and the documentation burned in 1983. So a null here **bounds the claim at home scale**; it does not disprove an effect in a large device. Two things make the test worth running anyway: (1) the claimed effect is **35 % of a 225 kg assembly** — a 35 % weight change is enormous, and a mechanism that produces it in a 225 kg device should produce *something* in a 1 kg one; and (2) the archive's **own** small-prototype test found nothing at a *larger* scale than this card's, so the card replicates the archive's own null at a different scale rather than inventing a new one.
