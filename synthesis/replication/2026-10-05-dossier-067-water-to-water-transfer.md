## The claim (as asserted by the source)

**Primary source.** Prof. Dr. Bernd Kröplin's Stuttgart water-research group ("Welt im Tropfen" / World in the Drop) has for more than 15 years studied information storage in water, examining drying drops under the dark-field microscope. Its own account states, verbatim:

> "For we have established that water drops **exchange information over a distance of about 1.5 m in the laboratory and influence one another**." — Prof. Dr. Bernd Kröplin

The group extends the claim to people: adults (≈70% body water) sitting in a bus or lecture hall "have already exchanged information among themselves before they have even greeted one another: they are a great 'water-information soup'."

**Convergence source.** A Dutch overview article (waterandlight.eu) reports an Italian study of waters from so-called "magical" places (Lourdes, Fatima, Medjugorje): "the high frequencies of the water from Medjugorje were **spontaneously transferred to ordinary tap water from Milan**. This happened not only when the water samples were mixed, but also **when they were placed close to one another**. Water thus proved capable of emitting and receiving frequencies."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** if a sealed jar of plain water sits about a metre from a sealed jar of *structured* water — no contact, no mixing, no field applied — does the plain water's dried-drop structure change, and does it change *toward* the structured water's own signature?

## Honest status of the claim

The archive holds **two independent statements of the claim and no independent test.**

- The primary source is a **research group's own web page**, not a peer-reviewed paper. It describes the phenomenon but gives no numbers and no protocol.
- The convergence source is a **promotional overview article**; the underlying Italian study is not named and no DOI is given. It is a second *statement*, not a second *measurement*.
- The mainstream objection is **severe, and stronger here than for a direct imprint.** Hydrogen bonds in liquid water break and re-form on a **~1 picosecond (10⁻¹² s)** timescale — the Max Planck Institute for Polymer Research / AMOLF measured the longest-lived local water structures at "up to about 1 ps" (*Nature Communications*, 2015), and Dzwolak (Polish Academy of Sciences, 2008) states the objection plainly: the picosecond timescale "radically shortens the lifetime of any structure that could be 'imprinted' and fixed in such an environment." For a **non-contact** transfer there is not even a proposed mechanism, so the objection is stronger than for the imprint cards (028, 042).
- **No card and no dossier in the queue tests non-contact transfer.** A grep of `synthesis/quest-queue/` and `synthesis/replication/` for `non-contact` / `distance` / `transfer` returns no water card.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the expected result is a **refutation** — the transfer receiver is indistinguishable from the control receiver beyond ordinary drift, and the mainstream picosecond objection stands. That is a complete, useful result and earns the Skeptic's Star. A clean, blinded discrimination of transfer-receiver from control-receiver would be the surprise and would require a second independent family before it is believed.

## Why it matters

- It is the queue's **first card on non-contact water-to-water transfer** — the load-bearing claim under the whole water-memory tradition (imprint, homeopathy, Emoto's crystals, Kröplin's light waters). Cards 028 and 042 test an imprint made *into* water; this tests whether water can *send* it to a **separate** water body through space.
- It is the queue's **first card whose control is a matched sender** — same water, same volume, same temperature, same jar, same distance; the *only* difference between arms is the sender's state. That isolates the sender's state as the single variable.
- It is the queue's **first card whose discriminator is direction** — does the receiver move *toward* the sender's own signature (information transfer), or merely drift away from its own baseline (relaxation / noise)? A generic drift is not a transfer.
- It is a **cheap, teachable measurement skill**: drying a drop on a slide and photographing it under a microscope is the method the entire "water revitalisation" market implicitly appeals to (see card 061), and this card applies it to the claim that would make the whole market coherent.

## Replicability: `home`

- Cost: **~$50–200** — a USB digital microscope (or a phone macro lens), clean glass slides, two pairs of identical sealed glass jars, a thermometer, a ruler/calibration slide, and a source of "structured" water (a vortexed sample, or a commercial/pilgrimage water).
- Safety: **none** beyond ordinary glassware.
- Accessibility: an evening to set up, 24 h to expose, an afternoon to photograph and score.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Four identical sealed glass jars** (two senders, two receivers) — same size, same glass, same lid.
- **Plain water** for both receivers and for the control sender (same source, same volume, same temperature).
- **Structured water** for the transfer sender (vortexed for ~5 min, or a commercial/pilgrimage water).
- **A USB digital microscope** (dark-field if available — the source's own method — otherwise bright-field) or a phone macro lens.
- **Clean glass slides** (identical, degreased) and a pipette (fixed drop volume).
- **A thermometer** (to match sender/receiver temperature) and a **calibration ruler** (to measure ring diameter on the photo).
- **A camera/phone** — to photograph the pre-registration sheet and the slides.

## Protocol (pre-registered home test)

**Phase 1 — pre-register, before the first run.**
1. Write down: the water source(s), the structured-water method, the jar type, the drop volume, the drying conditions (temperature, humidity, time), the exposure time (24 h), the distance (1 m), the number of repeats (≥3, randomised), and the scoring rubric. **Photograph the sheet before running.**

**Phase 2 — set up the two arms (randomised, blinded).**
2. Fill two receivers (R-transfer, R-control) and two senders (S-structured, S-plain) from the same plain-water source; bring all four to the **same temperature**.
3. Place the jars in a still, temperature-stable room: **Arm 1** = R-transfer beside S-structured, ~1 m apart; **Arm 2** = R-control beside S-plain, ~1 m apart. Randomise which arm is on which side of the room; the person who later scores the slides must not know which is which.
4. Leave **24 h**.

**Phase 3 — sample, dry, photograph (blinded).**
5. From each receiver take a fixed-volume drop onto a clean slide; dry under identical conditions; photograph under the microscope at fixed magnification. Label the slides with **random codes**, not arm names.
6. Repeat the whole exposure ≥3 times (fresh jars, randomised side assignment each time).

**Phase 4 — score and check.**
7. **Blinded discrimination:** a rater who does not know the codes' meaning tries to sort the slides into two groups and to name which group is the transfer arm. Score the hit rate against a 50% chance baseline.
8. **Direction check (the discriminator):** also photograph the *senders'* own dried drops. Does the transfer receiver's pattern move **toward** the structured sender's signature, or merely away from its own baseline?
9. **Objective metric:** measure the dried-drop **ring diameter** (mm) on each photo with the calibration ruler.

## Measurable outcome

- **Primary:** the blinded rater's **discrimination hit rate** (transfer-receiver vs control-receiver) against a 50% chance baseline, over ≥3 repeats.
- **Discriminator:** whether the transfer receiver's pattern moves **toward** the structured sender's own signature (transfer) or merely drifts from its own baseline (relaxation/noise).
- **Secondary (objective):** the **ring diameter** of the dried drop (mm), transfer vs control.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (the source's claim):** a blinded rater discriminates transfer-receiver from control-receiver **above chance**, **and** the transfer receiver's pattern moves **toward** the structured sender's own signature — requires a second independent replication.
- **PASS (refutation — the expected, complete result):** the blinded discrimination is **at chance** and the ring diameters do not differ beyond measurement noise — the source's claim is not reproduced, and the mainstream picosecond objection stands. (Skeptic's Star)
- **FAIL (void, not a null):** the slides cannot be dried or photographed reproducibly (streaks, dust, inconsistent drop volume), or the room temperature drifts more than a few degrees across the exposure — the run says nothing about the claim.
- **ARTIFACT:** the two arms differ in **temperature, jar type, position, or light** (a mundane gradient, not a transfer); **or** the receiver's change tracks **evaporation/contamination** (detected by a control receiver in the same position with no sender); **or** the rater can infer the arm from slide order or labelling (blinding broken).

## Confounds and controls

- **Matched sender is the whole control.** The control sender must be the *same plain water*, same volume, same temperature, same jar, same distance — otherwise the comparison measures the sender's *material*, not its *state*.
- **Position/light/temperature gradient.** Randomise side assignment across repeats; measure and record the temperature at each jar.
- **Drop volume and drying conditions must be identical** — use a fixed pipette and dry all slides together.
- **Blinding must be real** — random codes, a rater who cannot see the setup, and no arm-revealing order.
- **A "no sender" control** (a receiver alone in the same position) separates transfer from ordinary drift.
- **The rater's own expectation** is the largest single confound in any perceptual test — hence the chance baseline and the pre-registered rubric.
