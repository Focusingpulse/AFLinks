## The claim (as asserted by the source)

1. **Bare pyramid:** no weight change (the source's own null, printed as such).
2. **Pyramid + magnet pre-irradiation:** a **stable weight loss** of tens of milligrams — 30 mg average, 50 mg maximum — claimed to exceed the measurement error by more than 100×.
3. **Sign dependence:** **reversing the flux configuration produces a weight gain** of 10 to 15 mg. The orientation of the pre-irradiation flux is claimed to set the *sign* of the effect.
4. **Mechanism asserted:** buoyancy in an "axion of the torsion fluid." *(Unmeasured; not part of this test.)*

## Replicability: `home`

- **Cost:** ~$60–260. A **0.001 g (1 mg) analytical balance** is the main purchase and the sensitivity floor; a 0.01 g balance is sufficient to see a claimed 30–50 mg effect but not to resolve the balance's own drift cleanly — record which you used.
- **Safety:** none beyond ordinary lab care. No mains, no heat, no pressure.
- **Accessibility:** an afternoon to build and pre-register, a day to run the arms with repeats.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A pyramidal chamber:** a tetrahedral or four-sided pyramid, **~70 cm** to match the source (cardboard, foam board, or thin wood; record the material and the base/apex geometry exactly — proportion is a claimed design variable in this family).
- **An identical non-pyramidal control chamber** of the same wall material, same internal volume, same footprint (**the shape control** — the source omits it).
- **An analytical balance** with a pan that fits inside the chamber (0.001 g preferred; record resolution).
- **A test mass** — a stable, non-magnetic, non-hygroscopic object (e.g. a sealed glass vial of dry sand, or a brass block). Same object in every arm; handle with tongs or gloves.
- **A magnet** (a bar or horseshoe magnet; record pole strength if marked) — for the pre-irradiation step.
- **A thermometer and a hygrometer** — the confound is a temperature or humidity gradient, not the pyramid.
- **A camera/phone** — photograph the pre-registration sheet and each arm's reading.

## Protocol (pre-registered home test)

**Phase 1 — pre-register, before the first run.**
1. Write down: the chamber geometry and material, the control chamber, the balance model and resolution, the test mass, the magnet and its orientation conventions, the four arms, the equilibration time per arm, the number of repeats (≥3, randomised order), and the success criterion. **Photograph the sheet before running.**

**Phase 2 — the four arms.**
2. Place the balance and the chamber in a still, temperature-stable room; let the balance and the mass reach room temperature (≥30 min).
3. **Arm A — bare pyramid (no magnet):** mass inside the pyramid, no pre-irradiation.
4. **Arm B — magnet-primed pyramid, orientation 1:** pre-irradiate the interior with the magnet in one defined orientation (e.g. north pole toward the base), then weigh the mass inside.
5. **Arm C — magnet-primed pyramid, orientation 2:** the **flux reversal** — the same magnet, poles reversed — then weigh again. *This is the source's own sign knob and the card's decisive discriminator.*
6. **Arm D — the shape control:** the same magnet protocol inside the **non-pyramidal** control chamber, so the comparison separates *shape* from *magnet* from *handling*.
7. Repeat each arm ≥3 times, **randomising arm order**, and record the balance reading, room temperature, and humidity at each.

**Phase 3 — score and check.**
8. Compute the **drift-corrected mass change** for each arm (mass in chamber vs mass on the open bench, measured the same session).
9. Compare **Arm B vs Arm C** (does the sign flip?) and **Arm B vs Arm D** (is any effect the pyramid, or the magnet, or handling?).
10. Repeat on a second day with a fresh pre-registration if the first run is ambiguous.

## Measurable outcome

- **Primary:** the **drift-corrected mass change** (mg) in each arm, with the balance's resolution stated as the floor.
- **Discriminator:** whether Arms B and C differ in **sign** at all (the source's own claim).
- **Control:** whether Arm D (non-pyramidal, same magnet) shows the same change as Arm B.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (the source's claim):** a **stable, repeatable mass change** appears in the magnet-primed pyramid (B and C) that is **absent in the bare pyramid (A)** and **absent in the shape control (D)**, and the **sign flips between Arms B and C** — requires a second independent replication.
- **PASS (refutation — the expected, complete result):** all four arms are indistinguishable from balance noise and from the open-bench baseline; or any change tracks **temperature/humidity/handling** and vanishes once corrected. (Skeptic's Star)
- **FAIL (void, not a null):** the balance cannot resolve the claimed magnitude on a stable reading, or the room drifts enough across the run to swamp the measurement — the run says nothing about the claim.
- **ARTIFACT:** the change appears in **Arm D** as well (a magnet effect, not a shape effect); **or** it tracks the operator's handling order (detected by randomising and re-running); **or** it correlates with a temperature or humidity gradient across the run.

## Confounds and controls

- **The shape control is the whole point.** Without Arm D, any effect is as likely to be the magnet or the handling as the pyramid.
- **Temperature and humidity** change balance readings; log both, and correct against the open-bench baseline.
- **Static and magnetism.** A non-magnetic test mass is mandatory; an analytical balance's pan can be moved by electrostatic charge — ground the operator and avoid synthetic clothing.
- **The undefined sign knob is the standing caveat.** A null from a *reconstructed* orientation is **not yet a refutation** — it may be the wrong knob. Record the orientation convention you chose so the next attempt can vary it.
- **Handling order** must be randomised; expectation is a real confound even for a balance operator.

- One attempt → **inconclusive**, filed as a single data point.
- A refutation is a **result**, not a failure of the run — it earns the Skeptic's Star and closes the claim honestly.
