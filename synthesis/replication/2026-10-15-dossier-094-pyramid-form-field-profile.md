## The claim under test

The **form effect** (Russian: *эффект формы*; French: *ondes de forme*) is the claim that a body of a certain geometric configuration radiates a field that is **not electrical, magnetic or electromagnetic**, and that this field measurably changes the structure of water held near or inside the form. The Kovalenko corpus attributes it to a **torsion field** — the spin polarisation of the physical vacuum — sourced from the uncompensated spins of the surface-layer atoms of the form.

**The specific, geometric prediction this card tests** is stated in the source's own abstract:

> "A region of the volume of the base-less pyramid near its apex was found with an anomalously high degree of influence of the internal torsion field on the structure of water. … The presence of a lower base leads to compensation, by the field of its inner surface, of the field at the pyramid apex and to the absence of the generation effect."

In plain terms: **the same paper pyramid, with its own paper base on or off, is predicted to do opposite things to water inside it** — generation of small clusters near the apex without the base, fusion of clusters with it.

**The source's mechanism, quoted (recorded as the source's own, tested not at all by the card):** the internal field's horizontal components from opposite faces are antiparallel and mutually cancel; at the apex, where opposite surfaces are closest, that cancellation is maximal, so the resultant is **purely vertical and maximal**; descending, the vertical component's density falls inversely with the growing cross-section; the base's inner surface adds an upward-directed field that cancels the apex vertical component.

## The source's own numbers (what the family is asked to beat)

Apparatus, quoted: *"A paper hollow single-layer four-sided pyramid without a base and with a base was used as the acting form. The width of the base of a side face was 25 cm, the edge length 24 cm, the height of the pyramid 17 cm."* Exposure: **10 minutes**. Measurement: laser scattering indicatrices before and after, reduced to the **relative indicatrix** `R(θ) = I_act(θ) / I_init(θ)`, with `n = 34` angles over `4° < θ < 70°`; `R > 1` means small clusters were **generated**, `R < 1` means they **fused** into larger ones. `N` is the total relative cluster concentration (initial = 100%).

**Internal field, base-less pyramid (the headline result):**

| Sample position | R behaviour | N (total cluster concentration) |
|---|---|---|
| Upper level (near apex) | `R > 1` at `θ > 20°`, rising with angle — "very intense generation of small clusters" | **~204%** |
| Middle level | `R > 1`, similar but weaker | **~170%** |
| Lower level | `R < 1` — "significant slowing of the restructuring process" | **~92%** |

**Internal field, pyramid WITH its base:** *"the relative scattering indicatrices … measured at the corresponding levels along the pyramid's height (curves 1-3), are similar to one another, which indicates the same restructuring mechanism, almost independent of the position of the sample along the pyramid's height and consisting in the formation of super-large clusters through the fusion of small and medium clusters of the original water in the absence of generation of small clusters."*

**External field (20 cm from a side face), base-less:** decreases from the apex downward — generation at the upper level, fusion (`R << 1`) at the middle, "slightly less than 1" at the lower. **With the base:** the base governs the lower half; `N(act) ~ 65.5%`.

**The corroborating paper's numbers** (same apparatus, different water batch): internal field of the base-less pyramid `N(act) = 148.6%`; external `147.5%`; a **mental action alone** (a phrase repeated for 10 minutes) produced **fusion**, `N = 78%`; the pyramid with a base, internal, `N = 135%` immediately after and `100.5%` after a two-hour hold.

**⚠ The two papers disagree on the magnitude.** For the same condition (internal field, base-less pyramid) one reports `N ~ 204%` and the other `148.6%` — a 37% spread. The papers use different initial water and different sample placements, so this is not necessarily an error, but **it is an internal inconsistency the card records rather than smooths**, and it is one reason the card's pass criterion is *direction and reproducibility*, not a magnitude match.

## The rival explanation

There is no published rival *experiment* — the rival is a **mechanism objection**, and it is a strong one:

1. **The torsion-field mechanism is not mainstream physics.** The Russian Academy of Sciences declared torsion fields pseudoscience in the 1990s; the claim-status record this card retries carries that verdict in its own `grounds_for_status` (`dismissal`, `funding-cutoff`, `institutional-death`). The form field's proposed carrier — "uncompensated spins of surface-layer atoms" producing a static vacuum polarisation — has no accepted physical basis.
2. **The measurement is optical and the effect is claimed to be large.** A claimed 100%+ change in cluster concentration from a sheet of paper is not a small effect; it is the kind of effect that either reproduces easily or does not exist. That cuts both ways and the card says so.
3. **The ordinary confounds are water's own settling and the instrument's drift.** Water's scattering changes as it ages, warms, and degasses; a laser/photodiode rig drifts with temperature and alignment. The source measured each condition **once**, with no paired control cuvette measured at the same moment — so a slow common drift would appear as an effect. **This is exactly the arm card 058 added for the cylinder, and this card carries it over.**

## Apparatus

1. **The form.** A hollow, single-layer **paper** four-sided pyramid. Recorded dimensions from the source: **base face width 25 cm, side-edge length 24 cm, height 17 cm.** Build **two identical copies** — one with its paper base glued on, one without. (Or build one and keep the base as a separate panel that can be taped on and off; if so, record the tape mass and keep it identical between conditions.)
2. **A laser and a detector arm.** A red laser pointer (650 nm, **under 1 mW** — never look into the beam) fixed on one side of a glass cuvette; a photodiode or a phone camera in manual mode on a cardboard arm that swings around the cuvette at marked angles. The source used 34 angles over 4°–70°; **10–12 marked angles (e.g. 4, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70°) is enough to see the shape of the curve.** Work in a dark room or under a dark cloth.
3. **Cuvettes.** At least four identical glass cuvettes (the source used a glass cuvette, internal D 8 mm, wall 0.75 mm, H 90 mm — any small identical glass cuvettes will do, but they must be **identical**).
4. **Stands.** Rod-like wooden stands that hold a cuvette at the **upper, middle and lower** levels of the pyramid's height, both **inside on the axis of symmetry** and **outside at 20 cm from a side face**.
5. **One water batch.** Draw all samples from one batch at one time. The source used drinking water from a single artesian well with a **low** initial small-cluster concentration, which it says gave stable parameters — **record your water's source and, if you can, its initial R̄, and use the same source for every session.**
6. **A thermometer.** Room temperature logged at every reading.

## Protocol (pre-register before trial 1)

1. **Write the pre-registration sheet first and photograph it:** pyramid dimensions, base on/off, the sample positions, the angles, the exposure time, the session count, the predicted direction per position, the operator's name.
2. **Instrument check — do this before anything else.** Measure a **known-different pair** (distilled vs tap water, or tap water vs tap water with one drop of milk in a litre) and confirm the difference clears your noise floor. **If it does not, the run is VOID, not null** — the rig cannot see a real difference, so it cannot be trusted to see a claimed one.
3. **Noise floor.** Ten repeated readings of one cuvette at one angle. That standard deviation is the threshold every claimed effect must beat.
4. **Baseline.** Measure the full angular sweep of every cuvette, in every position, before any exposure. Write it down.
5. **Exposure.** Put cuvette A **inside** the pyramid on the axis at the **upper** level, cuvette B at the **middle**, cuvette C at the **lower**; cuvette D **outside** at 20 cm from a side face at the upper level. Hold **10 minutes** (the source's time). Then measure all four sweeps again.
6. **The base arm.** Repeat the whole cycle with the base **on**. **Have someone else set the base on or off without telling you which** — the operator must not know the condition. This is the card's central control and the source did not blind it.
7. **The paired control.** Keep a fifth cuvette **several metres away** in the same room, measured at the **same moments** as the others. If the control drifts, the others' drift is not the form.
8. **Three sessions on different days.** Re-randomise the base condition each session. A single session is one measurement, not a result.
9. **Analysis.** For each position and each base condition: the mean relative indicatrix `R̄` over the measured angles, and the total relative cluster concentration `N`. Then the discriminator: **is `R̄ > 1` (generation) at the upper level without the base, and `R̄ < 1` (fusion) at the same level with the base?**

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS — the claim survives.** Without the base, the **upper-level internal** sample shows `R̄ > 1` (generation) and `N > 100%`; **with** the base, the same position shows `R̄ < 1` (fusion) and `N < 100%`; the paired control stays flat; and the pattern reproduces in **≥ 2 of 3 sessions**. This is the source's own sharpest geometric prediction, and it would be the first independent confirmation of any part of the form-effect corpus.
- **PASS — the rival is sufficient (the expected, ordinary result).** `R̄ ≈ 1` at every position in both base conditions, or the base-less/based difference does not reproduce across sessions. The form does nothing measurable to water; the source's 204%/170%/92% table was the water's own settling and the instrument's drift read against no paired control. **(Skeptic's Star.)**
- **FAIL.** `R̄` differs from 1 but **not** in the pre-registered direction, or the base-less and based conditions move **together** (a common attractor, not a base effect).
- **VOID (not a null).** The instrument check did not separate the known-different pair; the water's initial state could not be classified; fewer than 3 sessions; the arms were not measured in the same session; room temperature moved more than a degree between baseline and final; the control cuvette sat within a metre of the pyramid; the base condition was not blinded.
- **ARTIFACT.** `R̄` tracks room temperature, time of day, or the water's own aging rather than the form; the reading follows the operator's expectation; the reading follows the cuvette's position in the room rather than its enclosure.

## Honest status of the claim

1. **The mechanism is not mainstream and this card does not endorse it.** The torsion-field carrier has no accepted physical basis; the Russian Academy of Sciences dismissed the programme; the claim-status record this card retries carries `status: suppressed` with `grounds_for_status: [dismissal, funding-cutoff, institutional-death]`. **The card tests the measurement, not the mechanism.**
2. **The source has no independent replication.** Both papers come from the same laboratory (Kherson National Technical University), one with a student as corresponding author, published in the university's own journal. **No third party has reported these numbers.**
3. **The two source papers disagree with each other** on the magnitude of the same condition (204% vs 148.6%). Recorded, not smoothed.
4. **The source did not blind the base condition and did not use a paired control cuvette.** Both are added here.
5. **The effect claimed is large, which is a reason to run it, not a reason to believe it.** A 100%+ change in cluster concentration from a sheet of paper would be easy to see if it were real. The card is written so that the ordinary null is a complete and publishable result.
6. **The archive's own position on water memory is a counterweight, not a support.** The archive holds the TU Wien result that water's ordering around ions is statistical and sub-second, and that "water memory" is not confirmed. This card does not claim memory; it claims a **structural** change measurable while the water is in the form.

## What would change the verdict

- A **positive** result (generation without the base, fusion with it, reproduced blind in ≥ 2 of 3 sessions) → the form-effect corpus gains its first independent data point and the claim-status record's `retry_now` item (1) is partially answered at household scale.
- A **negative** result (R̄ ≈ 1 everywhere) → the pyramid form-field claim joins the archive's null column, and the 204%/170%/92% table is reclassified as an un-controlled drift measurement.
- **Either way the card is worth running**, because it is the cheapest test of the largest claim in the form-effect corpus, and because the base-subtraction is a control the source itself supplied and never blinded.
