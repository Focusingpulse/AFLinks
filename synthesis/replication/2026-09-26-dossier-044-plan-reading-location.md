## The claim (as asserted by the source)

Turenne devotes Chapter XIII of his 1935 book to what he calls *"the most delicate chapter of the book"* — **reading on plans.** The claim, stated in his own framing:

> *"reading on plans"* is a part of radiesthesia in which **a plan or map of a place carries enough of that place's wave information that a detector held over the plan responds to the same features the detector would find in the place itself.**

He sets it beside the acceptance of radio waves passing through walls: just as a receiving set picks up the Eiffel Tower's waves through masonry, *"the present generation was born to understand radiesthesia, and one of its most delicate parts, reading on plans."* The chapter's programme names the applications he claims: **architects' plans** (locating the harmful zones in a building from its drawing), **Michelin road maps**, **searching for water and metals on plans**, **readings on anatomical plates and on photographs**, and **contaminated houses** (reading a house's harmful zones from its plan).

**This is the classic map-dowsing / teleradiesthesia claim**, and it is the *representation* claim, not the *field* claim:

| | What is being tested | Existing card |
|---|---|---|
| **Field claim** | Do two dowsers physically walking the same room agree on where the zones are? | Card 005 (Blind Geopathic Zone Mapping) |
| **Representation claim** | Does a **2D plan or photograph** of the room carry enough information for a dowser to locate the same zones **without entering the room**? | **This card — none exists** |

The two are different claims. Card 005 is about **inter-operator agreement in the physical room**; this card is about **whether a representation of the room carries the information at all.** A family can pass 005 and fail this, or vice versa — and the failure modes mean different things (005 failing says the rods found nothing shared; this failing says the plan carries nothing, which is the stronger and more surprising negative).

### Why the plan claim is the more striking one

The physical claim (005) can be explained without any exotic mechanism — an operator's body might genuinely respond to something in the room (ideomotor response to a real stimulus, or to expectation). The **plan** claim cannot be explained that way: the operator is in a different room, holding a sheet of paper. If a plan-reading operator locates a zone that a blind physical measurement then confirms, the information had to travel through the plan. That is why Turenne calls it *"the most delicate chapter"* — and why it is worth a clean test.

### Honest status of the claim

**Unreplicated, and the mechanism is not established.** The mainstream account of dowsing is the **ideomotor effect** — the operator's own expectation produces the rod/pendulum movement, with no external signal. Map dowsing is *more* vulnerable to this, not less: the operator usually knows the building, so expectation alone can produce a plausible-looking plan. **That is exactly why the protocol must be blind and must include a cross-room chance baseline** — the discriminating test is not "did the operator mark zones?" (they always will) but "did the plan's zones match the *actual* room better than a different room's plan would?"

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** this card cannot prove that a plan carries wave information. It can only test whether a plan-reading operator's marks **beat chance** at predicting the physical room. If they do, that is a real, surprising, publishable result the archive wants. If they do not, the honest negative is that map dowsing, in this family's hands, is expectation — and that is equally valuable.

## Why it matters

- It is the **first representation-claim card in the queue.** Every dowsing card so far (005, 011, 034, 035) tests the *physical field* — a person, a room, a water line, a substance. None tests whether a **representation** carries the information. The plan-reading claim is the one that, if true, would be the most consequential (it would mean location information is available without proximity) and, if false, is the cleanest demonstration of the ideomotor account.
- It is a genuine family education: **blind protocols, chance baselines, and the difference between "the operator marked something" and "the marks predicted the room."** Kids can be the referee and the physical measurer.
- It links a **fresh primary source** (Turenne 1935, translated 2026-09-26) that is the root of the whole French radiesthesia → BioGeometry lineage the library tracks.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build cost: **~$5** (two sets of coat-hanger L-rods, or one pendulum on a 20 cm thread; a pen and paper for the plan; a tape measure).
- Safety: **none.** No mains, no chemicals, no heat, no sharp tools beyond bending a coat hanger.
- Accessibility: an afternoon. The referee and the physical measurer are ideal kid jobs. The plan can be hand-drawn to scale or a printed photograph.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Two detector sets** — two pairs of L-rods bent from wire coat hangers (~$0), or two pendulums (a nut on a 20 cm thread). Two sets so the plan-reader and the physical measurer use separate instruments (no shared "memory" of the instrument).
- **A to-scale plan** of the target room — hand-drawn on graph paper from tape-measure dimensions, or a printed photograph of the empty room. **Mark an unambiguous origin and orientation** (a corner + a north arrow) so plan coordinates map to room coordinates.
- **A second plan** — a plan of a *different* room of similar size (the cross-room chance control).
- **Tape measure** and chalk/tape for laying a 0.5 m grid on the room floor.
- **A sealed scoring sheet** — the pre-registered thresholds and the scoring rule, written before any measurement.
- **Phone camera** for evidence.

## Protocol (blind, cross-room baseline)

1. **Pre-register.** Write the thresholds and the scoring rule on the sheet *before* anyone dowses. Nominate a **referee** (holds the key) and a **physical measurer** (walks the room). The plan-reader must **not** be the physical measurer, and must not watch the physical measurement.
2. **Choose two rooms** of similar size — the **target room** and the **control room**. The plan-reader is told only which plan is which; they must not know which room is the target *for the measurement* (or, if they do, the control room's plan is the check).
3. **Plan reading (blind).** The plan-reader, alone, holds the detector over the **target plan** and marks every point where the detector responds, recording each as (x, y) on the graph paper. Then does the same on the **control plan**. Neither mark set is seen by the physical measurer.
4. **Physical measurement (blind).** The physical measurer, who has not seen the plan marks, walks the **target room** on the 0.5 m grid with the second detector and marks every response point on a fresh floor map. Then does the same in the **control room**.
5. **Score.** The referee overlays:
   - **Plan-vs-physical (target):** what fraction of the plan-reader's marks fall within one grid cell (0.5 m) of a physical mark in the *same* room? Call this **A**.
   - **Cross-room chance baseline:** what fraction of the plan-reader's marks fall within one grid cell of a physical mark in the *other* room? Call this **B**. (This is the chance level — the plan and the room are unrelated, so any overlap is coincidence.)
   - The **discriminating quantity is A − B**, not A alone.
6. **Repeat** for three sessions (three separate blind readings), alternating which room is the target, so drift and familiarity cancel.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS:** target plan-vs-physical agreement **A ≥ 70 %** AND **A − B ≥ 25 points** (i.e. the plan predicts its own room at least 25 points better than a different room), in **≥ 2 of 3 sessions**.
- **FAIL:** **A − B ≤ 10 points** in ≥ 2 of 3 sessions — the plan carries no information about its room beyond chance; map reading in this family's hands is expectation (Skeptic's Star; a complete and valuable result).
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** between the two, or fewer than 3 sessions.
- **VOID:** the plan-reader saw the room or the physical marks before reading, or the plan's scale/orientation was ambiguous — report the resolution achieved and the control required, **not** a verdict.

## Confounds named in advance (each is an explicit ARTIFACT test)

- **Familiarity** — if the plan-reader knows the target room well, expectation alone can produce plausible marks. Mitigation: the plan-reader must not have walked the room recently; the control room must be equally familiar (or equally unfamiliar).
- **Plan quality** — a vague plan gives fewer anchors. Mitigation: use the same plan style for target and control.
- **Operator drift** — a dowsing response can change across a session. Mitigation: alternate target/control, three sessions.
- **Scoring bias** — the referee must not know which marks are "supposed" to match. Mitigation: the referee scores both A and B with the same rule, blind to which room is the target.

## What a result would mean

- **PASS** would be the queue's first evidence that a *representation* carries location information — a striking result that would need independent replication before any claim beyond "in this family's hands."
- **FAIL** would be the honest negative: map dowsing is expectation, and the physical dowsing cards (005, 035) stand alone. That is a real result about the tradition, and it is the one the archive most needs, because the tradition's defenders cite map reading as its strongest evidence.
