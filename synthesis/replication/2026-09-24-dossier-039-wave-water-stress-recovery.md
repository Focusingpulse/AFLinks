## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The paper's argument runs: water can carry "information"; drinking water that has had "vibrational-wave energy" transferred into it harmonizes stress responses and draws out the body's normal reaction; therefore **drinking wave water should speed recovery from a mental stressor.**

The paper is careful about what it claims. Its conclusion is hedged with *可能性が考えられる* ("it is possible to think that…"), and the discussion says the study *"is nothing more than observing the results of a phenomenon"* (現象の結果を観察しているに過ぎない) and that **where in the psychological or physiological stress response the water acted is unknown** (どこに波動水が作用したのかは不明である).

Two claims are separable, and only one is what this card tests:

1. **The general claim (NOT tested here, and not what the paper measured):** the product's own stated scope. Table 1 lists dozens of transferred "energies" — harmonizing the brainstem, all internal organs, the skeleton, blood vessels and nerves; neutralizing electromagnetic waves and geopathic stress; neutralizing pollen, allergens, heavy metals, dioxins, PCBs, exhaust gases; suppressing bacteria and mould; fat breakdown; and — stated in the table — *"neutralizing cancer-related waves"* (ガンに関わる波動を中和する) and *"the vibrational energy of somatids"* (ソマチッドの振動波エネルギー). **None of that was tested. This card makes no treatment claim of any kind and tests none of it.**
2. **The measured claim (TESTED HERE):** that drinking wave water, compared with drinking plain water, speeds recovery of the EEG α/β ratio after a 15-minute arithmetic stressor.

**Claimants:** Egawa Yōsuke (Kokushikan University); the Japanese 波動水 ("wave water") tradition; the product's maker, Holistic Conditioning Research Institute
**Verification status:** **never independently replicated.** The only prior work cited is by the same author, at the same institution, on the same product. No replication by any other group appears in the archive or in the paper's own citation list.
**Mainstream position:** the mechanism (a "vibrational-wave" imprint in water) has no accepted physical basis; the *endpoint* (α/β ratio as an index of mental tension) is an ordinary, widely used EEG measure and is not in dispute.

## What the source actually found — and the finding its own numbers contain

**Design as published.** n = 7 healthy male students (age 21.1 ± 0.8 y, height 171.3 ± 4.6 cm, weight 64.9 ± 3.7 kg), all free of head/neck injury in the preceding three months, written informed consent, run under the university's human-research ethics rules. Three arms, one arm per week, **randomized**, three weeks to complete all three — a crossover:

- **CONTROL** — stress load only, then 2 minutes seated rest.
- **NMW** — stress load, then 150 mL of commercial natural mineral water (Suntory "Minami Alps no Tennensui") drunk within 2 minutes.
- **WW** — stress load, then 150 mL of wave water drunk within 2 minutes.

**Stressor:** 15 minutes of one-digit addition under time pressure, using the Uchida-Kraepelin test method (内田クレペリン検査).
**EEG:** 60 s, eyes closed, before and after; electrodes at both earlobes (+, reference) and left/right frontal (−), body earth on the forehead; measured in a shielded room; the analysed window is 10–40 s (30 s); sampled at 200 Hz; FFT; α = 8–13 Hz, β = 13–30 Hz; endpoint = **α/β power ratio** (a fall = tension, a rise = relaxation).
**Blinding:** subjects did not know which water they received, and *"by a third party's check, the experimenter also could not know which water was being given"* (第３者のチェックにより測定験者にも、どの水を摂取させているかは分からないように注意した).
**Wave water preparation:** 0.45 mL of 大光明清水 sprayed into 2 L of clean drinking water and stirred; made fresh each time. The concentrate is made by placing a bottle on a "plate detector" connected to a **Reyonex "Reyometer Digital"** wave-measuring device, transferring each frequency individually for 10 minutes.
**Statistics:** paired t-test within arms; one-way ANOVA between arms; Fisher's PLSD for multiple comparisons; significance at 5%.

**Results as published (α/β ratio, µV):**

| Arm | Before stress | After stress | After intake |
|---|---|---|---|
| CONTROL | 2.12 ± 0.91 | 0.75 ± 0.25 | 0.75 ± 0.25 |
| NMW (plain water) | 1.76 ± 0.81 | 1.20 ± 1.01 | 1.20 ± 1.01 |
| WW (wave water) | 2.18 ± 0.96 | 1.48 ± 0.60 | 1.48 ± 0.60 |

The ratio fell after the stressor in **all three arms** — the stressor worked. After intake, **WW (1.48 ± 0.60) was higher than CONTROL (0.75 ± 0.25)**. And then the sentence that matters: **there was no difference between CONTROL and NMW, and no difference between NMW and WW** (CONTROLとNMW、NMWとWWとでは差がなかった).

**★ The finding the paper's own numbers contain, and the reason this card is designed the way it is.** Plain water moved the ratio from 0.75 to 1.20 — **most of the distance to WW's 1.48** — and was statistically indistinguishable from *both* the no-water arm and the wave-water arm. The paper's own text says the plain-water relaxation *"does not exceed the placebo effect"* (プラシーボ効果の域を出ない). So the published result **does not separate the imprint from the water.** The only significant comparison is WW vs CONTROL, and CONTROL is the arm that drank nothing at all. **The discriminating prediction is therefore WW vs NMW — imprinted water against plain water — not WW vs CONTROL.** A card built on WW-vs-CONTROL would be testing "drinking water helps you calm down," which is not the claim.

**Other weaknesses, named so the card can answer them:**

- **n = 7**, all male, all university students, single site, single ethnic and age band.
- **Baseline imbalance:** pre-stress baselines differ across arms (2.12 / 1.76 / 2.18) in a 7-subject crossover, and the headline comparison is a **between-arm comparison of post-stress values**, not of within-subject recovery deltas. The NMW arm's post-stress SD is 1.01 on a mean of 1.20 — that arm is noisy.
- **No correction for multiple comparisons**; α = 0.05 with post-hoc Fisher's PLSD.
- **The product's claimed scope is vastly wider than the endpoint tested** (see claim 1 above), including cancer — a scope the study does not touch.
- **The imprinting apparatus is unobtainable.** The Reyonex device and the Daikomyo concentrate are proprietary and the production method is undisclosed. **This card cannot reproduce the source's water**, and it says so.

## The protocol — three arms, one subject, blinded, pre-registered

The testable core: **if the imprint does anything, imprinted water should produce a larger α/β recovery than plain water handled identically. If imprinted water ≈ plain water, the effect — whatever it is — is the water, not the imprint, and the claim is retired at home scale.**

### Materials (~$120–440)

1. **A consumer or single-channel EEG headset** that reports band power for α (8–13 Hz) and β (13–30 Hz) — **~$100–400** (a used or entry-level consumer headset is sufficient; a research-grade rig is not needed and is not the point). **This is the queue's first card whose apparatus is a purchased instrument rather than hardware-store parts.** A family without a headset should not run this card.
2. **A printed one-digit addition sheet** (Uchida-Kraepelin style: rows of single digits to be summed pairwise, worked left to right) and a timer — free.
3. **Imprinting rig (~$20–40):** ~20–40 m of enamelled magnet wire wound into a coil sized to sit around a 2 L glass jar, driven by a **low-voltage, battery-powered** source able to output a clean **7.8 Hz** (a phone/PC tone generator into a small audio amplifier, or a function-generator board). **Never connect the coil to mains power.**
4. **Two identical opaque bottles** with coded labels, and a **sealable code key**.
5. **A third person** who is neither subject nor analyst — the **coder**. They prepare and hand over the water and hold the key.
6. A notebook or spreadsheet; a phone camera.

### Design — the parts that make it a measurement

1. **One subject at a time.** Each family member is their own subject; do not pool people who ran different numbers of sessions. **Pre-register the number of sessions per arm before the first run** — recommend **≥6 sessions per arm** (18 sessions total, one per day) so a within-subject comparison has any power at all.
2. **Three arms:** **WW** (imprinted water), **NMW** (plain water from the same source bottle), **CONTROL** (no drink — 2 minutes seated rest).
3. **Blinding.** The coder prepares 150 mL in an opaque coded bottle for WW and NMW; for CONTROL the coder hands over an empty coded bottle. **The subject and the analyst do not know the code**, and the key stays sealed until the numbers are written down.
4. **Randomization.** The coder works from a pre-written randomized arm schedule (drawn before the first session), so the arm order is not chosen by anyone who knows the hypothesis.
5. **The session, in order:** 5 minutes seated rest → **60 s eyes-closed EEG (baseline)** → **15 minutes of one-digit addition** under time pressure → **2 minutes**: drink 150 mL within the 2 minutes, or sit quietly (CONTROL) → **60 s eyes-closed EEG**. Analyse the **10–40 s window**, exactly as the source did.
6. **The imprinting, and the control for it.** Coil around the jar, energized at 7.8 Hz for 30 minutes, then pour into the coded bottle. **The plain-water arm gets the identical treatment with the coil NOT energized** — same jar, same 30-minute wait, same pour. The only difference between WW and NMW is the field. **Say this in the report**: the protocol substitutes the archive's own reproducible imprinting method for the source's proprietary device, so the card tests the **class** ("water imprinted by a declared procedure") and not the Reyonex device.
7. **Endpoint per session:** the **recovery ratio** = (post-stress α/β) ÷ (baseline α/β), computed per session; plus the raw α/β values, so anyone can re-analyse.

### The assay's own positive control

**The α/β ratio must fall across the stressor in the CONTROL arm.** If it does not, the assay is not measuring what the source measured, and **the run is void** — not a refutation. This is the same discipline the queue applies elsewhere: a test whose instrument did not register the known effect has produced no information.

### Pre-registered pass/fail

- **PASS (the imprint does something):** across sessions, the WW arm's mean recovery ratio is **significantly greater than the NMW arm's** (within-subject paired comparison on per-session recovery ratios — Wilcoxon signed-rank at α = 0.05 with the pre-registered N) **AND** the CONTROL arm shows the expected stress-induced α/β drop (assay valid) **AND** the direction repeats in a second block of sessions.
- **FAIL (the likely outcome, and a complete result):** **WW ≈ NMW.** The effect, if any, is the water, not the imprint. Honest negative, Skeptic's Star.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** the assay is invalid (no α/β drop in CONTROL); fewer than the pre-registered sessions completed; electrode artefact or headset noise dominated the band powers; the subject could taste or guess the arm; or a session was disturbed (phone, interruption, illness, poor sleep — log these and exclude them by a rule written down in advance, not after seeing the numbers).

### Optional secondary readout (a DIFFERENT test — not a substitute)

A family without an EEG can run the **same three arms with heart-rate recovery** as the endpoint (heart rate at 0, 2 and 5 minutes after the stressor, from a chest strap or a phone camera). **This is a different endpoint and it does not replicate the source.** Report it as its own test with its own honest caveat, and never present an HR result as a wave-water replication.

## Replicability: `homelab`

- Build cost: **~$120–440**, dominated by the EEG headset (~$100–400) plus ~$20–40 of coil and signal-source parts.
- Session burden: **≥18 sessions** (6 per arm) at ~25 minutes each, one per day — three to four weeks. This is the real cost, and it is why the tier is wood.
- Safety: **the EEG headset is a passive sensor and a consumer headset is not a medical device.** The coil is **low-voltage and low-frequency — drive it from a battery-powered amplifier or a certified low-voltage supply and never from mains.** The stressor is mild but real.
- **Adult-only self-experiment.** Not a treatment, not a therapy, and not a medical claim of any kind. **Do not run this with children.** Skip it if you have a heart condition, are pregnant, take cardiac or psychiatric medication, or find timed arithmetic distressing.

## What a result means (and what it does not)

- A **pass** means: on your bench, with your headset, over your pre-registered sessions, imprinted water produced a larger α/β recovery than plain water handled identically. That is a real, local, measured result — and it is **not** a replication of the source, because the water is not the source's water.
- It does **not** mean any of the product's Table 1 claims. It says nothing about electromagnetic stress, heavy metals, bacteria, fat, somatids, or cancer. The card tests one endpoint and makes no treatment claim.
- A **fail** means imprinted water and plain water were indistinguishable — the effect is the water, not the imprint. That is a complete result and earns the Skeptic's Star.
- The confounds to name: **the α/β ratio is a within-subject index and is sensitive to arousal, alertness, caffeine, time of day, sleep debt and electrode placement** — pre-register the session time, hold caffeine fixed, and log sleep. **Expectation is not fully controlled even with a blinded subject** (the coder knows, and the subject may guess). And **a null does not prove the source's water is inert** — it tests a substitute imprinting method, not the Reyonex device.
