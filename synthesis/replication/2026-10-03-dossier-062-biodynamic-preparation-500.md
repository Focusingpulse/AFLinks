## The claim (as asserted by the source)

The synthesis describes the preparations and their claimed role, translated:

> "These preparations are made from minerals, plants, and animal components by the farmers themselves and are used in small quantities comparable to homeopathic compositions. They have a balancing effect under one-sided growing conditions and in this way promote the 'natural development and integrity of the plant' (Lammerts van Bueren & Struik 2005). Six plant preparations are used to improve the compost and the decomposition of the manure (compost preparations), one sprayed preparation promotes soil fertility ('horn manure' preparation) [preparation 500], and another sprayed preparation is used to improve the ripening of the cultivated plants ('horn silica' preparation) [preparation 501]."

The preparation-isolation evidence the synthesis reports (its own summary of the trials):

> "Zaller & Köpke (2004) found in a nine-year trial comparing compost with and without biodynamic preparations or only with the yarrow preparation that just 100 days after fertilization, an accelerated rate of decomposition of the cotton strips had taken place in the variants with preparations. They also showed a lower basal respiration in the topsoil layer and higher in the subsoil, as well as a greater earthworm biomass."

> "Carpenter-Boggs (2000) could not reproduce in a significant way the differences in a two-year comparison between biodynamic and organic fertilization. However, the study shows that after the application of all the biodynamic measures, including the spray preparations, soil respiration and the carbon mineralization measured after 10 days increased, and the fatty acid profile in the soil had partially changed."

> "Likewise, the variants fertilized with manure with biodynamic preparations always show higher levels of hydrolyzable proteins or amino acids in the soil than those with manure without preparations (Scheller & Raupp, 2005)."

> "The germination capacity of broad-leaved dock (Rumex obtusifolius) was significantly lower after one month in manure treated with the biodynamic compost preparations. Its germination rate was 18% compared to the control variant without preparations, which had a germination rate of 28% (Zaller, 2007)."

**What is being tested, in one sentence:** does applying the biodynamic horn-manure preparation (BD 500) to a garden bed change measurable **soil biological activity** — the rate at which buried cotton strips decompose, the earthworm count, and the crop yield — compared with an untreated bed and a stirred-water sham?

## Honest status of the claim

**The archive holds the review and no home test of it.** A grep for `biodynamic`, `horn manure`, `BD500`, `preparation 500`, and `cotton strip` across `living-library/synthesis/quest-queue/` and `synthesis/replication/` returns **no card and no dossier** on the preparations themselves. The queue's nearest food cards test germination (015), fermentation (010), ripening (020), sowing timing (032), growth stimulation (026), storage (033, 043), planting order (038), the soil community (049) and weed control (056) — **none tests a preparation the grower makes and applies.**

Four things the card must say out loud, because they are what a family is actually testing:

1. **The effect the source reports is small and inconsistent, and the synthesis says so.** Carpenter-Boggs (2000) "could not reproduce in a significant way the differences"; the four-year liquid-manure experiment "found no significant differences" (Elers & Schmidt 2011); Burkitt et al. (2007) "found no differences" except lower phosphorus. The positive results (Zaller & Köpke 2004; Scheller & Raupp 2005) are real but modest. **A home test is a vote, not a verdict.**
2. **The preparation is applied at a homeopathic dilution.** The synthesis states the preparations are "used in small quantities comparable to homeopathic compositions." The card therefore must include a **sham arm** — plain water stirred identically and applied identically — so that the *stirring, the water, and the act of applying* cannot be mistaken for the preparation.
3. **The endpoint must be an assay that can detect a difference at all.** The synthesis's own home-replicable endpoint is the **cotton-strip decomposition test** (Zaller & Köpke 2004). The card adds an **assay positive control** (a compost-added bed) so that "no difference" is not confused with "the assay could not have seen a difference."
4. **The expected result is a null, and that is a complete result.** The preparation's reported effects are small, the dilution is homeopathic, and a single-season home trial is underpowered. The most likely outcome is that the BD 500 bed matches the control and the sham. That is a clean, complete refutation of the *home-scale* claim, scored as such — not a failure.

**Under-promise, stated plainly:** the likely outcome is that the horn-manure preparation does not visibly change cotton-strip decomposition, earthworm count, or yield at home scale in one season, and the honest verdict is *"the preparation's soil effect is not detectable in a one-season home trial."* A clear, replicating positive (BD 500 ≥ 1.3× both control and sham) would be genuinely surprising and would justify the taller follow-up (a multi-season trial, the full preparation set, and a soil-respiration assay).

## Replicability: `home`

- Build/obtain cost: **~$40–200** — a cow horn (~$20–40; a purchased BD 500 preparation is an acceptable substitute if no horn is available), fresh cow manure (free from a farm), a bucket and a wooden spoon or whisk for stirring (~$10), unbleached cotton fabric or cotton strips (~$10), a kitchen scale (~$20), a spade, and a compost bed for the positive control.
- Safety: **low.** No high voltage, no chemicals. The only real hazards are the spade and the manure hygiene (gloves). Family-safe; suitable for a supervised child.
- Accessibility: the preparation is **seasonal** — the horn is buried in autumn and retrieved in spring, so the earliest a family can run the full test is the spring after making it. A purchased BD 500 shortens the loop to one growing season.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **A cow horn** (or a purchased BD 500 preparation) — the classic sheath.
- **Fresh cow manure** — packed into the horn.
- **A garden bed or plot**, split into matched sub-plots.
- **Unbleached cotton fabric / cotton strips** — the decomposition assay (the same material in every bed).
- **A kitchen scale** (0.1 g resolution) — to weigh the strips before burial and after retrieval.
- **A bucket and a wooden spoon or whisk** — for the one-hour vortex stirring.
- **A spade** — to bury the horn and to lift a standardised soil block for the earthworm count.
- **A compost bed** — the assay positive control.
- **A log sheet** — for pre-registration and the weekly record.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Phase 0 — assay check (mandatory).**
1. Prove the **cotton-strip assay can detect a difference at all**: bury matched strips in a **compost-added bed** and in an **untreated bed**, retrieve after the same interval, and weigh. The compost bed's strips must lose **clearly more mass** than the untreated bed's.
2. **If the assay cannot separate the compost bed from the untreated bed, a no-difference result is VOID, not a null.** "No difference" and "the assay could not have seen a difference" are different results and get filed identically if this check is skipped.

**Phase 1 — make the preparation (or use a purchased one).**
3. Pack fresh cow manure into a cow horn; bury it **pointed-end down, ~40–60 cm deep**, in autumn; retrieve in spring. (Record the burial and retrieval dates and the soil temperature if available.)

**Phase 2 — prepare and pre-register.**
4. Split the plot into **matched sub-plots** (≥3 per arm, ≥1 m² each, same soil, same light, same slope):
   - **A — BD 500:** stir 25–50 g of the preparation in 10–15 L of water for **one hour**, alternating the stirring direction to form a vortex; apply as a fine spray/drench over the bed.
   - **B — control:** untreated.
   - **C — sham:** 10–15 L of plain water, **stirred identically for one hour** and applied identically — this controls for the stirring, the water, and the application.
5. Write the **pre-registration sheet** before applying anything: the arms, the preparation quantity, the stirring time, the application rate, the number of sub-plots, the cotton-strip burial depth and interval, the earthworm-count method, the crop, and the thresholds. Photograph it.

**Phase 3 — apply, bury, grow.**
6. Apply the arms on the same day, in the same way, and water all beds identically thereafter.
7. Bury **matched cotton strips** (weighed, labelled) at a fixed depth in every sub-plot; retrieve after **30–60 days**, clean, dry, and re-weigh. Record the mass loss.
8. Count **earthworms** per sub-plot with a standardised method (a fixed-volume soil block, hand-sorted, or a mustard/onion solution extraction).
9. Grow the same crop in every bed and weigh the harvestable yield per sub-plot.

**Phase 4 — score.**
10. Compute the mean cotton-strip mass loss, earthworm count, and yield per arm. Compare **A vs B** (the preparation effect) and **A vs C** (the preparation effect net of handling).
11. Repeat the run in a second season with a fresh preparation.

## Pass / fail (pre-registered)

- **PASS (claim survives — surprising):** the BD 500 bed shows **≥ 1.3×** the cotton-strip mass loss of **both** the control and the sham, **and** a higher earthworm count or yield, replicating in a second season — the preparation's soil effect is real at home scale and needs the taller follow-up (a multi-season trial, the full preparation set, a soil-respiration assay).
- **PASS (claim refuted — the expected, complete result):** the BD 500 bed is **at the control and sham level** on every endpoint (within the noise band) in two seasons — the preparation does not change measurable soil biological activity at home scale. A complete result; Skeptic's Star.
- **FAIL (void, not a null):** the **assay positive control** (the compost bed) does not show clearly greater decomposition than the untreated bed — the assay measures nothing and the run is void.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** fewer than 3 sub-plots per arm; one season; the sub-plots differed by >30 % at baseline; a pest, frost, drought or flood hit one arm and not the others; the cotton strips were not matched or not weighed before burial; the preparation was not made or sourced as stated; the stirring time or application rate differed between arms.
- **ARTIFACT:** the **sham (C) is distinguished from the control (B)** — handling, stirring or water alone produces the signal; the result tracks **sub-plot position, light, slope or drainage** rather than the arm; the operator knew which bed was which (unblinded application or harvest); or the result disappears when the sub-plots are re-randomised in position.

## Evidence

Photo of the preparation and its burial/retrieval + the **pre-registration sheet** (arms, quantity, stirring time, application rate, N, endpoints, thresholds) + the **assay-check result** (compost bed vs untreated bed, cotton-strip mass loss) + the weighed cotton strips before burial and after retrieval, per sub-plot + the earthworm counts per sub-plot + the per-sub-plot yield + the results table with the A-vs-B and A-vs-C ratios shown + the recorded burial/retrieval dates and conditions + the second-season repeat + the void/artifact checks reported whether or not they void the run.

## Confounds (named in advance)

- **The dilution is homeopathic — the central confound.** The preparation is applied at a very low concentration, so the *stirring, the water, and the application* are plausible causes of any effect. **The sham arm (C) is the whole experiment.**
- **Season and weather.** A single season is a sample of one; drought, frost or a pest can swamp a small effect. Repeat in a second season.
- **Sub-plot position.** Light, slope, drainage and edge effects vary across a plot. Randomise the arms and record the layout.
- **The assay's sensitivity.** Cotton-strip decomposition responds to temperature and moisture as well as to biology. Keep the beds watered identically and record the weather.
- **The operator's expectation.** The whole card is built around the sham and the positive control; if the operator knows which bed is which at harvest, the run is weakened.
- **The preparation's provenance.** A home-made preparation and a purchased one are not the same object. Record which was used.
- **The compost positive control.** If the compost bed does not out-decompose the untreated bed, the assay cannot see a difference and the run is void.

## Verdict rules

BD 500 ≥ 1.3× both control and sham on cotton-strip mass loss, replicating in a second season → **the claim survives** (surprising; needs the taller follow-up). BD 500 at the control and sham level in two seasons → **the home-scale claim is refuted** (the expected, complete result). The assay positive control fails → **void, not a null**. Sham distinguished from control, or the result tracks position, or the operator was unblinded → **artifact**. Fewer than 3 sub-plots per arm, one season, or baseline mismatch → **inconclusive, not a refutation**.
