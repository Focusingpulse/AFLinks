## The claim

The biodynamic sowing calendar (Maria Thun, building on Steiner's agriculture lectures) classifies every day by the Moon's position against the **sidereal** zodiac, and assigns the day a "plant part" quality from the **trigon** the Moon occupies:

| Element day | Trigon | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaf day** | Water | Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces |
| **Fruit day** | Fire | Aries, Leo, Sagittarius |
| **Root day** | Earth | Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn |
| **Flower day** | Air | Gemini, Libra, Aquarius |

The practical claim — the thing a gardener actually *does* — is that **sowing a leaf crop on a leaf day produces a better leaf crop than sowing it on a root day**, and vice versa. This dossier tests the leaf-crop/leaf-day arm, because it is the arm with a published trial attached.

## What the evidence actually says (as the scout report states it)

**For the claim — one trial, one re-analysis:**

- **Bishop lettuce trial (1976)**, reported in Kollerstrom & Staudenmaier (2001): 36 sowings over 3 sidereal months, one row per zodiac sign.
  - Leaf-day sowings: **29.6 oz ± 25.6 (n=8)** vs others **−9.6 oz ± 32 (n=24)**; t=3.0, **p<0.001**.
  - Root-day sowings: **−34.1 oz ± 34.8 (n=8)** vs others **11.6 oz ± 28.1 (n=24)**; t=3.6, **p<0.001**.
  - Interpretation in the report: a wave-like pattern with alternating maxima and minima over a ~9-day cycle.
- **Kollerstrom & Staudenmaier's re-analysis of Spiess's data:** ~**7% yield increase** for Earth-constellation sowings (radish and carrot), obtained by reading deviations from a moving average rather than fitting a polynomial trend.

**Against the claim — the long-term trials and the physics:**

- **Spiess (1990a,b)** — 8-year rye and 3-year radish chronobiological trials at Dottenfelder Hof (a biodynamic farm), 16–20 seeding dates per month, randomized complete blocks: largest lunar effects at *emergence*, largely washed out by compensatory growth by final yield; **the sidereal (Thun) trigon relationships were not confirmed.**
- **Mayoral et al. (2020)** — review of 120+ references: lunar gravity is ~300,000× weaker than Earth's and lunar illuminance ~128,000× weaker than sunlight, both negligible for plant physiology; concludes that popular lunar-phase agricultural practices have no scientific backing. (Predates Singiri et al. 2023, which found a real *moonlight* signalling effect — see dossier 015 — but says nothing about the calendar.)

**Honest status of the claim, stated up front.**

1. **The sidereal arm is the weakest arm of the lunar-planting claim.** The one long-term trial *designed* to test it (Spiess, 8 years) found nothing, and the positive result is a **post-hoc re-analysis** of that same trial's data using a different smoothing method. A re-analysis that finds an effect the original analysis did not is a hypothesis, not a confirmation.
2. **Bishop's trial is a single unreplicated run with small N** (n=8 per group). The effect size is large and the p-values strong, which is exactly why it is worth a replication — and exactly why one run cannot settle it.
3. **The scout report explicitly names this as a replication candidate**: *"Element-day sowings — Bishop protocol (36 sowings over 3 sidereal months) could be replicated with larger N and pre-registration."* This dossier is that replication, at home scale.
4. **A null result is the more likely outcome and is a complete, publishable result.** It would be the first home-scale burial of the calendar claim, and it would sit alongside Spiess's null rather than contradicting it.

**A note on what this card is NOT testing.** It is not testing whether moonlight affects plants (that is dossier 015, and the evidence there is a real mechanistic study). It is not testing the biodynamic *preparations* (a separate claim, with its own evidence: supported in greenhouse pots, null at field scale over 27 years — see `sources/steiner-roots-2026-09-09.md`). It is testing one specific, checkable thing: **does the sidereal calendar predict which sowing day gives the better crop?**

## Why it matters

- It is the **practical heart of the biodynamic calendar** — the thing the calendar is *for*. The archive holds a large Steiner/biodynamic corpus and this specific claim has never been tested at home.
- It is **free of apparatus**. No device, no purchased input, no special material. Seeds, pots, soil, a scale, and a free calendar. This is the cheapest card in the queue and the only one a family can run with what is already in the shed.
- It teaches the scientific method where it is hardest: **you must not know which pot is which while you measure it.** The blind is the whole lesson, and it is a lesson a nine-year-old can hold.
- The counter-evidence is genuinely strong, which makes the test *honest* rather than promotional. The card is a test of a claim, not an endorsement of a calendar.

## Replicability: `home`

- **Cost:** ~$30–50 (one packet of a fast uniform crop, 27 small pots or a 27-cell tray, potting mix, a kitchen scale that reads to 1 g, labels). A biodynamic sowing calendar is available free online from biodynamic associations; do not buy one.
- **Safety:** none beyond ordinary garden practice. No mains, no chemicals, no heat.
- **Time:** 27 consecutive days of sowing (5 minutes/day) + one 28-day crop cycle + weighing. ~2 months wall-clock.
- **Accessibility:** the sowing and watering are a young child's job. The weighing and the unblinding are the adult's. The blind is the point — set it up so the person weighing cannot see the calendar key.

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- **Seed:** ONE packet of a fast, uniform, leaf-harvested crop. **Radish, mustard greens, or lettuce** — Bishop used lettuce; radish and mustard are faster and more uniform. The whole run must come from a single packet so genetics are not a variable.
- **Containers:** **27 identical small pots** (or one 27-cell seed tray). Identical size and identical medium is the whole game.
- **Medium:** one batch of potting mix, pre-mixed and used for every pot by volume.
- **Water:** one measured volume per pot per day (e.g. 50 mL), identical for all.
- **Scale:** a kitchen scale reading to 1 g.
- **Labels:** pots numbered **1–27 only**. No dates on the pots.
- **Calendar key:** a biodynamic sowing calendar, written down separately and **sealed** — the person weighing must not see it.
- **A daily rotation plan:** pots move one position along the shelf every day, so no pot sits in the same light for the run.

## Protocol

1. **Pre-register.** Before sowing anything, write down and sign: the crop, the seed count per pot, the water volume per pot per day, the harvest age, the pass/fail thresholds (below), and who will weigh. Put it in the family log. Do not change it mid-run.
2. **Sow one pot per day for 27 consecutive days** — one full sidereal month (27.32 days). Same seed count, same depth, same medium, same water. Label the pot with its **number only** (1–27), in sowing order.
3. **Rotate daily.** Move every pot one place along the shelf each day so position and light are averaged across the run. Water all pots by the same measured volume.
4. **Harvest each pot at the same age** — e.g. 28 days after *its own* sowing date. Cut the whole pot's tops at soil level.
5. **Weigh blind.** Weigh each pot's fresh mass in grams. **The weigher must not know the sowing dates or the calendar.** Record number → mass. Do this before anyone looks at the calendar.
6. **Unblind.** Only now, look up the sowing date for each pot number and classify it by element day using the calendar. Group the masses: leaf days, root days, fruit days, flower days.
7. **Compare group means** and report every pot, not just the means. A single 40 g outlier in a group of six is the whole result, and hiding it would be the failure mode this card exists to prevent.

## Pass / fail criteria (pre-registered)

- **PASS:** leaf-day pots average **≥20% more fresh mass** than root-day pots, **and** the group ordering runs in the direction the claim predicts (leaf > flower/fruit > root, or at minimum leaf > root). Report the actual magnitude — do not round it up.
- **FAIL:** all element-day groups fall within **±10%** of each other. → **The sidereal calendar does not predict sowing outcome at home scale.** Skeptic's Star material. This is a genuinely valuable result: it would be the first home-scale test of the calendar claim, and it would agree with Spiess's 8-year null.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** germination below 50% in any group; any pot lost, knocked over, or dried out (log it as excluded, do not delete it); the calendar key was consulted before weighing; or the group spread within a single element day exceeds 40% of the mean (which means the run was too small or the conditions were not matched).

**Report the two directions separately.** The leaf-day result and the root-day result are independent findings and either can stand alone.

## Under-promise, stated plainly

Six or seven pots per element day cannot resolve a 7% effect — the size Kollerstrom's re-analysis claims. It **can** resolve the effect size Bishop reported, which is enormous (a swing from +29.6 oz to −34.1 oz between leaf and root days). If the claim is that large, a family will see it. If it is not, the family will see parity, and parity is the honest answer the archive wants.

## What would change the verdict

- **A second independent household** running the same protocol with the same result moves the dossier from `protocol` to `replicated` or `refuted`. One shelf is an anecdote; two is a datum.
- **A 3-sidereal-month run** (Bishop's full design, 36 sowings) with pre-registration is the natural second-stage dossier — it is the design the archive has never had.
- **A positive result here** would be the first home-scale support for the biodynamic calendar. **A negative result** would be the first honest burial of it, and would be worth publishing just as loudly.
