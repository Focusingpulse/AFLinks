## The Claim

The psi-track tradition holds that **an intense thought leaves a track in space** — a
narrow, roughly 1–1.5 m wide line emanating from the thinker's body at about navel
height — and that a person with a dowsing rod can follow that track from the point
of sending to the object or place the thought was directed at, even with no prior
knowledge of where it is.

The claim has one documented experimental lineage in the archive:

- **Göte Andersson** (a Värmland artist) discovered the effect in 1987 while trying
  to measure an aura with a dowsing rod.
- **Jens A. Tellefsen** (physicist, Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm) and
  **Nils-Olof Jacobson** (psychiatrist) designed and ran the scientific experiments,
  with **Kristina Anjou**.
- Their paper, *"Dowsing Along the Psi Track,"* won the **Imich Prize** (awarded by
  the Society for Psychical Research, 1994; 600 USD of a split 1,000 USD prize) and
  was published in the *Journal of the Society for Psychical Research*.
- **Reported result:** of **40 double-blind trials** across six occasions over three
  years, the group found the hidden object in **32 cases**, typically within about
  half an hour, in fields of 100 × 100 m or larger.

**Honest status of the claim — stated up front:**

- **This is contested parapsychology.** The psi-track has never been independently
  replicated outside the Swedish group, and the broader psi literature (ganzfeld,
  telepathy meta-analyses) has a mixed replication record. The Imich Prize citation
  itself is unusual: the committee split the prize because *"none of the entries
  merited the 1,000-dollar prize"* and explicitly excluded professional
  parapsychologists — so the award is a signal of interest, not of independent
  confirmation.
- **The source describes its own controls.** The experiment area is swept with the
  rod first to establish that nothing there (water veins, fracture zones) already
  triggers the rod; the dowser is kept fully blind to the hiding place; the hider
  leaves the area. These are real design elements, and a home replication must keep
  them.
- **A null is the likely outcome on a family bench** — and a null is a genuinely
  useful result: it converts an unexamined belief into a measured null with the
  archive's own source cited.
- **Safety and honesty:** this is a test of a phenomenon, **not** advice to rely on
  dowsing in a real emergency. Never substitute a dowsing protocol for a proper
  search-and-rescue call.

**Claimant:** Göte Andersson, Jens A. Tellefsen, Nils-Olof Jacobson (Sweden, 1987–1994)
**Verification status:** unverified; no independent replication known to the archive; the reported 32/40 result has never been reproduced outside the original group
**Mainstream position:** no accepted mechanism by which a thought produces a spatially extended, dowsing-detectable track; the reported effect is attributed to sensory leakage, ideomotor effects, and chance unless controlled

## The Protocol — three roles, one blind, two endpoints

The testable core: **if a thought leaves a track, a dowser kept blind to the hiding
place should follow it to the object at a rate above chance, and with a distance
error smaller than a random guess.** If the result is at chance, the claim is
retired honestly.

### Materials (~$0–30)

1. **Dowsing rods** — a forked branch (free) or a pair of angle rods (~$0–15).
   Angle rods react at the track's outer edges; forked branches find the middle.
2. **A hidden object** — a small, distinctive item with a personal connection to the
   sender (a rock crystal, a watch, a house key). One per trial.
3. **A large open area** — ideally ≥ 30 × 30 m, free of water veins and fracture
   zones (swept first); a clean field, a large park, or a swept gym floor.
4. **Markers** (flags/cones) and a **tape measure** or phone GPS for distance error.
5. **A sealed key** — the hider writes the true location and seals it; nobody else
   sees it until scoring.
6. **Log sheets** and a **timer**.

### Roles (the experiment is inherently multiplayer)

- **The hider** — places the object, records its true location, seals the key, and
  leaves the area.
- **The sender** — stations at a marked sending location, concentrates intensely on
  the object (visualization), and creates the track. The sender does **not** know
  where the object is.
- **The dowser** — works slowly in widening circles from the sending location,
  marks rod reactions, follows the resulting line, and declares a final location.
  The dowser must have **no knowledge** of the hiding place.
- **The scorer** — measures the distance between the declared location and the true
  location, records the result, and keeps the key sealed until all trials are scored.

(Sender and dowser may be the same person — the source notes the track stays at the
sending location once the thought is released — but never the hider.)

### Procedure

1. **Sweep the area.** Before any trial, sweep the whole area with the rod to
   establish a clean baseline — no water veins or fracture zones that already
   trigger it.
2. **Hide and seal.** The hider places the object somewhere in the area, records the
   true location, seals it, and leaves.
3. **Send.** The sender stations at the marked sending location and concentrates on
   the object for a fixed interval (the source uses ~5–10 minutes).
4. **Dows.** The dowser searches from the sending location, following the track, and
   declares a final location. Time to declaration is recorded (source reports ~30
   min typical).
5. **Score blind.** The scorer measures the distance error and records hit/miss.
   Define "hit" **before** the run (e.g. within 2 m of the object).
6. **Repeat.** Run **≥ 10 trials** on different days, re-randomizing the object
   location and re-coding each trial. Rotate roles between people across trials.
7. **Unseal and analyze.** Unseal the key only after all trials are scored. Compare
   the hit rate against the chance rate implied by the area and the hit radius, and
   the mean distance error against a random-guess baseline.

### Controls and confounds (document all)

- **Sensory leakage:** the hider must not be visible or audible to the dowser; the
  hider's path must not leave usable tracks (the source notes the hider leaves the
  area). Consider having the hider approach the spot by an indirect route.
- **Ideomotor effect:** the dowser's own expectations can move the rod. Keep the
  dowser fully blind and rotate roles; an experienced dowser's prior beliefs are a
  confound.
- **Chance baseline:** define the hit radius and the search area *before* the run so
  the chance rate is computable. A 2 m radius in a 30 × 30 m field is ~1.4% per
  trial; in a 100 × 100 m field it is ~0.13%.
- **Ground truth:** sweep first (water veins/fracture zones); avoid areas with
  buried pipes or cables.
- **Scoring bias:** the scorer must be blind to the dowser's confidence and must not
  adjust the hit radius after the fact.
- **Operator:** rotate roles across trials so no single person's skill/belief drives
  the result.

## Pass/Fail Criteria (pre-registered)

**PASS (supports the claim):**
- Hit rate (within the pre-registered radius) is **≥ 2× the computed chance rate**,
  AND
- Mean distance error is **significantly below** the random-guess baseline
  (e.g. mean error < half the expected random distance), AND
- The result **replicates in a second block** of ≥ 10 trials with roles rotated
  and the object location re-randomized

**FAIL (refutes, for this family/bench):**
- Hit rate at or below the computed chance rate, OR
- Mean distance error no better than random guess, OR
- Dowsers track a systematic direction (e.g. always toward the sending location or
  toward a fixed landmark) rather than the object

**INCONCLUSIVE:**
- Hit rate between chance and the 2× threshold
- Fewer than 10 trials completed
- Area not swept clean (rod already reacting before any trial)
- Roles not rotated / dowser not fully blind

**Honest note:** the most likely outcome for a family is **FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE**,
and that is a genuinely useful outcome: it converts "thoughts leave tracks" from an
unexamined belief into a measured null on your own field, with the archive's own
source cited. A clean FAIL earns the Skeptic's Star. A PASS would be a serious
anomaly worth telling the world about — which is why the protocol is blinded before
anyone touches a rod.

1. **The field** — before and after sweeping, with the sending location marked
2. **The sealed key** (photographed sealed, then opened *after* all trials are scored)
3. **Per-trial log** — hider, sender, dowser, scorer; object true location (sealed);
   declared location; distance error; hit/miss; time to declaration
4. **The dowsing line** — marked with cones/flags and photographed
5. **The pre-written hit-radius definition** (so the chance rate is auditable)
6. **The scoring table** and the unsealing photo
