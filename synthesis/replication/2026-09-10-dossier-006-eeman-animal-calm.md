## The claim (as asserted by the tradition)

Some biofield practitioners report that placing an animal (dog, cat, horse, livestock) in an Eeman-style circuit — copper coil contacts at head and extremities, no power source — produces visible calm: reduced restlessness, slower breathing, less reactive behavior.

**Honest framing:** This is a *claim* from the subtle-energy tradition, adjacent to animal healing and biofield work. The dossier exists to test ONE measurable facet cheaply: does the circuit change observable animal behavior vs a sham (same setup, disconnected wire)? We hold no brief — the test is the point.

## Why it matters

Animal calm is a practical concern for families with pets, homesteaders with livestock, and anyone managing animal stress (vet visits, travel, storms). A $10 device that actually calms animals would be valuable. A negative result is equally valuable: it tells families the tradition's claim does not replicate under blind test, saving them money and expectation. Either outcome feeds the archive.

## Replicability: `home`

- Build cost: **~$10** (same as human Eeman — insulated copper wire + contacts)
- Safety: trivial (no power, no heat, no electricity)
- Accessibility: kitchen table in an evening; same apparatus as human test

## Apparatus (Bill of Materials)

- ~5–10 m insulated copper wire (~0.5–1 mm²; hookup wire works)
- One piece of flexible foam/board (~40 × 20 cm) or just tape channels
- Two flat metal/foil contact pads (or bare-wire loops) — sized for animal's head and rear contact
- A 10-minute timer and a way to observe/record animal behavior (phone camera works)

## Protocol (Test A — behavior observation, blind)

1. Build TWO identical-looking mats: the **circuit** (coil laid in a figure-eight, connections joined, contacts at head and rear) and the **sham** (identical layout, but one wire CUT so it is not a closed circuit — visually identical).
2. Recruit one animal + one helper (the helper knows which is which; the animal obviously doesn't). Label mats X and Y.
3. Place animal on mat X for 10 minutes. Observer (who doesn't know which is which) records: restlessness (scale 0-10), breathing rate (count breaths/30s), vocalizations, reactivity to stimuli. Then 10 minutes off-mat. Then mat Y, same observation.
4. Repeat 5+ sessions on different days, randomizing order each time.
5. Analyze: behavior scores circuit vs sham. Test for consistent difference (same direction ≥4/5 sessions).

## Pass / fail criteria (pre-registered)

- **Circuit shows consistently calmer behavior vs sham** (lower restlessness, slower breathing, fewer vocalizations — same direction ≥4/5 sessions, delta ≥2 points restlessness or ≥3 breaths/30s) → the device has a measurable effect on animals; escalate to species-specific follow-up.
- **No consistent difference** (chance-level across sessions) → the tradition's animal-calm claim does not replicate under blind test; log it honestly (Skeptic's Star material).
- Either outcome is a win for the archive: a data point where there was none.
