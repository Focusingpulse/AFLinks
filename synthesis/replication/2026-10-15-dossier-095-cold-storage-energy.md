## The claim under test

The source is a comparative study, carried out in the **Department of Thermal Engineering of Gdańsk University of Technology**, of a refrigeration device run with and without **HPT® torsion-wave polarizers** — passive devices made by the Polish firm **P.P.H.U. TORST**, which the source describes as *"concentrators, polarizers and generators of HPT® torsion waves"* that *"require no power supply."*

The claim is applied and quantitative:

> *"During operation with the thermostat, an average driving-power consumption lower by 13.5% was recorded in the case of the installed polarizers (series 1 and 3). A shorter relative operating time of the aggregate was also found."*

and, in the second experiment:

> *"After the polarizers were installed in the chamber (curves E and F), a shortening of both the cooling and the freezing time of the water was found — on average by about 37%."*

and, structurally:

> *"An influence of the polarizers on the structure of the frost forming was observed. This concerns both the frost accumulating on the evaporator and the icing of the expansion valve … Moreover, a specific structure of the frozen condensate was observed."*

**The mechanism the source invokes** (recorded as the source's own, tested not at all by this card): torsion fields — the spin polarisation of the physical vacuum — act on matter, *"for example, by changing the properties of water"*; the polarizers *"purify and"* concentrate that field; the changed water then freezes differently, which changes how frost forms on the evaporator, which changes how often the compressor runs.

## The source's own numbers (what the family is asked to beat)

**Experiment 1 — the refrigeration device.** A teaching stand: refrigeration chamber **~1.3 m³**, aggregate rated power **435 W**. Four test series, in **continuous mode** (defrost every 20 h for 30 min) and in **thermostat mode** (cut-out **+2 °C**, switching differential **3 K**). The thermal load was *"the heat flux penetrating through the chamber casing"*, with **trays of water** placed in the chamber *"to simulate the process of moisture evaporating from goods."*

| Measurement | Without polarizers | With polarizers |
|---|---|---|
| Evaporator frosting (thermostat mode) | *"complete coverage of the evaporator tubes with frost"* | *"a shorter zone of greater frost formation and a thinner layer of frost on the tubes"* |
| Average drive power (thermostat mode, series 1 & 3) | baseline | **−13.5%** |
| Relative operating time of the aggregate | baseline | shorter |
| Evaporator frosting (continuous mode) | uniform | less frost, *"more 'fluffy' structure"* |
| Expansion-valve frost | uniform | *"beaded"*, later *"plate-like"*; *"needle-like inclusions with vertical orientation"* in the condensate-tray ice |
| Internal coefficient of performance | **~2.5** | **~2.5** (unchanged) |

**Experiment 2 — the freezing time of water.** A 28 m³ universal freezing chamber served by two 6 kW compressor aggregates; a **20 dm³ bucket of water** with a temperature-recorder sensor immersed in it, taken from **+15 °C to −1.5 °C** during normal chamber operation. *"After the polarizers were installed in the chamber … a shortening of both the cooling and the freezing time of the water was found — on average by about 37%."*

## The source's own counterweights (quoted, not paraphrased)

The source states its own central limitation plainly, and the card carries it as the primary counterweight:

> *"During the tests, the thermal load of the refrigeration chamber was not determined. There is therefore no possibility of determining the coefficient of cooling performance. The internal coefficient of cooling performance, determined on the basis of the measured instantaneous pressure and temperature values in the refrigeration circuit, remained at a similar level (about 2.5 in the case of operation with the thermostat) — regardless of the presence of the polarizers."*

> *"The ambient temperature of the chamber (which affects the thermal load) was similar for all measurement series, but not identical. It was not regulated."*

Read together, these two sentences are the sharpest thing in the paper: **the source measured a 13.5% power saving while its own measurement of the machine's efficiency did not move.** If the chamber's thermal load and the machine's COP are both unchanged, the drive power should be unchanged. So the saving must come from somewhere the study did not measure — most plausibly the **unregulated ambient temperature**, which sets the thermal load the compressor has to remove and which the authors admit was *"not identical"* between series. The card's design is built to close exactly that hole.

Three further limits, stated and not hidden:

1. **Author conflict.** Henryk Pacocha is P.P.H.U. TORST — the firm that manufactures the polarizers under test. The co-author is a university department, which is what makes the study worth reading at all; but the paper is not independent of the product.
2. **No blinding, no randomisation, no replication.** The four series are described but not randomised; the operator knew which configuration was running; and the paper reports no second laboratory, no repeat campaign, and no attempt to reproduce by anyone else in the twenty years since publication.
3. **The mechanism is not mainstream.** The Russian Academy of Sciences dismissed the torsion programme in the 1990s; the claim-status record this card sits beside carries status **suppressed** with grounds [dismissal, funding-cutoff, institutional-death].

## The device problem — stated, not hidden

**The HPT® polarizer is proprietary.** The source publishes no construction detail, no dimensions, no materials, and no schematic — only *"Fig. 1. Diagram of the refrigeration device after installation of the polarisers"* — and says explicitly that *"the type and manner of use of this technology is developed individually, depending on the type of application and installation, the size of the devices, the method of mounting, etc."* It is a commercial product sold to industry, not to households.

**Consequence, and the card says it in the quest text:** this card **cannot** test the HPT® device. What it can test is the **class claim the source itself makes** — that a passive, unpowered device placed in a refrigeration appliance changes the appliance's energy use and the freezing behaviour of water. The card therefore names a **buildable passive device** (a hand-wound copper coil) and a **matched inert control** (the same length of the same wire, unwound, of the same mass), and it states the asymmetry honestly:

- **A positive result** (the coil arm uses measurably less energy than the matched inert arm, at a fixed thermostat setting and a logged ambient) would be the **first independent confirmation of any part of this claim**, and would justify chasing the proprietary device.
- **A null result** refutes the **class claim** — that a passive unpowered object of this kind does anything to a freezer — and does **not** by itself refute the specific HPT® product, which this card never tests. That asymmetry is written into the pass/fail.

## The rival explanation

There is no published rival *experiment*; the rival is the **ordinary thermal explanation**, and it is strong:

1. **A freezer's power draw is dominated by its thermal load**, which is set by ambient temperature, door openings, the mass and temperature of what is put in, and the frost layer on the evaporator. The source controlled none of these across series and admits the ambient *"was not regulated."*
2. **Frost is a real, large, self-reinforcing variable.** A thicker frost layer insulates the evaporator and lengthens every subsequent compressor run. Any device — or any change in how the door is opened, or in how long the chamber was left closed — that happens to leave less frost will show a lower power figure *without any field being involved*. The source's own frost photographs are the most interesting thing in the paper and also the most likely to be a **consequence** of a different duty cycle rather than its cause.
3. **The freezing-time result is the same problem in a different room.** A 28 m³ chamber served by two 6 kW aggregates is a large, slow, poorly-controlled thermal system; *"the measurements were carried out during the normal operation of the chamber"* and *"it was not possible to determine the electricity consumption separately for the chamber under consideration."* A 37% change in the time to freeze a 20 dm³ bucket is a large claim measured in an uncontrolled working cold store.
4. **The internal COP did not move.** This is the source's own null, and it is the reason the card's primary endpoint is **energy per unit of fixed thermal work**, not energy per day.

## Protocol (pre-registered)

**Apparatus.** A household chest freezer or fridge-freezer (the family's own), a **plug-in energy meter** that logs cumulative kWh, two **identical 1 L water bottles** (same shape, same fill), a **thermometer or data logger** for the ambient air of the room and for the water, and a **passive device** the family builds: **30 turns of 1.5 mm² insulated copper wire wound into a 15 cm coil**, taped to the inside wall of the freezer. The **control** is the *same length of the same wire*, unwound into a straight bundle of the **same mass**, taped in the same place with the same tape.

**Instrument check (must pass, or the run is VOID, not null).** Before anything else, run two 24 h baseline periods with **no device at all** and confirm the freezer's kWh figures agree within the meter's own resolution and the room's normal temperature swing. If two identical no-device days differ by more than the effect you are trying to detect, the freezer is too noisy for this card and the honest outcome is to say so.

**Design.** A **blinded, alternating ABAB** schedule over **8 periods of 24 h** (A = coil, B = matched inert control), with the device swapped by a second person who does not tell the operator which arm is running, and the arm recorded on a sealed sheet. Held constant across all periods: thermostat setting, freezer contents (a fixed, weighed load), door-opening rule (log every opening), and defrost state (defrost fully before period 1 and do not defrost again).

**Primary endpoint.** **kWh per 24 h, normalised to the logged mean ambient temperature** (regress each period's kWh on its mean ambient and compare the arms at a common ambient). The source's claim predicts the coil arm is **≥ 13.5% lower**.

**Secondary endpoints.**
- **Freezing time of 1 L of water** from +15 °C to 0 °C, measured in each arm, with the bottle placed in the same position and the water starting at the same temperature. The source's claim predicts **~37% shorter**.
- **Frost structure** — photographs of the evaporator and of the ice in the condensate tray at the end of each period, described against the source's own vocabulary (*"beaded"*, *"plate-like"*, *"needle-like inclusions with vertical orientation"*).
- **Duty cycle** — the fraction of each 24 h period the compressor is running (audible or vibration-sensed), which is the closest a household can get to the source's own COP question.

**Blinding.** The operator does not know which arm is running. The person who swaps the device does not read the meter. The sealed sheet is opened only after all 8 periods are logged.

**Repeat.** The full 8-period set is run **twice** (16 periods total), on separate weeks, re-randomising the arm order the second time.

## Pass/fail

- **PASS (the claim survives):** the coil arm's ambient-normalised kWh is **≥ 10% lower** than the matched inert arm's, in **both** weeks, with the instrument check passed and the blinding held; and the 1 L freezing time is shorter in the coil arm in both weeks. This would be the first independent confirmation of any part of the claim.
- **PASS (the rival is sufficient — the expected, ordinary result):** the two arms' ambient-normalised kWh agree within the meter resolution and the between-period scatter, in both weeks, with the instrument check passed. **A passive device of this kind does nothing measurable to a freezer's energy use** (Skeptic's Star). This is the result the card expects and it is a complete result.
- **FAIL:** the arms differ but **not** in the pre-registered direction (the matched inert arm uses less), or the difference appears in one week and reverses in the other.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** the instrument check did not pass (two no-device days differed by more than the target effect); the ambient temperature moved by more than 3 K within a period; the freezer contents or door-opening count differed materially between arms; the blinding was broken; fewer than 8 periods per week; the meter's resolution is coarser than the effect being sought.
- **ARTIFACT:** the kWh figure tracks the ambient temperature, the door-opening count, the frost state, or the week rather than the arm; the frost photographs differ because the defrost state differed; the operator's expectation is visible in the arm order.
