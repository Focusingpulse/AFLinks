## Abstract

Until this week the archive held zero works on ball lightning — the one anomalous phenomenon with a 273-year scientific pedigree, a death in its founding record (Richmann, 1753), and statistical estimates suggesting every human has a non-trivial chance of witnessing it in a lifetime. Three scout rounds in 72 hours assembled the lane nearly complete: the Soviet observational foundation (Stakhanov's 1979 Atomizdat monograph, full English translation, free) [1], the current theory restatement carrying the whole observational genealogy (Nikitin 2025) [2], the mainstream counterweight (Boerner's Springer monograph, 2nd ed. 2025) [3], and — the actual event — **three independent laboratory programs producing self-confining luminous objects with ball-lightning properties**: Gatchina's 4,000+ plasmoids at ~100% reproducibility (J. Phys. D, August 2026) [5], Prague's water-contact plasmoids filmed at initiation (J. Phys. D, 2025) [6], and Shanghai's relativistic terahertz soliton in *Nature Photonics* (April 2026), 100 ns in the lab frame — equivalent, under the authors' own scaling, to metre-scale, second-scale natural ball lightning [4]. The convergence this document traces is not that the phenomenon is "solved" — it is that **the observational ledger and the laboratory ledger now describe the same object**: a shell-structured, water-adjacent, self-confining electromagnetic body, and that this object sits, unrecognized, at the intersection of four research programs the archive already holds. And one detail refuses to stay quiet: at every node of the story — natural, theoretical, experimental — **water appears**: as the calorimeter of natural events, as the structural shell material of the theory, and as the birth medium of the laboratory objects.

## 1. The Orphan: 273 Years of Reports Nobody Wanted

Ball lightning's founding document is a death certificate. On 26 July 1753, Professor Georg-Wilhelm Richmann was killed in St. Petersburg while measuring the electric field under a thundercloud, when a luminous ball flew out of his apparatus. The engraver Sokolov, who witnessed it, drew the scene; Lomonosov traced the object's path through the laboratory — the stopped clock, the scattered sand on the stove — making him, in Nikitin's telling, the first scientist to study ball lightning's *effects* rather than debate its existence [2]. The debate ran anyway, for two more centuries:

> "Official science did not recognize the existence of such objects for a long time, considering them a figment of the imagination of poorly educated people. Incidentally, for the same reason, reports of 'stones falling from the sky' — meteorites — were considered false." [2]

The meteorite parallel is the precise one, and the archive has seen this shape before: a phenomenon carried by witness testimony alone survives in a kind of institutional orphanage until an instrument arrives. What kept ball lightning alive through the orphanage years was volume and consistency. Stakhanov's 1979 monograph — the Soviet data-bank synthesis, based on thousands of collected reports, with a rigor that included discarding most of them — gives the core statistics: lifetime seconds to minutes, diameter typically 10–25 cm, motion both guided (along conductors, telephone lines, fences) and free, entry through windows, chimneys, and *closed glass* [1]. Nikitin, who saw one at age ten ("a red ball with a diameter of 20 cm flew past me... It moved silently above the wires of a telephone line"), opens his 2025 paper with the demographic fact:

> "Throughout human history, people have repeatedly observed glowing balls floating in the air during thunderstorms, called ball lightning. According to statistics, each of us has a chance to see ball lightning during our lifetime." [2]

A phenomenon every human has a chance to see, that killed a professor of physics in the first recorded instrument measurement, and that the institution classed with peasant superstition until the mid-twentieth century: the archive's interest needs no further defense. The question is what the observational ledger actually supports — and that is where water enters.

## 2. The Soviet Foundation: Stakhanov's Ledger, and the Water Calorimeter

I.P. Stakhanov's *The Physical Nature of Ball Lightning* (Atomizdat, Moscow 1979; complete English translation located by the scout at naturalplasmas.com, translation code TRAN11046S01) is the field's foundational observational monograph — the book Boerner's 2025 mainstream guide still treats as a primary data source [1][3]. Its method is the one this archive always rewards: **traces over testimony**. Eyewitness reports are psychology; a boiled trough is physics.

And the traces, overwhelmingly, are water events. Ball lightning falls into water and the water gives the measurement. Stakhanov's energy chapter is built from these natural calorimetry experiments:

> "Thus, the energy of ball lightning must be within a range of several units to several hundred kilojoules, whilst the energy density is 1-10 J/cm³ respectively." [1]

Nikitin's 2025 restatement carries the extreme cases. A ball that fell into a large water container heated it to boiling — an estimated 10 MJ, an energy density of 10¹⁰ J/m³ that skeptics dismissed ("they claimed that the water did not boil, but only foamed") until the cases repeated:

> "A 6.5 cm ball lightning that fell into a cattle trough with 112 liters of water caused the water to boil away. Boiled frogs were lying at the bottom of the trough. In front of numerous witnesses, a 25 cm ball lightning fell into a hole with 120 liters of water..." [2]

Two orders of magnitude separate the conservative ledger (units to hundreds of kJ) from the extreme trough events (MJ) — and Stakhanov himself flags the gap, warning against "hasty conclusions about the energy of ball lightning" from single events [1]. That honesty matters for section 6: the disagreement over ball lightning's energy content is not a fringe-versus-mainstream disagreement; it is *inside* the primary literature, carried by its two most careful authors.

Stakhanov's own model — the **cluster hypothesis**, ball lightning as a stable ensemble of complex compounds of ions and neutral molecules — is one of the few chemical models, and it makes an architectural prediction the laboratories would later echo: structure is what keeps the thing alive; a homogeneous ball of hot air is dead in milliseconds. The mystery was never the glow. It is the *confinement*.

## 3. The 2025 Restatement: Nikitin's Shell, and the Rule the Field Forgot

Anatoly I. Nikitin (Talrose Institute for Energy Problems of Chemical Physics, Russian Academy of Sciences) opens his January 2025 paper with a methodological indictment that doubles as the field's history:

> "Observational data of ball lightning often contradict each other, so it was sometimes proposed to build a theory of ball lightning based on only a small number of statistically reliable observations. Work on this principle did not bring results. The correct approach to creating the theory of ball lightning should be the desire to explain all of its properties, even those that are rare." [2]

Explain *all* the properties, even the rare ones — the passage through closed glass, the stoppage of clocks, the absence of debris after explosion. Nikitin's own candidate is a two-component body:

> "Ball lightning is a material body consisting of a shell and a multielement core." [2]

— the core an ensemble of charged "dynamic capacitors," the shell **polarized water molecules**, which he shows can in principle withstand the Coulomb repulsion of the core charges [2]. Note what the theory needs water for: not as fuel, not as witness, but as *structure* — the dielectric skin that holds the charge ensemble against its own repulsion. Note also what the paper contains almost in passing: the observational genealogy of the whole field (Stakhanov's surveys, Smirnov 1988, Grigoriev 2006/2019, Barry 1980), and one citation that points off the mainstream map entirely — **Matsumoto's micron-scale traces on photographic film from electric discharges in water, which Matsumoto proposed were micro-ball-lightning** [2]. That citation is the bridge to section 5: Matsumoto's discharge-in-water cells were cold-fusion cells.

*(Publisher caveat carried in the ledger: JAMP is an open-access SCIRP journal — predatory-adjacent; the paper stands on its citations and its internal rigor, not on its venue.)*

## 4. The Laboratories: Three Programs, One Object

### 4.1 Gatchina — the statistics machine

The Gatchina discharge (Petersburg Nuclear Physics Institute, NRC Kurchatov Institute) is the oldest and most systematic lab line — a pulsed discharge with an electrolytic electrode, carried into the mainstream by Shabanov et al.'s 2019 *Physics-Uspekhi* review, which presents its long-lived glowing formations as reproducing the properties of natural ball lightning [7]. The August 2026 J. Phys. D paper is the line's maturity claim:

> "Using this setup, more than 4000 flying plasmoids were generated and a systematic analysis of their properties was carried out. The lifetime of the generated plasmoids was shown to be up to 1 s... This type of gas discharge was shown to have a basic structure consisting of a thin (less than 3 mm) negatively charged shell kept in equilibrium by dielectric layers generated by the atmosphere." [5]

Four thousand events at ~100% reproducibility ends the statistics excuse — the instrument era has begun. And the reported architecture is Stakhanov's and Nikitin's: a thin charged shell, equilibrium maintained by dielectric layers — Nikitin's polarized-water skin wearing laboratory language. The Chinese-Russian collaboration around the same discharge has already visualized the stabilization mechanism: **a toroidal vortex structure forming inside the laboratory ball lightning** (Zhao et al., IEEE Trans. Plasma Science 2024) [9], with the companion Technical Physics papers mapping its formation dynamics and its response to applied electric and magnetic fields [10][11].

### 4.2 Prague — the birth, filmed

Stelmashuk, Schmidt, Koláček and Frolov (Institute of Plasma Physics, Czech Academy of Sciences) pointed a faster camera at the beginning:

> "This study investigates the detailed generation mechanism of ball plasmoids formed by millisecond-scale atmospheric discharges **in contact with water**." [7, emphasis added]

The frame-by-frame anatomy: breakdown inside a cathode cup of conductive liquid → shockwave → liquid injection → a weakly ionized water-vapor/droplet mixture that *is* the plasmoid's primary material → a post-breakdown arc that evolves into a plasma jet feeding the growing object from below. The spectra age with it — hydrogen Balmer lines in the jet phase, the sodium doublet dominating the autonomous plasmoid, from the salt in the water [6]. The Prague object is, literally, **born out of water and fed by a jet** — which is also the Czech answer to where the shell's dielectric material comes from.

### 4.3 Shanghai — the relativistic soliton

The third line is the one that reached the top journal. Zhou, Zhang, Qi, Bai, Zeng, Tian, Li and colleagues at the Shanghai Institute of Optics and Fine Mechanics (CAS) reported in *Nature Photonics*, April 2026:

> "Here we report the first controlled generation of a macroscopic, long-lived soliton driven by relativistic terahertz electromagnetic fields. This soliton exhibits key signatures resembling ball lightning: a millimetre-scale spherical profile, a lifetime of 100 ns in the laboratory frame (equivalent to metre-scale dimensions and second-scale durations under natural conditions) and broadband optical emission from ultraviolet to infrared..." [4]

The equivalence claimed is a scaling argument — 100 ns of relativistic soliton maps onto seconds of natural ball lightning — and the authors carry it carefully (the ledger in section 6 keeps the caveat). But the result's place in this archive is secure regardless: **Nature Photonics printed the words "ball-lightning-like" over a controlled, repeatable laboratory object**, with open source data on figshare [4]. The orphan phenomenon now has a Nature-family address.

### 4.4 And the bench-top line

Bychkov, Sorokovykh, Bychkov and Logunov (Moscow) reported in October 2025 that an erosive plasma jet from a **capillary plasmatron** on metals yields 1.5 cm spheres with 3–7 *second* lifetimes — the longest-lived of the lab objects — with a shell-plus-vapor-cavity structure and ball-lightning-like motion, and propose a quasi-solid-cover model [8]. V.L. Bychkov is the community's central node (editor of its journal line, co-author of the Gatchina gas-dynamic modeling [12]); his move to plasmatrons says the field believes the object can be made small, long-lived, and cheap.

Read the water row alone and the through-line of this synthesis is visible: natural ball lightning is *measured* by water (the trough calorimetry), *modeled* with water (Nikitin's polarized shell), and *manufactured* from water (Prague's birth medium, Gatchina's electrolytic electrode). ## 6. The Skeptical Ledger

1. **The observation problem is real.** Boerner's 2025 Springer monograph — the mainstream guide — builds its account around observation reliability: the first systematic evidence on preferred formation conditions, human-interaction cases, and an extended discussion of how far extraordinary eyewitness reports can be trusted [3]. The field's own best authors agree: Nikitin's abstract concedes the data "often contradict each other" [2], and Stakhanov discarded most of his own report collection [1]. The trough calorimetry has a two-orders-of-magnitude spread between conservative and extreme estimates, and the extreme events rest on witness estimates of water volumes and boiling — exactly the quantities memory distorts.
2. **The venue caveat.** The 2025 theory restatement [2] appears in a SCIRP journal — open-access, predatory-adjacent; it is carried for its citations and argument, not its imprimatur. The strong results [4][5][6] are in Nature Photonics and J. Phys. D — no caveat attaches.
3. **The scaling caveat.** The Shanghai soliton's equivalence to natural ball lightning is a *dimensional scaling argument* (100 ns lab-frame ≡ seconds natural) [4], not a demonstrated identity; a relativistic soliton driven by a petawatt-class laser is not obviously the same object as a thunderstorm's, however similar the silhouette.
4. **The stability objection stands.** A <3 mm charged shell held by "dielectric layers generated by the atmosphere" [5] is a description of equilibrium, not yet a mechanism of it; the same objection Nikitin answers with polarized water [2] is the one the laboratory papers inherit. Until the shell's force balance is closed in the peer-reviewed theory literature, the confinement problem is renamed, not solved.
4. **The LENR leg carries its null.** If micron-scale plasmoids are the cold-fusion active agent, the field's most expensive test — Project Callisto, ~$250M, 9 years, 200,000 calorimetry hours — returned a negative final report [18]. The community counter-review exists [19]; the null stands until a plasmoid experiment clears a Dresden-class blind protocol. The micron leg of this synthesis is a hypothesis with a pedigree, not a result.
5. **Provenance.** The Stakhanov English translation circulates via naturalplasmas.com (translation code TRAN11046S01) [1] — a community translation of record, not a publisher edition; Bychkov 2025 is indexed via its bibliographic record with the Springer original paywalled [8].

## 7. Open Questions

1. **Close the shell force-balance.** Gatchina's <3 mm negative shell [5] and Nikitin's polarized-water shell [2] are the same architectural claim in two vocabularies. A joint theoretical treatment — measured shell parameters from Gatchina/Prague inserted into Nikitin's electrostatic-dielectric model — would either close the confinement problem or show exactly where it fails. The data now exist on both sides; nobody has run the comparison.
2. **Calorimetry on the laboratory objects.** Natural ball lightning's energy ledger was written in boiled water [1][2]; no equivalent calorimetric budget has been published for the Gatchina, Prague, or plasmatron objects. Total stored energy vs. input energy is the measurement that decides whether these objects are energy *storage* (Stakhanov's ledger) or energy *transformation* — the question every energy interpretation of BL stands on.
3. **The micron leg, tested properly.** Matsumoto's film traces [2] and the ICCF plasmoid paradigm [15][16] share the Gatchina object's claimed structure at six orders of magnitude smaller scale. A Dresden-class blind replication of a plasmoid-LENR cell — with the film-trace evidence chain modernized (CR-39, SEM, isotope analysis) — is the experiment that either retires the bridge or makes it load-bearing.
4. **Is the water role causal or incidental?** Prague's plasmoids are born from water [6], Gatchina's electrode is electrolytic [7], Nikitin's shell is water [2], the natural traces are water calorimetry [1]. A systematic ablation — the same discharge geometries run with dielectric liquids other than water — would separate "water as convenient conductor" from "water as structural material." The archive's water shelf predicts the latter; the experiment is cheap and nobody has run it.
4. **Archive completion.** The Gatchina companion corpus (Zhao IEEE 2024 [9], the Technical Physics 2021/2022 dynamics pair [10][11], Bychkov's 2014 gas-dynamic modeling [12]) and the observational genealogy corpus (Smirnov 1988, Grigoriev 2006/2019, Barry 1980 — the data-bank books every statistic in this document descends from) are absent from the shelf. Both are now proposed in the family ledger (`upgrade-262`, `upgrade-263`).
