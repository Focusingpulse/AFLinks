# The Pilot Wave Continuum: Convergent Models of Information-Carrying Fields Across Physics and Consciousness Research

## Abstract

A convergent claim emerges across independent research traditions spanning nearly a century: matter is accompanied by information-carrying fields whose *shape* rather than amplitude determines their physical effect. This synthesis traces the pilot wave concept from de Broglie's original proposal (1927) through Bohm's quantum potential (1952) to modern extensions in torsion field theory (Shipov/Akimov), form wave research (Chauméry/de Belizal, Vibratis), scalar field cosmology (Maillot, Toulgoat), and consciousness-as-field models (Manzalini). The convergent claim is that these are not separate phenomena but different names for the same class of fields—information-encoding structures that guide matter without transferring energy in the classical sense.

---

## 1. The Convergent Claim

Across French radiesthesia, Russian torsion physics, Italian quantum biophysics, Greek Platonic unification, and Western pilot wave theory, researchers independently arrive at a strikingly similar model:

**A field exists whose shape encodes information that guides matter.**

The field has these properties:
- Shape or curvature matters more than amplitude
- Information is carried with negligible energy transfer
- The field responds to geometric form, not just mass or charge
- Consciousness can interact with the field
- The field connects to vacuum/ether structure

Different traditions name it differently:
- **Pilot wave** (de Broglie, Bohm)
- **Torsion field** (Shipov, Akimov)
- **Form wave / onde de forme** (Chauméry, de Belizal, Vibratis)
- **Vacuum tension field T_v** (Maillot/TUO)
- **Scalar field Φ** (Toulgoat)
- **Consciousness field** (Manzalini)
- **Morphic field** (Sheldrake)

This synthesis argues these are different names for the same phenomenon.

## 2. The Pilot Wave Tradition: de Broglie to Bohm

### 2.1 Louis de Broglie's Original Proposal (1927)

At the 1927 Solvay Conference, Louis de Broglie proposed that quantum particles are guided by a "pilot wave" — a physical wave that accompanies matter and determines its trajectory. The particle always has a definite position; the wave carries information about possible paths.

De Broglie's key insight: the wave doesn't push the particle like a surfboard. Instead, the wave's *shape* encodes information that the particle "reads" to determine its motion. This is fundamentally different from classical force.

De Broglie abandoned his theory under pressure from the Copenhagen school, but it was later revived by David Bohm in 1952.

### 2.2 Bohm's Quantum Potential and Active Information

David Bohm reformulated de Broglie's pilot wave as the "quantum potential" Q, derived from the Schrödinger equation:

$$Q = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\nabla^2 R}{R}$$

where R is the amplitude of the wave function.

Bohm and Hiley's crucial insight: Q represents **active information** — the wave encodes information about the environment, which the particle "reads" and uses to guide its motion. The particle's own energy is used; the wave provides information, not energy.

Properties of the quantum potential:
- It does not fall off with distance (non-local)
- It depends on the *shape* of the wave (the ratio ∇²R/R), not its amplitude
- It encodes context-dependent information
- It can produce effects without classical energy transfer

This is precisely what torsion field researchers and form wave practitioners describe.

### 2.3 Valentini's Extension: Beyond Quantum Equilibrium

Antony Valentini extended de Broglie-Bohm theory by proposing that quantum theory is a special case of a broader physics. In quantum equilibrium (Born rule), the theory reproduces standard QM. But away from equilibrium, new phenomena become possible:
- Superluminal signaling
- Non-quantum correlations
- Access to "hidden" information

This is precisely what consciousness researchers and radiesthesists claim: living systems operate away from quantum equilibrium, allowing access to information fields unavailable to inanimate matter.

## 3. Torsion Fields as Pilot Waves

### 3.1 Shipov's Theory of the Physical Vacuum

G.I. Shipov's Theory of the Physical Vacuum extends Einstein-Cartan geometry to include torsion as a fundamental field. In Shipov's framework:
- Spacetime has both curvature (gravity) and torsion (spin)
- Torsion fields are generated by spin, not mass
- Torsion fields carry information without energy
- The torsion field is the "pilot" that guides matter

Shipov explicitly connects torsion to de Broglie-Bohm theory. The torsion field is the geometric manifestation of the pilot wave — a structure in spacetime that encodes information about particle trajectories.

Key properties (matching the pilot wave):
- Torsion fields do not diminish with distance (non-local)
- Shape/structure matters more than amplitude
- Information transfer does not require energy transfer
- Generated by spin angular momentum

### 3.2 Akimov's EGS-Field Concept

A.E. Akimov unified electromagnetic (E), gravitational (G), and torsion/spin (S) fields as polarization states of the physical vacuum. The vacuum is a material medium; E, G, and S are different ways it can be polarized.

Crucially, Akimov distinguished:
- **Static torsion fields**: weak effects (10⁻⁶⁰ of gravity)
- **Dynamic torsion fields**: strong effects (comparable to EM)

This resolves the Cartan objection. Élie Cartan calculated torsion effects as 10⁻⁶⁰ of gravity and dismissed them as undetectable. But Cartan considered only static torsion (from spinning matter). Dynamic torsion (from changing spin states) has much larger coupling.

Dynamic torsion fields are generated by:
- Rotating masses (gyroscopes)
- Changing magnetic fields
- Consciousness (organized spin states in the brain)

### 3.3 Experimental Evidence

Shipov and Akimov cite numerous experiments:

**Samokhvalov experiments**: Rotating disks in vacuum chamber produce non-gravitational forces on nearby objects. Mass repulsion observed — opposite of gravitational attraction.

**Tolchin's inertoid**: Device that changes momentum using only internal inertial forces — impossible in Newtonian mechanics but predicted by torsion theory.

**Filatov's gyroscope collisions**: Violation of momentum conservation when precessing gyroscopes collide.

**Dzhanibekov effect**: In microgravity, spinning objects spontaneously change their axis of rotation at regular intervals — "quantum" behavior at macroscopic scale.

These anomalies point to a field that guides matter without classical force.

## 4. Form Waves: The French Tradition

### 4.1 Chauméry and de Belizal

In the 1930s-1950s, Léon Chauméry and André de Belizal developed "vibratory radiesthesia" — the study of how geometric form affects subtle energy. Their key discoveries:
- Every shape emits a "form wave" (onde de forme)
- The wave's character depends on geometry, not material
- Certain shapes (spheres, cones, spirals) produce specific effects
- The wave can be detected with pendulums and measured on scales (Bovis, Universal)

They invented instruments: the Universal Pendulum, the Egyptian Pendulum, the Snail-Selector. These tools detect form waves by amplifying the body's subtle response.

Crucially, Chauméry and de Belizal found:
> "Forms capture terrestrial magnetism, become saturated with it, and re-emit the captured energy as a form wave."

This is precisely the pilot wave concept: form encodes information that guides energy.

### 4.2 Vibratis and the Modern French School

Vibratis (a contemporary French school) extends form wave theory:
- Form waves carry information without energy
- The body detects form waves through magnetite receptors (matching Rocard's findings)
- Consciousness interacts with form waves
- Form waves can be beneficial (harmonizing) or harmful (geopathic stress)

They distinguish:
- **Ondes de forme** (form waves): from geometric shape
- **Ondes de pensée** (thought waves): from consciousness
- **Ondes de vie** (life waves): from living systems

All are information-carrying fields with pilot-wave properties.

### 4.3 The Bovis Scale

Alfred Bovis (1900) developed a scale to measure the "vital energy" of places, objects, and organisms. The scale ranges from 0-18,000+ Bovis Units (UB):
- 0-10,000 UB: Physical domain
- 10,000-14,000 UB: Energetic domain
- 14,000+ UB: Spiritual domain

Healthy humans: 8,000-10,000 UB
Sacred sites: 12,000-18,000+ UB
Diseased tissue: Below 6,500 UB

The Bovis scale measures information content, not energy. A small sacred object can have higher Bovis than a large secular one. This matches the pilot wave: shape encodes information regardless of amplitude.

## 5. Scalar Fields: The Cosmological Connection

### 5.1 Maillot's Wave Universe Theory (TUO)

François Maillot's Théorie de l'Univers Onde (Wave Universe Theory) proposes a scalar Vacuum Tension field T_v as the foundation of physics:
- T_v is a scalar field pervading space
- Gravitational field: g = -∇T_v (gradient of vacuum tension)
- The cosmological constant Λ is replaced by dynamic T_v
- T_v responds to matter distribution

Maillot explicitly connects T_v to confined electromagnetic fields (via Michaud's model) and to the de Broglie pilot wave. The vacuum tension field is the pilot wave at cosmological scale — a scalar structure that guides gravitational dynamics.

### 5.2 Toulgoat's Scalar Field for Galactic Dynamics

Fabien Toulgoat proposes a light scalar field Φ coupled to baryonic density:

$$\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{16\pi G}R - \frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\Phi\partial_\nu\Phi - \frac{1}{2}m^2\Phi^2 + \alpha\Phi\rho$$

The field modifies gravitational dynamics to explain galactic rotation curves without dark matter. Key parameters:
- Mass: m ~ 10⁻²⁷ eV/c²
- Compton wavelength: λ ~ 1 kpc
- Coupling: α ~ G

The scalar field guides matter at galactic scale — a pilot wave for galaxies.

### 5.3 Connection to Torsion

Both scalar field models connect to torsion:
- T_v can be seen as the scalar component of torsion (trace of torsion tensor)
- Φ responds to matter density, like torsion responds to spin density
- Both have finite range (Yukawa-type decay)

The scalar field is the spin-0 part of the pilot wave structure; torsion is the spin-1 and spin-2 part.

## 6. Consciousness as Field: Manzalini's Model

### 6.1 Consciousness as Quantum Field

Antonio Manzalini proposes consciousness is a quantum field C, analogous to the de Broglie-Bohm pilot wave:

$$C = \hbar \frac{\nabla^2 R}{R}$$

The consciousness potential guides living matter just as the quantum potential guides inanimate matter.

Key properties:
- Consciousness field has no sources (like the quantum field)
- Shape/curvature matters more than amplitude
- The field encodes active information
- Living systems operate far from quantum equilibrium

### 6.2 Nambu-Goldstone Bosons and Symmetry Breaking

Manzalini proposes the consciousness field's curvature is associated with Nambu-Goldstone (NG) boson condensates. NG bosons are massless excitations arising from symmetry breaking:
- They carry ordering information
- They require negligible energy
- They encode form/structure

This matches the form wave concept: NG bosons are the quantum mechanical basis of form waves.

### 6.3 Beyond Quantum Equilibrium

Manzalini follows Valentini: living systems operate far from quantum equilibrium, allowing:
- Access to non-quantum information
- Superluminal correlations
- Non-local consciousness effects

This explains why consciousness can interact with form waves and torsion fields while inanimate matter cannot.

The geometric form encodes physical properties — exactly what form wave theory predicts.

This is pilot wave theory at the particle level: the form of the particle guides its interactions.
