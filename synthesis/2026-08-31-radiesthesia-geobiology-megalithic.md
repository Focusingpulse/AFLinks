# The Radiesthesia-Geobiology-Megalithic Continuum: From Form Waves to Site Harmonization

*Synthesis by Drunvalo — 2026-08-31*

---

## Abstract

At least six interconnected traditions — French radiesthesia (Chaumery/de Belizal, Enel), German geobiology (Hartmann, Curry), French cosmotellurism (Peyré), neo-druidic megalithic studies (Bouchet, Altenbach/Legrais), practical site harmonization (Brocéliande practitioners), and French theoretical physics (Besson's RGH) — converge on a striking claim: geometric form and earth energies interact in measurable ways that can be detected, mapped, and modified. This synthesis traces that thread through the AFLinks archive, identifies convergent claims across traditions that developed largely in isolation, notes the Western vs. French-German intersections, and proposes next questions.

Where previous syntheses traced geometry as informational encoding (2026-08-30) and the vacuum as organizational substrate (2026-08-31), this one traces the **practical methodology** — how researchers developed tools, protocols, and measurement systems to detect and work with subtle energies in specific locations.

---

## The Convergent Claim

All six traditions assert that:

1. **The Earth emits detectable radiations.** Underground water, geological faults, mineral deposits, and geometric structures emit subtle fields detectable by pendulum, rod, or antenna.
2. **These radiations form networks.** The Earth is crisscrossed by regular grids (Hartmann, Curry, Peyré) and irregular features (watercourses, faults, vortices) that can be mapped.
3. **Geometric form modifies the field.** Shapes — whether natural (megaliths, mountains) or artificial (pyramids, radiesthetic piles, buildings) — emit "form waves" that interact with earth energies.
4. **Living systems respond.** Plants, animals, and humans show measurable responses to these energies — beneficial, harmful, or neutral depending on the specific configuration.
5. **Harmonization is possible.** Through geometric placement, material selection, or energetic intervention, problematic sites can be corrected and beneficial sites enhanced.

This is not identical to mainstream geophysics, which studies measurable electromagnetic, gravitational, and seismic phenomena. These traditions study **subtle fields** — phenomena that are detectable by dowsing instruments but not by conventional sensors.

## The Traditions

### 1. French Radiesthesia: Chaumery and de Belizal's Form Waves

In the 1930s, Léon Chaumery and André de Belizal developed a systematic theory of "form waves" (ondes de forme). Key contributions:

- **Spectrum of forms.** Every geometric shape emits a specific "vibratory color" detectable by pendulum. This spectrum is analogous to the light spectrum — continuous and systematically organized.
- **Negative green (V-).** A desiccating wave discovered in the King's Chamber of the Great Pyramid. Concentrated at one-third the height of a pyramid oriented to cardinal points, it mummifies organic matter. Chaumery allegedly died from exposure to this wave.
- **Radiesthetic pile.** A stack of spheres or disks that generates polarized form waves (+ and -), analogous to an electric battery. This device can emit beneficial or harmful waves depending on configuration.
- **Carrier wave.** A principle whereby a geometric form can "transmit" energetic information at a distance — the basis of radionics.

**Megalithic connection:** Chaumery and de Belizal conducted extensive experiments at the Great Pyramid, concluding that its shape and orientation create specific form wave concentrations. They proposed that ancient builders understood and utilized these principles.

### 2. Russian-French Esoteric Science: Enel's EIFS

Prince Michel Vladimirovich Skariatine (1883–1963), known by his initiatic name Enel, represents a unique bridge between Russian esotericism, French radiesthesia, and Egyptian temple science.

Key contributions:

- **EIFS (Emergence Influence Forms).** Enel developed this concept to describe how form influences emergence — how geometric structure shapes the coming-into-being of phenomena. This bridges form wave theory with organizational field concepts.
- **Egyptian temple science.** Initiated in Egypt by Maspero (director of antiquities), Enel spent decades researching the sacred meaning of hieroglyphs. His works *La langue sacrée* (1934), *Les origines de la Genèse* (1935), and *Le Message du Sphinx* (1936) propose that Egyptian temples were designed as energetic devices.
- **Lakhovsky collaboration.** Enel experimented with Georges Lakhovsky's Multiple Wave Oscillator (OLOM), developing original protocols and obtaining therapeutic results.
- **Cross-traditional synthesis.** Enel met Master Philippe and Papus in Russia, collaborated with Colonel de Rochas and Count Piobb in France, and maintained connections across esoteric and scientific marginalia.

**Megalithic connection:** Enel's Egyptian research proposes that temples were built at specific locations and with specific geometries to interact with earth and cosmic energies — a direct parallel to megalithic site selection claims.

### 3. German Geobiology: Hartmann and Curry Networks

In the 1950s, German physician Ernst Hartmann (1915–1992) founded the "Geobiology Research Circle" and systematized the detection of earth energy grids.

Key contributions:

- **Hartmann network (H-network).** A global grid of telluric radiations oriented north-south and east-west. Lines are approximately 2.0–2.5m apart, with intersections forming "Hartmann knots" that can be harmful to living systems.
- **Curry network.** A diagonal grid discovered by Manfred Curry, oriented at 45° to the Hartmann network. Curry lines are approximately 3.0–3.5m apart.
- **Health effects.** Hartmann documented correlations between grid crossings and disease, particularly cancer. Sleeping or working at crossings for extended periods was claimed to be harmful.
- **Harmonization protocols.** Developed methods for detecting and neutralizing harmful zones, including copper rods, specific materials, and geometric placements.

**Megalithic connection:** Hartmann and Curry networks provide a systematic framework for analyzing megalithic site placement — the claim that ancient builders located structures at beneficial grid intersections.

### 4. French Cosmotellurism: Peyré Network

Doctor Peyré of Bagnoles-de-l'Orne, working in the 1940s, discovered an additional network with different properties.

Key contributions:

- **Peyré network.** An east-west oriented network distinct from Hartmann and Curry. Lines are approximately 4.0–4.5m apart.
- **Chakra correspondence.** The Peyré network is claimed to have specific effects on the human energy system, particularly the heart chakra (fourth chakra).
- **Therapeutic applications.** Peyré proposed that beneficial Peyré crossings could be used for healing, while harmful crossings should be avoided.

**Megalithic connection:** The Peyré network adds another layer to the energetic analysis of sacred sites — the claim that megalithic builders utilized multiple overlapping networks.

### 5. Neo-Druidic Megalithic Studies: Bouchet to Altenbach/Legrais

From the 1960s onward, French neo-druidic writers developed elaborate theories connecting megalithic sites to telluric energies.

Key figures:

- **Paul Bouchet (Grand Druid Bod Koad):** Created the "College of Druids, Eubages, Bards, and Ovates of the Gauls" in 1942. His 1968 work *Science et philosophie des druides* proposed that druids inherited Atlantean knowledge of telluric currents and erected megaliths on energetic high points.
- **Gilbert Altenbach and Boune Legrais:** Their 1987 work *Lieux magiques et sacrés de la France* proposed discovery circuits of cosmo-telluric high places, including Brocéliande. They claimed religious buildings were erected on ancient megalithic and druidic sites initially raised on telluric high points.
- **Adolphe Landspurg and Norbert L'Hostis:** Their 1999 work *Bretagne magique et vibratoire* proposed detailed energetic circuits with vibratory measurements using the Bovis scale and geodynamiter.

**Megalithic connection:** This tradition explicitly claims that megalithic sites were selected and designed to utilize telluric energies — that ancient builders possessed systematic knowledge now being rediscovered.

### 6. Practical Site Harmonization: Brocéliande Practitioners

Contemporary practitioners in the Brocéliande forest demonstrate the practical application of these traditions.

Key practices:

- **Site analysis.** Systematic detection of Hartmann, Curry, and Peyré networks; watercourses; CT chimneys; vortices; etheric objects; and vibratory rates.
- **Guardian contact.** Protocols for communicating with site guardians (spirit entities associated with specific locations) to receive guidance on harmonization.
- **Energetic circuits.** Design of walking circuits that alternately expose visitors to positive and negative polarity points, cleansing and recharging energy bodies.
- **Vibratory measurement.** Use of Bovis scale (units measuring "vibratory rate") to quantify site energy and track changes from before/after harmonization.

**Megalithic connection:** The Hotié de Viviane study demonstrates how these methods are applied to a specific megalithic site — mapping networks, identifying vortices, designing circuits, and measuring effects.

### 7. French Theoretical Physics: Besson's RGH

Laurent Besson's Hypercomplex General Relativity (RGH) represents an attempt to formalize vacuum structure using quaternionic fields and Weyl gauge potentials.

Key contributions:

- **Quaternionic field H_ij(x).** A matrix-represented field that couples to geometry.
- **Weyl gauge potential Φ_μ.** An emergent gauge field with non-trivial commutators.
- **Ghost-free conditions.** Explicit Sylvester criterion for stability.
- **Big bounce.** Geometric quantum contributions generating ρ_Θ(a) ~ a⁻⁴, preventing singularities.

**Connection to radiesthesia:** While Besson does not reference radiesthesia directly, the mathematical structure — a field on a substrate that couples to geometry and generates organizational effects — parallels the claims of form wave theory. The quaternionic field H_ij is a formalized "form field."

## Western vs. French-German Intersections

### Western Mainstream
- Radiesthesia and geobiology are classified as pseudoscience.
- No Western university program teaches these methods.
- Hartmann's medical degree did not prevent marginalization.
- Besson publishes on Zenodo, outside mainstream journals.

### French-German Program
- Abbé Bouly coined "radiesthesia" in 1926 and was initially taken seriously by some scientists.
- Hartmann founded a research circle that persisted until his death in 1992.
- The Brocéliande tradition shows continuous practice from the 1980s to present.
- Besson's RGH represents an attempt to formalize these concepts mathematically.

### Key Intersection
The most striking convergence is between Enel's EIFS and Besson's RGH:

- **Enel:** EIFS (Emergence Influence Forms) describes how geometric form shapes the coming-into-being of phenomena. Form influences emergence.
- **Besson:** The quaternionic field H_ij couples to geometry and generates organizational effects (bounce, modified Friedmann equations).

Both describe a field that is shaped by geometry and in turn shapes physical reality. Enel approaches this from esoteric empirical practice; Besson from mathematical physics. The structural similarity is remarkable.

## What's Missing

1. **No controlled experiments comparing detection methods.** Different practitioners use different tools (pendulum, Lecher antenna, Bovis scale, geodynamiter). No published study compares whether these methods detect the same phenomena or produce consistent results.

2. **No mainstream verification of network claims.** Hartmann, Curry, and Peyré networks have not been detected by conventional geophysical instruments. The claim that they exist but are only detectable by dowsing remains unverified by mainstream standards.

3. **No theoretical unification of form waves and networks.** Chaumery/de Belizal's form waves and Hartmann's networks are studied by different communities using different frameworks. No attempt has been made to formally relate them.

4. **No systematic study of megalithic site selection.** The claim that megaliths were placed at beneficial grid crossings is widespread but not systematically tested. No study has compared megalithic site locations to network maps across a large sample.

5. **The guardian contact phenomenon is unstudied.** Practitioners claim to communicate with site guardians and receive guidance. This phenomenon — whether interpreted psychologically or ontologically — has not been studied by mainstream researchers.

## Proposed Next Questions

1. **Can form wave effects be detected by instruments other than the pendulum?** If Chaumery/de Belizal's claims are correct, specific geometric shapes should emit detectable fields. Can these be measured by electromagnetic sensors, thermal cameras, or other conventional instruments?

2. **Do Hartmann/Curry/Peyré network maps correlate with independent geological data?** If the networks are real, they should correlate with measurable geological features — fault lines, water tables, mineral deposits. A systematic comparison would test this.

3. **Can Besson's RGH field H_ij be related to form wave theory?** If both describe geometry-coupled organizational fields, there may be a formal relationship. Exploring this could bridge esoteric practice and mathematical physics.

4. **Do megalithic site locations correlate with network beneficial zones?** A systematic study of megalithic sites across Brittany (or elsewhere) could test whether they are preferentially located at grid intersections or other network features.

5. **What is the phenomenology of guardian contact?** The Brocéliande practitioners' claims of communicating with site guardians deserve serious study — whether as psychological phenomena, cultural practices, or something else.
