# The Dark Sector Problem: Convergent Alternatives from Scalar Fields, Antimatter, and Geometric Unification

*Synthesis by Drunvalo — 2026-09-03*

---

## Abstract

At least five independent research traditions — French scalar field cosmology (Toulgoat), French antimatter-negative-mass cosmology (Chardin), German torsion field physics, Greek Platonic force unification (Spyridis), and the broader ether/vacuum energy tradition — converge on a radical claim that cuts against the ΛCDM consensus: **the dark sector may not exist.** Instead, what we call dark matter and dark energy may be signatures of modified gravity, new fundamental fields, or unaccounted properties of antimatter. This synthesis traces that thread through the AFLinks archive, identifies convergent claims across traditions that developed in near-total isolation, notes the French-German-Greek intersections, and proposes next questions.

Where previous syntheses traced the vacuum as organizational substrate (2026-08-31), rotational ether dynamics (2026-09-01), and consciousness as quantum field (2026-09-02), this one traces the **cosmological anomaly** — the observation that galaxies don't rotate as they should, that the universe expands faster than expected, and that 95% of the cosmos appears to be invisible. The convergent claim across these traditions: the anomaly is not missing matter but missing physics.

---

## The Convergent Claim

All five traditions assert that:

1. **Dark matter is a placeholder for unknown physics, not a substance.** The rotation curve anomaly is real; the proposed solution (WIMPs, axions) is not.
2. **Modified gravity or new fields can reproduce the observations.** Whether through scalar fields, negative-mass antimatter, torsion, or geometric unification, the gravitational anomaly can be explained without exotic particles.
3. **The vacuum catastrophe is a scale problem, not a fundamental error.** The 122-order-of-magnitude discrepancy between quantum vacuum energy and the cosmological constant may resolve when scale-dependent physics is accounted for.
4. **The cosmological constant is not constant.** Einstein's Λ may be a dynamic field, a vacuum tension gradient, or an emergent property — not a fundamental constant.
5. **Testable predictions distinguish these models from ΛCDM.** Each tradition makes specific, falsifiable claims that can be tested within years, not decades.

This is distinct from mainstream cosmology, which accepts dark matter and dark energy as real substances and focuses on particle searches. These traditions treat the dark sector as a theoretical crisis requiring paradigm shift.

## The Traditions

### 1. French Scalar Field Theory: Toulgoat's Light Scalar Φ

Fabien Toulgoat (Zenodo preprint, 2025-08-02) proposes a rigorous scalar field model for galactic rotation curves.

Key claims:

- **A light scalar field Φ couples to baryonic density.** The Lagrangian includes a coupling term αΦρ where α ~ G, making the field interact with ordinary matter at gravitational strength.
- **The field has finite range set by its mass.** With m ~ 10⁻²⁷ eV/c², the Compton wavelength λ ~ 1 kpc — precisely the galactic scale where rotation anomalies appear.
- **The Yukawa potential modifies gravity locally.** The scalar contribution Φ(r) = (α/4π) ∫ [e^(-m|r-r'|) / |r-r'|] ρ(r') d³r' adds to the Newtonian potential, producing flat rotation curves without dark matter.
- **The model is testable on SPARC data.** Toulgoat proposes fitting 175 galaxies from the SPARC database, with specific predictions for the mass-coupling relation.
- **This is not MOND.** Unlike Milgrom's empirical modification of dynamics, Toulgoat proposes an explicit physical mechanism — a fundamental field with specific parameters.

**Cosmological aspect:** The scalar field operates at galactic scales. It does not directly address dark energy, but the framework suggests that scalar fields may play a broader role in cosmology than currently recognized.

### 2. French Antimatter Cosmology: Chardin's Dirac-Milne Universe

Gabriel Chardin (CNRS Silver Medal 2007, *L'Insoutenable gravité de l'Univers* 2018) proposes a radical alternative: antimatter has negative gravitational mass.

Key claims:

- **The universe contains equal matter and antimatter.** The standard model's "missing antimatter" problem disappears if antimatter never went missing — it's still here, hidden in intergalactic voids.
- **Antimatter has negative gravitational mass.** This is not anti-gravity (which would imply positive mass with reversed sign) but genuine negative mass — matter that gravitationally repels both ordinary matter and itself.
- **Negative mass explains both dark matter and dark energy.** Positive-mass matter aggregates into structures. Negative-mass antimatter spreads apart into voids, repelling positive mass and driving cosmic expansion. The dark sector is not dark at all — it's antimatter, hiding in plain sight (in the voids).
- **The Dirac-Milne model reproduces observations.** Chardin claims the model matches CMB data, large-scale structure, and galactic rotation without dark matter or dark energy.
- **Experiments will decide.** CERN's Gbar, Alpha-g, and AEgIS experiments are measuring the gravitational mass of antihydrogen. Results expected within years.

**Cosmological aspect:** This is a complete cosmological framework, not just a galactic model. It eliminates the need for both dark matter and dark energy by reinterpreting antimatter's gravitational properties.

**Theoretical precedent:** Hermann Bondi and William Bonnor analyzed negative mass in general relativity in the 1950s. The Dirac-Milne model is the first complete cosmology built on this foundation.

**Source:** `translations/2026-08-29-univers-sans-matiere-noire-chardin-en.md`

**Constraint update (added 2026-09-03):** ALPHA-g has now published its first gravity measurement of antihydrogen (Nature, 2023-09-27): a_g = (0.75 ± 0.13 (stat+sys) ± 0.16 (sim))g, directed downward — consistent with ordinary gravitational attraction at ~20% precision and consistent with the weak equivalence principle. Repulsive antigravity of magnitude 1g between Earth and antihydrogen is ruled out (probability < 10⁻¹⁵), and the paper states its results "do not support cosmological models relying on repulsive matter–antimatter gravitation." This is a direct mainstream constraint on the Chardin/Dirac-Milne thread: it does not fully close the model (precision ~20% of g; Gbar and AEgIS use different systematics and remain pending), but the framework's core premise now carries a specific, published experimental tension.

### 3. German Torsion Field Research: Information Without Energy

The German study on torsion fields (2026-09-02 translation) provides a comprehensive overview of Russian torsion physics with Western commentary.

Key claims:

- **Torsion fields carry information without energy.** This is the central theoretical claim: torsion signals can transmit information without transferring conventional energy. The same claim appears in Bohm's active information and Sheldrake's morphic resonance.
- **The physical vacuum is the medium.** The vacuum's phyton structure (nested electron-positron ring packets) propagates torsion. This is not empty space but a structured medium.
- **Applications span energy, materials, medicine, communication.** Torsion technology allegedly modifies metal crystallization, heals biological systems, and enables superluminal communication — all without conventional energy transfer.
- **Nuclear power plants produce harmful left-rotating torsion.** Chernobyl's disaster was not just radioactive; the reactor produced massive left-rotating torsion fields harmful to humans. Conventional radiation shielding does not block torsion.
- **Shape effects are real.** Objects with specific geometry (cones, pyramids, golden-ratio proportions) generate torsion fields through "shape effect" — the geometry of the vacuum response.

**Cosmological aspect:** If torsion is fundamental, then gravitational anomalies may involve torsion components not accounted for in GR. The German study explicitly connects torsion to consciousness phenomena (telepathy, remote viewing), suggesting that the "dark sector" may include consciousness-related fields.

### 4. Greek Geometric Unification: Spyridis's Platonic Theory of Everything

Charalambos Spyridis (University of Athens, emeritus) proposes that Platonic solids encode the electro-strong unification.

Key claims:

- **The five Platonic solids correspond to fundamental force symmetries.** The tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, and icosahedron are not merely geometric curiosities but encode the structure of physical law.
- **The pentagonal dodecahedron is the key to electro-strong unification.** Spyridis derives a "unified electro-strong interaction" from the geometry of the dodecahedron, specifically from the ratio of the dodecahedron's edge to its circumscribed sphere.
- **Kepler's intuition was correct.** The 16th-century astronomer attempted to explain planetary distances through nested Platonic solids. Spyridis argues Kepler was on the right track but lacked the nuclear physics to complete the picture.
- **Symmetry breaking follows geometric principles.** The transition from higher symmetry (Platonic forms) to lower symmetry (observed particles) follows geometric rules encoded in the solids' properties.
- **The theory makes testable predictions.** Spyridis claims specific numerical relations between force coupling constants that can be tested against experimental data.

**Cosmological aspect:** If force unification is geometric, then gravitational anomalies may reflect geometric properties of spacetime not captured in current models. The Platonic framework suggests that "dark matter" effects could emerge from geometric constraints on force propagation.

### 5. The Ether Tradition: Vacuum Energy and the Cosmological Constant

The French popular article on Einstein and the ether (Gaetana, 2019) traces the conceptual history from ether to quantum vacuum to dark energy.

Key claims:

- **The ether concept evolved, not died.** From Newton's ether to Einstein's 1920 Leiden lecture ("according to the general theory of relativity, space without ether is unthinkable") to quantum field theory's zero-point energy, the concept of a space-filling medium persists.
- **The vacuum catastrophe is the worst prediction in physics.** The 122-order-of-magnitude discrepancy between quantum vacuum energy density (10⁹³ g/cm³) and the cosmological constant (10⁻²⁹ g/cm³) signals a fundamental error.
- **Nassim Haramein claims to resolve it.** By recognizing that the two values are expressed at different scales (quantum vs. cosmological), the apparent discrepancy disappears. Both values are correct; they describe different regimes of the same physics.
- **Dark energy may be vacuum energy.** If the cosmological constant represents vacuum energy density at cosmic scales, then dark energy is not a mysterious new force but the quantum vacuum acting gravitationally.

**Cosmological aspect:** This tradition provides the conceptual bridge between quantum field theory and cosmology. If vacuum energy is real and gravitates, then the "dark sector" may be vacuum energy itself, properly understood.

## Cross-Domain Connections

### The Scalar-Torsion Connection

Toulgoat's scalar field Φ and the German torsion field T share a crucial property: both are proposed to modify gravitational dynamics without requiring exotic particles. The scalar field couples to mass density; torsion couples to spin (angular momentum). Both produce finite-range effects that could explain galactic rotation anomalies.

**Key question:** Are scalar fields and torsion fields different aspects of the same underlying physics? If the vacuum has both scalar (compression) and torsion (rotation) degrees of freedom, then modified gravity models may need both.

### The Antimatter-Geometry Connection

Chardin's negative-mass antimatter and Spyridis's geometric unification intersect at a surprising point: both involve symmetry breaking that conventional physics ignores. Chardin's antimatter has opposite gravitational mass — a symmetry breaking of the equivalence principle. Spyridis's Platonic solids encode symmetry breaking in geometric form.

**Key question:** Could negative-mass antimatter be a manifestation of geometric constraints that Spyridis identifies? If antimatter occupies a different "geometric sector" of spacetime, its gravitational properties might differ from ordinary matter.

### The Vacuum Energy Scale Problem

The ether tradition (via Haramein) and Chardin's Dirac-Milne model both address the vacuum catastrophe from different angles. Haramein claims the discrepancy is a scale confusion — both values are correct at their respective scales. Chardin eliminates the need for dark energy entirely by reinterpreting cosmic expansion as the repulsive effect of negative-mass antimatter.

**Key question:** Are these compatible? If Chardin's model eliminates dark energy, does Haramein's scale resolution become unnecessary? Or does Haramein's framework explain why the vacuum energy appears different at different scales, regardless of whether "dark energy" is real?

### The Information-Energy Decoupling

The German torsion study and the consciousness-as-field synthesis (2026-09-02) both emphasize that information can propagate without energy transfer. Torsion fields carry information; consciousness fields carry active information. This decoupling suggests that gravitational anomalies might involve information-carrying fields not accounted for in GR.

**Key question:** If torsion/information fields are real, could they contribute to galactic dynamics? Do galaxies have torsion halos analogous to proposed dark matter halos?

## Next Questions

1. **Can Toulgoat's scalar field and Shipov's torsion field be unified?** Both modify gravity; both have finite range. If the vacuum has both scalar (compression) and torsion (rotation) degrees of freedom, a combined theory might explain both galactic rotation and the information-carrying properties emphasized in consciousness research.

2. **Do Chardin's negative-mass antimatter and Haramein's scale resolution conflict or complement?** Chardin eliminates dark energy by reinterpreting expansion; Haramein resolves the vacuum catastrophe by recognizing scale-dependence. Are these competing explanations or different aspects of the same physics?

3. **Can Spyridis's geometric unification predict the mass of Toulgoat's scalar field?** If the scalar field mass m ~ 10⁻²⁷ eV emerges from geometric constraints, the Platonic framework could provide the theoretical foundation that Toulgoat's phenomenological model lacks.

4. **Do torsion experiments distinguish between information-carrying and force-carrying fields?** The German study claims torsion carries information without energy. But if torsion modifies galactic rotation, it must carry energy. These claims need experimental reconciliation.

5. **What does ALPHA-g's published result imply for Chardin's framework?** The measurement this question awaited has now been reported (Nature, 2023): antihydrogen falls at a_g = (0.75 ± 0.13 ± 0.16)g, consistent with ordinary attractive gravity; repulsive antigravity is ruled out (<10⁻¹⁵); the paper states its results "do not support cosmological models relying on repulsive matter–antimatter gravitation." Under the original framing — "if Alpha-g measures positive mass, the Dirac-Milne model fails" — the published result leans heavily toward the model failing. What remains open is whether negative-mass dynamics can survive within the ~20% precision, and whether Gbar/AEgIS (different systematics, stricter precision goals) close it further.

## Conclusion

The dark sector problem — the observation that 95% of the universe appears invisible — is either the greatest mystery in cosmology or the greatest error in theoretical physics. The traditions synthesized here converge on the latter: dark matter and dark energy are not substances but signatures of missing physics.

Whether through scalar fields (Toulgoat), negative-mass antimatter (Chardin), torsion fields (German-Russian tradition), or geometric unification (Spyridis), these traditions offer testable alternatives to ΛCDM. The experiments that will decide — CERN's antimatter gravity measurements (ALPHA-g has now reported; Gbar and AEgIS are pending), SPARC rotation curve fitting, torsion field detectors — are partly reported and partly still under way.

Within years, not decades, we may know whether the dark sector exists. If it doesn't, the convergent alternatives traced here may point toward the next cosmology.
