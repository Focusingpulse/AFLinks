# Crop Formations

**One line:** the geometry is real and measured, almost every formation is made by people, the hidden construction lines are the signature of the *method* rather than of a *mind*, and the one anomaly in the plants that has never been explained was tested against a man-made control in 2014 and did not reproduce. What is left is a small, genuine, unresolved question, and one experiment that would close it.

---

## 1. The making — settled, with a scope

In September 1991 two English landscape painters, **Doug Bower and Dave Chorley**, told the London tabloid *Today* that they had been making crop circles since 1978, and then made one in front of a researcher who declared it genuine before they revealed themselves. They used planks and a rope.

A second, documented generation followed and made the largest and most intricate designs. In 1996 **Rod Dickinson** gave an account of the **Stonehenge Julia Set** (149 circles, roughly 500 feet across) to researcher Michael Lindemann: it was made the previous night by three people in about two and three-quarter hours. Asked about the famous claim that it had appeared in broad daylight in 45 minutes, his answer was that the pilot who reported it "just didn't see it" on the first pass.

In November 2000 a Wiltshire man became the first person in Britain prosecuted for making one. He had made a **seven-pointed star** over three nights with planks, bamboo poles and rope, specifically to disprove a researcher's published claim that a seven-pointed pattern of that type could not be made by humans. He was fined £100 plus £40 costs, with around £200 of crop damage estimated.

**Three things worth holding about that record:**

- **The count does not agree with itself.** The same week, the same story: one paper reported "about 2,000" circles, another "as many as 25 to 30 new circles each growing season," and the testimony transcript says about 200 by the early 1980s. Three sources, three figures. A number that cannot agree with itself across one news cycle is a claim about a memory.
- **The scope is narrower than it is usually used for.** The confession covers circles in the UK from 1978 to 1991. It does not cover every formation worldwide, and it does not cover what came after.
- **A number that never moves is a prior, not a finding.** One well-known researcher was quoted around 2000 saying that 20% of circles were not man-made, based on a magnetometer survey. The makers' own site points out that this was the same figure he had been quoting *before* he did the research. That is the shape to watch for on both sides of this subject.

## 2. The method — how they are actually built

The documented technique is not exotic. A wooden board roughly a metre long with a rope attached at both ends; you stand on the board and trod the crop down while walking the arc. That is the method described in the peer-reviewed control experiment below, and it is the method the original two men used.

What makers add is planning and measurement:

- **A central circle is laid down first.** Not because the design needs it, but to create a working area: you damage less crop if you have a place to stand and measure from.
- **A "work line" is stepped into the grain** along the spine of the formation, and the centres of all the main circles are placed along it.
- **Arcs are drawn from multiple centre points with a tape measure.** Dickinson's own account of the Julia Set is that this is exactly how the spiral was made: move the centre point around the first circle, lengthen the tape for each new part of the arc.

Modern makers are reported to use design software, GPS to stake the pattern and lasers for straight runs. One physicist who has studied the structures at length argues further that the most defensible physical explanation for the plant changes is that makers **superheat the stalk joints** (which softens them so they bend without breaking) and then sculpt while the plants are still flexible. Whether or not that is what is happening, note where it points: at tooling, not at visitors.

⚠ **A legal note, not a how-to.** Making a formation in someone's field without their permission damages a growing crop. It is criminal damage, and it was prosecuted as criminal damage in Britain in 2000. The documented makers of the large formations now work on commission, with the landowner's permission, for advertisers and television. If you want to make one, the same rule that applies to anything else on someone else's land applies here.

## 3. The geometry — real, measured, and the tell is in the scaffolding

**Gerald Hawkins**, a retired Boston University astronomer, worked from published ground surveys and photographs of **18 formations (1981–1988)**. In **11 of the 18** he found ratios of small whole numbers matching the **diatonic scale**, the ratios that define the intervals between the white keys of a piano. He went on to derive five Euclidean theorems from the patterns, the fifth general enough to generate the other four, and challenged mathematicians to find it.

Two things are true at once here, and both matter:

**The measurement is real.** Hawkins was working from survey data collected by other people, and the ratios are arithmetic on measured dimensions, not impressions.

**But the inference does not follow.** The four specific theorems turn out to be well-known results about the inscribed and circumscribed circles of a triangle, a square and a hexagon, with area ratios of 4:1, 2:1 and 4:3, known since the Pythagoreans. And small-integer ratios are exactly what you would expect to find somewhere in any collection of concentric and satellite circles, especially a sample chosen after the fact from formations that had already been published.

**And here is the part that answers the question properly.** Hawkins also found that the formations were built using **hidden "construction lines"**, lines used at the design stage that do not appear in the finished pattern, and read them as evidence of a designer's method.

Then Dickinson described building the same Julia Set, and explained what the construction line is: you need a large central area laid down first, *"from which you can measure out diameters to other parts of the formation,"* and every main circle's centre sits along a single work line stepping down the grain.

**The hidden construction line is the tram line.** It is there because you cannot measure and build in the dark without an anchor. Hawkins found the mark of the method and read it as the mark of a mind. That single inversion is the whole subject in miniature.

One more detail from the same account, and it is the most telling of all. Dickinson noted of the Julia Set's spiral that *"for the first three-quarters of its arc, it does follow a Fibonacci series,"* and then *"the last quarter doesn't. It just becomes a circular arc."* The precision degrades exactly where the construction method runs out. A message does not stop being a message three-quarters of the way around.

## 4. The dimensional question — one, two, three, nested

What has actually been **measured** is two-dimensional: Euclidean constructions and fractals, both in the plane.

- **"Three-dimensional"** is a rendering effect. Some formations read as three-dimensional objects because of the shading produced by the direction the stalks are laid. That is a technique, and it is one the documented makers demonstrably use. It is not an extra dimension in the data.
- **"Nested" is a fractal, which is an iteration rule:** repeat a shape at changing scale. That requires a rule, not an intelligence. A design program plus a tape measure supplies it, and Dickinson's "move the centre point and lengthen the tape for each new arc" is the iteration, performed by hand.
- **"Fourth-dimensional" and hyperdimensional readings** have no proposed test attached, which leaves the argument from exclusion: *we cannot explain it, therefore something else did it.* Ruling out known explanations does not install an unknown one. "Unexplained" is a status, not a finding.

**The question to ask of any dimensional claim: which instrument measured the dimension?** Nothing observes the "dimension" of a flattened patch of field.

## 5. The plant anomaly — the one live question

This is the part of the subject where real science was done, and where a real argument is still open. It deserves to be stated carefully, because both the believers and the debunkers routinely overstate it.

**What was published.** Over roughly a decade, a biophysicist working with a field-sampling network produced three papers in a peer-reviewed plant-physiology journal reporting structural changes in plants inside formations: enlarged stem nodes (pulvini), altered cell-wall pit structures, malformed or absent embryos, and in one case charring of the outer epidermal layer. A companion paper reported a node-length gradient that fell off with distance from a formation's centre, and fitted it to the absorption of electromagnetic energy through the atmosphere.

**What the response was.** A skeptical team obtained the original raw data and re-analysed it, concluding that the electromagnetic claims were not supported: that the sample sizes were too small, the criteria for what counted as a control were never stated up front, and that the data showed nothing beyond a difference between flattened and upright plants, which any flattening mechanism would produce. They also reported that a man-made formation in the Netherlands showed node elongation of up to 30%, which undercuts the argument that the effect is too large to be produced by treading crop down. The rebuttal argued that the critique contained a statistical error, having read averages of many stems as single measurements, and had the control positions wrong.

**And then the important one.** In 2014 the same Dutch researchers ran the experiment the whole dispute needed: they made a **man-made control circle** with board and rope, in the same crop, at the same maturity, the same diameter, the same sampling scheme, the same six-day delay and the same measuring procedure as a famous 1999 formation. That formation had shown a peak node length of **214% of control at its centre**, an average across all flattened stems of **171%**, and a clean radial symmetry. The man-made control produced a peak of **144%** and an average of **111%**, with **no radial symmetry** at all (regional correlation between the two, R = 0.06).

**So where does that actually leave it?** Three things, honestly:

1. **A man-made circle did produce a substantial node increase** (144% peak). So large node elongation is not by itself evidence of anything.
2. **The 1999 formation's specific structure was not reproduced** by the control, and the authors concluded that its node lengthening "could not be explained and remains anomalous."
3. **That is one experiment, not a closed case.** The authors say so themselves: they could not reproduce six days of unknown weather, soil and humidity, and the experiment was **not blind**. The skeptical team, meanwhile, has raw data from a man-made formation that did fit the energy model well. The two results point in different directions and neither has been independently repeated.

## 6. The test nobody has run

The dispute is not stuck because it is unresolvable. It is stuck because nobody has run the obvious experiment.

**A blind replication.** Plant formations in matched crop at matched maturity; measure node lengths and cell-wall structures with the measurers not knowing which samples came from which formation; decide the inclusion criteria and the analysis **before** collecting; reproduce the weather and soil conditions from records; and pre-register what result would count as anomalous. Both sides of this argument have effectively asked for exactly this. It has not been done.

Two things follow. First, until it is done, "unexplained" is the correct word and both "proven" and "debunked" are overstatements. Second, this is a cheap experiment. It needs a field, a board, a rope, a micrometer and somebody who does not already know the answer.

## 7. Where to read it

The primary documents, in the order that gets you to the actual state of the argument fastest:

- **Grassi, Cocheo & Russo (2005)**, "Balls of Light: The Questionable Science of Crop Circles," *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 19(2):159–170. The critique, written from the original raw data. Free PDF at cicap.org.
- **Haselhoff, Boerman & Bobbink (2014)**, "An Experimental Study for Reproduction of Biological Anomalies Reported in the Hoeven 1999 Crop Circle," *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 28(1):17–33. The control experiment above. It contains the method, the numbers, and its own limitations.
- **Levengood (1994)**, "Anatomical anomalies in crop formation plants," *Physiologia Plantarum* 92:356–363. The original anomaly paper. Its own text contains the caveat that node size alone cannot verify a "genuine" formation.
- **Levengood & Talbott (1999)**, "Dispersion of energies in worldwide crop formations," *Physiologia Plantarum* 105:615–624. The energy-model paper.
- **Hawkins' theorems**, summarised at vigay.com and cropcirclesecrets.org, with the derivations. Read them alongside the mathematical critique.
- **Circlemakers.org** for the makers' own accounts, including Dickinson on the Julia Set construction.
- **Radford (2022)**, "Revisiting the 'Stonehenge Surprise'," *Skeptical Inquirer*, for the daylight-appearance claim examined closely.

**Held in this archive:** *Nexus* magazine Vol 25 No 6 (Oct–Nov 2018), "Crop Circles of 2018" by Andy Thomas; the Rex Research holdings on Levengood; and KeelyNet's 2002 piece "Crop Circles a Hoax?". Search the vault for `crop circle` for the full set.

## 8. The method behind this audit

Five claims, five grades, no borrowing of credibility between them:

| Claim | Grade |
|---|---|
| Most formations since the late 1970s are human-made | **Verified**, scope: UK 1978–1991 documented; later and elsewhere inferred |
| Plants inside formations show anatomical changes | **Contested.** The floor claim (flattened plants differ from upright ones) is solid; the mechanism is not |
| The geometry encodes mathematics | **Measured.** The inference that this implies a non-human source is **unsupported** |
| Formations encode a dimensional ladder | **Unfalsifiable as stated** |
| Formations are schematics or engines | **Unfalsifiable as stated** |

The pattern to carry into any other contested field: **the practice and the mechanism are different claims, and one can be true while the other is invented.** Here the practice is real (formations get made; stalks bend; plants differ). The mechanism story is the part that keeps failing its tests.
