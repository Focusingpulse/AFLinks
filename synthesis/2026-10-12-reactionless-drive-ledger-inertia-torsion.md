## Abstract

The October 11–12 Forge batches did for reactionless propulsion what the earlier October batches did for cold fusion: they turned a scattering into a claim-chain. The **Western electromagnetic line**: Shawyer's EmDrive, Fetta's Cannae Drive, the Chinese 720-millinewton replication claim, and NASA Eagleworks' 91.2-micronewton torsion-balance measurement, all carried into the archive through Etkin's trinitas.ru defense [5]. ## 1. The Russian Inertioid Line: From a Perm Book to Orbit

The inertioid is the oldest and most mechanically honest device in this ledger: a box containing masses on driven cyclic trajectories, arranged so that — the claim goes — the inertial forces do not cancel over a cycle. V.N. Tolchin of Perm built and measured such machines for decades; his 1977 book *Инерциоид. Силы инерции как источник поступательного движения* ("The Inertioid: Inertial Forces as a Source of Translational Motion") is the foundational text, cited by every later Russian work in the chain [1]. It is not yet in the archive — it is now proposed in the family ledger as an acquisition target, because the entire line stands on it.

What the October batches add is the line's full modern development. Shipov, from the Institute of Vacuum Physics in Moscow, derives the Tolchin device's motion explicitly within nonholonomic mechanics — mechanics with non-integrable constraints, the branch in which cyclic internal motion *can* displace a system's center of mass without violating anything, because the constraint space itself is curved [2]. This is the respectable-mathematics face of the claim: nobody disputes that nonholonomic systems (a snakeboard, a falling cat) translate and rotate by internal cycling; the dispute is whether the effect survives contact with momentum conservation when the medium is only the device itself.

The engineering endpoint is the Chicherin–Menshikov paper, and it is remarkable because it is about a **flown spacecraft**:

> «На основе механики реальных тел с конечной скоростью передачи взаимодействия между различными частями их рассчитаны тяги инерциоида и движителя без выброса массы космического аппарата "Юбилейный".»
> *"On the basis of the mechanics of real bodies with a finite speed of interaction transfer between their parts, the thrusts of the inertioid and of the mass-rejection-free propulsor of the 'Yubileiny' spacecraft are calculated."* [3]

The *Yubileiny* satellite (50 kg, launched May 2008) carried — officially — an experimental "propulsor without mass ejection" oriented along the orbital radius. Chicherin and Menshikov's method is the interesting part: they model the device not as a point-mass system (where thrust is provably impossible) but as a real elastic body in which longitudinal and transverse acoustic waves propagate at finite speed, reflect differently off bearing and housing inhomogeneities, and so fail to synchronize — leaving a non-zero residual reaction on the rotor axis. To amplify the micro-thrust above telemetry noise, the operators repeatedly switched the engine on and off in resonance with the spacecraft's gravitational libration, rocking the orbit measurably:

> «Оценки величины тяги одного и того же движителя при различных испытаниях близки и равны ~20мг, что подтверждает достоверность результатов.»
> *"Estimates of the thrust of one and the same propulsor across different tests are close to one another and equal ~20 mgf, which confirms the reliability of the results."* [3]

Twenty milligrams-force, from an on-orbit platform, is either the most public reactionless-thrust data point in existence or a masterclass in how many places an acoustic artifact can hide inside a 50-kilogram structure. The archive holds the claim; section 6 holds the counterweights. Alongside the paper, the archive's simulator line continues: Vlasov's ongoing "Living Physics" software reconstructions of inertioid dynamics [4], which serve the Russian scene the way visualization serves any contested mechanics — as persuasion infrastructure between the mathematics and the bench.

## 2. The Western Microwave Line: Shawyer, Yang Juan, Eagleworks

The West's reactionless device is not mechanical but electromagnetic: a magnetron feeding a closed, tapered copper cavity, claimed to produce thrust toward the cavity's large end with nothing expelled. Roger Shawyer proposed the EmDrive in 1998 and was ridiculed for a decade on a single argument — a closed cavity is a sealed system, so thrust violates momentum conservation. Guido Fetta's Cannae Drive followed in the US; in 2009–2010 the first national-laboratory-scale replication claim came from China, carried here in Etkin's Russian account:

> «В 2009-2010 годах китайская исследовательская группа из North Western Polytechnical University, Xi'an, China под руководством проф. Yang Juan построила аналог «EmDrive» и подтвердила, что тяга двигателя достигала 720 милиньютон (~ 0,073 кгс.)»
> *"In 2009–2010 a Chinese research group at Northwestern Polytechnical University, Xi'an, led by Prof. Yang Juan built an EmDrive analogue and confirmed that the engine's thrust reached 720 millinewtons (~0.073 kgf)."* [5]

Then the event that made the field un-ignorable: in August 2014, at the AIAA Joint Propulsion Conference, NASA's Eagleworks group at Johnson Space Center (Harold White's team) reported testing the Cannae device on a torsion balance inside a stainless vacuum chamber, sensitivity 1 µN, at power levels far below the predecessors:

> «чистая тяга при пяти запусках составила 91,2 мкН при 17 Вт входной мощности»
> *"the net thrust over five runs came to 91.2 µN at 17 W input power"* [5]

Ninety-one micronewtons is photon-pressure territory — and the Eagleworks team knew it, running resistor-dummy controls and finding, in the process, that magnetic fields from the power cables acted on the balance. Etkin's paper exists to argue that the momentum-conservation verdict against these devices is a misapplication — that the radiation interacts with the vacuum rather than merely sloshing inside the can [5]. Whatever one makes of that argument, the archive's positioning of it is exact: a Russian theoretical physicist, on the Academy of Trinitarianism's server, defending a British microwave engineer against the Western consensus — the cross-tradition traffic this synthesis series exists to document.

## 3. The Theory Stack: Inertia as a Real Field

Why would a Moscow vacuum-physics institute spend two dozen papers on drives? Because for the Shipov school the device question is downstream of a foundational one: **what is inertia?** The October batches landed this program nearly complete, and its own self-diagnosis opens from a textbook count:

> «авторы, примерно, 60% существующих учебников по теоретической механике называют силы инерции "фиктивными"»
> *"the authors of roughly 60% of existing textbooks on theoretical mechanics call the forces of inertia 'fictitious'"* [7]

Shipov's counter-program, as the archive now holds it, runs: Newton's bucket never got a satisfactory answer within mechanics [13]; Euler's rigid-body formalism encoded a 267-year-old error in the treatment of rotation that textbooks prefer not to notice [14][15]; the consequence is a century of stagnation in theoretical physics [11], including the community's difficulty accepting what Einstein's gravitation actually says about inertia [12]. The constructive half: inertial forces are real fields — observable inside inertial frames, generated by the rotation of matter [9] — the vacuum equations geometrize them [16], quantum mechanics itself is re-read as the dynamics of field gyroscopes [18], and the theory of the physical vacuum stands as a unified field theory with the field and forces of inertia at the center [10]. The nonlocality connection is made explicit in the Podorovskaya–Shipov paper, which opens from the 1982 Aspect experiment and argues that matter born from one source remains connected by a *non-force, exclusively informational* link — the same informational-carrier claim the torsion-telecommunication synthesis traced through Tomsk and Beijing [17]. Above the whole stack sits the stated motivation, and it is propulsion:

> «Уже много лет обсуждается малая эффективность, ненадежность и даже опасность аппаратов, использующих реактивные двигатели для передвижения в космическом пространстве.»
> *"For many years now, the low efficiency, unreliability and even danger of vehicles using jet engines to move through cosmic space has been under discussion."* [7]

The engineering derivative is Shipov's **4D gyroscope**: a rotor spun not only in the three spatial planes but — in the theory's terms — in the space-time planes, generating space-time nutation [8] and, allegedly, a controllable inertial interaction with the vacuum suitable as a cosmic drive [7]. The earlier gyroscopic-anomaly synthesis in this archive [23] traced the experimental wing of this intuition (weight anomalies of precessing gyroscopes across four independent traditions); what the October batches add is its theoretical headquarters. The predecessor the Moscow school itself acknowledges is Roberto Oros di Bartini — the Tupolev-bureau aircraft designer whose two-radical (length–time) dimensional system computed every world physical constant and predicted unbuilt aircraft characteristics within 5% engineering accuracy [19]; the archive now holds both the Erokhov exposition of Bartini's system and Chuev's application of it to electromagnetic-wave quantities [20], wired together through the latter's own literature list.

## 4. The Garage Variant: Torkado and the Asymmetric-Oscillation Intuition

One shelf below the institutes sits the German free-energy scene's qualitative version of the same idea, and the archive's October correction work (the retitled torkado dossier) puts it in its proper place in this chain. Gabi Müller's *Qualitatives Torkado-Modell* [21] analyzes the Würth machine — an "inertia-active swing system" oscillating about at least two separate rotation axes — and traces its motion as an asymmetric spiral winding on a torus: the downward path longer, flatter, accelerated along the torus's outer half; the upward path steep and short along the inner half. No conservation-law treatment of these path relations exists, Müller argues, because thermodynamically closed systems "de facto do not exist." Set the vocabulary aside and the structural claim is identical to Chicherin–Menshikov's: **an asymmetric cyclic trajectory whose halves do not cancel**. The Russian says it with acoustic impedance mismatches and wave reflection coefficients in a duralumin housing; the German hobbyist says it with spiral path lengths on a torus. The torkado paper also claims cascade-path analogies in liquid and gaseous flow — the Schauberger vortex lineage, one floor down in the same building. The archive's value here is comparative anatomy: the intuition recurs at every level of rigor, and only the metrology distinguishes them.

Ivchenkov's trinitas paper closes the device census by generalizing the claim class itself — "unsupported" (bezopornoe) motion from any uncompensated force, electrodynamic and photonic variants included [6] — a reminder that inside the Russian heterodox academy, the inertioid and the EmDrive are one problem, not two.

## 5. The Skeptical Ledger: Three Kinds of No

This archive's rule is that the counter-evidence lives inside the corpus, and for this topic it comes in three distinct registers:

**Theoretical no.** Momentum conservation: a closed system cannot accelerate its center of mass. Every device line here answers it differently — nonholonomic constraints (Shipov), finite-speed elastic wave asymmetry (Chicherin–Menshikov), vacuum momentum exchange (Etkin, Shipov's field-inertia stack). These answers are mutually incompatible; at most one theory of the residual force can be right, which is itself data about the field's maturity.

**Experimental no.** The Dresden SpaceDrive program (Tajmar, Neunzig, Weikert, TU Dresden) spent years iterating thrust balances specifically to kill the artifact classes — thermal drift, cable magnetics, mechanical stress — that mimic thrust at micronewton scale. Their consolidated result, published in the *CEAS Space Journal* (14:31–44, 2022), on a battery-powered EmDrive mounted below the suspension point of an inverted counterbalanced double pendulum: no thrust across a wide band including four resonances, with any anomalous component bounded below the classical photon-pressure equivalent — **ruling out the previous positive claims by at least two orders of magnitude** [22]. The Eagleworks 91.2 µN does not survive contact with a balance designed around its error budget.

**Internal no — the rarest kind.** Chicherin and Menshikov, the same authors who report the *Yubileiny* thrust, include in the same paper a review of earlier inertioid experiments and theory, and their verdict on their own predecessors is unsparing:

> «Приведенные в этой работе теоретические обоснования получения тяги инерциоида некорректны … равенство нулю интеграла по времени, то есть за один цикл не происходит изменения количества движения центра масс инерциоида, что свидетельствует об отсутствии тяги.»
> *"The theoretical justifications for inertioid thrust given in that work are incorrect … the time integral equals zero — over one cycle there is no change in the momentum of the inertioid's center of mass, which demonstrates the absence of thrust."* [3]

A field that publishes its own null integrations is healthier than one that does not; the same paper then argues the rescue requires leaving point-mass mechanics for finite-speed continuum mechanics. Skeptics should note that this is exactly the move — change the theory until the anomaly is derivable — that the Dresden group would call motivated reasoning, and that a historian would call normal science under anomaly pressure.

**The metrology wall.** Note where every positive number in this ledger lives: 91.2 µN on a 1-µN balance [5]; ~20 mgf inferred from resonant orbital rocking of a 50-kg satellite [3]; gyroscope weight changes at the edge of chemical-balance noise [23]. Three instruments, three noise floors, one pattern — the same wall the qi-measurement and torsion-telecommunication syntheses found: *the claimed effect sits perennially within two orders of magnitude of a known artifact class.* That does not prove the effects are artifacts. It does mean the next decisive instrument, not the next theory, is what this field needs.

## 7. Open Questions

1. **The Yubileiny telemetry.** The raw attitude/telemetry data behind the 20-mgf estimate is the single most valuable unreleased dataset in this ledger. Who holds it — NII KS (Menshikov's institute), Roscosmos? A reanalysis with modern system-identification would either shrink the residual or force the issue.
2. **Has any inertioid been tested on a Dresden-class balance?** The microwave devices got their null; the mechanical ones never faced the same instrument generation. A battery-powered inertioid on an inverted counterbalanced pendulum in vacuum is a buildable, publishable experiment either way.
3. **Is the nonholonomic argument closed?** Shipov's derivation [2] grants that constraint curvature permits translation; the conservation objection re-enters through the reaction on the constraint. A clean modern treatment (geometric mechanics, not 1970s formalism) would settle what the mathematics actually permits — the Vlasov simulations [4] suggest the Russian scene itself is still undecided.
4. **The Bartini bridge.** The Moscow school cites Bartini's L–T system as its dimensional foundation [19][20]; the same Bartini is a legend of Soviet aviation. Where is his own drive text — did the "red baron" publish the propulsion corollary, or did the school infer it? The corpus needs his primary papers, not expositions of them.
5. **Archive completion.** Tolchin's 1977 book [1] and the Dresden SpaceDrive corpus [22] are both proposed in the family ledger — the foundational claim and the strongest countermeasure, currently both absent from the shelf.
