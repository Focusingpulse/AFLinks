# The Practice Layer: How Radiesthesia Travels — Missionary Manuals, Engineering Faculties, and the Torsion-Vocabulary Drift

*Synthesis by Drunvalo — 2026-10-11*

---

## Abstract

With the October 2026 batches the archive's radiesthesia cluster crossed a density threshold: roughly sixty works in about twenty languages, from an 1854 skeptical treatise to a 2026 Czech e-zin, covering a single practice — the detection of hidden water, objects, and "fields" by a human operator with a rod, pendulum, or bare hands. At this density the interesting question is no longer *whether the effect is real* (the verification-rail synthesis carries that ledger [30]) but **how the practice itself survives and spreads without a single accredited institution behind it.** Read as one corpus, the cluster answers its own question: radiesthesia travels as *pedagogy* (manuals, correspondence courses, missionary handbooks), it periodically collides with *engineering faculties* (Zürich, Delft, Oulu, TNO, Québec), it lives day-to-day as *applied professional craft* (clinics, factories, expert reports), and — the newest signal — it is undergoing a **vocabulary drift**: the mechanism-language of the practice layer is switching from the French *ondes de forme* to the Russian *torsion field*, not through the theory debate traced in the form-field-continuum synthesis [31] but through shop-floor documents: radionic tables, geobiology checklists, and refrigeration trade press. This synthesis maps the three carrier classes, names the drift, follows the Egyptian thread back to Cairo, and keeps the skeptical ledger inside the corpus where the corpus itself keeps it.

## 1. Three Carrier Classes, One Practice

The cluster sorts into three transmission classes, each with its own document genre:

The distribution is the finding. The **Latin and Slavic languages produce the manuals and the clinics**; the **Germanic and Nordic languages produce the controlled tests**. This is not a value judgment — it is a division of documentary labor visible in the corpus itself, and it predicts where each kind of knowledge lives: the practice knowledge is in the pedagogical line, the metrology is in the Northern belt, and neither side reads the other much. The archive now holds both, which is precisely what makes the cluster useful.

## 2. The Transmission Machine: Radiesthesia as Pedagogy

The French founding is well traced elsewhere in the archive (the form-wave-lineage synthesis [32]); the short version is that a priest, Abbé Bouly, coined the word *radiesthésie* in 1930 [1], and a practitioner-priest, Abbé Mermet, became its saint of method [2]. What the new additions make visible is the *machinery* that carried the practice out of France and kept it alive for a century without a university behind it: **the teachable manual.**

Three French documents show the machine's gears:

- **Bourdoux's *Notions Pratiques de Radiesthésie pour les Missionnaires*** [3] — a manual written by a former missionary in Mato Grosso *for use in the missions*, through at least five editions. Radiesthesia here is missionary infrastructure: a water-finding and diagnostic technique carried overseas by a religious network, taught in the field, revised per edition. The clerical-propagation lane that began with Bouly and Mermet becomes, in Bourdoux, an export technology.
- **Claudel's *Manuel élémentaire de Radiesthésie et Téléradiésthésie*** [4] — a ten-week correspondence course: the practice formatted as distance education, week by week, exercise by exercise. The reprint publisher's note that the source has become "impossible to find" marks it as the rarity-recovery tier — pedagogy that survived only because someone reprinted it.
- **Le Gall's *Toute la Radiesthésie en neuf leçons* (1985)** [5] — the nine-lesson format: the practice as evening-class curriculum.

The vernacular layer then reissues the same machine in every language the archive covers. Romania has **Manolea's *Manual de Radiestezie*** [6], a practical handbook with the author's own anatomical drawings and a preface by a professor. Russia has **Ermakov's *Book of the Beginning Dowser* (2007)** [7] — and, far more remarkably, the Soviet-academy monograph: **Valdmanis, Dolacis, Kalnin, *Lozokhodstvo: vekovaya zagadka* (Riga: Zinatne, 1979)** [8], an instrumental and biophysical investigation of the dowsing effect published by the Latvian Academy's own press — the closest thing the corpus has to a state-science treatment from inside the practice tradition. Poland has Sitkowski's manuals [9] and Kiszkowski's *Radiesthesia — Remarks of a Believing Physicist* (Studia Gdańskie, 2007) [39], the physicist-believer genre in one title. Brazil runs a whole course ecosystem [40]. Costa Rica contributes the ethnographic mirror: the University of Costa Rica's study of the *Zahorí* — the water-witcher's beliefs and the radiesthetist's sensitivity — treating the practice as living folklore to be documented, which is also a form of transmission [22].

The pattern across all of them: **the manual is the institution.** Where there is no faculty, no journal with impact factor, no licensure, the practice reproduces through documents that say *do this, then this, then check.* That is why the corpus's pedagogical layer is so much larger than its theoretical one — and why a translation pipeline that captures manuals, as Forge's does, captures the tradition's actual circulatory system.

## 3. The Northern Testing Belt

Against the pedagogical line stands a second geography: the engineering faculties and public institutes of the North, which keep taking the practice seriously enough to test — and whose tests keep producing instruments.

- **Oulu, Finland (1976–1983)** [10]: a controlled dowsing study run jointly by the university's departments of *public health and biophysics*, published with an English summary (ISBN 951-98841-0-6). Seven years of institutional attention — the longest single-university engagement in the corpus.
- **ETH Zürich** [11]: Gassmann's *Report on Experiments with the Dowsing Rod* — the Swiss polytechnic's brush with the rod.
- **TNO / Royal Netherlands Academy (1955)** [12]: a working group of the Royal Netherlands Academy of Sciences testing dowsing paths against livestock health in **19 Frisian cattle sheds** in the winter of 1952–53 — including a trial with **monozygotic twin calves** at Utrecht's veterinary faculty. The dowsing rod for agriculture, examined at the highest Dutch scientific level, seventy years ago.
- **Sweden** [13]: the psi-track experiments (Andersson/Tellefsen/Jacobson).
- **TU Delft (2004)** [14]: the most revealing document of the belt. The university journal *Delta* reports that two Civil Engineering & Geosciences researchers, prof.ir. Cor van Kruijsdijk and dr.ir. Evert Slob, designed dowsing experiments after a civil engineer who dowsed, Rinus Spruit, knocked on their door. The framing sentence is the whole engineering stance in one line:

> «Met een wichelroede in de hand wordt de mens meetinstrument voor het bepalen van energievelden.»
> *"With a dowsing rod in hand, the human being becomes the measuring instrument for determining energy fields."* [14]

And because an engineer cannot leave a human measuring instrument alone, they tried to delete him:

> «Om de mens, die nodig is tijdens het wichelroede lopen, te vervangen, hebben onderzoekers een houten wichelroede in piramidevorm gemaakt.»
> *"To replace the human needed during dowsing, the researchers built a wooden dowsing rod in the form of a pyramid."* [14]

The TU Delft team aligned a wooden **pyramid** to magnetic north, hung metal foil on one face, coupled it through a capacitor to ground, and measured the voltage continuously — the dowser replaced by a form antenna. Note what just happened: an engineering faculty, trying to objectify dowsing, independently reinvented the form-wave instrument (pyramid as emitter-receiver) that French physical radiesthesia had asserted since Chaumery. Nobody at Delft was citing de Belizal; the engineering logic converged on the same shape.

- **INRS Québec (2025–2026)** [15]: the current, best-designed test — a 100 m² excavated Battleship grid, iron vs. plastic pipes, empty vs. water-filled, 54 participants (27 experienced, 27 trained novices), co-designed with a practitioner, explicitly built to discriminate Rocard's magnetic sub-hypothesis rather than re-confirm the Munich global null. Results at EGU26.
- **AFSCET (2025)** [16]: Bricage's meta-analysis asking the association's own question — can dowsing practice be tested as a useful prediction method — from inside the French practitioner milieu.

The belt's convergent finding is subtle and worth stating plainly: **the global effect under blind conditions is null to weak, but the contact between dowsing and engineering never leaves empty-handed.** It produces a pyramid antenna at Delft, a discrimination grid at Québec, a twin-calf protocol at Utrecht. The institutional tests are best read as instrumentation research funded by a question.

## 4. The Applied Layer: Clinics, Factories, Expert Reports

Between the manuals and the tests sits the layer where radiesthesia actually earns its keep — and where the documents have the most unusual genres.

**The Egyptian peer-reviewed application.** In 2021 the *International Design Journal* (Egypt) published **Zainab Abdel Hafiz Farghaly et al., "The effect of employing Radiesthesia to improve the performance of the sewing workers in readymade garments industry"** [20] — a professor of manufacturing applying radiesthesia to worker performance in the garment industry, in a design journal, with a DOI. Whatever its methods section shows, this is the practice crossing into peer-reviewed industrial ergonomics — from an Egyptian university, which matters for §6.

**The Brazilian clinic.** The ARACÊ journal's experience report on **implementing radiesthesia/radionics in an integrative-medicine outpatient clinic in São Paulo** [19] — the practice as public-health-adjacent service, documented in the experience-report genre that Brazilian integrative medicine uses for acupuncture and reiki alike.

**The German expert report.** Padligur's *Gutachten einer radiästhetischen Bohrpunktbestimmung* [17] is a **bore-point determination written as a formal survey report**: survey area, geology, hydrology, water veins, a practice example from Westfalenpark. The *Gutachten* is the German document-genre of expert opinion — the form an engineer signs. Radiesthesia here wears the full document-form of consulting engineering; the genre itself is the claim to professional standing.

**The professional journal.** The Swiss VRGS issue *RR 3/2025 Wasserader* [18] — a trade journal for working dowsers, whose editorial opens, in the genre's own voice: *"Water is an organism and acts planetarily: a being with consciousness and memory."* Inside the practice, that sentence is not metaphor; it is the professional cosmology.

**The anomaly-center measurement.** From the Ukrainian SRCAA "Zond" anthology: Kibitkin's **measurement of source border fields using dowsing methodology** [21] — dowsing operationalized as a field-mapping instrument by a research center that treats anomalies as its beat.

## 5. The Vocabulary Drift: From *Ondes de Forme* to *Campi Torsionali*

The French physical-radiesthesia tradition built its mechanism-vocabulary between 1930 and 1990: *ondes de forme*, *physique micro-vibratoire*, the colored emanations of Chaumery and de Belizal [32]. The Belgian Servranx house still sells that vocabulary — the new reissue of *Constructions des appareils d'ondes de formes soi-même* [23] is DIY form-wave apparatus craft, unchanged.

But across the practice layer the archive now documents a different mechanism-language moving in — the Russian one. Four independent witnesses:

1. **Italian radionics** now sells **"tavole radioniche torsionali"** — active radionic tables grounded explicitly in the Cartan→Shipov lineage:

> «Nel 1913 il fisico francese Eli Cartan dimostrò per primo che il flusso di spazio e tempo possedeva in se stesso anche un movimento di rotazione o spiraleforme conosciuto come torsione. […] Nel 1993 il fisico russo Gennadij Shipov aveva provato teoricamente le idee di Cartan.»
> *"In 1913 the French physicist Élie Cartan was the first to show that the flow of space and time itself possessed a rotational or spiral movement known as torsion. […] In 1993 the Russian physicist Gennady Shipov gave theoretical proof of Cartan's ideas."* [24]

The same page lists the classic Akimov-school properties — zero field energy, information transmitted without energy transfer, intensity independent of distance, unshieldable — as the operating basis of its commercial tables (DNA-repair cards, 528 Hz holographic chips). This is the torsion vocabulary fully absorbed into a *product line*.

2. **Italian geobiology**: Benda's overview treats *geopatie, reticoli Hartmann e Curry, campi torsionali* — torsion fields as one item on the standard geobiology checklist, next to the Hartmann grid [25].

3. **Polish trade press**: a refrigeration-industry article explains energy savings in cooling systems by way of "mysterious torsion waves" (*zagadkowe fale torsyjne*) [26] — the vocabulary at work in HVAC marketing.

4. **Czech psychotronics**: the e-zin *Cesty psychotroniky* passes on the origin story of the whole vocabulary, and with it the corpus's most interesting bridge figure:

> «Britské ministerstvo obrany zaujalo tvrzení ruských vědců, že rotující těleso vytváří pole, které prostupuje všemi materiály.»
> *"The British Ministry of Defence was struck by the Russian scientists' claim that a rotating body creates a field that penetrates all materials."* [27]

The MoD asked **Vincent Reddish** — former director of the Edinburgh observatory, developer of a southern-sky telescope, and *a passionate dowser* — to evaluate the claim. Reddish ran experiments and found the rod reacts to overhead wires **whether or not current flows through them**, mapped the wires' "sphere of influence," and observed interference patterns that, in his reading, behaved more like waves *reflected* from the lines than emitted by them. An astronomer-royal-grade scientist doing dowsing fieldwork for the Ministry of Defence: the torsion story's Western entry point runs straight through the practice layer, decades before the tables went torsional.

And in France itself, the Risy field notebooks simply use both vocabularies at the same workbench — the form wave as the thing handled, the *champ de torsion* as its explanation [34].

The drift's significance is easy to miss if you only watch the theory level, where the French–Russian meeting remains unfinished [31]. **At the practice level the meeting is over and the Russian vocabulary won the mechanism slot.** The form wave is still what practitioners *do*; the torsion field is what they now *call the cause*. Whether this relabeling changes any practice — whether a "torsional" table is built, sold, or used differently from a form-wave graph — is an open empirical question the archive cannot yet answer, and one of the best next questions this synthesis leaves on the table.

## 6. The Egyptian Return

One thread deserves its own section because it closes a loop the archive has been tracing for months. The Egyptian pendulum was the founding artifact of French physical radiesthesia — Chaumery and de Belizal's instrument, tied to the negative-green carrier line that runs from their 1936 patent to BioGeometry [33]. The thread then appears in Ledein's *The Astonishing Possibilities of the Egyptian Pendulum and the Pyramid* [28], keeping the Egyptian-form claim alive in the French popular line.

The October batches return the thread to Egypt itself, in two utterly different registers: **Farghaly's peer-reviewed industrial application from an Egyptian design faculty** [20], and the **Arabic introduction to Ibrahim Karim's BioGeometry** circulating in Arabic forums [29]. The shape-resonance lineage that left Cairo for Paris in the 1930s is now present in Egypt simultaneously as academic ergonomics and as a native shape-science with its own Arabic pedagogical literature. Of all the corpus's convergences, this one is the most structural: a practice exported, theorized abroad, and re-imported twice — once as science, once as tradition.

## 7. The Skeptical Ledger, Carried Inside

This corpus is unusual among the archive's clusters in that it ships with its own critique, and any honest synthesis must carry that critique intact:

- The encyclopedic layer frames the practice as outside science: Spanish Wikipedia's *Radiestesia*, framed "as incompatible with current scientific knowledge" [35]; the Dutch Skepsis museum's radionics exhibit, which documents the devices and the claim that personal items take on their wearer's characteristics, as a museum of error [36].
- The practitioners carry the caveat themselves: the Greek Fotiadis, writing *for* a dowsing audience, explicitly warns **how expectation influences the result** [37] — an ideomotor admission from inside the practice. Pietrzak's Polish school notes that the Bovis scale "closes at 10,000 units" [38] — an insider flag that the field's favorite ruler is a convention, not a constant.
- The Northern belt's global results under blind conditions are null to weak (§3); the INRS design [15] exists precisely because the Munich-style global null stopped being informative and only sub-hypotheses (Rocard's magnetic line) remain live.

The verification-rail synthesis [30] traced this ledger from Chevreul's 1854 experimental psychology to Argenton 2007. The point for the present map: **the skeptical rail is not an attack on this corpus from outside; it is one of the corpus's own carrier classes**, and the practice has visibly absorbed it — the manuals now teach protocol discipline *because* the tests kept showing what expectation does.

## 8. Convergences and Next Questions

**Three convergent claims now have multi-tradition, multi-language support in the corpus:**

1. **The human operator is replaceable in principle.** Independently asserted by TU Delft's pyramid antenna [14], the Russian instrumental-detector line (via the Reddish/MoD story [27] and the Tomsk detectors in the torsion-telecommunication synthesis [41]), Kibitkin's field measurements [21], and the torsional-table product line [24]. Every tradition that touches the practice eventually tries to delete the human. None has conclusively succeeded; the attempt itself is the convergence.
2. **Geometry emits.** The Egyptian pendulum, the form-wave apparatus, the Delft pyramid, the torsional tables, and BioGeometry's shapes all assert that form itself is an active parameter. This is the corpus's deepest continuity and the one with the clearest counter-ledger (§7).
3. **Protocol is what transmits.** Manuals on four continents converge on the same teaching elements — the witness, the scale, the disciplined question — because that is what a practice without institutions needs in order to reproduce.

**Next questions the archive can now support:**

- Can Farghaly's ergonomics design [20] be replicated outside Egypt? It is the only peer-reviewed industrial application in the cluster; a replication attempt (or a methods critique) would be high-value.
- Does the torsion relabeling change practice? Compare construction instructions for pre-1990 form-wave graphs [23] vs. post-2010 "torsional" tables [24] — if the only difference is vocabulary, the drift is marketing; if the geometries changed, it is physics migration.
- What did Reddish actually publish? His dowsing studies are referenced [27] but not in the archive; the MoD evaluation context makes them the most interesting missing documents in this cluster. (Logged as an upgrade target.)
- Does the INRS magnetic sub-hypothesis survive EGU26? [15] — the one live experimental question with a date on it.
